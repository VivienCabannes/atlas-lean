    Fourier transform on finite groups

    The non-commutative Fourier transform \(\hat P(\rho) = \sum_s P(s)\rho(s)\) of a function on a finite group at a representation \(\rho\): it turns convolution into composition, vanishes on the uniform distribution at non-trivial irreducibles, is a scalar on class functions (Schur's lemma), and satisfies the Plancherel formula. It is the tool behind rates of convergence of random walks on finite groups.

    DefinitionConvolution on a finite group#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.RepresentationTheory.FourierTransformFiniteGroup.conv

    For functions \(P, Q : G \to R\) on a finite group, with values in a semiring \(R\),

    \[(P * Q)(s) = \sum_{t \in G} P(s t^{-1})\, Q(t).\]

    For probabilities: to reach \(s\), first pick \(t\) from \(Q\), then \(s t^{-1}\) from \(P\). In general \(P * Q \ne Q * P\). The \(k\)-step law of the random walk driven by a probability \(Q\) is the convolution power \(Q^{*k}\).

    On complex-valued functions this is the convolution already in the graph, by agreement with the repository's convolution; it is restated for values in any semiring so that real probabilities can be convolved and compared in total variation.

    def conv {R : Type*} [Semiring R] (P Q : G → R) : G → R :=
  fun s => ∑ t, P (s * t⁻¹) * Q t
    Background
    Background

    DefinitionFourier transform at a representation#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.RepresentationTheory.FourierTransformFiniteGroup.fourier

    For a function \(P : G \to \mathbb{C}\) on a finite group and a representation \(\rho\) of \(G\) on a complex vector space \(V\), the Fourier transform of \(P\) at \(\rho\) is the endomorphism

    \[\hat P(\rho) = \sum_{s \in G} P(s)\, \rho(s)\]

    of \(V\). Diaconis and Shahshahani write it \(\rho(P)\). At the trivial one-dimensional representation it is the total mass of \(P\); for an abelian group and a character \(\psi\) it is the discrete Fourier transform \(\sum_s \psi(s) P(s)\).

    noncomputable def fourier (ρ : Representation ℂ G V) (P : G → ℂ) : Module.End ℂ V :=
  ∑ s, P s • ρ s

    TheoremFourier transform of a class function at an irreducible representation#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.RepresentationTheory.FourierTransformFiniteGroup.fourier_eq_smul_one_of_isClassFunction

    Let \(\rho\) be an irreducible representation of a finite group \(G\) on a finite-dimensional complex vector space of dimension \(d_\rho\), with character \(\chi_\rho\). If \(P : G \to \mathbb{C}\) is constant on conjugacy classes, \(P(h g h^{-1}) = P(g)\), then

    \[\hat P(\rho) = \Bigl(\frac{1}{d_\rho} \sum_{s \in G} P(s)\, \chi_\rho(s)\Bigr) I .\]

    Diaconis and Shahshahani write the same sum grouped by conjugacy classes, \(\frac{1}{d_\rho} \sum_i P_i\, n_i\, \chi_i\). Proof: \(\hat P(\rho)\) commutes with every \(\rho(h)\) (reindex by conjugation), hence is a scalar by Schur's lemma; take traces.

    This is what makes conjugation-invariant walks, such as random transpositions, tractable: their transforms are scalars, and convolution powers become powers of numbers.

    theorem fourier_eq_smul_one_of_isClassFunction [FiniteDimensional ℂ V]
    (ρ : Representation ℂ G V) [ρ.IsIrreducible] (P : G → ℂ)
    (hP : ∀ g h : G, P (h * g * h⁻¹) = P g) :
    fourier ρ P = ((Module.finrank ℂ V : ℂ)⁻¹ * ∑ s, P s * ρ.character s) • 1
    noncomputable def fourier (ρ : Representation ℂ G V) (P : G → ℂ) : Module.End ℂ V :=
  ∑ s, P s • ρ s

    TheoremThe uniform distribution has zero Fourier transform at non-trivial irreducibles#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.RepresentationTheory.FourierTransformFiniteGroup.fourier_uniform_eq_zero

    Let \(\rho\) be an irreducible representation of a finite group \(G\) on a finite-dimensional complex vector space, and suppose some \(\rho(g)\) is not the identity. Then the Fourier transform of the uniform distribution \(U(s) = 1/|G|\) at \(\rho\) is zero:

    \[\hat U(\rho) = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{s} \rho(s) = 0 .\]

    Proof: \(S = \sum_s \rho(s)\) commutes with every \(\rho(h)\), so it is a scalar \(c\) by Schur's lemma; and \(\rho(h) S = S\) for every \(h\), so \(c \ne 0\) would force every \(\rho(h)\) to be the identity. Diaconis derives the same fact from the orthogonality of matrix coefficients (Serre, Proposition 4).

    theorem fourier_uniform_eq_zero [FiniteDimensional ℂ V] (ρ : Representation ℂ G V)
    [ρ.IsIrreducible] (hρ : ∃ g, ρ g ≠ 1) :
    fourier ρ (fun _ => ((Fintype.card G : ℂ))⁻¹) = 0
    noncomputable def fourier (ρ : Representation ℂ G V) (P : G → ℂ) : Module.End ℂ V :=
  ∑ s, P s • ρ s

    TheoremPlancherel formula on a finite group#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.RepresentationTheory.FourierPlancherel.plancherel

    Let \(G\) be a finite group and \(V_i\) (\(i \in \iota\)) a complete set of irreducible complex representations of \(G\) (pairwise non-isomorphic simple objects of FDRep ℂ G, every simple object isomorphic to one of them), of dimensions \(d_i\). For every \(P : G \to \mathbb{C}\),

    \[\sum_{s \in G} |P(s)|^2 = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_i d_i \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\hat P(V_i)\, \hat P(V_i)^*\bigr), \qquad \hat P(V_i) = \sum_s P(s)\, V_i(s).\]

    The sources take each \(V_i(s)\) unitary; then \(\hat P(V_i)^* = \sum_s \overline{P(s)}\, V_i(s^{-1})\) is the transform of \(s \mapsto \overline{P(s^{-1})}\), which is how the Lean statement writes it without choosing an inner product.

    Proof: expanding the traces, the right side is \(\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{s,t} P(s)\,\overline{P(t^{-1})} \sum_i d_i \chi_i(st)\), and \(\sum_i d_i \chi_i(g) = |G|\,[g = 1]\) leaves \(\sum_s |P(s)|^2\). Taking \(P\) the point mass at the identity gives \(\sum_i d_i^2 = |G|\).

    theorem plancherel {ι : Type} [Fintype ι] (V : ι → FDRep ℂ G) [∀ i, Simple (V i)]
    (hinj : ∀ i j, Nonempty (V i ≅ V j) → i = j)
    (hsurj : ∀ W : FDRep ℂ G, Simple W → ∃ i, Nonempty (W ≅ V i))
    (P : G → ℂ) :
    ((∑ s, ‖P s‖ ^ 2 : ℝ) : ℂ) = ((Fintype.card G : ℂ))⁻¹ *
      ∑ i, (Module.finrank ℂ (V i) : ℂ) *
        LinearMap.trace ℂ (V i)
          (fourier (V i).ρ P * fourier (V i).ρ (fun s => (starRingEnd ℂ) (P s⁻¹)))
    noncomputable def fourier (ρ : Representation ℂ G V) (P : G → ℂ) : Module.End ℂ V :=
  ∑ s, P s • ρ s

    TheoremCharacter of the regular representation#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.RepresentationTheory.FourierPlancherel.sum_finrank_mul_character

    Let \(V_i\) (\(i \in \iota\)) be a complete set of irreducible complex representations of a finite group \(G\) (pairwise non-isomorphic simple objects of FDRep ℂ G, every simple object isomorphic to one of them), with dimensions \(d_i\) and characters \(\chi_i\). Then for every \(g \in G\)

    \[\sum_i d_i\, \chi_i(g) = \begin{cases} |G| & g = 1, \\ 0 & g \ne 1. \end{cases}\]

    The left side is the character of the regular representation, which contains each irreducible representation \(d_i\) times; Diaconis's proof of Fourier inversion (Saint-Flour notes, Theorem 4.1(a)) rests on this identity, citing Serre. Here it is proved from the completeness of irreducible characters among class functions and their orthonormality: the class function \(|G|\,\delta_1\) is a combination \(\sum_i c_i \chi_i\), and pairing with \(\chi_j\) gives \(c_j = \chi_j(1) = d_j\).

    theorem sum_finrank_mul_character [DecidableEq G] {ι : Type} [Fintype ι] (V : ι → FDRep ℂ G)
    [∀ i, Simple (V i)] (hinj : ∀ i j, Nonempty (V i ≅ V j) → i = j)
    (hsurj : ∀ W : FDRep ℂ G, Simple W → ∃ i, Nonempty (W ≅ V i)) (g : G) :
    ∑ i, (Module.finrank ℂ (V i) : ℂ) * (V i).character g =
      if g = 1 then (Fintype.card G : ℂ) else 0

    TheoremTrace of a product of Fourier transforms#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.RepresentationTheory.FourierPlancherel.trace_fourier_mul_fourier

    For every representation ρ with character χ_ρ = Tr ∘ ρ, Tr(P̂(ρ) Q̂(ρ)) = ∑_s ∑_t P(s) Q(t) χ_ρ(s t), by linearity of the trace (Mathlib's LinearMap.trace is 0 on infinite-dimensional spaces, where both sides vanish). A direct computation from the definitions of the transform and of the character in Diaconis and Shahshahani 1981, Section 2; it is the first step of the proof of plancherel.

    theorem trace_fourier_mul_fourier {V : Type*} [AddCommGroup V] [Module ℂ V]
    (ρ : Representation ℂ G V) (P Q : G → ℂ) :
    LinearMap.trace ℂ V (fourier ρ P * fourier ρ Q) =
      ∑ s, ∑ t, P s * Q t * ρ.character (s * t)
    noncomputable def fourier (ρ : Representation ℂ G V) (P : G → ℂ) : Module.End ℂ V :=
  ∑ s, P s • ρ s
