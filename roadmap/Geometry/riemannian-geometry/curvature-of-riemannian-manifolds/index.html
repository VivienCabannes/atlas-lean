    Curvature of Riemannian manifolds

    DefinitionCurvature expression of a covariant derivative on extensions#✓fully proved

    CovariantDerivative.extendCurvature

    Let \(\nabla\) be a covariant derivative on a vector bundle \(V\) over a manifold \(M\), let \(x \in M\), let \(u, v \in T_x M\) and let \(\sigma \in V_x\). Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be the vector fields on \(M\) whose coordinates in the preferred local trivialization of \(TM\) at \(x\) are constant, equal to those of \(u\) and \(v\), on the base set of that trivialization, with a fixed but unspecified value at each point outside it; let \(S\) be the section of \(V\) obtained in the same way from \(\sigma\) and the preferred local trivialization of \(V\) at \(x\). The curvature expression of \(\nabla\) at \(x\) on \(u\), \(v\), \(\sigma\) is the vector

    \[R(u, v)\sigma = \nabla_u (\nabla_Y S) - \nabla_v (\nabla_X S) - \nabla_{[X, Y](x)} S \in V_x,\]

    where \(\nabla_Y S\) is the section \(y \mapsto \nabla_{Y(y)} S\) and \([X, Y]\) is the Lie bracket.

    When \(\nabla\) sends \(C^2\) sections to \(C^1\) sections (for instance the Levi-Civita connection of a \(C^\infty\) metric), this is the curvature tensor \(R(X, Y) = \nabla_X \nabla_Y - \nabla_Y \nabla_X - \nabla_{[X, Y]}\) applied to \(u\), \(v\), \(\sigma\), and it does not depend on the extensions. For other connections it can depend on values of \(\nabla\) that the axioms of a covariant derivative leave unconstrained.

    def extendCurvature (cov : CovariantDerivative I F V) (x : M) (u v : TangentSpace I x) (σ : V x) :
    V x :=
  cov (fun y ↦ cov (extend F σ) y (extend E v y)) x u
    - cov (fun y ↦ cov (extend F σ) y (extend E u y)) x v
    - cov (extend F σ) x (mlieBracket I (extend E u) (extend E v) x)

    DefinitionRicci contraction of the curvature expression#✓fully proved

    CovariantDerivative.extendRicci

    Let \(M\) be a manifold modelled on a finite-dimensional normed space over a nontrivially normed field \(\mathbb{K}\), let \(\nabla\) be a covariant derivative on the tangent bundle of \(M\), let \(R\) be its curvature expression on extensions, let \(x \in M\) and let \(v, w \in T_x M\). With \(e_1, \dots, e_n\) the fixed basis of \(T_x M\) chosen for every finite-dimensional space and \(e_1^*, \dots, e_n^*\) its coordinate functionals, the Ricci contraction is

    \[\operatorname{Ric}(v, w) = \sum_{i=1}^n e_i^*(R(e_i, v) w) \in \mathbb{K},\]

    the trace of \(z \mapsto R(z, v) w\) computed in that basis (Lang, Lecture Notes on Riemannian Geometry, Def. 2.7). No metric is involved.

    When \(z \mapsto R(z, v) w\) is linear, as it is for a connection that sends \(C^2\) sections to \(C^1\) sections, this is its trace and does not depend on the basis.

    def extendRicci (cov : CovariantDerivative I E (TangentSpace I : M → Type _)) (x : M)
    (v w : TangentSpace I x) : 𝕜 :=
  ∑ i, (Module.finBasis 𝕜 (TangentSpace I x)).coord i
    (cov.extendCurvature x (Module.finBasis 𝕜 (TangentSpace I x) i) v w)

    DefinitionRiemann curvature endomorphism#✓fully proved

    Manifold.riemannCurvature

    For a point \(x\) of a \(C^\infty\) Riemannian manifold \((M, g)\) with Levi-Civita connection \(\nabla\), and tangent vectors \(u, v, w \in T_x M\),

    \[R(u, v) w = (\nabla_X \nabla_Y W - \nabla_Y \nabla_X W - \nabla_{[X, Y]} W)(x),\]

    where \(X\), \(Y\), \(W\) are the vector fields extending \(u\), \(v\), \(w\) that have constant coordinates in the preferred chart at \(x\). Since the Levi-Civita connection of a \(C^\infty\) metric is \(C^\infty\), this is the textbook curvature endomorphism and does not depend on the extensions.

    This is the sign convention of [Lang, Def. 2.1] and [Gud, Def. 8.8]; the opposite sign convention is also common.

    def riemannCurvature (x : M) (u v w : TangentSpace I x) : TangentSpace I x :=
  (CovariantDerivative.leviCivitaConnection I M).extendCurvature x u v w

    Manifold.riemannCurvature_antisymm

    Let \((M, g)\) be a \(C^\infty\) Riemannian manifold. Its Riemann curvature endomorphism \(R\) is antisymmetric in its first two arguments: \(R(u, v) w = -R(v, u) w\) for every point \(x \in M\) and all tangent vectors \(u, v, w \in T_x M\).

    theorem riemannCurvature_antisymm (x : M) (u v w : TangentSpace I x) :
    riemannCurvature I x u v w = - riemannCurvature I x v u w

  (CovariantDerivative.leviCivitaConnection I M).extendCurvature x u v w

    DefinitionSectional curvature#✓fully proved

    Manifold.sectionalCurvature

    Let \((M, g)\) be a \(C^\infty\) Riemannian manifold modeled on a finite-dimensional real space (boundary and corners allowed), with Riemann curvature endomorphism \(R\), and let \(x \in M\). The sectional curvature of tangent vectors \(u, v \in T_x M\) is

    \[K(u, v) = \frac{\langle R(u, v) v, u \rangle}{\|u\|^2 \|v\|^2 - \langle u, v \rangle^2},\]

    the sectional curvature of the plane spanned by \(u\) and \(v\) when they are linearly independent ([Lang, Def. 2.4], [Gud, Def. 8.14]). When \(u\) and \(v\) are linearly dependent the denominator vanishes and \(K(u, v)\) is \(0\) by definition.

    With the sign convention used here for \(R\), the round unit sphere has \(K = 1\).

    def sectionalCurvature (x : M) (u v : TangentSpace I x) : ℝ :=
  inner ℝ (riemannCurvature I x u v v) u / (‖u‖ ^ 2 * ‖v‖ ^ 2 - inner ℝ u v ^ 2)
    def riemannCurvature (x : M) (u v w : TangentSpace I x) : TangentSpace I x :=
  (CovariantDerivative.leviCivitaConnection I M).extendCurvature x u v w

    Manifold.sectionalCurvature_smul_right

    Let \((M, g)\) be a \(C^\infty\) Riemannian manifold modeled on a finite-dimensional real space (boundary and corners allowed), and let \(x \in M\), \(u \in T_x M\) and \(c \in \mathbb{R}\).     Manifold.sectionalCurvature_self

    For every point \(x\) of a \(C^\infty\) Riemannian manifold \((M, g)\) and every tangent vector \(u \in T_x M\), the sectional curvature formula \(K\) gives \(K(u, u) = 0\): its denominator \(\|u\|^2 \|u\|^2 - \langle u, u \rangle^2\) vanishes, where \(\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle\) and \(\|\cdot\|\) are the inner product and norm on \(T_x M\) given by \(g\), and a quotient by \(0\) is taken to be \(0\).

    DefinitionRicci curvature#✓fully proved

    Manifold.ricci

    For a point \(x\) of a \(C^\infty\) Riemannian manifold \((M, g)\) and tangent vectors \(v, w \in T_x M\), the Ricci curvature is \(\operatorname{Ric}_x(v, w) = \sum_i e_i^*(R(e_i, v) w)\), where \(R\) is the Riemann curvature endomorphism of the Levi-Civita connection of \(g\), \((e_i)\) is a fixed basis of \(T_x M\) and \((e_i^*)\) is its dual basis; the contraction itself uses no metric. Since \(z \mapsto R(z, v) w\) is linear, this is its trace and does not depend on the basis ([Lang, Def. 2.7]). The unit \(n\)-sphere has \(\operatorname{Ric} = (n - 1) g\).

    def ricci (x : M) (v w : TangentSpace I x) : ℝ :=
  (CovariantDerivative.leviCivitaConnection I M).extendRicci x v w

    Manifold.scalarCurvature

    The scalar curvature of a \(C^\infty\) Riemannian manifold \((M, g)\) at a point \(x\) is \(\sum_i \operatorname{Ric}_x(e_i, e_i)\), the metric trace of the Ricci curvature \(\operatorname{Ric}\), where \((e_i)\) is a fixed orthonormal basis of \(T_x M\) ([Gud, Def. 8.32]). The unit \(n\)-sphere has scalar curvature \(n (n - 1)\).

    def scalarCurvature (x : M) : ℝ :=
  ∑ i, ricci I x (stdOrthonormalBasis ℝ (TangentSpace I x) i)
    (stdOrthonormalBasis ℝ (TangentSpace I x) i)

    Manifold.IsEinstein

    A \(C^\infty\) Riemannian manifold \((M, g)\) is Einstein when there is a real constant \(\lambda\) such that \(\operatorname{Ric}_x(v, w) = \lambda \, g_x(v, w)\) for every point \(x \in M\) and all tangent vectors \(v, w \in T_x M\), where \(\operatorname{Ric}\) is the Ricci curvature ([Lang], Einstein manifolds). Ricci-flat is the case \(\lambda = 0\).
