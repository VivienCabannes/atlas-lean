    DehnInvariant.dehnInvariant

    The Dehn invariant of a list of edges is the sum, over the edges, of \(\ell \otimes \theta\) in the Dehn group, where \(\ell\) is the length of the edge and \(\theta\) its dihedral angle taken modulo \(2\pi\).

    DehnInvariant.exists_linearMap_of_irrational

    Let \(\alpha\) be a real number such that \(\alpha / \pi\) is irrational. Then there is a \(\mathbb{Q}\)-linear map \(f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) with \(f(\pi) = 0\) and \(f(\alpha) \neq 0\).

    The idea of the proof: \(\alpha\) lies outside the \(\mathbb{Q}\)-span of \(\pi\), and a linear functional on the quotient separates it.

    theorem exists_linearMap_of_irrational {α : ℝ} (hirr : Irrational (α / π)) :
    ∃ f : ℝ →ₗ[ℚ] ℝ, f π = 0 ∧ f α ≠ 0

    DehnInvariant.tmul_ne_zero_of_irrational

    Let \(\ell\) and \(\alpha\) be real numbers with \(\ell \neq 0\) and \(\alpha / \pi\) irrational. Then the pure tensor \(\ell \otimes [\alpha]\) is nonzero in the Dehn group \(\mathbb{R} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} (\mathbb{R} / 2\pi\mathbb{Z})\), where \([\alpha]\) is the class of \(\alpha\) modulo \(2\pi\).

    The proof applies a \(\mathbb{Q}\)-linear functional that vanishes at \(\pi\) but not at \(\alpha\).

    DehnInvariant.dehnInvariant_cube_ne_dehnInvariant_tetrahedron

    Suppose that \(\arccos(1/3) / \pi\) is irrational. Then for all real numbers \(\ell\) and \(\ell'\) with \(\ell' \ne 0\), the edge list of a cube of side \(\ell\) and the edge list of a regular tetrahedron of side \(\ell'\) have different Dehn invariants.

    Since scissors-congruent polyhedra have equal Dehn invariants, under this hypothesis a cube and a regular tetrahedron of equal volume are not scissors-congruent.

    theorem dehnInvariant_cube_ne_dehnInvariant_tetrahedron {ℓ ℓ' : ℝ} (hℓ' : ℓ' ≠ 0)
    (hirr : Irrational (tetrahedralAngle / π)) :
    dehnInvariant (cubeEdges ℓ) ≠ dehnInvariant (tetrahedronEdges ℓ')
