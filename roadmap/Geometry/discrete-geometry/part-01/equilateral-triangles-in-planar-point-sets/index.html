    For a real number \(s\), a finite set \(T\) of points in the Euclidean space \(\mathbb{R}^d\) is an equilateral triangle of side \(s\) when \(T\) has exactly three elements and any two distinct points of \(T\) are at distance \(s\).

    MathlibExt.Geometry.ErdosProblem755Wanted.TUnit

    For natural numbers \(d\) and \(n\), \(T_1(d, n)\) is the supremum in \(\mathbb{N}\), over all sets \(P\) of exactly \(n\) points in the Euclidean space \(\mathbb{R}^d\), of the number of unit equilateral triangles spanned by \(P\) (the three-element subsets of \(P\) whose points are pairwise at distance \(1\)). When such sets exist this is the largest such number; when none exist (only for \(d = 0\) and \(n \ge 2\)) it is \(0\).

    noncomputable def TUnit (d n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  sSup {m : ℕ | ∃ P : Finset (EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin d)),
    P.card = n ∧ unitEquilateralTriangleCount d P = m}
