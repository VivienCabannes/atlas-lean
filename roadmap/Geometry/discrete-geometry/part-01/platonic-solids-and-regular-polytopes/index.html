    Let \(V, E, F, p, q\) be natural numbers with \(V, E, F > 0\) and \(p, q \ge 3\), and let \(l > 0\) and \(d > 0\) be real numbers. Let \(x_0, \dots, x_{V-1}\) be points of the Euclidean space \(\mathbb{R}^3\), let \(K\) be their convex hull and let \(\partial K\) be its topological boundary. Let each edge \(e < E\) be an ordered pair \((a_e, b_e)\) of point indices, with segment \([x_{a_e}, x_{b_e}]\), and let each face \(f < F\) be a list \(\phi_f(0), \dots, \phi_f(p-1)\) of point indices, read cyclically: positions are taken modulo \(p\), the sides of \(f\) are the pairs \((\phi_f(i), \phi_f(i+1))\), and the filled face is the convex hull of \(x_{\phi_f(0)}, \dots, x_{\phi_f(p-1)}\). An edge \(e\) lies on side \(i\) of \(f\) when \((a_e, b_e)\) equals \((\phi_f(i), \phi_f(i+1))\) or \((\phi_f(i+1), \phi_f(i))\). Assume: \(K\) is convex and contains every \(x_i\); no \(x_i\) lies in the convex hull of the \(x_j\) with \(j \ne i\); the differences \(x_i - x_j\) span a space of dimension \(3\); every edge segment has length \(l\); for every face \(f\) and position \(i\), \(\operatorname{dist}(x_{\phi_f(i)}, x_{\phi_f(i+1)}) = l\) and \(\operatorname{dist}(x_{\phi_f(i-1)}, x_{\phi_f(i+1)}) = d\); the indices \(\phi_f(0), \dots, \phi_f(p-1)\) of each face are distinct; no vertex \(x_{\phi_f(k)}\) of a face lies in the convex hull of the other vertices of that face; two sides \((i_1, i_1+1)\) and \((i_2, i_2+1)\) of a face whose four endpoint positions are all different have disjoint segments; some edge lies on each side of every face, and every edge lies on a side of some face; every edge lies on a side of exactly two faces; every edge segment and every filled face is contained in \(K\); the differences of the vertices of each face span a space of dimension at most \(2\); for every face \(f\) there are a nonzero vector \(n\) and a real \(c\) with \(\langle n, x \rangle \le c\) for all \(x \in K\) and \(\langle n, x_{\phi_f(i)} \rangle = c\) for every position \(i\); two edges \(e_1, e_2\) with \((a_{e_1}, b_{e_1})\) equal to \((a_{e_2}, b_{e_2})\) or \((b_{e_2}, a_{e_2})\) are equal; two faces with the same set of indices are equal; every \(x_i\), every edge segment and every filled face is contained in \(\partial K\); every point of \(\partial K\) lies in some filled face; every point index occurs in exactly \(q\) faces; and \(2E = pF\), \(2E = qV\) and \(V - E + F = 2\). Then \((p, q, V, E, F)\) is one of \((3, 3, 4, 6, 4)\) (tetrahedron), \((4, 3, 8, 12, 6)\) (cube), \((3, 4, 6, 12, 8)\) (octahedron), \((5, 3, 20, 30, 12)\) (dodecahedron) or \((3, 5, 12, 30, 20)\) (icosahedron).

    Conversely, each of these five tuples \((p, q, V, E, F)\) is realized: there are points, edges, faces and reals \(l, d > 0\) satisfying all the conditions above, except that the points, edge segments and filled faces are required to lie in \(\partial K\) but not separately in \(K\).

    Euler's relation \(V - E + F = 2\) and the incidence counts \(2E = pF\), \(2E = qV\) are hypotheses, and the classification is up to the tuple \((p, q, V, E, F)\), not up to congruence. Together they give \(1/p + 1/q > 1/2\), which leaves the five tuples; explicit coordinates realize each one.
