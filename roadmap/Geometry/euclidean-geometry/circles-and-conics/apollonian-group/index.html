    Apollonian group

    By Descartes' circle theorem the signed curvatures of four mutually tangent circles form a zero of the Descartes quadratic form \(Q(v) = 2(v_1^2 + v_2^2 + v_3^2 + v_4^2) - (v_1 + v_2 + v_3 + v_4)^2\), a form of signature \((3, 1)\). Replacing one circle of such a configuration by the other circle tangent to the remaining three replaces one curvature \(v_i\) by \(2(\text{sum of the other three}) - v_i\). The four resulting linear maps \(S_1, \dots, S_4\) are the orthogonal reflections of \((\mathbb{R}^4, Q)\) in the hyperplanes \(Q\)-orthogonal to the standard basis vectors, and they generate the Apollonian group, a subgroup of the integral orthogonal group \(O_Q(\mathbb{Z})\). Its orbits stay on the cone \(Q = 0\), and integrality of a starting quadruple propagates along the orbit: this is why four integral curvatures give an integral Apollonian packing.

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Euclidean.ApollonianDescartesQuadruples.descartesForm

    For a quadruple \(v = (v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4)\) over a commutative ring, the Descartes quadratic form is $\(Q(v) = 2(v_1^2 + v_2^2 + v_3^2 + v_4^2) - (v_1 + v_2 + v_3 + v_4)^2 .\)$ A quadruple with \(Q(v) = 0\) is a Descartes quadruple. Over \(\mathbb{R}\) the form has signature \((3, 1)\), so the Descartes quadruples form a light cone. By Descartes' circle theorem the signed curvatures of four mutually tangent circles form a Descartes quadruple; Kontorovich and Oh use the form with an extra factor \(\tfrac12\).

    def descartesForm (v : Fin 4 → R) : R :=
  2 * ∑ i, v i ^ 2 - (∑ i, v i) ^ 2

    DefinitionApollonian flip#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Euclidean.ApollonianDescartesQuadruples.apollonianFlip

    For \(i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}\), the flip \(S_i\) replaces the \(i\)-th entry of a quadruple by twice the sum of the other three entries minus itself: $\(S_i(v)_i = 2 \sum_{j \ne i} v_j - v_i, \qquad S_i(v)_j = v_j \ (j \ne i).\)$ On the curvatures of a Descartes configuration, \(S_i\) replaces the \(i\)-th circle by the other circle tangent to the remaining three. \(S_i\) is multiplication by the matrix that agrees with the identity except in row \(i\), which is \((2, 2, 2, 2)\) with \(-1\) in position \(i\); these are the generators \(S_1, \dots, S_4\) of the Apollonian group.

    def apollonianFlip (i : Fin 4) (v : Fin 4 → R) : Fin 4 → R :=
  Function.update v i (2 * (∑ j, v j - v i) - v i)

    TheoremThe two roots of the Descartes relation#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Euclidean.ApollonianDescartesQuadruples.eq_or_eq_flip_of_descartesForm_update_eq_zero

    Over an integral domain, let \(v\) be a Descartes quadruple and suppose that replacing its \(i\)-th entry by \(x\) again gives a Descartes quadruple. Then \(x = v_i\) or \(x = 2\sum_{j \ne i} v_j - v_i\).

    Geometrically: given three mutually tangent circles of curvatures \(a, b, c\), the curvatures \(d, d'\) of the two circles tangent to all three are the two roots of the Descartes relation in the fourth variable, so \(d + d' = 2(a + b + c)\). The proof is Vieta's formula: with the other three entries fixed, \(Q\) is a monic quadratic polynomial in the \(i\)-th entry whose linear coefficient is \(-2\sum_{j \ne i} v_j\).

    theorem eq_or_eq_flip_of_descartesForm_update_eq_zero [IsDomain R] {v : Fin 4 → R} {i : Fin 4}
    {x : R} (hv : descartesForm v = 0) (hx : descartesForm (Function.update v i x) = 0) :
    x = v i ∨ x = 2 * (∑ j, v j - v i) - v i
    def descartesForm (v : Fin 4 → R) : R :=
  2 * ∑ i, v i ^ 2 - (∑ i, v i) ^ 2

    TheoremThe Apollonian flips are orthogonal reflections#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Euclidean.ApollonianDescartesQuadruples.apollonianFlip_eq_reflection

    Let \(B(u, w) = 2\sum_j u_j w_j - (\sum_j u_j)(\sum_j w_j)\) be the polar form of the Descartes form, so that \(B(v, v) = Q(v)\), and let \(e_i\) be the \(i\)-th standard basis vector, which has \(Q(e_i) = 1\). Then $\(S_i(v) = v - 2B(v, e_i)\, e_i ,\)$ the orthogonal reflection of the quadratic space \((R^4, Q)\) in the hyperplane \(Q\)-orthogonal to \(e_i\); in Lean it is Mathlib's Module.reflection for the linear form \(f = 2B(\cdot, e_i)\), which satisfies \(f(e_i) = 2\). Hence the Apollonian group \(\langle S_1, S_2, S_3, S_4 \rangle\) is a reflection group inside the orthogonal group \(O_Q\). Geometrically (Oh, Section 2) the same group is generated by the inversions in the four circles dual to a Descartes configuration, which act on hyperbolic 3-space as reflections in hyperbolic planes.

    theorem apollonianFlip_eq_reflection (i : Fin 4) (v : Fin 4 → R) :
    apollonianFlip i v = Module.reflection (flipForm_single (R := R) i) v
    def apollonianFlip (i : Fin 4) (v : Fin 4 → R) : Fin 4 → R :=
  Function.update v i (2 * (∑ j, v j - v i) - v i)
