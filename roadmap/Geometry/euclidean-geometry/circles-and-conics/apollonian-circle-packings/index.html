    Apollonian circle packings

    An Apollonian circle packing starts from four mutually tangent circles and keeps adding, for every three mutually tangent circles already present, the other circle tangent to all three (by the theorem of Apollonius there are exactly two). Here a packing is generated from a Descartes configuration by swaps; circles have positive radius, which covers bounded packings. By Descartes' circle theorem the curvatures transform under the Apollonian group, and the products curvature × center obey the same relation (the complex Descartes theorem). Geometrically the Apollonian group is a geometrically finite Kleinian group of infinite covolume whose limit set is the residual set of the packing (Oh, Section 2); the mixing of its frame flow on hyperbolic 3-space gives the asymptotic count \(N_P(T) \sim c \cdot T^{\alpha}\) of circles of curvature at most \(T\), with \(\alpha \approx 1.30568\) the Hausdorff dimension of the residual set (Kontorovich and Oh).

    Not yet formalised: that the signed curvatures of a Descartes configuration in this circle-level sense form a Descartes quadruple. The Descartes theorems available are stated with centre distances (externally or internally tangent circles), and the converse step from a one-point intersection to the centre distance is missing.

    TheoremExternally tangent spheres meet at exactly one point#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Euclidean.ApollonianCirclePacking.inter_eq_singleton_of_isExtTangentAt

    Let \(s_1, s_2\) be spheres of positive radii in a Euclidean affine space that are externally tangent at \(p\) in the sense of Mathlib's Sphere.IsExtTangentAt (\(p\) lies on both spheres and on the segment joining their centers). Then \(s_1 \cap s_2 = \{p\}\). Mathlib characterises external tangency by the distance of the centers (Sphere.isExtTangent_iff_dist_center) but does not state this uniqueness: any common point \(q\) satisfies \(d(c_1, q) + d(q, c_2) = r_1 + r_2 = d(c_1, c_2)\), so by strict convexity \(q\) is the point of the segment at distance \(r_1\) from \(c_1\), which is \(p\).

    theorem inter_eq_singleton_of_isExtTangentAt {s₁ s₂ : Sphere P} {p : P}
    (h : s₁.IsExtTangentAt s₂ p)
    (h₁ : 0 < s₁.radius) (h₂ : 0 < s₂.radius) : (s₁ : Set P) ∩ s₂ = {p}

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Euclidean.ApollonianCirclePacking.isExtTangentAt_lineMap

    If the centers \(c_1, c_2\) of two spheres of positive radii \(r_1, r_2\) are at distance \(r_1 + r_2\), then the spheres are externally tangent, in the sense of Mathlib's Sphere.IsExtTangentAt, at the point \(c_1 + \frac{r_1}{r_1 + r_2}(c_2 - c_1)\) of the segment of centers.     DefinitionDescartes configuration#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Euclidean.ApollonianCirclePacking.IsDescartesConfiguration

    A Descartes configuration is a quadruple of four distinct, pairwise tangent circles such that the three points at which any one circle touches the other three are distinct, that is, no three of the circles have a common tangent at a common point. Tangency is a one-point intersection. A configuration of concentric circles or a pencil of circles tangent at one point is excluded. The tests check that the circles of radii \(6, 3, 3, 2\) centred at \(0, 3, -3, 4i\) (curvatures proportional to \((-1, 2, 2, 3)\)) form a Descartes configuration whose first circle encloses the others.

    def IsDescartesConfiguration (C : Fin 4 → PlaneCircle) : Prop :=
  Function.Injective C ∧ ∃ p : Fin 4 → Fin 4 → ℂ,
    (∀ i j, i ≠ j → TangentAt (C i) (C j) (p i j)) ∧
      ∀ i j k, i ≠ j → i ≠ k → j ≠ k → p i j ≠ p i k
    structure PlaneCircle extends EuclideanGeometry.Sphere ℂ where
  radius_pos : 0 < radius

    InductiveConfigurations reachable by Apollonian swaps#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Euclidean.ApollonianCirclePacking.IsReachable

    The configurations reachable from \(C_0\) are generated by swaps: in a reachable configuration \(C\), replace the circle \(C_i\) by a different circle \(D\) such that \(D\) and the three circles \(C_j\), \(j \ne i\), again form a Descartes configuration. By the theorem of Apollonius there are exactly two circles tangent to three mutually tangent circles with distinct tangency points, so \(D\) is the other one; in a bounded packing it is never a line.

    inductive IsReachable (C₀ : Fin 4 → PlaneCircle) : (Fin 4 → PlaneCircle) → Prop
  | base : IsReachable C₀ C₀
  | swap {C : Fin 4 → PlaneCircle} (i : Fin 4) (D : PlaneCircle) :
      IsReachable C₀ C → D ≠ C i → IsDescartesConfiguration (Function.update C i D) →
        IsReachable C₀ (Function.update C i D)
    structure PlaneCircle extends EuclideanGeometry.Sphere ℂ where
  radius_pos : 0 < radius

    DefinitionResidual set of an Apollonian packing#✓fully proved

    TheoremComplex Descartes theorem for signed radii#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Euclidean.ComplexDescartesTheorem.complex_descartes_of_signed

    Let \(c_1, \dots, c_4 \in \mathbb{C}\) and nonzero real numbers \(s_1, \dots, s_4\) satisfy \(|c_i - c_j|^2 = (s_i + s_j)^2\) for \(i \ne j\), and let \(k_i = 1/s_i\). Then $\(\Big(\sum_i k_i c_i\Big)^2 = 2 \sum_i (k_i c_i)^2 \qquad \text{and} \qquad \Big(\sum_i k_i\Big)^2 = 2 \sum_i k_i^2 .\)$ The \(s_i\) are signed radii: positive for a circle whose interior is disjoint from the others, negative for a circle enclosing the others, so that tangent circles have centers at distance \(|s_i + s_j|\).

    Proof. Put \(u_j = c_j - c_1\) and \(p_{jl} = \langle u_j, u_l \rangle\); the hypotheses determine every \(p_{jl}\) in terms of the \(s_i\). The Gram determinant of the three plane vectors \(u_2, u_3, u_4\) vanishes, and in terms of the radii it is \(-4\) times the numerator of \((\sum k_i)^2 - 2 \sum k_i^2\): this is the real Descartes relation. The number \(F_0 = (\sum_j k_j u_j)^2 - 2 \sum_j (k_j u_j)^2\) has \(|F_0|^2\) equal to a polynomial in the \(p_{jl}\), by the planar identity \((a \times b)(c \times d) = (a \cdot c)(b \cdot d) - (a \cdot d)(b \cdot c)\); substituting the radii, it is a multiple of the same numerator, so \(F_0 = 0\). In the same way \(G = (\sum_i k_i)(\sum_j k_j u_j) - 2\sum_j k_j^2 u_j\) vanishes, and expanding around \(c_1\) gives \((\sum k_i c_i)^2 - 2\sum (k_i c_i)^2 = F_0 + 2 c_1 G + c_1^2 \big((\sum k_i)^2 - 2 \sum k_i^2\big) = 0\). This argument is a direct verification written for the formalisation; Lagarias, Mallows and Wilks obtain the theorem from the matrix generalisations of their Section 3 (curvature-center coordinates).

    Let four circles in the plane \(\mathbb{C}\) have centers \(c_i\), radii \(r_i > 0\) and signed curvatures \(k_i\), in either configuration of Descartes' circle theorem: (a) pairwise externally tangent, \(|c_i - c_j| = r_i + r_j\), with \(k_i = 1/r_i\); or (b) one circle \(e\) internally tangent to the three others, \(|c_e - c_j| = r_e - r_j\), which are pairwise externally tangent, with \(k_e = -1/r_e\) and \(k_j = 1/r_j\) for \(j \ne e\). Then the products \(k_i c_i\) of curvature and center satisfy the same relation as the curvatures: $\(\Big(\sum_{i=1}^4 k_i c_i\Big)^2 = 2 \sum_{i=1}^4 (k_i c_i)^2 \quad \text{in } \mathbb{C}.\)$ Lagarias, Mallows and Wilks state it for all Descartes configurations, lines included (with a suitable convention for \(k_i c_i\) on a line); the statement here has the hypotheses of the formalised Descartes' circle theorem and follows from the version for signed radii. Replacing \(c_i\) by \(c_i + w\) and comparing coefficients of \(w\) gives the real Descartes relation and the mixed relation \(\sum k_i (k_i c_i) = \tfrac12 (\sum k_i)(\sum k_i c_i)\) (their equation (2.3)). Lagarias, Mallows and Wilks also show, through the curvature-center coordinates of their Section 3, that the products curvature × center transform under the Apollonian group like the curvatures; that consequence is not formalised here. Example: in the configuration \((-1, 2, 2, 3)\) with bounding circle of radius \(1\) centred at \(0\), the products are \((0, 1, -1, 2i)\), and \((2i)^2 = -4 = 2(0 + 1 + 1 - 4)\).

    MathlibExt.Geometry.ApollonianCirclePackingWanted.kontorovich_oh_circle_count

    Let \(P\) be a bounded Apollonian circle packing, presented by a Descartes configuration \(C_0\) that contains its bounding circle \(C_{0,e}\) (the other three circles are internally tangent to it), let \(N_P(T)\) be the number of circles of \(P\) of curvature at most \(T\), and let \(\alpha\) be the Hausdorff dimension of the residual set of \(P\). Then there is a constant \(c = c(P) > 0\) with $\(N_P(T) \sim c \cdot T^{\alpha} \qquad (T \to \infty).\)$ Every bounded packing has such a presentation, for instance by its root quadruple, whose negative entry is the bounding circle (Kontorovich and Oh, Definition 2.2). Numerically \(\alpha \approx 1.30568\) (McMullen); Boyd had shown \(\log N_P(T) / \log T \to \alpha\). The theorem is stated here only for bounded packings; Kontorovich and Oh also treat the packing between two parallel lines, counting circles in a period.

    The proof reduces the count to the orbit of the root quadruple under the Apollonian group \(A < O_Q(\mathbb{Z})\) (Kontorovich and Oh, Theorem 2.3 and Section 2), then counts this orbit in the light cone of \(Q\) through the equidistribution of closed horospheres in the infinite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifold \(A \backslash \mathbb{H}^3\); Patterson–Sullivan theory enters through the spectral decomposition of that manifold (Kontorovich and Oh, introduction). Oh's survey (Section 3) explains the count through the finiteness and mixing of the Bowen–Margulis–Sullivan measure for the frame flow (her Theorem 3.6).
