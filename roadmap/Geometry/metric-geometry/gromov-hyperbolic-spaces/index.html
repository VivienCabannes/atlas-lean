    Gromov-hyperbolic spaces

    Gromov's hyperbolicity via the four-point inequality on the Gromov product, its base-point independence, first examples, and the equivalence with thin geodesic triangles (Gromov, Hyperbolic groups, 1987, §1 and §6.3).

    DefinitionGromov \(\delta\)-hyperbolic space#✓fully proved

    Metric.IsDeltaHyperbolic

    A pseudometric space X is δ-hyperbolic (in the sense of Gromov) if 0 ≤ δ and the Gromov product satisfies the four-point inequality (x ∣ z)_w ≥ min (x ∣ y)_w (y ∣ z)_w - δ for all points w x y z, where (x ∣ y)_w = Metric.gromovProduct w x y (basepoint first, as in Mathlib).

    This is Gromov's Definition 1.1.C: the \(\delta\)-inequality (*) of §1.1, \((x \cdot y)_w \ge \min((x \cdot z)_w, (y \cdot z)_w) - \delta\), required at every base point \(w\). By his Corollary 1.1.B, the inequality at a single base point already gives it at every base point with \(2\delta\) in place of \(\delta\).

    def IsDeltaHyperbolic (X : Type*) [PseudoMetricSpace X] (δ : ℝ) : Prop :=
  0 ≤ δ ∧ ∀ w x y z : X, min (gromovProduct w x y) (gromovProduct w y z) - δ ≤ gromovProduct w x z

    TheoremThe four-point inequality from one base point#✓fully proved

    Metric.four_point_of_basepoint

    Fix a base point \(o\) and write \((x \cdot y)\) for the Gromov product at \(o\). If \((x \cdot y) \ge \min((x \cdot z), (y \cdot z)) - \delta\) for all \(x, y, z\), then for all \(x, y, z, t\) $\((t \cdot y) + (z \cdot x) \ge \min\big((t \cdot z) + (y \cdot x),\ (x \cdot t) + (y \cdot z)\big) - 2\delta.\)$

    Each of the two products on the left is bounded below by two instances of the \(\delta\)-inequality, and an elementary case analysis on the four remaining products concludes. This is Gromov's Lemma 1.1.A (stated there without proof as "obvious").

    theorem four_point_of_basepoint {o : X} {δ : ℝ}
    (h : ∀ x y z : X, min (gromovProduct o x y) (gromovProduct o y z) - δ ≤ gromovProduct o x z)
    (x y z t : X) :
    min (gromovProduct o t z + gromovProduct o y x) (gromovProduct o x t + gromovProduct o y z)
      - 2 * δ ≤ gromovProduct o t y + gromovProduct o z x

    Metric.isDeltaHyperbolic_two_mul_of_basepoint

    If a pseudometric space \(X\) satisfies the \(\delta\)-inequality \((x \cdot y)_o \ge \min((x \cdot z)_o, (y \cdot z)_o) - \delta\) for one base point \(o\) and all \(x, y, z\), and \(\delta \ge 0\), then \(X\) is \(2\delta\)-hyperbolic with respect to every base point, that is, IsDeltaHyperbolic X (2 * δ).

    The proof is Gromov's: the left side of the four-point inequality at \(o\) does not depend on \(o\), and equals \((t \cdot y)_x - \min((t \cdot z)_x, (y \cdot z)_x)\), which is the \(\delta\)-inequality at the base point \(x\).

    Metric.isDeltaHyperbolic_of_forall_dist_le

    If all distances in a pseudometric space \(X\) are at most \(d\), and \(d \ge 0\), then \(X\) is \(d\)-hyperbolic: every Gromov product lies between \(0\) and \(d\). Hyperbolicity is a large-scale property, invisible on bounded sets; finite groups are word-hyperbolic for this reason.

    TheoremThe real line is 0-hyperbolic#✓fully proved

    Metric.isDeltaHyperbolic_real

    The real line \(\mathbb{R}\) is \(0\)-hyperbolic: for all \(w, x, y, z\), \((x \cdot z)_w \ge \min((x \cdot y)_w, (y \cdot z)_w)\). On the line, \((x \cdot y)_w\) is the length of the common part of the segments \([w, x]\) and \([w, y]\), and the inequality follows by cases on the relative order of the points. In contrast, \(\mathbb{R}^n\) is not hyperbolic for \(n \ge 2\).

    DefinitionThin geodesic triangles#✓fully proved

    Metric.TrianglesThin

    Every geodesic triangle in \(X\) is \(\delta\)-thin in Gromov's sense: for every vertex \(x\), all geodesic segments \(\gamma\) from \(x\) to \(y\) and \(\gamma'\) from \(x\) to \(z\), and every \(t\) with \(0 \le t \le (y \cdot z)_x\), one has \(d(\gamma t, \gamma' t) \le \delta\).

    Gromov defines thinness through the comparison tripod \(T\) of a triangle \(\Delta\) with vertices \(x_0, x_1, x_2\): the legs of \(T\) have lengths \((x_j \cdot x_k)_{x_i}\), the map \(f_\Delta : \Delta \to T\) is isometric on each side, and \(\Delta\) is \(\delta\)-thin if \(f_\Delta(u) = f_\Delta(v)\) implies \(d(u, v) \le \delta\). Two points of different sides have the same image exactly when they lie on the two sides from a common vertex, at the same distance \(t \le (y \cdot z)_x\) from it; so in a geodesic space this definition, quantified over every vertex and every choice of sides, says that all geodesic triangles are \(\delta\)-thin. In a space that is not geodesic it also constrains pairs of segments from a common vertex with no third side, so it is then slightly stronger than Gromov's condition. In a space with no geodesic segments it holds vacuously.

    def TrianglesThin (X : Type*) [PseudoMetricSpace X] (δ : ℝ) : Prop :=
  ∀ (x y z : X) (γ γ' : ℝ → X), IsGeodesicSegment γ x y → IsGeodesicSegment γ' x z →
    ∀ t : ℝ, 0 ≤ t → t ≤ gromovProduct x y z → dist (γ t) (γ' t) ≤ δ
    def IsGeodesicSegment (γ : ℝ → X) (x y : X) : Prop :=
  γ 0 = x ∧ γ (dist x y) = y ∧
    ∀ s ∈ Icc 0 (dist x y), ∀ t ∈ Icc 0 (dist x y), dist (γ s) (γ t) = |s - t|

    Metric.IsDeltaHyperbolic.trianglesThin

    If \(X\) is \(\delta\)-hyperbolic, then every geodesic triangle in \(X\) is \(4\delta\)-thin.

    For points \(u = \gamma t\) and \(v = \gamma' t\) on the sides from \(x\) to \(y\) and from \(x\) to \(z\) with \(t \le (y \cdot z)_x\), one has \((u \cdot y)_x = t = (z \cdot v)_x\), and two applications of the \(\delta\)-inequality at \(x\) give \((u \cdot v)_x \ge t - 2\delta\), that is \(d(u, v) \le 4\delta\).

    The constant is weaker than the source: Gromov's Lemma 6.3.A and Proposition 6.3.C give \(2\delta\), through the approximation of finite subsets of a hyperbolic space by trees (§6.1), which is not formalised.

    Metric.isDeltaHyperbolic_of_trianglesThin

    If \(X\) is a geodesic space whose geodesic triangles are all \(\delta\)-thin and \(\delta \ge 0\), then \(X\) is \(\delta\)-hyperbolic.

    Given \(w, x, y, z\), let \(t_0 = \min((x \cdot y)_w, (y \cdot z)_w)\) and take the points at distance \(t_0\) from \(w\) on segments from \(w\) to \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\). Thinness of the triangles \(wxy\) and \(wyz\) puts the points on \([w, x]\) and \([w, z]\) within \(2\delta\) of each other, and the triangle inequality through \(x\) and \(z\) then gives \((x \cdot z)_w \ge t_0 - \delta\).

    This is stronger than the source: Gromov's Lemma 6.3.B and Proposition 6.3.C conclude \(2\delta\)-hyperbolicity, because the lower bound (**) in his proof, \(|x_1' - x_2'| \ge 2(t - (x_1 \cdot x_2) - \delta)\), holds without the \(-\delta\).
