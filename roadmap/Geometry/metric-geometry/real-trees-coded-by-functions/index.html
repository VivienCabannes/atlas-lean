    Real trees coded by functions

    A continuous function g codes a tree: d_g(s, t) = g s + g t - 2 min_{[s ∧ t, s ∨ t]} g is a pseudometric whose metric quotient is the real tree T_g (Le Gall, Random trees and applications, Section 2.2). It is the continuous version of the coding of a plane tree by its contour path; coded by the normalised Brownian excursion, it gives Aldous's continuum random tree, the starting point of the Brownian snake and of the Brownian map.

    LemmaFour-point inequality for the minimum#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.TreeCoding.treeMin_four_point

    For the minimum function m_g, for all x, y, z, w, min (m_g(x, y) + m_g(z, w)) (m_g(y, z) + m_g(x, w)) ≤ m_g(x, z) + m_g(y, w). With d_g = g + g - 2 m_g, this is the four-point condition satisfied by the distance of a real tree (Le Gall, Random trees and applications, Theorem 2.2).

    lemma treeMin_four_point (hg : Continuous g) (x y z w : ℝ) :
    min (treeMin g x y + treeMin g z w) (treeMin g y z + treeMin g x w) ≤
      treeMin g x z + treeMin g y w

    MathlibExt.TreeCoding.abs_treeDist_sub_le

    If g, h are continuous and |g - h| ≤ ε everywhere, then |d_g(s, t) - d_h(s, t)| ≤ 4 ε for all s, t.
