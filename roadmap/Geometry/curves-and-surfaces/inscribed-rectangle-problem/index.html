    DefinitionInscribed rectangle#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.InscribedRectanglesWanted.InscribedRectangles.IsInscribedRectangle

    Let \(\gamma\) be a map from the unit circle to \(\mathbb{R}^2\). For points \(a, b \in \mathbb{R}^2\) and an angle \(\theta \in \mathbb{R}\), the rotated rectangle given by \(a\), \(b\) and \(\theta\) is inscribed in \(\gamma\) when its four vertices are pairwise distinct and each lies in the image of \(\gamma\).

    The map \(\gamma\) need not be smooth, or even a Jordan curve, for this definition.

    def IsInscribedRectangle (a b : ℝ × ℝ) (θ : ℝ) (γ : Circle → ℝ × ℝ) : Prop :=
  Function.Injective (rectangleCorners a b θ) ∧ ∀ i : Fin 4, rectangleCorners a b θ i ∈ Set.range γ
    noncomputable def rectangleCorners (a b : ℝ × ℝ) (θ : ℝ) : Fin 4 → ℝ × ℝ :=
  ContinuousLinearMap.rotation θ ∘ axisAlignedRectangleCorners a b

    DefinitionAspect ratio#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.InscribedRectanglesWanted.InscribedRectangles.aspectRatio

    For points \(a = (a_1, a_2)\) and \(b = (b_1, b_2)\) of \(\mathbb{R}^2\), the aspect ratio of the axis-aligned rectangle with opposite corners \(a\) and \(b\) is \(|a_1 - b_1| / |a_2 - b_2|\), the ratio of its horizontal side length to its vertical side length; it is \(0\) when \(a_2 = b_2\).

  |a.1 - b.1| / |a.2 - b.2|

    For \(z, w \in \mathbb{C}\) and an angle \(\theta\), \(R(z, w, \theta)\) is the list of four points \(z e^{i\theta}\), \((\operatorname{Re} w + i \operatorname{Im} z) e^{i\theta}\), \(w e^{i\theta}\), \((\operatorname{Re} z + i \operatorname{Im} w) e^{i\theta}\): the vertices, in order, of the axis-parallel rectangle with opposite corners \(z\) and \(w\), rotated about the origin by \(\theta\).     MathlibExt.Geometry.RectangularPegProblemWanted.RectangularPegProblem.theorem_1

    Let \(\gamma : S^1 \to \mathbb{C}\) be a \(C^\infty\) embedding of the unit circle (a smooth Jordan curve in the plane). For \(z, w \in \mathbb{C}\) and an angle \(\theta\), write \(R(z, w, \theta)\) for the list of the four points \(z e^{i\theta}\), \((\operatorname{Re} w + i \operatorname{Im} z) e^{i\theta}\), \(w e^{i\theta}\), \((\operatorname{Re} z + i \operatorname{Im} w) e^{i\theta}\), in this order: the vertices of the (possibly degenerate) axis-parallel rectangle with opposite corners \(z\) and \(w\), rotated about the origin by \(\theta\). Then for all \(z\), \(w\), \(\theta\) there exist \(z'\), \(w'\), \(\theta'\) such that \(R(z, w, \theta)\) is similar to \(R(z', w', \theta')\), meaning that for some real \(r > 0\) the distance between the \(i\)-th and \(j\)-th vertices of \(R(z, w, \theta)\) is \(r\) times the distance between the \(i\)-th and \(j\)-th vertices of \(R(z', w', \theta')\) for all \(i, j\), and all four vertices of \(R(z', w', \theta')\) lie on \(\gamma\).
