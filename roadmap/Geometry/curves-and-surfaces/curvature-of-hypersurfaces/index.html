    EuclideanGeometry.IsLocalDefiningFunction

    Let \(E\) be a real inner product space, \(S \subseteq E\), \(\rho : E \to \mathbb{R}\) and \(p \in E\). Then \(\rho\) is a local defining function of \(S\) at \(p\) when \(\rho(p) = 0\), \(\rho\) is of class \(C^2\) at \(p\), its differential \(D\rho(p)\) is nonzero, and for all \(x\) in some neighborhood of \(p\), \(x \in S\) if and only if \(\rho(x) = 0\). In particular \(p \in S\).

    Every point of a \(C^2\) hypersurface has local defining functions, and every point of a regular level set of a \(C^2\) function is of this form.

    def IsLocalDefiningFunction (S : Set E) (ρ : E → ℝ) (p : E) : Prop :=
  ρ p = 0 ∧ ContDiffAt ℝ 2 ρ p ∧ fderiv ℝ ρ p ≠ 0 ∧ ∀ᶠ x in 𝓝 p, x ∈ S ↔ ρ x = 0

    DefinitionSecond fundamental form of a level set#✓fully proved

    EuclideanGeometry.levelSetSecondFundamentalForm

    Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real inner product space, \(\rho : E \to \mathbb{R}\) and \(p \in E\). Write \(\nabla\rho(p)\) for the gradient of \(\rho\) at \(p\) and \(D^2\rho(p)\) for the second derivative of \(\rho\) at \(p\) (a bilinear form on \(E\)). The second fundamental form at \(p\) of the level set of \(\rho\) through \(p\), with respect to the unit normal \(\nabla\rho(p) / \|\nabla\rho(p)\|\), is defined for all \(u, v \in E\) by \(\mathrm{II}(u, v) = D^2\rho(p)(u, v) / \|\nabla\rho(p)\|\), and it is \(0\) when \(\nabla\rho(p) = 0\). When \(\rho\) is \(C^2\) near \(p\) and \(\nabla\rho(p) \neq 0\), for tangent vectors \(u\), \(v\) (orthogonal to \(\nabla\rho(p)\)) it equals \(\langle D_u \nu, v \rangle\) for the unit normal field \(\nu = \nabla\rho / \|\nabla\rho\|\).

    The formula is the second fundamental form (independent of the defining function, up to the sign fixed by the normal) only for tangent \(u\) and \(v\): two defining functions of the same hyperplane can give different values on the normal vector.

    def levelSetSecondFundamentalForm (ρ : E → ℝ) (p u v : E) : ℝ :=
  fderiv ℝ (fderiv ℝ ρ) p u v / ‖∇ ρ p‖

    DefinitionMean curvature of a level set#✓fully proved

    EuclideanGeometry.levelSetMeanCurvature

    Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real inner product space, \(\rho : E \to \mathbb{R}\) and \(p \in E\). Write \(\nabla\rho(p)\) for the gradient of \(\rho\) at \(p\), \(D^2\rho(p)\) for the second derivative of \(\rho\) at \(p\) (a bilinear form on \(E\)), and \(\Delta\rho(p) = \sum_i D^2\rho(p)(e_i, e_i)\) for a fixed orthonormal basis \((e_i)\) of \(E\). The mean curvature at \(p\) of the level set of \(\rho\) through \(p\), with respect to the unit normal \(\nu = \nabla\rho(p) / \|\nabla\rho(p)\|\), is \(H = (\Delta\rho(p) - D^2\rho(p)(\nu, \nu)) / \|\nabla\rho(p)\|\), and \(H = 0\) when \(\nabla\rho(p) = 0\).

    This is the trace of the second fundamental form on the tangent hyperplane \(\nabla\rho(p)^\perp\), the sum of the principal curvatures, and it equals \(\operatorname{div}(\nabla\rho / \|\nabla\rho\|)(p)\) when \(\rho\) is \(C^2\) near \(p\) and \(\nabla\rho(p) \neq 0\). Since \(D^2\rho(p)\) is bilinear, \(\Delta\rho(p)\) does not depend on the choice of orthonormal basis.

    def levelSetMeanCurvature (ρ : E → ℝ) (p : E) : ℝ :=
  ((∑ i, fderiv ℝ (fderiv ℝ ρ) p (stdOrthonormalBasis ℝ E i) (stdOrthonormalBasis ℝ E i)) -
      fderiv ℝ (fderiv ℝ ρ) p (∇ ρ p) (∇ ρ p) / ‖∇ ρ p‖ ^ 2) / ‖∇ ρ p‖

    EuclideanGeometry.levelSetSecondFundamentalForm_smul

    Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real inner product space. For every \(\rho : E \to \mathbb{R}\), all \(p, u, v \in E\) and every real \(c \neq 0\), the second fundamental form at \(p\) of the level set of \(c\rho\) through \(p\), evaluated at \((u, v)\), equals \(c / |c|\) (the sign of \(c\)) times the second fundamental form at \(p\) of the level set of \(\rho\) through \(p\), evaluated at \((u, v)\).

    theorem levelSetSecondFundamentalForm_smul (ρ : E → ℝ) (p u v : E) {c : ℝ} (hc : c ≠ 0) :

      c / |c| * levelSetSecondFundamentalForm ρ p u v
    def levelSetSecondFundamentalForm (ρ : E → ℝ) (p u v : E) : ℝ :=
  fderiv ℝ (fderiv ℝ ρ) p u v / ‖∇ ρ p‖

    TheoremMean curvature of the unit sphere#✓fully proved

    EuclideanGeometry.levelSetMeanCurvature_sphere

    Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real inner product space of dimension \(n\). For every \(p \in E\) with \(\|p\| = 1\), the mean curvature at \(p\) of the level set of \(\rho(x) = \|x\|^2 - 1\) through \(p\) is \(n - 1\). That is, the unit sphere of \(E\) has mean curvature \(n - 1\) with respect to the outward normal, the mean curvature being the sum (not the average) of the principal curvatures.

    levelSetMeanCurvature (fun x : E ↦ ‖x‖ ^ 2 - 1) p = finrank ℝ E - 1

    EuclideanGeometry.levelSetMeanCurvature_affine

    Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real inner product space.
