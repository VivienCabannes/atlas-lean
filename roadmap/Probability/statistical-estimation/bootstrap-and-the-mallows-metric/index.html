    Bootstrap and the Mallows metric

    Efron's bootstrap estimates the sampling distribution of a statistic by recomputing it on samples drawn with replacement from the data. Bickel and Freedman (1981) proved that the bootstrap of the mean is asymptotically valid whenever the variance is finite. Their tool is the Mallows metric \(d_p\), which is the Wasserstein distance \(W_p\): the empirical law converges to the true law in \(d_2\), and normalised sums contract \(d_2\), so the bootstrap law of the normalised mean inherits the central limit theorem. The contraction lemmas are proved here in a separable Hilbert space; the bootstrap limit theorems are recorded as wanted statements. The bootstrap does not always work: for the sample maximum of uniform data, Bickel and Freedman's Counter-example 2, proved here, shows that the bootstrap law keeps an atom of mass about \(0.63\) where the true limit law is continuous.

    TheoremMallows metric of sums of independent variables#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.Statistics.MallowsMetric.wasserstein_conv_le

    Let \(1 \le p < \infty\) and let \(\mu_1, \nu_1, \mu_2, \nu_2\) be Borel probability measures on a separable normed group. Then $\(d_p(\mu_1 * \mu_2, \nu_1 * \nu_2) \le d_p(\mu_1, \nu_1) + d_p(\mu_2, \nu_2),\)$ where \(d_p = W_p\) is the Mallows (Wasserstein) metric and \(*\) is convolution. In terms of random variables: if \(U_1, U_2\) are independent and \(V_1, V_2\) are independent, then \(d_p(U_1 + U_2, V_1 + V_2) \le d_p(U_1, V_1) + d_p(U_2, V_2)\).

    Bickel and Freedman state the inequality for \(m\) summands; the Lean statement is the case of two summands, from which the general case follows by induction. No moment assumption is needed, since \(d_p\) takes values in \([0, \infty]\).

    The proof couples the two pairs independently and applies Minkowski's inequality in \(L^p\) of the product coupling. Taking infima over the two couplings gives the claim without using that the infimum is attained (Bickel and Freedman start from optimal couplings, their Lemma 8.1).

    theorem wasserstein_conv_le {p : ℝ} (hp : 1 ≤ p) (μ₁ ν₁ μ₂ ν₂ : ProbabilityMeasure E) :
    wasserstein p (conv μ₁ μ₂) (conv ν₁ ν₂) ≤ wasserstein p μ₁ ν₁ + wasserstein p μ₂ ν₂
    noncomputable def wasserstein (p : ℝ) (μ ν : ProbabilityMeasure X) : ℝ≥0∞ :=
  transportCost p μ ν ^ (1 / p)

    TheoremMallows metric of convolution powers, general \(p\)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.Statistics.MallowsMetric.wasserstein_convPow_le

    For \(1 \le p < \infty\), Borel probability measures \(\mu, \nu\) on a separable normed group and every \(m \ge 0\), $\(d_p(\mu^{*m}, \nu^{*m}) \le m \, d_p(\mu, \nu),\)$ where \(\mu^{*m}\) is the law of the sum of \(m\) independent variables with law \(\mu\) (convolution powers). It is Lemma 8.6 of Bickel and Freedman for \(m\) identically distributed pairs, by induction from the two-summand case. In a Hilbert space with \(p = 2\) and equal means the factor \(m\) improves to \(\sqrt m\) (Mallows metric of convolution powers), which is what makes normalised sums contract.

    theorem wasserstein_convPow_le {p : ℝ} (hp : 1 ≤ p) (μ ν : ProbabilityMeasure E) :
    ∀ m : ℕ, wasserstein p (convPow μ m) (convPow ν m) ≤ m * wasserstein p μ ν
    noncomputable def wasserstein (p : ℝ) (μ ν : ProbabilityMeasure X) : ℝ≥0∞ :=

    TheoremScaling of the Mallows metric#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.Statistics.MallowsMetric.wasserstein_map_smul

    For every real scalar \(a\), every \(p > 0\) and all Borel probability measures \(\mu, \nu\) on a separable real normed space, $\(d_p(aU, aV) = |a| \, d_p(U, V),\)$ where \(d_p(aU, aV)\) is the distance between the laws of \(aU\) and \(aV\) for \(U \sim \mu\) and \(V \sim \nu\) (the images of \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) under \(x \mapsto a x\)).

    The inequality \(\le\) comes from mapping each coupling of \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) by \((x, y) \mapsto (ax, ay)\). For \(a \ne 0\) the reverse inequality is the same argument with \(a^{-1}\); for \(a = 0\) both sides vanish.

    theorem wasserstein_map_smul {p : ℝ} (hp : 0 < p) (a : ℝ) (μ ν : ProbabilityMeasure E) :
    wasserstein p (μ.map (a • ·)) (ν.map (a • ·)) = ‖a‖ₑ * wasserstein p μ ν
    noncomputable def wasserstein (p : ℝ) (μ ν : ProbabilityMeasure X) : ℝ≥0∞ :=
  transportCost p μ ν ^ (1 / p)

    TheoremMallows metric of sums in a Hilbert space#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.Statistics.MallowsMetric.transportCost_two_conv_le

    Let \(E\) be a separable real Hilbert space and let \(\mu_1, \nu_1, \mu_2, \nu_2\) be probability measures on \(E\) with finite second moments. If \(\mu_2\) and \(\nu_2\) have the same mean, then $\(d_2(\mu_1 * \mu_2, \nu_1 * \nu_2)^2 \le d_2(\mu_1, \nu_1)^2 + d_2(\mu_2, \nu_2)^2 .\)$ Here \(d_2^2\) is the optimal transport cost of order \(2\).

    Bickel and Freedman state the lemma for \(m\) summands and assume \(E(U_j) = E(V_j)\) for every \(j\). The Lean statement has two summands and assumes equality of means for the second pair only. For couplings \(\pi_1, \pi_2\) and \(a(x, y) = x - y\), the independent coupling of the two pairs has cost $\(\int \|a(z_1) + a(z_2)\|^2 \, d(\pi_1 \otimes \pi_2) = \int \|a\|^2 \, d\pi_1 + \int \|a\|^2 \, d\pi_2 + 2 \Bigl\langle \int a \, d\pi_1, \int a \, d\pi_2 \Bigr\rangle,\)$ and the cross term vanishes as soon as one mean displacement \(\int a \, d\pi_j\) is zero. For \(p = 2\) this improves the triangle-type bound Mallows metric of sums of independent variables.

    theorem transportCost_two_conv_le (μ₁ ν₁ μ₂ ν₂ : ProbabilityMeasure E)
    (hμ₁ : MemLp id 2 (μ₁ : Measure E)) (hν₁ : MemLp id 2 (ν₁ : Measure E))
    (hμ₂ : MemLp id 2 (μ₂ : Measure E)) (hν₂ : MemLp id 2 (ν₂ : Measure E))
    (hmean : ∫ x, x ∂(μ₂ : Measure E) = ∫ x, x ∂(ν₂ : Measure E)) :
    transportCost 2 (conv μ₁ μ₂) (conv ν₁ ν₂) ≤
      transportCost 2 μ₁ ν₁ + transportCost 2 μ₂ ν₂
    noncomputable def transportCost (p : ℝ) (μ ν : ProbabilityMeasure X) : ℝ≥0∞ :=
  ⨅ (π : ProbabilityMeasure (X × X)) (_ : IsCoupling μ ν π),
    ∫⁻ z, edist z.1 z.2 ^ p ∂(π : Measure (X × X))

    TheoremCentring decreases the Mallows metric by the squared distance of the means#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.Statistics.MallowsMetric.transportCost_two_center_add_le

    Let \(\mu, \nu\) be probability measures with finite second moments on a separable real Hilbert space, with means \(a = \int x \, d\mu\) and \(b = \int x \, d\nu\). For \(U \sim \mu\) and \(V \sim \nu\), $\(d_2(U - EU, V - EV)^2 + \|a - b\|^2 \le d_2(U, V)^2 ,\)$ where the left-hand distance is between the laws of \(U - a\) and \(V - b\).

    Bickel and Freedman prove the equality \(d_2(U, V)^2 = d_2(U - EU, V - EV)^2 + \|EU - EV\|^2\). This page is the inequality above, the half of the lemma used to centre the summands in Mallows' contraction; the equality is Mallows metric and centring. For every coupling \(\pi\) of \(\mu\) and \(\nu\), its image under \((x, y) \mapsto (x - a, y - b)\) couples the centred laws, and expanding the square gives \(\int \|(x - y) - (a - b)\|^2 \, d\pi = \int \|x - y\|^2 \, d\pi - \|a - b\|^2\).

    theorem transportCost_two_center_add_le (μ ν : ProbabilityMeasure E)
    (hμ : MemLp id 2 (μ : Measure E)) (hν : MemLp id 2 (ν : Measure E)) :
    transportCost 2 (μ.map (· - ∫ x, x ∂(μ : Measure E))) (ν.map (· - ∫ x, x ∂(ν : Measure E))) +
        ENNReal.ofReal (‖∫ x, x ∂(μ : Measure E) - ∫ x, x ∂(ν : Measure E)‖ ^ 2) ≤
      transportCost 2 μ ν

    TheoremMallows' contraction of normalised sums#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.Statistics.MallowsMetric.wasserstein_two_normalizedSum_le

    Let \(G\) and \(H\) be laws with finite second moments on a separable real Hilbert space. For \(m \ge 1\) let \(G^{(m)}\) be the law of $\(S_m(G) = m^{-1/2} \sum_{j=1}^m \bigl(Z_j(G) - E Z_j(G)\bigr)\)$ for i.i.d. \(Z_j(G) \sim G\), and similarly \(H^{(m)}\). Then $\(d_2\bigl(G^{(m)}, H^{(m)}\bigr) \le d_2(G, H).\)$

    In Lean, \(G^{(m)}\) is the image under \(x \mapsto m^{-1/2} x\) of the \(m\)-th convolution power of the law of \(Z - EZ\); for \(m = 0\) both normalised laws are the Dirac mass at \(0\) and the inequality is trivial. Bickel and Freedman state (2.2) on the real line, citing Lemma 3 of Mallows (1972), and point to their Lemmas 8.7 and 8.8 and the scaling (8.2); the Lean proof follows that route in a Hilbert space.

    This is the step that makes the bootstrap of the mean work: if \(F_n\) is close to \(F\) in \(d_2\), then the bootstrap law \(F_n^{(m)}\) is close to \(F^{(m)}\) uniformly in \(m\), and \(F^{(m)}\) is close to a normal law by the central limit theorem (bootstrap central limit theorem).

    theorem wasserstein_two_normalizedSum_le (μ ν : ProbabilityMeasure E)
    (hμ : MemLp id 2 (μ : Measure E)) (hν : MemLp id 2 (ν : Measure E)) (m : ℕ) :

    TheoremBickel–Freedman bootstrap central limit theorem for the mean#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Probability.Statistics.BootstrapWanted.bootstrap_mean_tendsto_gaussianReal

    Let \(X_0, X_1, \dots\) be i.i.d. real random variables with finite positive variance \(\sigma^2\), and let \(\mu_n\) be the mean of \(X_0, \dots, X_n\). Given the data, let \(X^*_1, \dots, X^*_m\) be drawn independently and uniformly from \(X_0, \dots, X_n\), with mean \(\mu^*_m\). Along almost every sample sequence, as \(n\) and \(m\) tend to infinity jointly, the conditional law of $\(\sqrt m \, (\mu^*_m - \mu_n)\)$ given the data converges weakly to \(N(0, \sigma^2)\).

    In Lean, a bootstrap sample is \(X_{I(1)}, \dots, X_{I(m)}\) for a uniformly random map \(I : \{1, \dots, m\} \to \{0, \dots, n\}\), so the conditional expectation of \(f(\sqrt m (\mu^*_m - \mu_n))\) is the average of that quantity over the \((n+1)^m\) maps \(I\). Weak convergence is tested against every bounded continuous \(f\), and "\(n, m \to \infty\) jointly" is the product of the two atTop filters. The sample has \(n + 1\) points to avoid the empty sample.

    Bickel and Freedman's proof: the conditional law of \(\sqrt m (\mu^*_m - \mu_n)\) is \(F_n^{(m)}\) in the notation of Mallows' contraction. Almost surely \(F_n \to F\) in \(d_2\), so \(d_2(F_n^{(m)}, F^{(m)}) \le d_2(F_n, F) \to 0\) uniformly in \(m\), while \(F^{(m)} \to N(0, \sigma^2)\) by the central limit theorem; and convergence in \(d_2\) implies weak convergence.
