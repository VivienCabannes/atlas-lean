    Cramér's theorem

    For i.i.d. random vectors with a finite moment generating function, the empirical means satisfy a large deviation principle whose rate function is the Legendre transform of the logarithmic moment generating function. The theorem is proved in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) under a finite moment generating function: the upper bound by Chernoff bounds in half-spaces and exponential tightness, the lower bound by exponential tilting, after a quadratic perturbation of the log-moment generating function reduces every point to a gradient point.

    TheoremCramér's theorem in ℝᵈ#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.CramerLDPLowerBound.satisfiesLDP_empiricalMeanMeasureVec

    Let \(X_0, X_1, \dots\) be independent, identically distributed random vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) with \(E[e^{\langle t, X_0 \rangle}] < \infty\) for every \(t \in \mathbb{R}^d\). Then the laws of the empirical means \(\frac1n \sum_{i<n} X_i\) satisfy the large deviation principle with the good rate function

    \[\Lambda^*(x) = \sup_{t} \bigl( \langle t, x \rangle - \log E[e^{\langle t, X_0 \rangle}] \bigr).\]

    The proof combines the upper bound for closed sets, the lower bound for open sets and the goodness of the rate function.

    In dimension one Varadhan proves the theorem directly: the Chebyshev bound \(P[\bar X_n \ge \ell] \le e^{-n(\theta \ell - \psi(\theta))}\) gives the upper bound for half-lines, and exponential tilting \(d\mu_{\theta_0}/d\mu = e^{\theta_0 x} / M(\theta_0)\), which makes \(\ell\) the mean, gives the lower bound. The proof here follows the same plan in \(\mathbb{R}^d\): half-space Chernoff bounds and exponential tightness above; below, the tilt is applied at points \(x = \nabla\Lambda(\theta)\), and a general \(x\) with \(\Lambda^*(x) < \infty\) is reached by maximising \(\langle x, t \rangle - \Lambda(t) - \varepsilon |t|^2/2\), whose maximiser \(\theta_\varepsilon\) satisfies \(\nabla\Lambda(\theta_\varepsilon) = x - \varepsilon\theta_\varepsilon \to x\); the log-moment generating function is differentiable by differentiation under the integral sign.

    Varadhan also obtains the theorem in a separable Banach space as a corollary of Sanov's theorem: the mean is a continuous function of the empirical measure on measures with bounded support, the general case follows by truncation, and the contracted rate \(\inf \{ \int b \log b \, d\alpha : \int z \, b(z) \, d\alpha(z) = x \}\) equals \(\Lambda^*(x)\) by a minimax argument (Section 3, Corollary 1.3 and Lemma 1.4 of the notes).

    TheoremCramér upper bound for closed sets#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.CramerLDPUpperBound.limsup_log_empiricalMeanMeasureVec_le

    Let \(X_0, X_1, \dots\) be independent, identically distributed random vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) with \(E[e^{\langle t, X_0 \rangle}] < \infty\) for every \(t\), and let \(\mu_n\) be the law of the empirical mean \(\frac1n \sum_{i<n} X_i\). For every closed set \(F \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d\),

    \[\limsup_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \log \mu_n(F) \le -\inf_{x \in F} \Lambda^*(x),\]

    which is the upper-bound half of Cramér's theorem in exactly the form required by the large deviation principle.

    Proof: for each \(x\) and \(q < \Lambda^*(x)\) pick \(t\) with \(\langle t, x \rangle - \Lambda(t) > q\); the open half-space where \(\langle t, \cdot \rangle - \Lambda(t) > q\) contains \(x\) and has \(\mu_n\)-mass at most \(e^{-nq}\) by the Chebyshev (Chernoff) bound. Finitely many such half-spaces cover a compact set. Chernoff bounds in the \(2d\) coordinate directions give exponential tightness, which passes from compact to closed sets.

    TheoremCramér lower bound for open sets#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.CramerLDPLowerBound.le_liminf_log_empiricalMeanMeasureVec

    Let \(X_0, X_1, \dots\) be independent, identically distributed random vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) with \(E[e^{\langle t, X_0 \rangle}] < \infty\) for every \(t\), and let \(\mu_n\) be the law of the empirical mean. For every open set \(U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d\),

    \[-\inf_{x \in U} \Lambda^*(x) \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \log \mu_n(U),\]

    in exactly the form required by the large deviation principle.

    At a point \(x = \nabla\Lambda(\theta)\) the weight \(e^{\langle \theta, S_n \rangle - n\Lambda(\theta)}\) tilts the sample so that \(x\) becomes the mean, and on the ball \(B(x, \delta)\) it is at most \(e^{n(\langle\theta, x\rangle - \Lambda(\theta)) + n|\theta|\delta}\); the tilted mass of the complement of the ball is exponentially small by Chernoff bounds in the \(2d\) coordinate half-spaces, which gives \(\liminf \frac1n \log \mu_n(B(x,\delta)) \ge -(\langle\theta, x\rangle - \Lambda(\theta)) - |\theta|\delta\). A general point is reached through the maximiser of \(\langle x, t\rangle - \Lambda(t) - \varepsilon|t|^2/2\).

    MathlibExt.Probability.CramerRateFunction.cramerRateFunction

    For a random vector \(X\) in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) under a measure \(\mu\), the Cramér rate function is the Legendre transform of the logarithmic moment generating function,

    \[\Lambda^*(x) = \sup_{t \in \mathbb{R}^d} \bigl( \langle t, x \rangle - \log E[e^{\langle t, X \rangle}] \bigr) \in [0, \infty],\]

    where \(\log E[e^{\langle t, X\rangle}] = \infty\) at directions with infinite exponential moment (such directions contribute nothing to the supremum). Under a probability measure the direction \(t = 0\) contributes \(0\), so \(\Lambda^* \ge 0\). It is convex and lower semicontinuous, vanishes at the mean when the moment generating function is finite, and is the rate function of Cramér's theorem for the empirical mean.

    In Varadhan's notes the rate function appears in dimension one as \(h(\ell) = \sup_\theta [\theta \ell - \psi(\theta)]\) with \(\psi = \log M\), equation (1.3), right after the Chebyshev bound \(\frac1n \log P[\bar X_n \ge \ell] \le -\sup_{\theta \ge 0} [\theta \ell - \psi(\theta)]\).
