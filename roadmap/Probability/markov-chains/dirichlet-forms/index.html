    Dirichlet forms

    A Dirichlet form is a closed symmetric form on \(L^2(X; m)\) on which the unit contraction \(u \mapsto (0 \vee u) \wedge 1\) operates. By the Beurling–Deny theorem these are exactly the forms of symmetric Markovian semigroups, and by Fukushima's theorem a regular Dirichlet form is the form of an \(m\)-symmetric Hunt process; Albeverio, Ma and Röckner extended this to quasi-regular forms on general (possibly infinite-dimensional) state spaces and showed that quasi-regularity is exactly the condition for a nice associated process (Fukushima, Oshima and Takeda, Dirichlet Forms and Symmetric Markov Processes, 2nd ed., 2011; Ma and Röckner, Introduction to the Theory of (Non-Symmetric) Dirichlet Forms, 1992).

    On a finite set everything is explicit: the form of a reversible sub-Markovian generator \(Q\) is \(\mathcal E(f, f) = \tfrac12 \sum_{x,y} m(x) Q(x,y) (f(x) - f(y))^2 + \sum_x m(x) k(x) f(x)^2\) with killing rate \(k = -\sum_y Q(\cdot, y)\), it has the Markov property, and conversely a symmetric form on \(\mathbb R^n\) is unit-contractive exactly when it is of this kind. The spectral gap and the log-Sobolev constant of a finite chain are the best constants in \(\lambda \operatorname{Var}(f) \le \mathcal E(f, f)\) and \(\alpha \mathcal L(f) \le \mathcal E(f, f)\).

    MathlibExt.Probability.DirichletForm.form

    For weights m and a matrix Q, E(f, g) = ½ ∑_{x,y} m(x) Q(x, y) (f x - f y)(g x - g y) + ∑_x m(x) k(x) f(x) g(x) with k = killingRate Q (Fukushima, Oshima and Takeda, Dirichlet Forms and Symmetric Markov Processes, 2nd ed., 2011, Example 1.2.5, equation (1.2.27), on a finite set). The diagonal entries of Q contribute only through k. FOT assume m symmetrising (m(x) Q(x, y) = m(y) Q(y, x), their (1.2.26)); the definition here makes sense for every (m, Q), and without detailed balance it is the form of the m-symmetrised generator (the Markov property below does not need detailed balance).

    On a finite set every bilinear form is closed, and the form is a Dirichlet form on \(\ell^2(m)\) as soon as \(m \ge 0\), the off-diagonal entries of \(Q\) are nonnegative and \(k \ge 0\) (see the Markov property). A rate matrix is the conservative case \(k = 0\).

    noncomputable def form (m : S → ℝ) (Q : Matrix S S ℝ) (f g : S → ℝ) : ℝ :=
  1 / 2 * ∑ x, ∑ y, m x * Q x y * (f x - f y) * (g x - g y) +
    ∑ x, m x * killingRate Q x * f x * g x
    def killingRate (Q : Matrix S S ℝ) (x : S) : ℝ :=
  -∑ y, Q x y

    MathlibExt.Probability.DirichletForm.unitContraction_le_iff

    For a symmetric matrix A on a finite set, the unit contraction operates on the form u ⬝ A u, i.e. E((0 ∨ u) ∧ 1) ≤ E(u) for every u, if and only if A(x, y) ≤ 0 for x ≠ y and every row sum ∑_y A(x, y) is nonnegative.
