    DefinitionBeing \(1/4\)-mixed at a given time#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.MarkovMixing.IsMixedAt

    Let \(S\) be a finite set, \(P\) a real \(S \times S\) matrix and \(\pi : S \to \mathbb{R}\). Then \(P\) is mixed at time \(t \in \mathbb{N}\) with respect to \(\pi\) if for every \(x \in S\) the total variation distance \(\frac{1}{2} \sum_{y \in S} |P^t(x, y) - \pi(y)|\) between the row \(x\) of the matrix power \(P^t\) and \(\pi\) is at most \(1/4\).

    The mixing time is the least \(t\) at which \(P\) is mixed.

    def IsMixedAt {S : Type*} [Fintype S] [DecidableEq S] (P : Matrix S S ℝ) (π : S → ℝ)
    (t : ℕ) : Prop :=
  ∀ x, tvDist (fun y => (P ^ t) x y) π ≤ 1 / 4
    noncomputable def tvDist {S : Type*} [Fintype S] (μ ν : S → ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (1 / 2) * ∑ y, |μ y - ν y|

    DefinitionCutoff for a family of distance profiles#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.MarkovMixing.HasCutoff

    A family of real numbers \(d_n(t)\), indexed by \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) and times \(t \in \mathbb{N}\), exhibits cutoff if there are real numbers \(t_n\) with \(t_n \to \infty\) such that for every \(\eta\) with \(0 < \eta < 1\), as \(n \to \infty\), \(d_n(\lceil (1+\eta) t_n \rceil) \to 0\) and \(d_n(\lfloor (1-\eta) t_n \rfloor) \to 1\). Here the ceiling and floor are taken in \(\mathbb{N}\), so a negative argument gives \(0\).

    def HasCutoff (d : ℕ → ℕ → ℝ) : Prop :=
  ∃ t : ℕ → ℝ, Filter.Tendsto t Filter.atTop Filter.atTop ∧ ∀ η : ℝ, 0 < η → η < 1 →
    Filter.Tendsto (fun n => d n ⌈(1 + η) * t n⌉₊) Filter.atTop (nhds 0) ∧
      Filter.Tendsto (fun n => d n ⌊(1 - η) * t n⌋₊) Filter.atTop (nhds 1)

    TheoremBeing 1/4-mixed persists#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.PoincareMixing.isMixedAt_mono

    If \(P\) is stochastic and \(\pi P = \pi\), a chain that is \(1/4\)-mixed at time \(t\) is \(1/4\)-mixed at every later time, because \(t \mapsto d_{TV}(P^t(x,\cdot), \pi)\) is nonincreasing for every start \(x\). So the single-time predicate IsMixedAt determines Sinclair's mixing time \(\tau_x(\varepsilon) = \min\{t : \Delta_x(t') \le \varepsilon \text{ for all } t' \ge t\}\) (A.     theorem isMixedAt_mono {π : S → ℝ} {P : Matrix S S ℝ} (hP : P ∈ rowStochastic ℝ S)
    (hπ : π ᵥ* P = π) {t t' : ℕ} (ht : IsMixedAt P π t) (htt' : t ≤ t') :
    IsMixedAt P π t'

    TheoremA Poincaré inequality makes a lazy reversible chain contract mean-zero functions#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.PoincareMixing.sum_mul_sq_mulVec_le_of_poincare

    Let \(K\) be stochastic and reversible with respect to a probability vector \(\pi > 0\), let \(P = (I + K)/2\), and suppose \(\sum_{x,y}(\psi(x)-\psi(y))^2\pi(x)\pi(y) \le B\sum_{x,y}(\psi(x)-\psi(y))^2\pi(x)P(x,y)\) for all \(\psi\), with \(B > 0\). Then every \(g\) with \(\sum_x \pi(x) g(x) = 0\) satisfies

    \[\sum_x \pi(x)\,(Pg)(x)^2 \;\le\; (1 - 1/B) \sum_x \pi(x)\, g(x)^2.\]

    This is weaker than the spectral statement: the bound \(\lambda_{\max} \le 1 - 1/B\) for the lazy chain, whose eigenvalues are nonnegative (A. Sinclair and M. Jerrum, Information and Computation 82 (1989), Proposition 3.2), would give the factor \((1 - 1/B)^2\). It is still enough for a mixing bound of the kind of A. Sinclair, Combinatorics, Probability and Computing 1 (1992), Proposition 1(i), with \(B\) in place of \((1-\lambda_{\max})^{-1}\) up to a factor \(2\); on the two-state lazy chain of the tests the true rate is \((1/4)^t\) against the proved \((1/2)^t\). The proof needs no eigenvalues: writing \(P = (I+K)/2\), the excess of \(\|g\|^2 - \mathcal{E}_P(g,g)\) over \(\|Pg\|^2\) is \((\|g\|^2 - \|Kg\|^2)/4 \ge 0\) because \(K\) is a contraction of \(L^2(\pi)\), and the Poincaré inequality gives \(\mathcal{E}_P(g,g) \ge \|g\|^2/B\).

    TheoremMixing from a Poincaré inequality, for a lazy reversible chain#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.PoincareMixing.sq_two_mul_tvDist_pow_le

    Let \(K\) be a stochastic matrix reversible with respect to a probability vector \(\pi > 0\), let \(P = (I + K)/2\) be its lazy version, and suppose \(P\) satisfies the Poincaré inequality \(\sum_{x,y}(\psi(x)-\psi(y))^2\pi(x)\pi(y) \le B\sum_{x,y}(\psi(x)-\psi(y))^2\pi(x)P(x,y)\) for all \(\psi\), with \(B \ge 1\). Then for every starting state \(x\) and every time \(t\),

    \[\bigl(2\, d_{TV}(P^t(x,\cdot), \pi)\bigr)^2 \;\le\; (1 - 1/B)^t\,\bigl(1/\pi(x) - 1\bigr).\]

    It is an \(L^2\) counterpart of Proposition 1(i) of A. Sinclair, Combinatorics, Probability and Computing 1 (1992) (stated there with \(\lambda_{\max}\), after Diaconis and Stroock), for the lazy chain of Sinclair's Section 2 and of Sinclair and Jerrum, Information and Computation 82 (1989), Proposition 3.2: the centred density \(P^t(x,\cdot)/\pi - 1\) has \(\pi\)-mean zero, each step of \(P\) multiplies its squared \(L^2(\pi)\) norm by at most \(1 - 1/B\), and Cauchy–Schwarz bounds twice the total variation distance by that norm. Laziness replaces the spectral bound on the smallest eigenvalue: no eigenvalues are computed. With canonical paths, \(B = \bar\rho(\Gamma)\) for the lazy chain gives the mixing time \(O(\bar\rho\,(\log \pi(x)^{-1} + \log \varepsilon^{-1}))\).

    theorem sq_two_mul_tvDist_pow_le {π : S → ℝ} (hπ : ∀ x, 0 < π x) (hπ1 : ∑ x, π x = 1)

      B * ∑ x, ∑ y, (ψ x - ψ y) ^ 2 * (π x * ((1 / 2 : ℝ) • (1 + K)) x y))
