    Free probability

    Free probability is noncommutative probability with free independence in place of independence. A noncommutative probability space is a unital complex algebra \(A\) with a linear functional \(\varphi\) such that \(\varphi(1) = 1\); unital subalgebras \((A_j)\) are free when every alternating product of centred elements is centred. Free independence describes large random matrices: Voiculescu showed that independent Wigner matrices, or \(A_N\) and \(U_N B_N U_N^*\) with \(U_N\) Haar unitary, are asymptotically free, so the spectrum of a sum is given by the free additive convolution of the spectra.

    The combinatorics is that of noncrossing partitions. Free cumulants \(\kappa_n\) are defined by the moment-cumulant formula \(m_n = \sum_{\pi \in NC(n)} \prod_{V \in \pi} \kappa_{|V|}\); they are additive for free variables, so the R-transform \(R(z) = \sum_n \kappa_{n+1} z^n\) linearises free convolution, as the logarithm of the Fourier transform linearises classical convolution. The semicircle law plays the role of the Gaussian: its only nonzero free cumulant is \(\kappa_2\), its R-transform is \(z\), and it is the limit in the free central limit theorem.

    No non-trivial free family is constructed here: the only instances proved are trivial (a family with at most one member, and families in which all but one member is the algebra of scalars). Non-trivial free families, such as the group algebra of a free group with its canonical trace or a free semicircular family on the full Fock space, exist but are only argued, not constructed.

    Proved here: the definitions of freeness, free cumulants and the R-transform, that free variables are uncorrelated, the moment-cumulant formula, \(\kappa_1\) and \(\kappa_2\) as mean and variance, and the free cumulants and R-transform of the semicircle law. Stated: additivity of free cumulants and the free central limit theorem.

    DefinitionFree independence#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.FreeProbability.IsFreelyIndependent

    Let \(A\) be a unital complex algebra and \(\varphi : A \to \mathbb{C}\) a linear functional. A family \((B_i)_{i \in \iota}\) of unital subalgebras of \(A\) is freely independent if $\(\varphi(a_1 a_2 \cdots a_n) = 0\)$ for every \(n \ge 1\), all indices \(k(1), \dots, k(n) \in \iota\) with \(k(1) \ne k(2) \ne \dots \ne k(n)\) (consecutive indices distinct), and all \(a_j \in B_{k(j)}\) with \(\varphi(a_j) = 0\).

    Repetitions such as \(k(1) = k(3)\) are allowed, which makes freeness a genuinely noncommutative notion: if \(x, y\) are free and centred, \(\varphi(xy) = 0\) but also \(\varphi(xyxy) = 0\), whereas for commuting independent variables \(\varphi(xyxy) = \varphi(x^2)\varphi(y^2)\). In Lean the words are indexed by Fin (n + 1), so that they are nonempty, and the definition does not itself require \(\varphi(1) = 1\); results that need it assume it.

    No non-trivial free family is constructed here: the only instances proved are trivial (a family with at most one member, and families in which all but one member is the algebra of scalars). Non-trivial free families, such as the group algebra of a free group with its canonical trace or a free semicircular family on the full Fock space, exist but are only argued, not constructed.

    def IsFreelyIndependent (φ : A →ₗ[ℂ] ℂ) {ι : Type*} (B : ι → Subalgebra ℂ A) : Prop :=
  ∀ (n : ℕ) (k : Fin (n + 1) → ι) (a : Fin (n + 1) → A),
    (∀ i : Fin n, k i.castSucc ≠ k i.succ) → (∀ i, a i ∈ B (k i)) → (∀ i, φ (a i) = 0) →
      φ (List.ofFn a).prod = 0

    TheoremScalars are free from everything#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.FreeProbability.isFreelyIndependent_of_eq_bot

    In a noncommutative probability space (\(\varphi(1) = 1\)), a family of unital subalgebras in which every member except one is the algebra of scalars \(\mathbb{C}1\) is freely independent. A centred scalar \(c1\) has \(c = \varphi(c1) = 0\), and in an alternating word of length at least two one of the first two letters is such a scalar, so the word is \(0\). In particular any element is free from \(0\). This is a trivial instance: with \(b = 0\), additivity of free cumulants reduces to \(\kappa_n(a) = \kappa_n(a)\) because every free cumulant of \(0\) vanishes, so it does not exercise freeness in any substantive way. Non-trivial free families are only argued, not constructed, in the repository.

    TheoremFree variables are uncorrelated#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.FreeProbability.IsFreelyIndependent.map_mul

    In a noncommutative probability space (\(\varphi(1) = 1\)), if \((B_k)\) is freely independent, \(i \ne j\), \(a \in B_i\) and \(b \in B_j\), then $\(\varphi(ab) = \varphi(a)\,\varphi(b).\)$ Proof: the centred elements \(a - \varphi(a)1 \in B_i\) and \(b - \varphi(b)1 \in B_j\) form an alternating word of length two, so \(\varphi\bigl((a - \varphi(a))(b - \varphi(b))\bigr) = 0\); expanding gives the identity. Longer alternating words give the genuinely free relations, for instance \(\varphi(abab) = \varphi(a^2)\varphi(b)^2 + \varphi(a)^2\varphi(b^2) - \varphi(a)^2\varphi(b)^2\).

    DefinitionFree cumulants of a moment sequence#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.FreeProbability.freeCumulant

    For a sequence \(m = (m_n)_{n \ge 0}\) of complex numbers, the free cumulants \(\kappa_n\), \(n \ge 1\), are the unique numbers satisfying the moment-cumulant formula $\(m_n = \sum_{\pi \in NC(n)} \prod_{V \in \pi} \kappa_{|V|} \qquad (n \ge 1),\)$ the sum running over noncrossing partitions of \(\{1, \dots, n\}\). The term of the one-block partition \(1_n\) is \(\kappa_n\) and every other partition has blocks of size less than \(n\), so the formula is triangular and is taken as the recursive definition $\(\kappa_n = m_n - \sum_{\pi \in NC(n),\ \pi \ne 1_n} \prod_{V \in \pi} \kappa_{|V|}.\)$ For an element \(a\) of a noncommutative probability space and \(m_n = \varphi(a^n)\), this is the cumulant \(k_n(a) = k_n(a, \dots, a)\) of Anderson, Guionnet and Zeitouni. For example \(\kappa_1 = m_1\) and \(\kappa_2 = m_2 - m_1^2\) is the variance. The value at \(n = 0\) is \(m_0\) and plays no role.

    noncomputable def freeCumulant (m : ℕ → ℂ) (n : ℕ) : ℂ :=
  m n - ∑ π ∈ (univ : Finset (_root_.NoncrossingPartition n)).erase ⊤,
    ∏ V ∈ π.val.parts, if _h : V.card < n then freeCumulant m V.card else 0
termination_by n

    TheoremMoment-cumulant formula#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.FreeProbability.moment_eq_sum_noncrossing_prod_freeCumulant

    For every moment sequence \(m\) and every \(n \ge 1\), $\(m_n = \sum_{\pi \in NC(n)} \prod_{V \in \pi} \kappa_{|V|},\)$ where \(\kappa\) are the free cumulants of \(m\).     MathlibExt.Probability.FreeProbability.rTransform

    TheoremThe free cumulants of the semicircle law#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.FreeProbability.freeCumulant_semicircle

    The free cumulants of the standard semicircle law \(\sigma\) are \(\kappa_2 = 1\) and \(\kappa_n = 0\) for every other \(n \ge 1\). In this sense \(\sigma\) is the free Gaussian: a classical Gaussian is characterised by the vanishing of its classical cumulants beyond the second.

    Proof: by strong induction on \(n\). For \(\pi \ne 1_n\), the induction hypothesis turns \(\prod_{V \in \pi} \kappa_{|V|}\) into \(1\) if \(\pi\) is a pair partition and \(0\) otherwise, so \(\sum_{\pi \ne 1_n} \prod_{V} \kappa_{|V|}\) counts the noncrossing pair partitions other than \(1_n\). The moment \(\int x^n\,d\sigma\) counts all noncrossing pair partitions of \(\{1, \dots, n\}\), so \(\kappa_n\) is \(1\) if the one-block partition \(1_n\) is a pair partition, that is if \(n = 2\), and \(0\) otherwise.

    theorem freeCumulant_semicircle {n : ℕ} (hn : 0 < n) :
    freeCumulant semicircleMoment n = if n = 2 then 1 else 0

    MathlibExt.Probability.FreeProbabilityWanted.freeCumulant_add_of_areFree

    Let \((A, \varphi)\) be a noncommutative probability space and let \(a, b \in A\) be free. Then for every \(n \ge 1\), $\(\kappa_n(a + b) = \kappa_n(a) + \kappa_n(b),\)$ where \(\kappa_n(x)\) is the free cumulant of the moment sequence \(k \mapsto \varphi(x^k)\). Equivalently, the R-transform is additive, \(R_{a+b} = R_a + R_b\), and the law of \(a + b\) is the free additive convolution of the laws of \(a\) and \(b\).

    The source states it for bounded operators; its proof is algebraic: expand \(\kappa_n(a + b)\) by multilinearity of the multivariate cumulants and use that mixed cumulants of free variables vanish (Theorem 5.3.15). Stated here, not proved.

    MathlibExt.Probability.FreeProbabilityWanted.free_central_limit

    Let \((A, \varphi)\) be a noncommutative probability space whose functional is a tracial state (\(\varphi(1) = 1\), \(\varphi(xy) = \varphi(yx)\), \(\varphi(x^* x) \ge 0\)), and let \(a_0, a_1, \dots\) be free self-adjoint elements with \(\varphi(a_i) = 0\), \(\varphi(a_i^2) = 1\) and \(\sup_i |\varphi(a_i^k)| < \infty\) for every \(k\). Then $\(X_N = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} a_i\)$ converges in moments to the standard semicircle law: for every \(p\), \(\varphi(X_N^p)\) tends to the number of noncrossing pair partitions of \(\{1, \dots, p\}\), which is \(\int x^p \, d\sigma(x)\).

    Proof in the source: by additivity of free cumulants, \(\kappa_p(X_N) = N^{-p/2} \sum_i \kappa_p(a_i)\), which tends to \(0\) for \(p \ge 3\), while \(\kappa_1(X_N) = 0\) and \(\kappa_2(X_N) = 1\); the moment-cumulant formula then leaves only the noncrossing pair partitions. Stated here, not proved. The hypotheses are met by a free semicircular family on the full Fock space, which is not constructed here.
