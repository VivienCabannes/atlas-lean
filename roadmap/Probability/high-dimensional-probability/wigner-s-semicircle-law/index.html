    Wigner's semicircle law

    For a Wigner matrix \(W_n\) (real symmetric, with independent, identically distributed upper-triangular entries of mean \(0\) and variance \(1\); Tao's Theorem 2.4.2 allows the diagonal a different law), the empirical spectral distribution of \(W_n / \sqrt{n}\) converges almost surely to the semicircle distribution \(\sigma\), with density \(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{4 - x^2}\) on \([-2, 2]\). The moment method proves it by showing that \(\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\operatorname{tr}(W_n/\sqrt{n})^{2k}\) tends to the Catalan number \(C_k\), which counts non-crossing pair partitions (equivalently Dyck words), and that \(C_k\) is the \(2k\)-th moment of \(\sigma\); concentration then upgrades convergence in expectation to almost sure convergence. The moment identities, the fact that \(\sigma\) is a probability measure and the fact that \(\sigma\) is determined by its moments are proved here; the law itself is stated.

    Let \(W\) be a Hermitian \(n \times n\) complex matrix with real eigenvalues \(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n\), listed with multiplicity.     MathlibExt.Analysis.WignerSemicircleWanted.Analysis.WignerSemicircleProblem.semicircleLaw

    TheoremWigner's semicircle law#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Analysis.WignerSemicircleWanted.Analysis.WignerSemicircleProblem.wigner_semicircle

    Let \((\Omega, \mu)\) be a probability space and let \(X_{ij} : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}\), for \(i, j \in \mathbb{N}\), be measurable functions. Suppose that the family \((X_{ij})_{i \le j}\) is mutually independent, that all \(X_{ij}\) with \(i \le j\) have the same distribution, and that for \(i \le j\) both \(X_{ij}\) and \(X_{ij}^2\) are integrable with \(\mathbb{E}[X_{ij}] = 0\) and \(\mathbb{E}[X_{ij}^2] = 1\). For \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(\omega \in \Omega\), let \(W_n(\omega)\) be the Wigner matrix of \(X\) and let \(L_n(\omega)\) be the image of its empirical spectral measure under \(x \mapsto x / \sqrt{n}\) (for \(n \ge 1\), the empirical spectral measure of \(W_n(\omega) / \sqrt{n}\)). Then for \(\mu\)-almost every \(\omega\), every bounded continuous function \(f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) satisfies

    \[\int f \, dL_n(\omega) \to \int f \, d\sigma \quad (n \to \infty),\]

    where \(\sigma\) is the semicircle distribution.

    Informally uses
    Informally uses
    • The even moments of the semicircle distribution are the Catalan numbers: the moment method shows that \(\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\operatorname{tr}(W_n/\sqrt{n})^{2k} \to C_k\) and identifies the limit through this computation.
    • The odd moments of the semicircle distribution vanish: the odd normalised traces tend to \(0\), the odd moments of the limit.
    Background
    Background
    • Talagrand's concentration inequality around a median: background; concentration of the linear statistics \(\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{tr} f(W_n/\sqrt{n})\) upgrades convergence in expectation to almost sure convergence.
    • Hoffman–Wielandt inequality for Hermitian matrices: background; it bounds how far the empirical spectral distribution moves under a perturbation of small Frobenius norm, which is how truncation reduces the theorem to bounded entries.
    • Lipschitz linear statistics of Hermitian spectra are stable: background; the form of that bound used for linear statistics \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_i f(\lambda_i)\).
    • Expected moments of a Wigner matrix converge to the semicircle moments: background; the first half of the moment method under moments of all orders (Anderson, Guionnet and Zeitouni, Lemma 2.1.6 in the authors' 2009 pre-publication manuscript).
    • Concentration of linear spectral statistics: background; for entries satisfying a logarithmic Sobolev inequality, the fluctuations of \(\operatorname{tr} f(X_N)\) are exponentially small at scale \(N^2\), which gives almost sure convergence by Borel–Cantelli.
    • Ben Arous–Guionnet large deviation principle: background; for Gaussian ensembles the empirical spectral measure satisfies a large deviation principle at speed \(N^2\) whose rate function vanishes only at \(\sigma\), another route to the law.
    • Strong Bai–Yin theorem, upper bound: background; under a fourth moment the spectrum also stays inside \([-2 - \varepsilon, 2 + \varepsilon]\).

    DefinitionSemicircle density#✓fully proved

    The semicircle density is the function \(p : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\), \(p(x) = \frac{\sqrt{4 - x^2}}{2\pi}\), where the square root of a negative number is taken to be \(0\). So \(p\) is \(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{4 - x^2}\) on \([-2, 2]\) and vanishes outside it.

    TheoremThe even moments of the semicircle distribution are the Catalan numbers#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.integral_pow_two_mul_semicircleReal

    For every \(k \in \mathbb{N}\), the semicircle distribution \(\sigma\) satisfies $\(\int x^{2k} \, d\sigma(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-2}^{2} x^{2k} \sqrt{4 - x^2} \, dx = C_k = \frac{1}{k + 1}\binom{2k}{k}.\)$ So \(\sigma\) has variance \(C_1 = 1\), fourth moment \(C_2 = 2\) and sixth moment \(C_3 = 5\). In the moment method for Wigner's semicircle law, \(C_k\) counts the non-crossing pairings that dominate \(\frac{1}{n}\,\mathbb{E}\operatorname{tr}(W_n / \sqrt{n})^{2k}\), and this identity recognises the limit as \(\sigma\).

    Proof: substitute \(x = 2\cos\theta\) with \(\theta \in [0, \pi]\), which turns the integral into \(\frac{2^{2k+2}}{2\pi}\int_0^\pi \cos^{2k}\theta \sin^2\theta \, d\theta\), and evaluate \(\int_0^\pi \cos^{2k}\theta \, d\theta = \pi \binom{2k}{k} / 4^k\) with the reduction formula \(\int_0^\pi \cos^{n+2} = \frac{n+1}{n+2}\int_0^\pi \cos^n\).

    ∫ x, x ^ (2 * k) ∂semicircleReal = catalan k

    TheoremThe semicircle distribution is determined by its moments#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.eq_semicircleReal_of_integral_pow_eq

    Let \(\nu\) be a finite measure on \(\mathbb{R}\) such that \(x \mapsto x^{2k}\) is \(\nu\)-integrable for every \(k\), and suppose that \(\int x^k \, d\nu = \int x^k \, d\sigma\) for every \(k \in \mathbb{N}\), where \(\sigma\) is the semicircle distribution. Then \(\nu = \sigma\).

    TheoremThe moments of the semicircle law count noncrossing pair partitions#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.integral_pow_semicircleReal_eq_card_noncrossing_pairPartition

    For every \(n \ge 0\), the \(n\)-th moment of the semicircle distribution \(\sigma(dx) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{4 - x^2}\,\mathbf{1}_{|x| \le 2}\,dx\) is the number of noncrossing pair partitions of \(\{1, \dots, n\}\): $\(\int x^n \, d\sigma(x) = \#\{\pi \in NC(n) : \text{every block of } \pi \text{ has two elements}\},\)$ which is \(C_{n/2}\) for \(n\) even and \(0\) for \(n\) odd.

    This is the form in which the semicircle law appears at the end of two different arguments. In the moment method for Wigner matrices, the Wigner words of length \(n + 1\) that dominate \(\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\operatorname{tr} X_N^{n}\) correspond to noncrossing pair partitions (Anderson, Guionnet and Zeitouni, Proposition 2.1.11). In the free central limit theorem, all free cumulants of \(N^{-1/2}(a_1 + \dots + a_N)\) except the second vanish in the limit, so the moment-cumulant formula over noncrossing partitions leaves exactly the noncrossing pair partitions (Lemma 5.3.32 there).

    MathlibExt.Probability.WignerMatrixMomentsWanted.concentration_trace_lipschitz

    Let \(N \ge 1\) and \(c > 0\), and let \(X_N\) be the real symmetric \(N \times N\) random matrix with independent entries \(X_{ij}\), \(i \le j\), whose laws satisfy the logarithmic Sobolev inequality with constant \(c/N\): \(\int f^2 \log \frac{f^2}{\int f^2} \le \frac{2c}{N} \int |f'|^2\). Then for every \(L\)-Lipschitz \(f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) and every \(\delta > 0\), $\(\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\bigl|\operatorname{tr} f(X_N) - \mathbb{E}\operatorname{tr} f(X_N)\bigr| \ge \delta N\Bigr) \le 2 \exp\Bigl(-\frac{N^2 \delta^2}{4 c L^2}\Bigr),\)$ where \(\operatorname{tr} f(X_N) = \sum_i f(\lambda_i)\). For the normalised trace \(\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{tr} f(X_N) = \int f \, dL_N\) this is a deviation of \(\delta\) at probability \(e^{-O(N^2)}\), the large deviation scale of the empirical spectral measure. The two formulations use different classes of test functions (Lipschitz densities there, differentiable \(f\) here).
