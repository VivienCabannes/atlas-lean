    Random walks on finite groups

    Random walks on a finite group \(G\) driven by a probability \(Q\): the \(k\)-step law is the convolution power \(Q^{*k}\) and the stationary law is uniform. Their rates of convergence in total variation are read off the Fourier transform of \(Q\) at the irreducible representations of \(G\) (the Diaconis–Shahshahani upper bound lemma), with lower bounds from test sets. Main examples: random walk on the circle \(\mathbb{Z}/n\) and the cube \(\mathbb{Z}_2^n\), random transpositions on \(S_n\) (mixing at \(\tfrac12 n\log n\)) and riffle shuffles (Bayer–Diaconis).

    TheoremUpper bound lemma for a random walk on a finite abelian group#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.RandomWalkFiniteGroup.four_mul_tvDist_addConvPow_sq_le

    Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group and \(P : G \to \mathbb{R}\) a function of total mass \(1\). If \(Q : G \to \mathbb{R}\) is the \(k\)-th convolution power of \(P\) (stated in Lean as: the complex values of \(Q\) are those of the \(k\)-th convolution power of \(P\)), then

    \[4\,\|Q - U\|_{TV}^2 \le \sum_{\psi \ne 1} |\hat P(\psi)|^{2k}.\]

    Since \(\hat{P^{*k}} = \hat P^{\,k}\), this is the upper bound lemma on a finite abelian group applied to \(Q\). It is the form in which Diaconis uses the lemma: equation (3.3) of the Saint-Flour notes for the cube \(\mathbb{Z}_2^n\), and the first display of the proof of Theorem 2.1 there for simple random walk on \(\mathbb{Z}/n\).

    theorem four_mul_tvDist_addConvPow_sq_le [DecidableEq G] (P Q : G → ℝ) (hP : ∑ x, P x = 1)
    (k : ℕ) (hQ : ∀ x, (Q x : ℂ) = addConvPow (fun x => (P x : ℂ)) k x) :
    4 * tvDist Q (uniform G) ^ 2 ≤
      ∑ ψ ∈ Finset.univ.filter (fun ψ : AddChar G ℂ => ψ ≠ 0),
        ‖dftFun (fun x => (P x : ℂ)) ψ‖ ^ (2 * k)
    noncomputable def addConvPow [DecidableEq G] (f : G → ℂ) : ℕ → G → ℂ

    TheoremUpper bound lemma on a finite abelian group#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.RandomWalkFiniteGroup.four_mul_tvDist_uniform_sq_le

    Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group, \(U\) the uniform distribution on \(G\) and \(P : G \to \mathbb{R}\) a function of total mass \(\sum_x P(x) = 1\). With \(\hat P(\psi) = \sum_x \psi(x) P(x)\) the discrete Fourier transform at a character \(\psi : G \to \mathbb{C}\),

    \[4\,\|P - U\|_{TV}^2 \le \sum_{\psi \ne 1} |\hat P(\psi)|^2,\]

    the sum running over the non-trivial characters, and \(\|P - U\|_{TV} = \tfrac12 \sum_x |P(x) - U(x)|\). No positivity of \(P\) is needed.

    The proof is Diaconis's: by Cauchy–Schwarz, \((\sum_x |P(x) - U(x)|)^2 \le |G| \sum_x (P(x) - U(x))^2\); by Plancherel the right side is \(\sum_\psi |\hat P(\psi) - \hat U(\psi)|^2\); finally \(\hat P(1) = \hat U(1) = 1\) and \(\hat U(\psi) = 0\) for \(\psi \ne 1\). For \(G = \mathbb{Z}/n\) this is Lemma 2.1 of the Saint-Flour notes. It is the abelian case of the upper bound lemma on a general finite group, where all irreducible representations are one-dimensional.

    theorem four_mul_tvDist_uniform_sq_le (P : G → ℝ) (hP : ∑ x, P x = 1) :
    4 * tvDist P (uniform G) ^ 2 ≤
      ∑ ψ ∈ Finset.univ.filter (fun ψ : AddChar G ℂ => ψ ≠ 0),
        ‖dftFun (fun x => (P x : ℂ)) ψ‖ ^ 2
    noncomputable def tvDist {S : Type*} [Fintype S] (μ ν : S → ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (1 / 2) * ∑ y, |μ y - ν y|

    TheoremFourier transform of the random-transposition measure#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.RandomWalkFiniteGroup.fourier_randomTransposition

    Let \(\rho\) be an irreducible representation of \(S_n\) on a finite-dimensional complex vector space of dimension \(d_\rho\), with character \(\chi_\rho\), and let \(\tau\) be any transposition. The Fourier transform of the random-transposition measure \(T\) at \(\rho\) is the scalar

    \[\hat T(\rho) = \Bigl(\frac1n + \frac{n-1}{n}\,\frac{\chi_\rho(\tau)}{d_\rho}\Bigr) I .\]

    \(T\) is a class function, so its transform is a scalar by Fourier transform of a class function at an irreducible representation; the scalar follows from \(T(\mathrm{id}) = 1/n\), the \(\binom n2\) transpositions of mass \(2/n^2\) and the constancy of \(\chi_\rho\) on transpositions. With the convolution law \(\widehat{T^{*k}}(\rho) = \hat T(\rho)^k\) this reduces the analysis of \(k\) random transpositions to powers of real numbers (the remark after Corollary 1 of the paper).

    theorem fourier_randomTransposition {n : ℕ} {V : Type*} [AddCommGroup V] [Module ℂ V]
    [FiniteDimensional ℂ V] (ρ : Representation ℂ (Equiv.Perm (Fin n)) V) [ρ.IsIrreducible]
    (τ : Equiv.Perm (Fin n)) (hτ : τ.IsSwap) :
    fourier ρ (fun σ => (randomTransposition n σ : ℂ)) =
      ((1 : ℂ) / n + ((n : ℂ) - 1) / n * (ρ.character τ / Module.finrank ℂ V)) • 1
    noncomputable def fourier (ρ : Representation ℂ G V) (P : G → ℂ) : Module.End ℂ V :=
  ∑ s, P s • ρ s

    DefinitionThe random-transposition measure on the symmetric group#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.RandomWalkFiniteGroup.randomTransposition

    Two positions \(L\) and \(R\) are chosen independently and uniformly among \(n\) and the cards there are swapped (nothing happens when \(L = R\)). The resulting probability on \(S_n\) is

    \[T(\mathrm{id}) = \frac1n, \qquad T(\tau) = \frac{2}{n^2} \text{ for each transposition } \tau, \qquad T = 0 \text{ elsewhere}.\]

    The Lean definition is this formula, as a function \(S_n \to \mathbb{R}\) (for \(n = 0\) Lean's convention \(1/0 = 0\) applies). The law after \(k\) random transpositions is the convolution power \(T^{*k}\).

    noncomputable def randomTransposition (n : ℕ) : Equiv.Perm (Fin n) → ℝ :=
  fun σ => if σ = 1 then 1 / n else if σ.IsSwap then 2 / (n : ℝ) ^ 2 else 0

    DefinitionNumber of rising sequences of an arrangement#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.RiffleShuffle.risingSequences

    Read \(\pi \in S_n\) as an arrangement of a deck, card \(\pi(i)\) at position \(i\). A rising sequence is a maximal run of consecutive face values \(v, v+1, \dots\) lying at increasing positions; for example \(1, 5, 2, 3, 6, 7, 4\) has the two rising sequences \(1, 2, 3, 4\) and \(5, 6, 7\). The runs break exactly at the values \(v\) whose successor \(v + 1\) lies above \(v\), that is \(\pi^{-1}(v+1) < \pi^{-1}(v)\), so the number of rising sequences is one plus the number of such \(v\). This is the Lean definition. Equivalently, \(\pi\) has \(r\) rising sequences iff \(\pi^{-1}\) has \(r - 1\) descents.

    noncomputable def risingSequences {n : ℕ} (π : Equiv.Perm (Fin n)) : ℕ :=
  1 + (Finset.univ.filter fun p : Fin n × Fin n =>
    p.2.val = p.1.val + 1 ∧ π⁻¹ p.2 < π⁻¹ p.1).card

    MathlibExt.Probability.RandomWalkFiniteGroupWanted.diaconis_shahshahani_lower

    Let \(T\) be the random-transposition measure on \(S_n\). For every choice of step numbers \(k = k(n)\), with \(c = (k - \tfrac12 n \log n)/n\),

    \[\|T^{*k} - U\| \ge 2\Bigl(\frac1e - \exp\bigl(-e^{-2c}\bigr)\Bigr) + o(1) \qquad (n \to \infty),\]

    in the \(\ell^1\) norm \(\|\cdot\| = 2\,\|\cdot\|_{TV}\). In Lean: for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), eventually in \(n\), \(2(e^{-1} - \exp(-e^{-2c})) - \varepsilon \le 2\,\|T^{*k(n)} - U\|_{TV}\). The bound is useful for \(c < 0\).

    The proof, which the paper credits to Charles Stein, uses the set \(A\) of permutations with a fixed point: \(U(A) \to 1 - 1/e\) (the matching problem), while \(T^{*k}(A)\) is at least the probability that \(2k\) balls dropped into \(n\) boxes leave a box empty (coupon collecting).

    Together with the upper bound, random transpositions exhibit cutoff at \(\tfrac12 n \log n\).

    theorem_wanted diaconis_shahshahani_lower (k : ℕ → ℕ) (ε : ℝ) (hε : 0 < ε) :
    ∀ᶠ n : ℕ in Filter.atTop,
      2 * (Real.exp (-1) - Real.exp (-Real.exp (-2 * (((k n : ℝ) - n * Real.log n / 2) / n))))
        - ε ≤ 2 * tvDist (convPow (randomTransposition n) (k n)) (uniform (Equiv.Perm (Fin n)))

    TheoremRandom transpositions: \(\tfrac12 n \log n + cn\) steps suffice (Diaconis–Shahshahani)#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Probability.RandomWalkFiniteGroupWanted.diaconis_shahshahani_upper

    Let \(T\) be the random-transposition measure on \(S_n\) and \(U\) the uniform distribution. There is a constant \(b > 0\) such that for all \(n \ge 10\) and all \(k\) with

    \[c = \frac{k - \tfrac12 n \log n}{n} > 0,\]

    the law \(T^{*k}\) of \(k\) random transpositions satisfies \(\|T^{*k} - U\| \le b\, e^{-2c}\).

    Diaconis and Shahshahani measure distance with the \(\ell^1\) norm \(\|T^{*k} - U\| = \sum_\pi |T^{*k}(\pi) - 1/n!|\), which is twice the total variation distance; the Lean statement writes \(2\,\|T^{*k} - U\|_{TV}\). The constant is universal: the paper computes it explicitly (end of Section 3 and Remark 1 of Section 5).

    Together with the matching lower bound this shows that random transpositions mix at \(\tfrac12 n \log n\), with a window of order \(n\): an instance of the cutoff phenomenon.

    The proof starts from the upper bound for random transpositions, the sum over irreducible representations proved here, and estimates it with Frobenius's character formula, the dimension bound \(d_\lambda \le \binom{n}{\lambda_1}\sqrt{(n-\lambda_1)!}\) (Corollary 2 of the paper) and a bound on the number of partitions; these estimates are not formalized.

    theorem_wanted diaconis_shahshahani_upper :
    ∃ b : ℝ, 0 < b ∧ ∀ n k : ℕ, 10 ≤ n →
      0 < ((k : ℝ) - n * Real.log n / 2) / n →
      2 * tvDist (convPow (randomTransposition n) k) (uniform (Equiv.Perm (Fin n))) ≤
        b * Real.exp (-2 * (((k : ℝ) - n * Real.log n / 2) / n))

    TheoremBayer–Diaconis formula for \(a\)-shuffles#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.RiffleShuffle.card_sort_eq

    An \(a\)-shuffle of a deck of \(n\) cards cuts the deck into \(a\) packets (multinomially) and riffles them together; for \(a = 2\) it is the Gilbert–Shannon–Reeds model of an ordinary riffle shuffle. Bayer and Diaconis prove that the probability that an \(a\)-shuffle produces the arrangement \(\pi\) is

    \[\frac{\binom{a + n - r}{n}}{a^n}, \qquad r = \text{number of rising sequences of } \pi .\]

    The Lean statement uses the inverse description of an \(a\)-shuffle (Section 3 of the paper): each position receives a pile label \(f(i) \in \{0, \dots, a - 1\}\) uniformly and independently, and the piles are stacked in label order, each keeping its internal order. The resulting inverse shuffle is the stable sorting permutation Tuple.sort f, so the \(a\)-shuffle equals \(\pi\) exactly when Tuple.sort f \(= \pi^{-1}\). The theorem states that, for every \(n\) and \(a\), exactly \(\binom{a + n - r}{n}\) of the \(a^n\) label maps \(f\) give \(\pi\); Lemma 1 of the paper shows that this description generates the same distribution as the others.

    The proof is the paper's stars-and-bars count, made bijective. With \(\sigma = \pi^{-1}\), the maps \(f\) with Tuple.sort f \(= \sigma\) are those for which \(g = f \circ \sigma\) is monotone and strictly increases at each descent of \(\sigma\) (\(\sigma(i+1) < \sigma(i)\)); there are \(r - 1\) descents. Adding to \(g(k)\) the number of non-descents before \(k\) turns these maps into the strictly increasing maps \(\{0, \dots, n-1\} \to \{0, \dots, a + n - r - 1\}\), of which there are \(\binom{a + n - r}{n}\).

    The same Lemma 1 says that an \(a\)-shuffle followed by a \(b\)-shuffle is an \(ab\)-shuffle, so \(m\) riffle shuffles form one \(2^m\)-shuffle (Theorem 1). From this formula the paper derives that \(\tfrac32 \log_2 n + \theta\) shuffles are necessary and sufficient, a cutoff (Theorem 2); for \(n = 52\) the total variation distance after \(m = 5, 6, 7, 8\) shuffles is \(0.924, 0.614, 0.334, 0.167\) (Table 1), whence "seven shuffles".

    theorem card_sort_eq (n a : ℕ) (π : Equiv.Perm (Fin n)) :
    (univ.filter (fun f : Fin n → Fin a => Tuple.sort f = π⁻¹)).card =
      (a + n - risingSequences π).choose n

    TheoremUpper bound lemma (Diaconis–Shahshahani)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.RandomWalkFiniteGroup.upper_bound_lemma

    Let \(G\) be a finite group and let \(V_i\) (\(i \in \iota\)) be a complete set of irreducible complex representations of \(G\): pairwise non-isomorphic simple objects of FDRep ℂ G, every simple object being isomorphic to one of them. Write \(d_i\) for the dimension of \(V_i\) and \(\hat P(V_i) = \sum_s P(s)\, V_i(s)\) for the Fourier transform. Let \(P : G \to \mathbb{R}\) have total mass \(1\) and let \(U\) be the uniform distribution. Then

    \[4\,\|P - U\|_{TV}^2 \le \sum_{i \,:\, V_i \text{ non-trivial}} d_i \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\hat P(V_i)\, \hat P(V_i)^*\bigr),\]

    where \(V_i\) is non-trivial when some \(V_i(g)\) is not the identity, and \(\|P - U\|_{TV} = \tfrac12 \sum_s |P(s) - U(s)|\).

    The source takes each \(V_i(s)\) unitary; then, \(P\) being real, \(\hat P(V_i)^* = \sum_s P(s)\, V_i(s^{-1})\) is the transform of \(s \mapsto P(s^{-1})\), which is how the Lean statement writes the trace without choosing an inner product. The Saint-Flour notes print the lemma with \(=\); their proof (Cauchy–Schwarz, then Plancherel) gives \(\le\), which is what is stated.

    The proof is Diaconis's: with \(f = P - U\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \((\sum_s |f(s)|)^2 \le |G| \sum_s f(s)^2\); the Plancherel formula turns the right side into \(\sum_i d_i \operatorname{Tr}(\hat f(V_i) \hat f(V_i)^*)\); the trivial term vanishes because \(f\) has total mass \(0\), and \(\hat f(V_i) = \hat P(V_i)\) for non-trivial \(V_i\) because the uniform distribution has zero transform there.

    For a finite abelian group every \(d_i = 1\) and the \(V_i\) are the characters; that case is stated with the discrete Fourier transform in Upper bound lemma on a finite abelian group.

            (fourier (V i).ρ (fun s => (P s : ℂ)) *
              fourier (V i).ρ (fun s => (P s⁻¹ : ℂ)))).re

    TheoremUpper bound for random transpositions#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.RandomWalkFiniteGroup.four_mul_tvDist_randomTransposition_sq_le

    Let \(V_i\) (\(i \in \iota\)) be a complete set of irreducible complex representations of \(S_n\), with dimensions \(d_i\) and characters \(\chi_i\), and let \(\tau\) be a transposition. For every \(k\), the law \(T^{*k}\) of \(k\) random transpositions satisfies

    \[4\,\|T^{*k} - U\|_{TV}^2 \le \sum_{i \,:\, V_i \text{ non-trivial}} d_i^2 \operatorname{Re}\Bigl(\frac1n + \frac{n-1}{n}\,\frac{\chi_i(\tau)}{d_i}\Bigr)^{2k}.\]

    (The characters of \(S_n\) are integers, so the real part is the number itself; the Lean statement keeps the real part to avoid proving this.)

    This is Lemma 14, equation (3.1), of Diaconis and Shahshahani, stated there for the \(\ell^1\) norm \(\|\cdot\| = 2\,\|\cdot\|_{TV}\) (hence the factor \(4\) here). The proof is theirs: the upper bound lemma applied to \(T^{*k}\); by the Fourier transform of the random-transposition measure, each transform \(\widehat{T^{*k}}(V_i)\) is the scalar \(c_i^k\) with \(c_i = \tfrac1n + \tfrac{n-1}{n}\,\chi_i(\tau)/d_i\), and \(T\) is invariant under inversion, so the term of \(V_i\) is \(d_i \cdot d_i c_i^{2k}\). Section 3 of Diaconis and Shahshahani starts from this inequality and bounds the sum, using Frobenius's formula for \(\chi_i(\tau)/d_i\), the dimension bound \(d_\lambda \le \binom{n}{\lambda_1}\sqrt{(n-\lambda_1)!}\) and monotonicity under the dominance order, to reach the \(\tfrac12 n \log n\) upper bound.
