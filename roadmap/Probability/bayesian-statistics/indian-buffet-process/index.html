    Indian buffet process

    A prior on binary feature matrices with N rows (objects) and an unbounded number of columns (latent features), introduced by Griffiths and Ghahramani as the feature analogue of the Chinese restaurant process (The Indian buffet process: an introduction and review, JMLR 12 (2011), Section 4). In the finite model with K features, feature k has probability πₖ ~ Beta(α/K, 1) and each object possesses it independently with probability πₖ. The paper computes closed forms for the finite model: the probability of a lof-equivalence class (matrices that differ only by the order of their columns, Equation 13) and the expected number of ones, N α / (1 + α/K) ≤ N α (Equation 11). Letting K → ∞ in the class probability gives the Indian buffet process (Equation 14), which can also be built from independent Poisson counts of the 2^N - 1 non-zero feature histories (Section 4.5); the number of features is then Poisson(α H_N) (Section 4.6).

    What is formalized here: the finite-model closed forms are recorded from the paper as definitions, not derived from the model, and the Lean proves their limits as K → ∞; the entry probability (α/K) / (α/K + 1) is derived from the beta–Bernoulli model; Equation 14 is shown to agree, matrix by matrix, with the joint probability of independent Poisson history counts, and such counts have a Poisson(α H_N) total. The correspondence between lof-classes and count vectors, the sequential buffet construction, and the beta process behind the prior (Thibaux and Jordan 2007, as reviewed by Griffiths and Ghahramani 2011, Section 7) are not formalized.

    TheoremEntry probability of the finite feature model#✓fully proved

    Cslib.MachineLearning.Bayesian.BetaBernoulliFeatures.entry_prob

    For α > 0 and K ≥ 1 features, an entry zᵢₖ of the finite feature model, with πₖ ~ Beta(α/K, 1) and zᵢₖ | πₖ ~ Bernoulli(πₖ), is one with probability (α/K) / (α/K + 1). The probability is computed from the model: the Bernoulli likelihood (a single Bin(1, πₖ) trial) is integrated against the Beta(α/K, 1) prior.     MathlibExt.Probability.IndianBuffet.finiteLofProb

    For a binary matrix Z with N rows and K₊ columns, with mₖ the number of ones in column k and K_h the number of columns with history h, the expression

    α^{K₊} / ∏_h K_h! · K! / (K₀! K^{K₊}) · (N! / ∏_{j=1}^{N} (j + α/K))^K · ∏ₖ (N - mₖ)! ∏_{j=1}^{mₖ-1} (j + α/K) / N!, K₀ = K - K₊.

    The infinite limit

    TheoremThe empty columns contribute exp(-α H_N)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.IndianBuffet.tendsto_emptyColumnsFactor

    For every real α and natural N,

    (N! / ∏_{j=1}^{N} (j + α/K))^K → exp(-α H_N) as K → ∞,

    where H_N = ∑_{j=1}^{N} 1/j is the N-th harmonic number. In the infinite limit of the finite feature model this is the contribution of the empty columns, whose number K - K₊ grows without bound (Griffiths and Ghahramani 2011, Equation 14).     TheoremHarmonic numbers from binomial coefficients#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.IndianBuffet.sum_choose_mul_factorial_div

    MathlibExt.Probability.IndianBuffet.prod_poissonMeasure_historyCount

    For every binary matrix Z whose columns are all non-zero, the product over the 2^N - 1 non-zero histories h of the Poisson(α (m_h - 1)! (N - m_h)! / N!) probabilities of the history counts K_h of Z equals the Indian buffet process formula α^{K₊} / ∏_h K_h! · exp(-α H_N) · ∏ₖ (N - mₖ)! (mₖ - 1)! / N! of the lof-class of Z (Griffiths and Ghahramani 2011, Section 4.5). Here m_h is the number of ones in h. The product of the Poisson factors exp(-λ_h) is exp(-α H_N) by Equation 16, and regrouping the columns of Z by history turns ∏_h λ_h^{K_h} into α^{K₊} ∏ₖ (mₖ - 1)! (N - mₖ)! / N!.

    This is a pointwise identity: the left side is the joint probability mass function of independent Poisson counts evaluated at the history counts of Z. Informally, since a lof-class is the same as a collection of histories with multiplicities, it identifies Equation 14 with the law of independent Poisson history counts, so Equation 14 is a probability distribution on classes (Griffiths and Ghahramani 2011, Section 4.5). The bijection between classes and count vectors and the normalisation over classes are not formalized.
