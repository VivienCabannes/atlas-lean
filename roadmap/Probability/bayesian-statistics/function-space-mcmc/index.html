    Function-space MCMC

    Markov chain Monte Carlo for posteriors with density \(\exp(-\Phi)\) against a Gaussian prior \(N(0, C)\), designed to make sense on function space so that its performance does not degrade under mesh refinement. A proposal reversible for the prior turns Metropolis–Hastings into an accept–reject step on differences of the potential alone, \(a(u, v) = \min\{1, \exp(\Phi(u) - \Phi(v))\}\). The preconditioned Crank–Nicolson (pCN) proposal \(v = \sqrt{1 - \beta^2}\, u + \beta \xi\), \(\xi \sim N(0, C)\), is such a proposal.

    DefinitionThe pCN proposal map#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.pcnProposal

    The preconditioned Crank–Nicolson proposal moves from \(u\) to

    \[ v = \sqrt{1 - \beta^2} \, u + \beta \, \xi , \]

    where, in the algorithm, \(\xi \sim N(0, C)\) is drawn from the prior independently of \(u\). The case \(\beta = 1\) is the independence sampler (proposals drawn from the prior) and \(\beta = 0\) never moves.

    noncomputable def pcnProposal (β : ℝ) (p : E × E) : E :=
  √(1 - β ^ 2) • p.1 + β • p.2

    TheoremThe joint law of a pCN step is symmetric#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.map_swap_pcn_eq

    Let \(\mu_0\) be a centred Gaussian measure on a separable Banach space and \(-1 \le \beta \le 1\). If \(u \sim \mu_0\) and \(\xi \sim \mu_0\) are independent and \(v = \sqrt{1 - \beta^2}\, u + \beta \xi\), then \((u, v)\) and \((v, u)\) have the same law.

    Dashti and Stuart prove this on a separable Hilbert space by computing the covariance of the centred Gaussian pair \((u, v)\): \(\mathbb E\, u \otimes u = \mathbb E\, v \otimes v = C\) and \(\mathbb E\, u \otimes v = \sqrt{1 - \beta^2}\, C\). The formal proof instead uses the rotation invariance of \(\mu_0 \otimes \mu_0\) (the key step in Mathlib's proof of Fernique's theorem) with angle \(\theta = \arcsin \beta\): the map \((u, \xi) \mapsto (\sqrt{1-\beta^2}\, u + \beta \xi, \ \beta u - \sqrt{1-\beta^2}\, \xi)\) is a reflection that preserves \(\mu_0 \otimes \mu_0\) and exchanges \(u\) and \(v\).

    theorem map_swap_pcn_eq (μ₀ : Measure E) [IsGaussian μ₀] (hμ₀ : μ₀[id] = 0) {β : ℝ}
    (hβ₁ : -1 ≤ β) (hβ₂ : β ≤ 1) :
    ((μ₀.prod μ₀).map fun p ↦ (p.1, pcnProposal β p)).map Prod.swap =
      (μ₀.prod μ₀).map fun p ↦ (p.1, pcnProposal β p)
    noncomputable def pcnProposal (β : ℝ) (p : E × E) : E :=
  √(1 - β ^ 2) • p.1 + β • p.2

    TheoremPrior reversibility of the pCN proposal#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.isReversible_pcnKernel

    For a centred Gaussian measure \(\mu_0\) on a separable Banach space and \(-1 \le \beta \le 1\), the pCN kernel \(Q\) satisfies detailed balance with respect to \(\mu_0\):

    \[ \int_A Q(u, B) \, \mu_0(du) = \int_B Q(u, A) \, \mu_0(du) \qquad \text{for all measurable } A, B . \]

    This is what makes the pCN method well defined on function space: combined with Metropolis–Hastings for a prior-reversible proposal, the acceptance probability involves only \(\Phi(u) - \Phi(v)\). Cotter, Roberts, Stuart and White show that, for their Crank–Nicolson family of proposals, the measure \(\mu(du) q(u, dv)\) and its transpose are equivalent, so that Metropolis–Hastings is defined on function space, if and only if \(\theta = \tfrac12\) (the CN and pCN proposals), while for the standard random walk proposal the transpose is not even absolutely continuous.

    theorem isReversible_pcnKernel (μ₀ : Measure E) [IsGaussian μ₀] (hμ₀ : μ₀[id] = 0) {β : ℝ}
    (hβ₁ : -1 ≤ β) (hβ₂ : β ≤ 1) : Kernel.IsReversible (pcnKernel μ₀ β) μ₀
    noncomputable def pcnKernel (μ₀ : Measure E) [IsProbabilityMeasure μ₀] (β : ℝ) : Kernel E E :=
  (Kernel.deterministic id measurable_id ×ₖ Kernel.const E μ₀).map (pcnProposal β)

    TheoremDetailed balance makes the joint law symmetric#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.map_swap_compProd_eq_of_isReversible

    If a Markov kernel \(Q\) is reversible with respect to a finite measure \(\mu_0\), then the joint law \(\mu_0(du) \, Q(u, dv)\) on pairs is invariant under \((u, v) \mapsto (v, u)\).     theorem map_swap_compProd_eq_of_isReversible (μ₀ : Measure U) [IsFiniteMeasure μ₀]
    (Q : Kernel U U) [IsMarkovKernel Q] (hQ : Q.IsReversible μ₀) :
    (μ₀ ⊗ₘ Q).map Prod.swap = μ₀ ⊗ₘ Q

    DefinitionMetropolis–Hastings acceptance probability for a prior-reversible proposal#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.mhAcceptPotential

    For a posterior \(\mu \propto e^{-\Phi} \mu_0\) and a proposal reversible for the prior \(\mu_0\), the acceptance probability is

    \[ a(u, v) = \min\{1, \exp(\Phi(u) - \Phi(v))\} . \]

    Moves that decrease the potential are always accepted. No density with respect to Lebesgue measure appears, which is why the formula makes sense in infinite dimensions.

    noncomputable def mhAcceptPotential (Φ : U → ℝ) (u v : U) : ℝ≥0∞ :=
  ENNReal.ofReal (min 1 (Real.exp (Φ u - Φ v)))

    DefinitionMetropolis–Hastings kernel for a prior-reversible proposal#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.mhKernel

    From \(u\), propose \(v \sim Q(u, \cdot)\) and accept it with probability \(a(u, v) = \min\{1, e^{\Phi(u) - \Phi(v)}\}\); otherwise stay at \(u\). As a Markov kernel,

    \[ P(u, dv) = a(u, v) \, Q(u, dv) + \Big( \int (1 - a(u, w)) \, Q(u, dw) \Big) \, \delta_u(dv) . \]
    noncomputable def mhKernel (Q : Kernel U U) [IsSFiniteKernel Q] (Φ : U → ℝ) : Kernel U U :=
  Q.withDensity (mhAcceptPotential Φ) +
    (Kernel.deterministic id measurable_id).withDensity
      fun u _ ↦ ∫⁻ w, (1 - mhAcceptPotential Φ u w) ∂Q u

    TheoremMetropolis–Hastings with a prior-reversible proposal is reversible for the posterior#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.isReversible_mhKernel

    Let \(\mu_0\) be a probability measure, \(Q\) a Markov kernel reversible with respect to \(\mu_0\) and \(\Phi\) measurable. Then the Metropolis–Hastings kernel with acceptance probability \(a(u, v) = \min\{1, e^{\Phi(u) - \Phi(v)}\}\) satisfies detailed balance with respect to the posterior \(\mu \propto e^{-\Phi} \mu_0\).

    The proof is that of Dashti and Stuart: \(e^{-\Phi(u)} a(u, v) = \min\{e^{-\Phi(u)}, e^{-\Phi(v)}\}\) is symmetric in \((u, v)\), and prior reversibility makes the joint law \(\mu_0(du) Q(u, dv)\) symmetric; the rejection part is supported on the diagonal and is symmetric automatically. The formal statement needs neither positivity of the normalising constant nor any topology on the state space; when \(Z = 0\) or \(e^{-\Phi}\) is not integrable, the posterior is the zero measure and the statement is vacuous.

    theorem isReversible_mhKernel (μ₀ : Measure U) [IsProbabilityMeasure μ₀] (Q : Kernel U U)
    [IsMarkovKernel Q] (hQ : Q.IsReversible μ₀) {Φ : U → ℝ} (hΦ : Measurable Φ) :
    (mhKernel Q Φ).IsReversible (posterior μ₀ Φ)
    noncomputable def mhKernel (Q : Kernel U U) [IsSFiniteKernel Q] (Φ : U → ℝ) : Kernel U U :=
  Q.withDensity (mhAcceptPotential Φ) +

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.invariant_mhKernel

    If \(Q\) is a Markov kernel reversible for the prior probability measure \(\mu_0\) and \(\Phi\) is measurable, then the posterior \(\mu \propto e^{-\Phi} \mu_0\) is invariant for the Metropolis–Hastings kernel \(P\) with acceptance probability \(\min\{1, e^{\Phi(u) - \Phi(v)}\}\): \(\int P(u, B) \, \mu(du) = \mu(B)\) for every measurable \(B\). It follows from reversibility, since a reversible Markov kernel leaves its measure invariant.

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.isReversible_pcn_mhKernel

    Let \(\mu_0\) be a centred Gaussian measure on a separable Banach space, \(-1 \le \beta \le 1\) and \(\Phi\) measurable. The pCN method (propose \(v = \sqrt{1 - \beta^2}\, u + \beta \xi\) with \(\xi \sim \mu_0\), accept with probability \(\min\{1, e^{\Phi(u) - \Phi(v)}\}\)) satisfies detailed balance with respect to the posterior \(\mu \propto e^{-\Phi} \mu_0\), and hence leaves it invariant.
