    Bayesian inverse problems

    The Bayesian formulation of an inverse problem \(y = G(u) + \eta\): the posterior is the prior \(\mu_0\) reweighted by the likelihood, \(\frac{d\mu^y}{d\mu_0}(u) = Z(y)^{-1} \exp(-\Phi(u; y))\) with normalising constant \(Z(y) = \int \exp(-\Phi(u; y)) \, \mu_0(du)\) and, for Gaussian noise \(\eta \sim N(0, \Gamma)\), the misfit \(\Phi(u; y) = \frac12 |\Gamma^{-1/2}(y - G(u))|^2\). Writing the posterior as a density against the prior, not against Lebesgue measure, is what survives when the unknown is a function. Under a lower bound and a local Lipschitz bound on \(\Phi\), the problem is well posed: the posterior is Lipschitz in the data in the Hellinger distance, and errors in the forward model transfer to posterior errors of the same order.

    When \(\Phi\) depends on data \(y\) this is the evidence \(Z(y)\).

    DefinitionPosterior measure of a potential#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.posterior

    The posterior of a prior \(\mu_0\) and a potential \(\Phi\) is the measure with density

    \[ \frac{d\mu}{d\mu_0}(u) = \frac{1}{Z} \exp(-\Phi(u)), \qquad Z = \int \exp(-\Phi) \, d\mu_0 , \]

    with respect to the prior. For the inverse problem \(y = G(u) + \eta\) with \(\eta \sim N(0, \Gamma)\) the potential is the misfit \(\Phi(u; y) = \frac12 |\Gamma^{-1/2}(y - G(u))|^2\), and Bayes' theorem identifies this measure with the conditional law of \(u\) given \(y\) (Dashti and Stuart, Theorem 3.4). It is a probability measure as soon as \(\exp(-\Phi)\) is integrable and \(Z > 0\) (posterior is a probability measure).

    noncomputable def posterior (μ₀ : Measure U) (Φ : U → ℝ) : Measure U :=
  μ₀.withDensity fun u ↦ ENNReal.ofReal (Real.exp (-Φ u) / normalizer μ₀ Φ)
    noncomputable def normalizer (μ₀ : Measure U) (Φ : U → ℝ) : ℝ :=
  ∫ u, Real.exp (-Φ u) ∂μ₀

    TheoremThe normalising constants are uniformly positive on bounded data sets#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.exists_pos_le_normalizer

    Let the unknown \(u\) live in a finite-dimensional normed space with prior probability measure \(\mu_0\), and let the potential \(\Phi(u; y)\) be continuous in \(u\) and satisfy, for \(\|y\|, \|y'\| \le r\),

    \[ \Phi(u; y) \ge -M_1(\|u\|), \qquad |\Phi(u; y) - \Phi(u; y')| \le M_2(\|u\|) \, \|y - y'\|, \]

    with \(M_2\) nondecreasing and \(\exp(M_1(\|u\|))\) integrable under \(\mu_0\) (Theorem 4.3's condition; the well-posedness theorem needs the stronger \(\exp(M_1)(1 + M_2^2) \in L^1\)). Then there is \(z > 0\) with \(z \le Z(y)\) for every \(\|y\| \le r\). The hypotheses also make \(\exp(-\Phi(\cdot\,; y))\) integrable, so each posterior is then a probability measure.

    This is Theorem 4.3 of Dashti and Stuart in the case \(X' = X\) finite-dimensional, where the uniform bound comes from a compact ball of positive prior mass on which \(\Phi(\cdot\,; 0)\) is bounded.

    TheoremHellinger perturbation bound for posteriors#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.hellingerDist_posterior_le

    On any measurable space, let \(\Phi_1\), \(\Phi_2\) be measurable potentials with

    \[ \Phi_i \ge -M_1, \qquad |\Phi_1 - \Phi_2| \le M_2 \, \delta, \]

    let \(w = e^{M_1}(1 + M_2^2)\) be \(\mu_0\)-integrable with \(\int w \, d\mu_0 \le W\), and let both normalising constants be at least \(z > 0\). Then the posteriors \(\mu_i \propto e^{-\Phi_i} \mu_0\) satisfy

    \[ d_{\mathrm{Hell}}(\mu_1, \mu_2) \le |\delta| \, \sqrt{\frac{W}{4z} + \frac{W^2}{2z^2}} . \]

    This is the common estimate inside the proofs of the well-posedness and approximation theorems, with an explicit constant: \(|Z_1 - Z_2| \le |\delta| W\), and \(2 d_{\mathrm{Hell}}^2\) splits into a term controlled by \(|e^{-\Phi_1/2} - e^{-\Phi_2/2}| \le \tfrac12 e^{M_1/2} |\Phi_1 - \Phi_2|\) and a term controlled by \(|Z_1^{-1/2} - Z_2^{-1/2}|\). No topology or dimension is involved; the dimension enters only through the lower bound on the normalising constants.

    theorem hellingerDist_posterior_le (μ₀ : Measure U) [SigmaFinite μ₀] {Φ₁ Φ₂ M₁ M₂ : U → ℝ}
    {δ z W : ℝ} (hΦ₁ : Measurable Φ₁) (hΦ₂ : Measurable Φ₂) (hM₁ : Measurable M₁)
    (hM₂ : Measurable M₂)
    (hlow₁ : ∀ u, -M₁ u ≤ Φ₁ u) (hlow₂ : ∀ u, -M₁ u ≤ Φ₂ u)
    (hdiff : ∀ u, |Φ₁ u - Φ₂ u| ≤ M₂ u * δ)
    (hint : Integrable (fun u ↦ Real.exp (M₁ u) * (1 + M₂ u ^ 2)) μ₀)
    (hW : ∫ u, Real.exp (M₁ u) * (1 + M₂ u ^ 2) ∂μ₀ ≤ W)
    (hz : 0 < z) (hZ₁ : z ≤ normalizer μ₀ Φ₁) (hZ₂ : z ≤ normalizer μ₀ Φ₂) :
    hellingerDist (posterior μ₀ Φ₁) (posterior μ₀ Φ₂) ≤
      |δ| * √(W / (4 * z) + W ^ 2 / (2 * z ^ 2))
    noncomputable def normalizer (μ₀ : Measure U) (Φ : U → ℝ) : ℝ :=
  ∫ u, Real.exp (-Φ u) ∂μ₀

    TheoremWell-posedness: the posterior is locally Lipschitz in the data in Hellinger distance#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.hellingerDist_posterior_le_mul_dist

    Let the unknown \(u\) live in a finite-dimensional normed space with prior probability measure \(\mu_0\). Let the potential \(\Phi(u; y)\) be continuous in \(u\) and satisfy, for \(\|y\|, \|y'\| \le r\),

    \[ \Phi(u; y) \ge -M_1(\|u\|), \qquad |\Phi(u; y) - \Phi(u; y')| \le M_2(\|u\|) \, \|y - y'\|, \]

    with \(M_1\), \(M_2\) nondecreasing and \(\exp(M_1(\|u\|))(1 + M_2(\|u\|)^2)\) integrable under \(\mu_0\). Then there is \(C\) such that

    \[ d_{\mathrm{Hell}}(\mu^y, \mu^{y'}) \le C \, \|y - y'\| \qquad \text{for all } \|y\|, \|y'\| \le r . \]

    So, unlike the classical inverse problem, the Bayesian inverse problem is well posed: small changes in the data give small changes in the posterior, and hence, by the control of expectations, in posterior means.

    This is Theorem 4.5 of Dashti and Stuart with \(X' = X\) finite-dimensional and Assumptions 4.2 at the fixed radius \(r\). For Gaussian noise and a continuous forward map with \(|G(u)| \le M(\|u\|)\) one can take \(M_1 = 0\). The test file checks a non-degenerate instance: standard Gaussian prior on \(\mathbb R\), forward map \(G = \sin\), misfit \(\Phi(u; y) = (y - \sin u)^2/2\).

    TheoremApproximation: forward-model error controls posterior error in Hellinger distance#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.BayesianInverse.hellingerDist_posterior_approx_le

    Let the unknown \(u\) live in a finite-dimensional normed space with prior probability measure \(\mu_0\). Let \(\Phi\) be continuous and \(\Phi^N\) measurable potentials with

    \[ \Phi(u), \Phi^N(u) \ge -M_1(\|u\|), \qquad |\Phi(u) - \Phi^N(u)| \le M_2(\|u\|) \, \psi(N), \qquad \psi(N) \to 0, \]

    where \(M_1\), \(M_2\) are nondecreasing and independent of \(N\) and \(\exp(M_1(\|u\|))(1 + M_2(\|u\|)^2)\) is integrable. Then there is \(C\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(N\),

    \[ d_{\mathrm{Hell}}(\mu, \mu^N) \le C \, |\psi(N)| . \]

    Typically \(\Phi^N\) is the misfit of a discretised forward model \(G^N\), and the theorem says that the posterior inherits the convergence rate of the forward solver.

    This is Theorem 4.9 of Dashti and Stuart with \(X' = X\) finite-dimensional (Assumptions 4.7).

    Related topics

    • Hellinger distance: the metric in which well-posedness and approximation are stated.
    • Function-space MCMC: sampling the posterior with Metropolis–Hastings chains that are well defined for function-valued unknowns.
    • Ensemble Kalman inversion: a derivative-free ensemble method for the same inverse problems.
    • Gaussian conditioning: for a linear forward map with Gaussian prior and Gaussian noise the posterior is Gaussian, given by the conditioning formula.
    • Gaussian process regression: a linear Bayesian inverse problem with a Gaussian prior on a function, observed through point evaluations with Gaussian noise.
    • Bayes' rule: the posterior as a disintegration of the joint law, which identifies the reweighted prior with the conditional law of the unknown given the data.
