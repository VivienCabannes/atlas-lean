    Empirical Bayes and Tweedie's formula

    In the Gaussian location model a parameter \(\mu\) is drawn from a prior \(g\) and one observes \(x \mid \mu \sim \mathcal N(\mu, \sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma^2\) known. The Bayes rule needs the prior, but Tweedie's formula expresses the posterior mean through the marginal density \(f(x) = \int \varphi_\sigma(x - \mu)\, g(d\mu)\) alone: \(\mathbb E[\mu \mid x] = x + \sigma^2 (\log f)'(x)\), and similarly \(\operatorname{var}[\mu \mid x] = \sigma^2 (1 + \sigma^2 (\log f)''(x))\). In empirical Bayes work many observations \(x_1, \dots, x_N\) share the same prior, so \(f\) can be estimated from all of them (Efron 2011, Tweedie's formula and selection bias). With a normal prior the formula gives linear shrinkage, and estimating the shrinkage factor from the data gives the James–Stein estimator.

    Robbins' formula \(\mathbb E[\theta \mid x] = (x + 1) f(x + 1)/f(x)\) is the Poisson counterpart, where \(f\) is the marginal probability of the count \(x\). Tweedie's formulas are proved here for a prior that is any finite nonzero measure (for example any probability prior, atoms allowed), and the posterior mean is identified with the conditional expectation \(\mathbb E[\mu \mid x]\) when the prior has a finite first moment.

    The model

    DefinitionMarginal density of a Gaussian location mixture#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.gaussianMixtureDensity

    Let \(g\) be a measure on \(\mathbb R\), the prior of a location parameter \(\mu\) (atoms are allowed), and let \(v \ge 0\). The marginal density is $\(f(x) = \int \varphi_v(x - \mu)\, g(d\mu), \qquad \varphi_v(t) = (2\pi v)^{-1/2} e^{-t^2/(2v)} .\)$ When \(g\) is a probability measure and \(v > 0\), \(f\) is the density of \(x\) in the model \(\mu \sim g\), \(x \mid \mu \sim \mathcal N(\mu, v)\) (Efron 2011, equations (1.2)–(1.3), with \(\sigma^2 = v\)).

    In Lean the integrand is Mathlib's gaussianPDFReal μ v x. For \(v = 0\) Mathlib sets that density to \(0\), so \(f = 0\); every result in this topic assumes \(v > 0\).

    noncomputable def gaussianMixtureDensity (g : Measure ℝ) (v : ℝ≥0) (x : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  ∫ μ, gaussianPDFReal μ v x ∂g

    DefinitionPosterior mean in the Gaussian location model#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.gaussianPosteriorMean

    For a prior \(g\) on \(\mathbb R\) and a variance \(v\), Bayes' rule gives the posterior mean of \(\mu\) given \(x\) in the model \(\mu \sim g\), \(x \mid \mu \sim \mathcal N(\mu, v)\) as the ratio $\(m(x) = \frac{\int \mu\, \varphi_v(x - \mu)\, g(d\mu)}{f(x)}, \qquad f(x) = \int \varphi_v(x - \mu)\, g(d\mu) .\)$ The ratio does not change when \(g\) is multiplied by a positive constant, so the results below allow any finite nonzero \(g\). If \(f(x) = 0\), Lean's convention \(a / 0 = 0\) makes \(m(x) = 0\), but the marginal density is positive as soon as \(g \ne 0\) is finite and \(v > 0\).

    That this ratio is a version of the conditional expectation \(\mathbb E[\mu \mid x]\) is proved separately, for priors with a finite first moment: see Tweedie's formula, conditional expectation form.

    noncomputable def gaussianPosteriorMean (g : Measure ℝ) (v : ℝ≥0) (x : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (∫ μ, μ * gaussianPDFReal μ v x ∂g) / gaussianMixtureDensity g v x
    noncomputable def gaussianMixtureDensity (g : Measure ℝ) (v : ℝ≥0) (x : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  ∫ μ, gaussianPDFReal μ v x ∂g

    DefinitionPosterior second moment in the Gaussian location model#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.gaussianPosteriorSecondMoment

    For a prior \(g\) and a variance \(v\), the posterior second moment of \(\mu\) given \(x\) is the Bayes ratio $\(\frac{\int \mu^2\, \varphi_v(x - \mu)\, g(d\mu)}{f(x)},\)$ with \(f\) the marginal density. With the posterior mean it gives the posterior variance used in Tweedie's formula for the posterior variance.

    DefinitionJoint law of a Gaussian location model#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.gaussianLocationJoint

    For a prior \(g\) on \(\mathbb R\) and a variance \(v\), the joint law of \((\mu, x)\) in the model \(\mu \sim g\), \(x \mid \mu \sim \mathcal N(\mu, v)\) is defined as the measure on \(\mathbb R \times \mathbb R\) with density \(\varphi_v(x - \mu)\) with respect to \(g \otimes \text{Lebesgue}\). That it has marginal \(g\) and conditional law \(\mathcal N(\mu, v)\) is the joint law of a Gaussian location model has the right marginal and kernel; it is a probability measure when \(g\) is one and \(v > 0\).

    noncomputable def gaussianLocationJoint (g : Measure ℝ) (v : ℝ≥0) : Measure (ℝ × ℝ) :=
  (g.prod volume).withDensity fun q => ((gaussianPDFReal q.1 v q.2).toNNReal : ENNReal)

    TheoremThe joint law of a Gaussian location model has the right marginal and kernel#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.gaussianLocationJoint_prod_apply

    For an s-finite prior \(g\), \(v > 0\) and measurable sets \(A, B \subseteq \mathbb R\), the joint law satisfies $\(\mathbb P(\mu \in A,\ x \in B) = \int_A \mathcal N(\mu, v)(B)\, g(d\mu) .\)$ Rectangles determine the measure, so this identifies the joint law with the composition of \(g\) and the Gaussian kernel \(\mu \mapsto \mathcal N(\mu, v)\), which is model (1.2) of Efron (2011).

    theorem gaussianLocationJoint_prod_apply (g : Measure ℝ) [SFinite g] {v : ℝ≥0} (hv : v ≠ 0)
    {A B : Set ℝ} (hA : MeasurableSet A) (hB : MeasurableSet B) :
    gaussianLocationJoint g v (A ×ˢ B) = ∫⁻ μ in A, gaussianReal μ v B ∂g
    noncomputable def gaussianLocationJoint (g : Measure ℝ) (v : ℝ≥0) : Measure (ℝ × ℝ) :=
  (g.prod volume).withDensity fun q => ((gaussianPDFReal q.1 v q.2).toNNReal : ENNReal)

    Tweedie's formulas

    TheoremThe marginal density of a Gaussian location mixture is differentiable#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.hasDerivAt_gaussianMixtureDensity

    Let \(g\) be a finite measure on \(\mathbb R\) and \(v > 0\). The marginal density \(f(x) = \int \varphi_v(x - \mu)\, g(d\mu)\) is differentiable at every \(x\), with $\(f'(x) = \int \frac{\mu - x}{v}\, \varphi_v(x - \mu)\, g(d\mu) .\)$ No smoothness, density or moment assumption on \(g\) is needed. The proof differentiates under the integral sign: \(\partial_x \varphi_v(x - \mu) = \frac{\mu - x}{v} \varphi_v(x - \mu)\), and the bound \(|t|\, e^{-t^2/(2v)} \le v + 1\) dominates this derivative by a constant, which is integrable because \(g\) is finite.

    Johnstone (draft, Section 2.3, before equation (2.26)) notes the stronger fact that \(f\) is analytic, from the exponential-family form of the Gaussian kernel; only differentiability is proved here.

    theorem hasDerivAt_gaussianMixtureDensity (g : Measure ℝ) [IsFiniteMeasure g] {v : ℝ≥0}
    (hv : v ≠ 0) (x : ℝ) :
    HasDerivAt (gaussianMixtureDensity g v)
      (∫ μ, (μ - x) / v * gaussianPDFReal μ v x ∂g) x
    noncomputable def gaussianMixtureDensity (g : Measure ℝ) (v : ℝ≥0) (x : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  ∫ μ, gaussianPDFReal μ v x ∂g

    TheoremThe marginal density of a Gaussian location mixture is positive#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.gaussianMixtureDensity_pos

    TheoremTweedie's formula#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.gaussianPosteriorMean_eq_add_mul_deriv_log

    Let \(g\) be a finite nonzero measure on \(\mathbb R\), the prior of \(\mu\), and let \(x \mid \mu \sim \mathcal N(\mu, v)\) with \(v > 0\). Write \(f\) for the marginal density and \(m\) for the posterior mean. Then for every \(x \in \mathbb R\), $\(m(x) = x + v\, (\log f)'(x) .\)$ The posterior mean is the unbiased estimate \(x\) plus a Bayes correction that involves only the marginal density, not the prior. In empirical Bayes work \(f\) is estimated from many observations sharing the prior, which avoids deconvolving \(f\) to recover \(g\).

    This is Efron (2011), equation (1.4), \(\mathbb E\{\mu \mid z\} = z + \sigma^2 l'(z)\) with \(l = \log f\) and \(\sigma^2 = v\); Efron attributes it to Robbins (1956), who credits Tweedie. Johnstone's draft states the multivariate unit-variance version as equation (2.26), the Brown–Tweedie formula.

    How the Lean statement relates to the sources:

    • Efron allows a prior "density" that may include atoms; the Lean statement takes any finite nonzero measure, with no density and no moment condition.
    • The left side is the Bayes ratio. Its identification with a conditional expectation, which needs \(\int |\mu|\, g(d\mu) < \infty\), is Tweedie's formula, conditional expectation form.
    • Efron derives (1.4) from the exponential-family form (2.1)–(2.8). The Lean proof is direct: by differentiation under the integral sign, \(v f'(x) = \int \mu\, \varphi_v(x - \mu)\, g(d\mu) - x f(x)\), and \(f > 0\).

    The second-order formula is Tweedie's formula for the posterior variance.

    theorem gaussianPosteriorMean_eq_add_mul_deriv_log (g : Measure ℝ) [IsFiniteMeasure g] [NeZero g]
    {v : ℝ≥0} (hv : v ≠ 0) (x : ℝ) :
    gaussianPosteriorMean g v x =
      x + v * deriv (fun y => Real.log (gaussianMixtureDensity g v y)) x
    noncomputable def gaussianPosteriorMean (g : Measure ℝ) (v : ℝ≥0) (x : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (∫ μ, μ * gaussianPDFReal μ v x ∂g) / gaussianMixtureDensity g v x

    TheoremTweedie's formula for the posterior variance#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.gaussianPosteriorSecondMoment_sub_sq_eq

    Under the hypotheses of Tweedie's formula (a finite nonzero prior \(g\) and \(v > 0\)), for every \(x \in \mathbb R\), $\(\mathbb E[\mu^2 \mid x] - \mathbb E[\mu \mid x]^2 = v\,\bigl(1 + v\, (\log f)''(x)\bigr),\)$ where both conditional moments are Bayes ratios (posterior second moment, posterior mean) and \(f\) is the marginal density. This is the second component of Efron (2011), equation (2.8), \(\operatorname{var}\{\mu \mid z\} = \sigma^2 (1 + \sigma^2 l''(z))\), with \(\sigma^2 = v\). Since the left side is a variance, it is nonnegative, so \((\log f)'' \ge -1/v\) everywhere (this consequence is not formalized).

    The proof differentiates under the integral sign a second time, so it shows along the way that \(f\) is twice differentiable; \((\log f)''\) in the statement is Lean's deriv (deriv (log ∘ f)).

    theorem gaussianPosteriorSecondMoment_sub_sq_eq (g : Measure ℝ) [IsFiniteMeasure g] [NeZero g]
    {v : ℝ≥0} (hv : v ≠ 0) (x : ℝ) :
    gaussianPosteriorSecondMoment g v x - gaussianPosteriorMean g v x ^ 2 =
      v * (1 + v * deriv (deriv fun y => Real.log (gaussianMixtureDensity g v y)) x)
    noncomputable def gaussianPosteriorSecondMoment (g : Measure ℝ) (v : ℝ≥0) (x : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (∫ μ, μ ^ 2 * gaussianPDFReal μ v x ∂g) / gaussianMixtureDensity g v x

    TheoremTweedie's formula, conditional expectation form#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.condExp_fst_gaussianLocationJoint

    Let \(g\) be a probability measure on \(\mathbb R\) with \(\int |\mu|\, g(d\mu) < \infty\) and let \(v > 0\). Under the joint law of \((\mu, x)\) in the model \(\mu \sim g\), \(x \mid \mu \sim \mathcal N(\mu, v)\), $\(\mathbb E[\mu \mid x] = m(x) \quad \text{almost surely},\)$ where \(m\) is the Bayes ratio of the posterior mean and the conditional expectation is taken with respect to the \(\sigma\)-algebra generated by \(x\). Combined with Tweedie's formula, \(\mathbb E[\mu \mid x] = x + v (\log f)'(x)\) almost surely: this is Efron (2011), equations (1.2)–(1.4), read as a statement about conditional expectations, with \(\sigma^2 = v\).

    The moment condition is needed for \(\mathbb E[\mu \mid x]\) to exist as a conditional expectation of an integrable variable; the pointwise formula needs no moment.

    The proof checks the defining property: for every measurable \(A\), \(\int_{x \in A} \mu = \int_{x \in A} m(x)\) under the joint law, by two applications of Fubini's theorem and \(m(x) f(x) = \int \mu\, \varphi_v(x - \mu)\, g(d\mu)\).

    theorem condExp_fst_gaussianLocationJoint (g : Measure ℝ) [IsProbabilityMeasure g]
    (hg : Integrable id g) {v : ℝ≥0} (hv : v ≠ 0) :
    (fun q : ℝ × ℝ => gaussianPosteriorMean g v q.2) =ᵐ[gaussianLocationJoint g v]
      (gaussianLocationJoint g v)[Prod.fst | MeasurableSpace.comap Prod.snd inferInstance]
    noncomputable def gaussianPosteriorMean (g : Measure ℝ) (v : ℝ≥0) (x : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (∫ μ, μ * gaussianPDFReal μ v x ∂g) / gaussianMixtureDensity g v x

    The normal prior

    TheoremPosterior mean in the normal–normal model#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.gaussianPosteriorMean_gaussianReal

    For the prior \(\mu \sim \mathcal N(0, A)\) with \(A \ge 0\) and \(x \mid \mu \sim \mathcal N(\mu, v)\) with \(v > 0\), the posterior mean is $\(\mathbb E[\mu \mid x] = \frac{A}{A + v}\, x \qquad \text{for every } x .\)$ This is Efron (2021), equation (3.10), \(\hat\theta^{\text{Bayes}} = M + B(x - M)\) with \(B = A/(A + \sigma_0^2)\), in the case \(M = 0\), \(\sigma_0^2 = v\); the Lean statement does not cover a nonzero prior mean \(M\). With \(v = 1\) and \(V = A + 1\) the factor is \(1 - 1/V\), Efron (2011), equation (3.8). Estimating \(1/V\) without bias by \((N - 2)/\sum_j z_j^2\) turns this Bayes rule into the James–Stein estimator: see the James–Stein shrinkage factor estimates \(1/V\) without bias. The case \(A = 0\), a point mass at \(0\), gives \(\mathbb E[\mu \mid x] = 0\).

    The Lean proof applies Stein's lemma to the prior: \(\int \mu\, \varphi_v(x - \mu)\, \mathcal N(0, A)(d\mu) = A \int \frac{x - \mu}{v}\, \varphi_v(x - \mu)\, \mathcal N(0, A)(d\mu)\), which is linear in the numerator and the marginal density.

    theorem gaussianPosteriorMean_gaussianReal (A : ℝ≥0) {v : ℝ≥0} (hv : v ≠ 0) (x : ℝ) :
    gaussianPosteriorMean (gaussianReal 0 A) v x = A / (A + v) * x
    noncomputable def gaussianPosteriorMean (g : Measure ℝ) (v : ℝ≥0) (x : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (∫ μ, μ * gaussianPDFReal μ v x ∂g) / gaussianMixtureDensity g v x

    TheoremRobbins' formula#✓fully proved

    ProbabilityTheory.poissonPosteriorMean_eq

    For every prior \(g\) on the Poisson rate and every count \(x \in \mathbb N\), $\(\mathbb E[\theta \mid x] = \frac{(x + 1)\, f(x + 1)}{f(x)},\)$ where the left side is the Bayes posterior mean and \(f\) is the marginal probability. This is Efron (2021), equation (1.7), Robbins' (1956) derivation. Replacing \(f(x)\) and \(f(x + 1)\) by the observed proportions \(y_x/N\) and \(y_{x+1}/N\) of counts equal to \(x\) and \(x + 1\) gives Robbins' empirical Bayes estimate \((x + 1) y_{x+1}/y_x\) (Efron 2021, equation (1.3)), which needs no knowledge of the prior. The estimation step is not formalized.

    The identity holds for any measure \(g\), with no moment assumption, because \(\theta\, e^{-\theta}\theta^x/x! = (x + 1)\, e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+1}/(x + 1)!\) pointwise. It reads as a statement about \(\mathbb E[\theta \mid x]\) when \(g\) is a probability measure: then both integrals are finite, since \(\theta\, e^{-\theta}\theta^x/x! \le x + 1\), and \(f(x) > 0\) for every \(x\) as soon as \(g\) is not the point mass at \(0\). For a measure with infinite mass both integrals may fail to converge, in which case Lean's integral convention makes the two sides equal to \(0\) and the formula says nothing about a posterior mean.

    theorem poissonPosteriorMean_eq (g : Measure ℝ≥0) (x : ℕ) :
    poissonPosteriorMean g x =
      (x + 1) * poissonMixtureMass g (x + 1) / poissonMixtureMass g x
