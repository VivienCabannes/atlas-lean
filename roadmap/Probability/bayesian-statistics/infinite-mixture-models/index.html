    Infinite mixture models

    The prior on class assignments of an infinite mixture model, obtained by letting the number of classes K tend to infinity in a finite Bayesian mixture with symmetric Dirichlet(α/K, …, α/K) weights (Griffiths and Ghahramani, The Indian buffet process: an introduction and review, JMLR 12 (2011), Section 2). Integrating out the weights (Dirichlet–multinomial conjugacy) gives an exchangeable law on partitions of the objects, with an explicit closed form; as K → ∞ the closed form converges to the law of the Chinese restaurant process (their Equation 5), so the infinite model has an unbounded number of classes, of which only finitely many are used by any finite data set. Here the finite-K partition probability is recorded from the paper as a closed form; what is formalized is the limit of that closed form, not its derivation from the model.

    The finite-mixture closed form and its limit

    DefinitionClosed-form partition probability of a finite symmetric Dirichlet mixture#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.InfiniteMixtureLimit.finitePartitionProb

    The expression

    K! / (K - K₊)! · (α/K)^{K₊} · ∏_{B ∈ π} ∏_{j=1}^{|B|-1} (j + α/K) / α^{(n)}, K₊ = |π|,

    for a partition π of n objects, where α^{(n)} = α (α + 1) ⋯ (α + n - 1) = Γ(n + α) / Γ(α) is the rising factorial. Griffiths and Ghahramani (2011, Section 2.2) give it as the probability that, in a Bayesian mixture with K classes, weights θ ~ Dirichlet(α/K, …, α/K) and assignments drawn independently from θ, the assignments induce the partition π: it is their Equation 4 multiplied by the K! / K₀! (K₀ = K - K₊) ways of labelling the blocks with distinct classes. Here it is the paper's closed form, taken as a definition; it is not derived from the Dirichlet–multinomial model. It vanishes when K < K₊.

    noncomputable def finitePartitionProb (α : ℝ) (K : ℕ) {n : ℕ}
    (π : Finpartition (univ : Finset (Fin n))) : ℝ :=
  (K.descFactorial #π.parts : ℝ) * (α / K) ^ #π.parts *
    π.cardProd (fun m ↦ ∏ j ∈ range (m - 1), ((j + 1 : ℝ) + α / K)) /
      (ascPochhammer ℝ n).eval α

    TheoremThe closed-form finite-mixture partition probability tends to the Chinese restaurant process law#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.InfiniteMixtureLimit.tendsto_finitePartitionProb

    For every α and every partition π of n objects, the closed-form finite-mixture partition probability (Griffiths and Ghahramani 2011, Equation 4 times K! / K₀!, recorded from the paper, not derived from the model here) converges, as K → ∞, to

    α^{|π|} ∏_{B ∈ π} (|B| - 1)! / α^{(n)},

    the law of the Chinese restaurant process. This is Griffiths and Ghahramani's Equation 5: the factor K! / ((K - K₊)! K^{K₊}) tends to 1 and each product ∏_{j=1}^{m-1} (j + α/K) tends to (m - 1)!. Together with the paper's derivation of the closed form, it identifies the prior on partitions of the infinite mixture model with the Chinese restaurant process; only the limit step is formalized.

    theorem tendsto_finitePartitionProb (α : ℝ) {n : ℕ}
    (π : Finpartition (univ : Finset (Fin n))) :
    Tendsto (fun K : ℕ => finitePartitionProb α K π) atTop (𝓝 (crpProb α π))

    Elementary limits

    TheoremFalling factorial over a power tends to one#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.InfiniteMixtureLimit.tendsto_descFactorial_div_pow

    For every fixed r, K! / ((K - r)! K^r) = K (K - 1) ⋯ (K - r + 1) / K^r → 1 as K → ∞. In the infinite limits of finite mixture and feature models (Griffiths and Ghahramani 2011, Equations 5 and 14) this is the factor that counts the ways of assigning the r occupied classes or features to distinct labels among K.

    theorem tendsto_descFactorial_div_pow (r : ℕ) :
    Tendsto (fun K : ℕ => (K.descFactorial r : ℝ) / (K : ℝ) ^ r) atTop (𝓝 1)

    TheoremShifted products tend to factorials#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.InfiniteMixtureLimit.tendsto_prod_range_add_div

    For every real α and natural n, ∏_{j=1}^{n} (j + α/K) → n! as K → ∞. It turns the gamma-function ratios Γ(m + α/K) / Γ(1 + α/K) of the finite mixture and feature models into the factorials (m - 1)! of their infinite limits (Griffiths and Ghahramani 2011, Equations 5 and 14).

    theorem tendsto_prod_range_add_div (α : ℝ) (n : ℕ) :
    Tendsto (fun K : ℕ => ∏ j ∈ range n, ((j + 1 : ℝ) + α / K)) atTop (𝓝 (n.factorial : ℝ))
