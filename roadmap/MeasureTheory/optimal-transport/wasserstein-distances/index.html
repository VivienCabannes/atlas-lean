    Wasserstein distances

    The Wasserstein distance of order \(p \ge 1\) between probability measures on a metric space is the \(p\)-th root of the least transport cost \(\int d(x, y)^p \, d\pi\) over couplings \(\pi\). Following Chapter 6 of Villani's Optimal Transport: Old and New, it is symmetric, vanishes on the diagonal, satisfies the triangle inequality (by the gluing lemma) and separates measures, so it is an extended metric on Borel probability measures over a Polish space; \(W_p\) increases with \(p\), and \(W_1\) controls the integrals of Lipschitz functions.

    DefinitionOptimal transport cost of order \(p\)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.MeasureTheory.OptimalTransport.Wasserstein.transportCost

    Let \(X\) be an extended metric space with a σ-algebra, let \(p \in \mathbb{R}\) and let \(\mu, \nu\) be probability measures on \(X\). The optimal transport cost of order \(p\) is $\(C_p(\mu, \nu) = \inf_{\pi} \int_{X \times X} d(x, y)^p \, d\pi(x, y) \in [0, \infty],\)$ the infimum running over all couplings \(\pi\) of \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) (probability measures on \(X \times X\) with marginals \(\mu\) and \(\nu\)). The integral is the Lebesgue integral of a \([0, \infty]\)-valued function, so \(C_p(\mu, \nu)\) is always defined. The Lean definition allows a pseudo-extended metric.

    noncomputable def transportCost (p : ℝ) (μ ν : ProbabilityMeasure X) : ℝ≥0∞ :=
  ⨅ (π : ProbabilityMeasure (X × X)) (_ : IsCoupling μ ν π),
    ∫⁻ z, edist z.1 z.2 ^ p ∂(π : Measure (X × X))
    def IsCoupling {X Y : Type*} [MeasurableSpace X] [MeasurableSpace Y]
    (μ : ProbabilityMeasure X) (ν : ProbabilityMeasure Y)
    (π : ProbabilityMeasure (X × Y)) : Prop :=
  (μ : Measure X) = (π : Measure (X × Y)).map Prod.fst ∧

    DefinitionWasserstein distance of order \(p\)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.MeasureTheory.OptimalTransport.Wasserstein.wasserstein

    Let \((X, d)\) be an extended metric space with a σ-algebra and let \(\mu, \nu\) be probability measures on \(X\). The Wasserstein distance of order \(p\) is $\(W_p(\mu, \nu) = \Bigl(\inf_{\pi} \int_{X \times X} d(x, y)^p \, d\pi(x, y)\Bigr)^{1/p} \in [0, \infty],\)$ the infimum over all couplings \(\pi\) of \(\mu\) and \(\nu\); that is, \(W_p = C_p^{1/p}\) for the optimal transport cost \(C_p\). Villani defines it for a Polish metric space and \(p \in [1, \infty)\); the Lean definition accepts any real \(p\) and any pseudo-extended metric space, and the theorems state the hypotheses they need. \(W_1\) is also called the Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance.

    \(W_p\) may be infinite; it is finite on the Wasserstein space \(P_p(X)\) of measures with a finite \(p\)-th moment (Villani, Definition 6.4).

    noncomputable def wasserstein (p : ℝ) (μ ν : ProbabilityMeasure X) : ℝ≥0∞ :=
  transportCost p μ ν ^ (1 / p)

    TheoremGluing lemma#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.MeasureTheory.OptimalTransport.Wasserstein.exists_gluing

    Let \(\pi_{12}\) be a probability measure on \(X_1 \times X_2\) and \(\pi_{23}\) a probability measure on \(X_2 \times X_3\) such that the second marginal of \(\pi_{12}\) equals the first marginal of \(\pi_{23}\). If \(X_3\) is a standard Borel space, there is a probability measure \(\pi\) on \((X_1 \times X_2) \times X_3\) whose marginal on \(X_1 \times X_2\) is \(\pi_{12}\) and whose marginal on \(X_2 \times X_3\) is \(\pi_{23}\).

    The proof disintegrates \(\pi_{23}\) with respect to its first coordinate, \(\pi_{23}(dx_2\,dx_3) = \pi_{23}(dx_3 \mid x_2)\, \mu_2(dx_2)\), and sets \(\pi(dx_1\,dx_2\,dx_3) = \pi_{12}(dx_1\,dx_2)\, \pi_{23}(dx_3 \mid x_2)\), as in Villani's informal argument. Villani assumes all three spaces Polish; only \(X_3\) needs to be standard Borel here.

    theorem exists_gluing {X₁ X₂ X₃ : Type*} [MeasurableSpace X₁] [MeasurableSpace X₂]
    [MeasurableSpace X₃] [StandardBorelSpace X₃]
    (π₁₂ : Measure (X₁ × X₂)) (π₂₃ : Measure (X₂ × X₃))
    [IsProbabilityMeasure π₁₂] [IsProbabilityMeasure π₂₃]
    (h : π₁₂.map Prod.snd = π₂₃.map Prod.fst) :
    ∃ π : Measure ((X₁ × X₂) × X₃), IsProbabilityMeasure π ∧
      π.map Prod.fst = π₁₂ ∧ π.map (fun z => (z.1.2, z.2)) = π₂₃

    TheoremTriangle inequality for the Wasserstein distance#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.MeasureTheory.OptimalTransport.Wasserstein.wasserstein_triangle

    Let \(X\) be a second-countable standard Borel pseudo-extended metric space with its Borel σ-algebra (for example a Polish metric space) and \(p \ge 1\). For all probability measures \(\mu_1, \mu_2, \mu_3\) on \(X\), $\(W_p(\mu_1, \mu_3) \le W_p(\mu_1, \mu_2) + W_p(\mu_2, \mu_3).\)$

    Proof: glue a coupling of \((\mu_1, \mu_2)\) to a coupling of \((\mu_2, \mu_3)\) along \(\mu_2\), read off a coupling of \((\mu_1, \mu_3)\), and apply the triangle inequality for \(d\) and Minkowski's inequality in \(L^p\). Villani glues optimal couplings; gluing arbitrary couplings and taking infima avoids the existence of optimal couplings.

    theorem wasserstein_triangle [BorelSpace X] [SecondCountableTopology X]
    [StandardBorelSpace X] {p : ℝ} (hp : 1 ≤ p) (μ₁ μ₂ μ₃ : ProbabilityMeasure X) :
    wasserstein p μ₁ μ₃ ≤ wasserstein p μ₁ μ₂ + wasserstein p μ₂ μ₃

    MathlibExt.MeasureTheory.OptimalTransport.Wasserstein.wasserstein_mono

    TheoremThe Wasserstein distance is finite on measures with finite \(p\)-th moments#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.MeasureTheory.OptimalTransport.Wasserstein.wasserstein_lt_top

    Let \(X\) be a second-countable pseudo-extended metric space whose σ-algebra contains the open sets, let \(p \ge 1\) and \(x_0 \in X\). If \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) are probability measures with finite \(p\)-th moments, \(\int d(x_0, x)^p \, d\mu(x) < \infty\) and \(\int d(x_0, y)^p \, d\nu(y) < \infty\), then \(W_p(\mu, \nu) < \infty\).

    Proof: by the triangle inequality and \((A + B)^p \le 2^{p-1}(A^p + B^p)\), the product coupling has cost at most \(2^{p-1}\bigl(\int d(x_0, x)^p \, d\mu + \int d(x_0, y)^p \, d\nu\bigr)\).

    MathlibExt.MeasureTheory.OptimalTransport.Wasserstein.eq_of_wasserstein_eq_zero

    Let \(X\) be a second-countable pseudo-extended metric space with its Borel σ-algebra and \(p \ge 1\). If \(W_p(\mu, \nu) = 0\) then \(\mu = \nu\).

    Villani's argument takes an optimal coupling, which must then be concentrated on the diagonal. The Lean proof avoids the existence theorem: \(W_1 \le W_p\) gives \(W_1(\mu, \nu) = 0\), so every bounded Lipschitz test function has the same integral against \(\mu\) and \(\nu\); the thickened indicators of a closed set \(F\) are such functions and their integrals converge to \(\mu(F)\) and \(\nu(F)\); closed sets generate the Borel σ-algebra. Together with symmetry, the triangle inequality and \(W_p(\mu, \mu) = 0\), this shows that \(W_p\) is an extended metric on Borel probability measures over a Polish space.

    Proof (the transference plan of Villani's proof of Theorem 6.15): take a Hahn set \(S\) for \(\mu - \nu\), split \(\mu = \alpha + m\) and \(\nu = \beta + m\) with common part \(m = \nu|_S + \mu|_{S^c}\), excess \(\alpha = (\mu - \nu)_+\) and deficit \(\beta = (\mu - \nu)_-\), both of mass \(a = \mu(S) - \nu(S) \le \sup_B |\mu(B) - \nu(B)|\), and use the coupling \(\pi = (\mathrm{id}, \mathrm{id})_\# m + a^{-1}\, \alpha \otimes \beta\), whose cost is at most \(D a\).
