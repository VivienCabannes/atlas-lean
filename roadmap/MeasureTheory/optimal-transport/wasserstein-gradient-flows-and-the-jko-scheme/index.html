    Wasserstein gradient flows and the JKO scheme

    Jordan, Kinderlehrer and Otto (1998) showed that the Fokker–Planck equation \(\partial_t \rho = \operatorname{div}(\rho \nabla \Psi) + \beta^{-1} \Delta \rho\) is the gradient flow of the free energy \(F(\rho) = \int \Psi \rho \, dx + \beta^{-1} \int \rho \log \rho \, dx\) for the quadratic Wasserstein distance \(W_2\). Since \((P_2(\mathbb{R}^n), W_2)\) is not a Riemannian manifold in the classical sense, they made "gradient flow" precise through an implicit time discretization, the JKO scheme: with time step \(h > 0\), the state \(\rho^{(k)}\) minimizes \(\tfrac12 W_2(\rho^{(k-1)}, \rho)^2 + h F(\rho)\). Their a priori estimates use only the metric: the minimizer beats the previous state, which gives a discrete energy inequality and, with the triangle and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities, a Hölder-\(\tfrac12\) bound in time. These are proved here for an arbitrary functional on an arbitrary metric space (the minimizing movement scheme). In a Hilbert space, a \(\lambda\)-convex functional makes each step a \((1 + \lambda h)^{-1}\)-contraction, the discrete form of the contraction of convex gradient flows. Existence and uniqueness of the Wasserstein step and convergence to the Fokker–Planck equation are stated as wanted results.

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Variational.MinimizingMovement.IsMinimizingMovementStep

    Let \((X, d)\) be a (pseudo)metric space, \(F : X \to \mathbb{R}\) a functional and \(h\) a time step. A point \(y\) is a minimizing-movement step from \(x\) if it minimizes $\(z \mapsto \tfrac12 d(x, z)^2 + h F(z)\)$ over \(X\). This is the implicit Euler scheme for the gradient flow of \(F\) written with the distance only: in a Hilbert space with \(F\) differentiable, a minimizer satisfies \(y = x - h \nabla F(y)\).

    Jordan, Kinderlehrer and Otto introduce the scheme (their (13)) with \(X\) the probability densities on \(\mathbb{R}^n\) with finite second moment, \(d\) the quadratic Wasserstein distance and \(F\) the free energy; the Lean statement keeps their normalization \(\tfrac12 d^2 + hF\) rather than \(d^2/(2h) + F\), which is equivalent for \(h > 0\).

    def IsMinimizingMovementStep (F : X → ℝ) (h : ℝ) (x y : X) : Prop :=
  ∀ z, dist x y ^ 2 / 2 + h * F y ≤ dist x z ^ 2 / 2 + h * F z

    A sequence \((\rho_k)_{k \ge 0}\) in a metric space solves the minimizing-movement scheme for \(F\) with time step \(h\) if every \(\rho_{k+1}\) is a minimizing-movement step from \(\rho_k\).     MathlibExt.Analysis.Variational.MinimizingMovement.IsMinimizingMovement.energy_sum_le

    For a time step \(h > 0\), along a minimizing-movement sequence, $\(\frac{1}{2h} \sum_{k=1}^{N} d(\rho_{k-1}, \rho_k)^2 + F(\rho_N) \le F(\rho_0) \qquad (N \ge 0).\)$ This is the discrete counterpart of the energy identity \(\int_0^T |\dot\rho|^2 \, dt + F(\rho(T)) = F(\rho(0))\) of a gradient flow, where the metric speed \(|\dot\rho|\) is approximated by \(d(\rho_{k-1}, \rho_k)/h\). Jordan, Kinderlehrer and Otto use it to obtain their a priori estimate (42) and, with (46), compactness of the interpolated solutions; it uses only that the step is a minimizer and holds for any functional on any metric space.

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Variational.MinimizingMovement.IsMinimizingMovement.dist_sq_le

    Along a minimizing-movement sequence with any time step \(h\), $\(d(\rho_j, \rho_{j+n})^2 \le 2hn \, \bigl(F(\rho_j) - F(\rho_{j+n})\bigr).\)$ Proof: by the triangle inequality and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, \(d(\rho_j, \rho_{j+n})^2 \le \bigl(\sum_k d(\rho_k, \rho_{k+1})\bigr)^2 \le n \sum_k d(\rho_k, \rho_{k+1})^2\), and the discrete energy inequality applied to the tail starting at \(\rho_j\) bounds the sum. In the time variable \(t_k = kh\) this reads \(d(\rho_j, \rho_k) \le \sqrt{2 (t_k - t_j) (F(\rho_j) - F(\rho_k))}\): the discrete trajectory is Hölder continuous of exponent \(\tfrac12\) in time.

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Variational.MinimizingMovement.IsMinimizingMovement.dist_sq_le_of_le

    If \(F \ge m\) everywhere and \(h > 0\), then along a minimizing-movement sequence, for \(j \le k\), $\(d(\rho_j, \rho_k)^2 \le 2 (k - j) h \, \bigl(F(\rho_0) - m\bigr),\)$ that is \(d(\rho_j, \rho_k) \le \sqrt{2 (t_k - t_j)(F(\rho_0) - m)}\) with \(t = kh\). The bound does not depend on \(h\), so the interpolated trajectories are equicontinuous in time; this is the compactness input of an Arzelà–Ascoli argument as \(h \to 0\).

    The Fokker–Planck free energy is not bounded below on all of \(P_2(\mathbb{R}^n)\); Jordan, Kinderlehrer and Otto bound it below on second-moment balls (their (14), \(S(\rho) \ge -C (M(\rho) + 1)^\alpha\) with \(\alpha < 1\)) and close the estimate with (46).

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Variational.MinimizingMovement.exists_isMinimizingMovementStep

    Let \(X\) be a proper metric space (closed balls are compact), \(F : X \to \mathbb{R}\) lower semicontinuous and bounded below by \(m\), and \(h > 0\). Then from every \(x\) there is a minimizing-movement step. Proof: \(G(z) = \tfrac12 d(x, z)^2 + h F(z)\) is lower semicontinuous, and \(G(z) \le G(x) = h F(x)\) forces \(d(x, z)^2 \le 2h (F(x) - m)\), so the minimum of \(G\) over a compact closed ball is a global minimum.

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Variational.MinimizingMovement.IsMinimizingMovementStep.inner_le

    Let \(H\) be a real inner product space, \(F : H \to \mathbb{R}\) \(\lambda\)-convex, i.e. $\(F((1-t)a + tb) \le (1-t) F(a) + t F(b) - t(1-t) \tfrac{\lambda}{2} \|a - b\|^2 \qquad (t \in [0, 1]),\)$ and \(h > 0\). If \(y\) is a minimizing-movement step from \(x\), then for every \(z \in H\), $\(\langle x - y, z - y \rangle \le h \bigl(F(z) - F(y)\bigr) - h \tfrac{\lambda}{2} \|z - y\|^2 .\)$ Thus \((x - y)/h\) is a \(\lambda\)-subgradient of \(F\) at \(y\); for differentiable \(F\) this is the implicit Euler equation \(y = x - h \nabla F(y)\).     TheoremContraction of the minimizing-movement step#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Variational.MinimizingMovement.IsMinimizingMovementStep.contraction

    Let \(H\) be a real inner product space, \(F\) \(\lambda\)-convex and \(h > 0\). If \(y_1\) and \(y_2\) are minimizing-movement steps from \(x_1\) and \(x_2\), then $\((1 + \lambda h) \, \|y_1 - y_2\| \le \|x_1 - x_2\| .\)$ When \(1 + \lambda h > 0\) the step is \((1 + \lambda h)^{-1}\)-Lipschitz, hence unique. Proof: add the variational inequalities of the two steps, tested against each other, and apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

    In Otto's picture, gradient flows of convex functionals contract, and contraction estimates for dissipative equations are consequences of the convexity of the driving functional in the right geometry; for the porous medium equation that convexity is McCann's displacement convexity in the Wasserstein space. This theorem is the time-discrete, Hilbert-space instance of that principle.

    theorem IsMinimizingMovementStep.contraction {F : H → ℝ} {lam h : ℝ}
    (hF : StrongConvexOn univ lam F) (hh : 0 < h) {x₁ x₂ y₁ y₂ : H}
    (hy₁ : IsMinimizingMovementStep F h x₁ y₁) (hy₂ : IsMinimizingMovementStep F h x₂ y₂) :
    (1 + lam * h) * ‖y₁ - y₂‖ ≤ ‖x₁ - x₂‖
    def IsMinimizingMovementStep (F : X → ℝ) (h : ℝ) (x y : X) : Prop :=

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Variational.MinimizingMovement.IsMinimizingMovement.contraction_pow

    Let \(H\) be a real inner product space, \(F\) \(\lambda\)-convex, \(h > 0\) with \(1 + \lambda h > 0\), and \((\rho^1_k)\), \((\rho^2_k)\) two minimizing-movement sequences. Then for every \(k\), $\((1 + \lambda h)^k \, \|\rho^1_k - \rho^2_k\| \le \|\rho^1_0 - \rho^2_0\|,\)$ by iterating the one-step contraction. With \(t = kh\), \((1 + \lambda h)^{-k}\) is the implicit-Euler approximation of the factor \(e^{-\lambda t}\) in the contraction \(\|\rho^1(t) - \rho^2(t)\| \le e^{-\lambda t} \|\rho^1(0) - \rho^2(0)\|\) of \(\lambda\)-convex gradient flows.

    DefinitionNegative Boltzmann entropy#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.Diffusion.JKOScheme.negEntropy

    For a measure \(\rho\) on \(E\) with density \(f = d\rho/dx\) with respect to Lebesgue measure, $\(S(\rho) = \int_E f \log f \, dx \in [-\infty, +\infty],\)$ computed in the Lean definition as the integral of the positive part of \(f \log f\) minus the integral of its negative part. \(-S\) is the Gibbs–Boltzmann entropy. On the admissible set the negative part is finite: Jordan, Kinderlehrer and Otto show \(S(\rho) \ge -C (M(\rho) + 1)^\alpha\) for every \(\alpha \in (n/(n+2), 1)\) (their (14)).

    The Lean definition uses the Radon–Nikodym derivative, so it is meaningful for absolutely continuous \(\rho\), which is how it is used.

    DefinitionFree energy of the Fokker–Planck equation#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.Diffusion.JKOScheme.freeEnergy

    For a potential \(\Psi\) and an inverse temperature \(\beta\), the free energy of a density \(\rho\) is $\(F(\rho) = E(\rho) + \beta^{-1} S(\rho) = \int \Psi \rho \, dx + \beta^{-1} \int \rho \log \rho \, dx,\)$ the potential energy plus \(\beta^{-1}\) times the negative entropy, with values in \([-\infty, +\infty]\). When \(Z = \int e^{-\beta \Psi} dx < \infty\) it is minimized by the Gibbs density \(Z^{-1} e^{-\beta\Psi}\), and it decreases along solutions of the Fokker–Planck equation \(\partial_t \rho = \operatorname{div}(\rho \nabla \Psi) + \beta^{-1} \Delta \rho\).

    noncomputable def freeEnergy (Ψ : E → ℝ) (β : ℝ) (ρ : Measure E) : EReal :=
  (potentialEnergy Ψ ρ : EReal) + ((β⁻¹ : ℝ) : EReal) * negEntropy ρ
