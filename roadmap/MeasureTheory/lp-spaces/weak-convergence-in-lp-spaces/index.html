    Let \(1 < p < \infty\) and let \(q\) be the conjugate exponent, so that \(1/p + 1/q = 1\). Write \(L^p(0,1)\) and \(L^q(0,1)\) for the Lebesgue spaces of real functions on \((0,1)\) with Lebesgue measure. Let \((f_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}\) be a sequence in \(L^p(0,1)\) with \(\|f_n\|_{L^p} \le 1\) for every \(n\) and \(f_n(x) \to 0\) for almost every \(x \in (0,1)\). Then for every \(g \in L^q(0,1)\),

    \[ \int_0^1 f_n(x)\, g(x)\, dx \to 0 \quad (n \to \infty). \]

    Since \(L^q(0,1)\) is the dual of \(L^p(0,1)\), this says that \((f_n)\) converges weakly to \(0\) in \(L^p(0,1)\).

    Bounded almost everywhere convergent sequences in \(L^p\), \(1 < p < \infty\), converge weakly. Let \(1 < p < \infty\) and let \(q\) be the conjugate exponent, \(1/p + 1/q = 1\). Let \(f\) and \(f_0, f_1, f_2, \dots\) be real-valued functions in \(L^p(\mathbb{R})\) such that \(f_n \to f\) almost everywhere and \(\sup_n \|f_n\|_{L^p} < \infty\). Then \(f_n\) converges weakly to \(f\): for every \(g \in L^q(\mathbb{R})\),

    \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}} f_n g\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}} f g\,dx. \]
