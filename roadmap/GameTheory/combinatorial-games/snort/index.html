    DefinitionSnort positions with a move bound#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.snortMoverWins

    Let \(G\) be a finite simple graph, let \(k\) be a natural number, and let \(A\) and \(B\) be finite sets of vertices of \(G\), the vertices coloured so far by the player to move and by the opponent. A legal move colours a vertex \(v\) that lies in neither \(A\) nor \(B\) and has no neighbour in \(B\). With move bound \(0\) the player to move loses. With move bound \(k + 1\) the player to move wins at \((A, B)\) exactly when some legal move \(v\) leaves a position \((B, A \cup \{v\})\) that the opponent, now to move with move bound \(k\), does not win.

    def snortMoverWins [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V] (G : SimpleGraph V) [DecidableRel G.Adj] :
    ℕ → Finset V → Finset V → Bool
  | 0, _, _ => false
  | k + 1, mine, theirs =>
      decide (∃ v : V, v ∉ mine ∧ v ∉ theirs ∧ (∀ w ∈ theirs, ¬ G.Adj v w) ∧
        snortMoverWins G k theirs (insert v mine) = false)

    DefinitionFirst player wins Snort#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.SnortFirstPlayerWins

    Let \(G\) be a finite simple graph. In Snort on \(G\) the two players alternately colour a vertex that is not yet coloured and is not adjacent to any vertex coloured by the opponent, and a player with no legal move loses. The first player wins Snort on \(G\) when the player who moves first has a winning strategy from the position in which no vertex is coloured.

    def SnortFirstPlayerWins [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V] (G : SimpleGraph V)

  snortMoverWins G (Fintype.card V) ∅ ∅ = true

    DefinitionSecond-player win in Snort#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.SnortSecondPlayerWins

    Let \(G\) be a finite simple graph. In Snort on \(G\), two players alternately colour a vertex of \(G\) that is not yet coloured, each with their own colour, and a player may not colour a vertex adjacent to a vertex of the opponent's colour; a player with no legal move loses. The second player wins Snort on \(G\) if, starting from the position in which no vertex is coloured, the player who moves first has no winning strategy.

    Every move colours a new vertex, so the game ends after at most \(|V(G)|\) moves and has no draws; hence this says that the second player has a winning strategy.

    def SnortSecondPlayerWins [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V] (G : SimpleGraph V)
    [DecidableRel G.Adj] : Prop :=
  snortMoverWins G (Fintype.card V) ∅ ∅ = false

    DefinitionSnort response pairs#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.IsSnortResponsePair

    In Snort on a finite graph \(G\), a pair \((u, v)\) of vertices is a response pair if \(u \neq v\), \(u\) and \(v\) are not adjacent (so the first player colouring \(u\) and the second answering \(v\) is legal), and the first player, now to move, loses from the position in which the first player has coloured \(u\) and the second player has coloured \(v\).

    The outcome is computed with a bound of \(|V(G)| - 2\) further moves, the number of uncoloured vertices, so the bound never cuts the game short.

    def IsSnortResponsePair [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V] (G : SimpleGraph V) [DecidableRel G.Adj]
    (u v : V) : Prop :=
  u ≠ v ∧ ¬ G.Adj u v ∧ snortMoverWins G (Fintype.card V - 2) {u} {v} = false
    def snortMoverWins [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V] (G : SimpleGraph V) [DecidableRel G.Adj] :
    ℕ → Finset V → Finset V → Bool
  | 0, _, _ => false

      decide (∃ v : V, v ∉ mine ∧ v ∉ theirs ∧ (∀ w ∈ theirs, ¬ G.Adj v w) ∧

    DefinitionOppositions#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.IsOpposition

    An opposition of a graph \(G\) is an automorphism \(f\) of \(G\) such that for every vertex \(v\), \(f(f(v)) = v\), \(f(v) \neq v\), and \(f(v)\) is not adjacent to \(v\); that is, an involutive automorphism moving every vertex outside its closed neighbourhood.

    def IsOpposition (G : SimpleGraph V) (f : G ≃g G) : Prop :=
  ∀ v, f (f v) = v ∧ f v ≠ v ∧ ¬ G.Adj v (f v)

    DefinitionOpposition matchings#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.IsOppositionMatching

    Let \(K\) be a graph. A subgraph \(M\) of \(K\) is an opposition matching of \(K\) if it is a perfect matching of \(K\) and for any two distinct edges \(ab\) and \(cd\) of \(M\), \(ac \in E(K) \iff bd \in E(K)\) and \(ad \in E(K) \iff bc \in E(K)\).

    Since both matching edges are present, this says that \(K\) induces on \(\{a, b, c, d\}\) either \(K_4\), a \(4\)-cycle, or just the two matching edges.

    def IsOppositionMatching (K : SimpleGraph V) (M : K.Subgraph) : Prop :=
  M.IsPerfectMatching ∧ ∀ a b c d, M.Adj a b → M.Adj c d → s(a, b) ≠ s(c, d) →
    (K.Adj a c ↔ K.Adj b d) ∧ (K.Adj a d ↔ K.Adj b c)

    DefinitionGraph product given by a Boolean formula#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.formulaProduct

    Let \(G\) and \(H\) be graphs with vertex sets \(V\) and \(W\), and let \(\varphi\) be a Boolean function of four Boolean arguments. The product \(G \varphi H\) is the graph on \(V \times W\) in which two distinct vertices \((g_1, h_1)\) and \((g_2, h_2)\) are adjacent exactly when \(\varphi(g_1 g_2 \in E(G), h_1 h_2 \in E(H), g_1 = g_2, h_1 = h_2)\) is true.

    def formulaProduct [DecidableEq V] [DecidableEq W] (φ : Bool → Bool → Bool → Bool → Bool)
    (G : SimpleGraph V) (H : SimpleGraph W) [DecidableRel G.Adj] [DecidableRel H.Adj] :
    SimpleGraph (V × W) :=
  SimpleGraph.fromRel fun x y => φ (G.Adj x.1 y.1) (H.Adj x.2 y.2) (x.1 = y.1) (x.2 = y.2)

    AbbreviationStrong product of graphs#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.strongProd

    For simple graphs \(G\) on \(V\) and \(H\) on \(W\), the strong product \(G \boxtimes H\) is the simple graph on \(V \times W\) in which \((g_1, h_1)\) and \((g_2, h_2)\) are adjacent if and only if \(g_1 = g_2\) and \(h_1 h_2\) is an edge of \(H\), or \(g_1 g_2\) is an edge of \(G\) and \(h_1 = h_2\), or \(g_1 g_2\) is an edge of \(G\) and \(h_1 h_2\) is an edge of \(H\).

    abbrev strongProd [DecidableEq V] [DecidableEq W] (G : SimpleGraph V) (H : SimpleGraph W)
    [DecidableRel G.Adj] [DecidableRel H.Adj] : SimpleGraph (V × W) :=
  formulaProduct (fun eG eH qG qH => (qG && eH) || (eG && qH) || (eG && eH)) G H

    DefinitionFirework of a graph#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.firework

    For a graph \(G\), vertices \(u\) and \(v\) of \(G\) and a natural number \(n\), the \(n\)-firework of \(G\) at \((u, v)\) is the graph obtained from \(G\) by adding \(2n\) new vertices, \(n\) of which are adjacent only to \(u\) and the other \(n\) adjacent only to \(v\).

    def firework (G : SimpleGraph V) (u v : V) (n : ℕ) : SimpleGraph (V ⊕ (Fin n × Bool)) :=
  SimpleGraph.fromRel fun x y => match x, y with
    | Sum.inl a, Sum.inl b => G.Adj a b
    | Sum.inl a, Sum.inr (_, false) => a = u
    | Sum.inl a, Sum.inr (_, true) => a = v
    | _, _ => False

    DefinitionCartesian grid#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.cartesianGrid

    For \(k \in \mathbb{N}\) and side lengths \(n_1, \dots, n_k \in \mathbb{N}\), the \(k\)-dimensional Cartesian grid has as vertices the tuples \((x_1, \dots, x_k)\) of integers with \(0 \le x_i < n_i\) for each \(i\), and two tuples are adjacent when they differ in exactly one coordinate, and there by exactly one.
