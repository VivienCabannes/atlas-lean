    Mean field games

    Lasry and Lions (2006–2007) model games with a continuum of identical, individually negligible players: each player optimises against the distribution \(m\) of the others, and an equilibrium is a distribution reproduced by the players' best responses. In the one-shot version on a state space \(Q\) with cost \(F(y, m)\), an equilibrium is a distribution supported on the minimisers of \(F(\cdot, m)\); the dynamic version is the mean field game system, a backward Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation for the value of a typical player coupled with a forward Fokker–Planck equation for the distribution of players. Under the Lasry–Lions monotonicity condition \(\int (F(y, m_1) - F(y, m_2))\, d(m_1 - m_2)(y) > 0\) for \(m_1 \neq m_2\) (crowd aversion) the equilibrium is unique, in both versions, by the same duality argument.

    Here the one-shot equilibrium on a finite state space, its characterisation, the Lasry–Lions uniqueness theorem and the pointwise identity behind uniqueness for the system are proved, and existence is recorded as wanted. The second-order mean field game system on \(\mathbb{R}^d\) is defined and its uniqueness theorem recorded as wanted; its existence theory, the first-order system and the convergence of \(N\)-player equilibria are not formalized.

    DefinitionMean field equilibrium on a finite state space#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.MeanFieldEquilibrium.IsMeanFieldEquilibrium

    A continuum of identical players each choose a state \(y\) of a finite set \(Q\); a player in state \(y\) pays \(F(y, m)\) when the population is distributed according to the probability vector \(m\). A probability vector \(m\) is a mean field equilibrium if $\(\sum_y F(y, m)\, m(y) \le \sum_y F(y, m)\, m'(y) \quad \text{for every probability vector } m',\)$ that is \(\int F(y, m)\, dm(y) = \inf_{m'} \int F(y, m)\, dm'(y)\): given the crowd, no player gains by deviating. Equivalently, \(m\) is supported on the minimisers of \(F(\cdot, m)\).

    In Lions' lectures such equilibria arise as limits of Nash equilibria of symmetric \(N\)-player games with costs \(F(x_i, \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j \ne i} \delta_{x_j})\) (Cardaliaguet, Theorems 2.4 and 2.7), where \(Q\) is a compact metric space; here \(Q\) is finite.

    def IsMeanFieldEquilibrium (F : (Q → ℝ) → Q → ℝ) (m : Q → ℝ) : Prop :=
  ((∀ y, 0 ≤ m y) ∧ ∑ y, m y = 1) ∧
    ∀ m' : Q → ℝ, ((∀ y, 0 ≤ m' y) ∧ ∑ y, m' y = 1) → ∑ y, F m y * m y ≤ ∑ y, F m y * m' y

    TheoremEquilibria are supported on minimisers#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.MeanFieldEquilibrium.isMeanFieldEquilibrium_iff

    A probability vector \(m\) is a mean field equilibrium if and only if every state charged by \(m\) minimises the cost: \(m(x) \ne 0\) implies \(F(x, m) \le F(y, m)\) for every \(y\).

    If some charged \(x\) had \(F(y, m) < F(x, m)\), moving the mass \(m(x)\) from \(x\) to \(y\) would lower \(\sum_y F(y, m) m'(y)\) by \(m(x)(F(x, m) - F(y, m)) > 0\). Conversely, if \(m\) is supported on minimisers then \(\sum_y F(y, m) m(y) = \min F(\cdot, m) \le \sum_y F(y, m) m'(y)\). Cardaliaguet tests with Dirac masses \(m' = \delta_x\) instead; on a finite set both arguments work.

    theorem isMeanFieldEquilibrium_iff :
    IsMeanFieldEquilibrium F m ↔ ((∀ y, 0 ≤ m y) ∧ ∑ y, m y = 1) ∧
      ∀ x, m x ≠ 0 → ∀ y, F m x ≤ F m y

    TheoremLasry–Lions uniqueness of mean field equilibria#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.MeanFieldEquilibrium.eq_of_isMeanFieldEquilibrium

    Suppose the cost satisfies the Lasry–Lions monotonicity condition $\(\sum_y (F(y, m_1) - F(y, m_2))(m_1(y) - m_2(y)) > 0 \quad \text{for probability vectors } m_1 \ne m_2 .\)$ Then there is at most one mean field equilibrium.

    If \(m_1\) and \(m_2\) are equilibria, \(\int F(\cdot, m_1)\, d(m_1 - m_2) \le 0\) and \(\int F(\cdot, m_2)\, d(m_2 - m_1) \le 0\); adding them gives \(\int (F(\cdot, m_1) - F(\cdot, m_2))\, d(m_1 - m_2) \le 0\), so \(m_1 = m_2\). The condition expresses crowd aversion: a cost that increases with the mass at the chosen state, such as \(F(y, m) = m(y)\), satisfies it. The same two-line argument proves uniqueness for the mean field game system, after the Lasry–Lions identity is used to dispose of the Hamiltonian terms.

    theorem eq_of_isMeanFieldEquilibrium

      0 < ∑ y, (F m₁ y - F m₂ y) * (m₁ y - m₂ y))

    DefinitionThe mean field game system#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.MeanFieldGameSystem.IsMFGClassicalSolution

    On \(\mathbb{R}^d \times [0, T]\), a classical solution of the mean field game system is a pair \((u, m)\), continuous on \(\mathbb{R}^d \times [0, T]\) and of class \(C^2\) in space and \(C^1\) in time on \(\mathbb{R}^d \times (0, T)\), with $\(\begin{aligned} -\partial_t u - \Delta u + \tfrac12 |Du|^2 &= F(x, m(t)), \\ \partial_t m - \Delta m - \operatorname{div}(m\, Du) &= 0, \\ m(0) = m_0, \qquad u(x, T) &= G(x, m(T)), \end{aligned}\)$ where \(m(t)\) is a probability density with finite first moment, identified with the probability measure it defines, so that the costs \(F, G : \mathbb{R}^d \times P_1 \to \mathbb{R}\) can be evaluated. The two equations hold on \(\mathbb{R}^d \times (0, T)\). Cardaliaguet's definition asks for the \(C^2\)/\(C^1\) regularity up to \(t = 0\) and \(t = T\); requiring it only in the open interval lets the Hölder initial densities and Lipschitz terminal costs of his existence theorem be data.

    The first equation is the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation of a typical player who, facing the crowd \(m\), controls \(dX_t = \alpha_t\, dt + \sqrt2\, dB_t\) to minimise \(\mathbb{E}[\int_0^T (\frac12|\alpha_t|^2 + F(X_t, m(t)))\, dt + G(X_T, m(T))]\); it runs backward from the terminal cost. Its optimal feedback is \(\alpha = -Du\) (Cardaliaguet, Lemma 3.7), and the second equation, a Fokker–Planck equation run forward from \(m_0\), says that \(m(t)\) is the law of the players who use it. An equilibrium is a fixed point: the crowd generated by the best responses is the crowd they respond to. In the formal definition the Fokker–Planck equation is \(\partial_t m = \texttt{fokkerPlanckOp}\ 1\ (-Du)\ m\), the repository's Fokker–Planck operator with unit diffusion and drift \(-Du\), and \(\Delta u = \operatorname{div} \nabla u\); the coordinates of \(\mathbb{R}^d\) are Fin d → ℝ.

    TheoremThe Lasry–Lions identity#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.MeanFieldEquilibrium.lasryLions_identity

    For real numbers \(m_1, m_2\) and vectors \(a, b\) of a real inner product space, $\(\frac{m_1 - m_2}{2}\,(|a|^2 - |b|^2) - \langle a - b,\, m_1 a - m_2 b\rangle = -\frac{m_1 + m_2}{2}\,|a - b|^2 .\)$

    The 2013 notes print the right-hand side of this display (proof of Theorem 3.6, p. 15) as \(-\frac{\bar m}{2}|Du_1 - Du_2|^2\) with \(\bar m = m_1 - m_2\). Expanding both sides gives the coefficient \(\frac{m_1 + m_2}{2}\) (take \(m_1 = m_2 = 1\): the left-hand side is \(-|a - b|^2\)), which is what the sign argument of the proof uses and what the Lean statement proves.

    This is the pointwise identity in the proof that the mean field game system $\(-\partial_t u - \Delta u + \tfrac12 |Du|^2 = F(x, m), \qquad \partial_t m - \Delta m - \operatorname{div}(m\, Du) = 0,\)$ \(m(0) = m_0\), \(u(T) = G(x, m(T))\), has at most one classical solution when \(F\) is strictly monotone and \(G\) monotone (Cardaliaguet, Theorem 3.6). For two solutions, test the Fokker–Planck equation for \(m_1 - m_2\) with \(u_1 - u_2\) and the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation for \(u_1 - u_2\) with \(m_1 - m_2\). With \(a = Du_1\), \(b = Du_2\) and densities \(m_1, m_2 \ge 0\), the identity turns the sum into \(\int\!\!\int (m_1 - m_2)(F(x, m_1) - F(x, m_2)) \le 0\), and monotonicity forces \(m_1 = m_2\). The system is defined and its uniqueness theorem recorded as wanted.

    theorem lasryLions_identity {V : Type*} [NormedAddCommGroup V] [InnerProductSpace ℝ V]
    (m₁ m₂ : ℝ) (a b : V) :
    (m₁ - m₂) / 2 * (‖a‖ ^ 2 - ‖b‖ ^ 2) - inner ℝ (a - b) (m₁ • a - m₂ • b) =
      -((m₁ + m₂) / 2) * ‖a - b‖ ^ 2
