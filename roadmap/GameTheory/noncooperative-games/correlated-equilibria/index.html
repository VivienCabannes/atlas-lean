    Correlated equilibria

    A correlated equilibrium (Aumann 1974) is a probability distribution on strategy profiles such that, if a referee draws a profile from it and privately tells each player its own coordinate, no player gains by disobeying its recommendation. In a finite game the correlated equilibria are cut out of the simplex of distributions by finitely many linear inequalities, so they form a compact convex polytope. This polytope contains the Nash equilibria, which are exactly its product distributions (both directions are proved, for Nash equilibria described by pure deviations; from the payoff-form definition of Nash's theorem only the inclusion is bridged), and is contained in the Hannan set of coarse correlated equilibria. Even simple games such as Chicken have correlated equilibria outside the convex hull of their Nash equilibria (Hart and Mas-Colell 2000, §2). Correlated equilibria are also what adaptive play reaches: the empirical distribution of play is a correlated approximate equilibrium exactly when every player's conditional regrets are small (Hart and Mas-Colell 2000).

    DefinitionCorrelated equilibrium#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.CorrelatedEquilibrium.IsCorrelatedEq

    A probability distribution \(\psi\) on the strategy profiles of a finite game with costs \(c_i\) is a correlated \(\varepsilon\)-equilibrium if for every player \(i\) and all strategies \(j, k\) of \(i\) $\(\sum_{s :\, s_i = j} \psi(s)\,\bigl(c_i(s) - c_i(k, s_{-i})\bigr) \le \varepsilon,\)$ and a correlated equilibrium when \(\varepsilon = 0\). Think of a referee who draws \(s\) from \(\psi\) and tells each player only its own coordinate \(s_i\): a player told to play \(j\) cannot gain by playing \(k\) instead, provided the others obey. With payoffs \(u_i = -c_i\) this is the definition of Hart and Mas-Colell; Aumann introduced the notion as a Nash equilibrium of the game extended by payoff-irrelevant correlated signals.

    The error is additive and summed over the event \(\{s_i = j\}\), as in Hart and Mas-Colell; it is not divided by the probability of that event.

    def IsCorrelatedEq (cost : ι → (∀ i, S i) → ℝ) (ψ : (∀ i, S i) → ℝ) (ε : ℝ) : Prop :=
  IsDistribution ψ ∧ ∀ i (j k : S i), ∑ s, ψ s * switchGain cost i j k s ≤ ε
    def IsDistribution (μ : (∀ i, S i) → ℝ) : Prop :=
  (∀ s, 0 ≤ μ s) ∧ ∑ s, μ s = 1

    DefinitionGain from a switched recommendation#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.CorrelatedEquilibrium.switchGain

    In a finite game with costs \(c_i\), the gain of player \(i\) at the profile \(s\) from playing \(k\) whenever it is told to play \(j\) is $\(g_i^{j\to k}(s) = \begin{cases} c_i(s) - c_i(k, s_{-i}) & \text{if } s_i = j, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\)$ With payoffs \(u_i = -c_i\) this is the bracket \(u_i(k, s_{-i}) - u_i(s)\) of the correlated equilibrium inequalities, restricted to the event \(\{s_i = j\}\).

    def switchGain (cost : ι → (∀ i, S i) → ℝ) (i : ι) (j k : S i) (s : ∀ i, S i) : ℝ :=
  if s i = j then cost i s - cost i (Function.update s i k) else 0

    TheoremCorrelated-equilibrium sums over a recommendation#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.CorrelatedEquilibrium.sum_mul_switchGain

    For a distribution \(\psi\) on profiles, \(\sum_s \psi(s)\, g_i^{j\to k}(s) = \sum_{s :\, s_i = j} \psi(s)\,\bigl(c_i(s) - c_i(k, s_{-i})\bigr)\): the sum used in the definition of correlated equilibria is the sum over the event \(\{s_i = j\}\) of Hart and Mas-Colell's Definition.

    theorem sum_mul_switchGain [Fintype ι] (cost : ι → (∀ i, S i) → ℝ) (ψ : (∀ i, S i) → ℝ) (i : ι)
    (j k : S i) :
    ∑ s, ψ s * switchGain cost i j k s =
      ∑ s ∈ univ.filter (fun s => s i = j),
        ψ s * (cost i s - cost i (Function.update s i k))

    TheoremThe set of correlated equilibria is compact#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.CorrelatedEquilibrium.isCompact_setOf_isCorrelatedEq

    For every \(\varepsilon\), the set of correlated \(\varepsilon\)-equilibria is compact: it is a closed subset of the simplex of probability distributions on the finite set of profiles.     TheoremCorrelated equilibria are coarse correlated equilibria#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.CorrelatedEquilibrium.IsCorrelatedEq.isCoarseCorrelatedEq

    Summing the correlated \(\varepsilon\)-equilibrium inequalities of player \(i\) over the recommended strategy \(j\) gives the coarse inequality for the fixed deviation \(k\), with error \(|S_i|\,\varepsilon\): $\(\mathbb E_\psi[c_i(s)] - \mathbb E_\psi[c_i(k, s_{-i})] = \sum_{j \in S_i} \sum_{s :\, s_i = j} \psi(s)\bigl(c_i(s) - c_i(k, s_{-i})\bigr) \le |S_i|\,\varepsilon .\)$ In particular every correlated equilibrium is a coarse correlated equilibrium, that is, lies in the Hannan set. The two sets coincide when every player has at most two strategies (Hart 2005, §10.1; not formalised here).

    theorem IsCorrelatedEq.isCoarseCorrelatedEq {cost : ι → (∀ i, S i) → ℝ}
    {ψ : (∀ i, S i) → ℝ} {ε δ : ℝ} (h : IsCorrelatedEq cost ψ ε)
    (hδ : ∀ i, (Fintype.card (S i) : ℝ) * ε ≤ δ) : IsCoarseCorrelatedEq cost ψ δ
    def IsCorrelatedEq (cost : ι → (∀ i, S i) → ℝ) (ψ : (∀ i, S i) → ℝ) (ε : ℝ) : Prop :=
  IsDistribution ψ ∧ ∀ i (j k : S i), ∑ s, ψ s * switchGain cost i j k s ≤ ε

    DefinitionProduct distribution of mixed strategies#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.CorrelatedEquilibrium.productDist

    The product distribution of mixed strategies \(\sigma_i\) is \(\psi(s) = \prod_i \sigma_i(s_i)\): the law of the profile when the players randomise independently.

    def productDist (σ : ∀ i, S i → ℝ) (s : ∀ i, S i) : ℝ :=

    TheoremCorrelated-equilibrium sums of a product distribution#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.CorrelatedEquilibrium.sum_productDist_mul_switchGain

    For the product distribution \(\psi\) of mixed strategies \(\sigma\), $\(\sum_{s :\, s_i = j} \psi(s)\bigl(c_i(s) - c_i(k, s_{-i})\bigr) = \sigma_i(j)\,\bigl(\bar c_i(j) - \bar c_i(k)\bigr),\)$ where \(\bar c_i\) are the expected costs of pure strategies against the others' mixed strategies. So a product distribution is a correlated equilibrium exactly when every strategy that \(\sigma_i\) plays with positive probability is a best response.

    theorem sum_productDist_mul_switchGain (cost : ι → (∀ i, S i) → ℝ) (σ : ∀ i, S i → ℝ) (i : ι)
    (j k : S i) :
    ∑ s, productDist σ s * switchGain cost i j k s =
      σ i j * (pureCost cost σ i j - pureCost cost σ i k)
    def productDist (σ : ∀ i, S i → ℝ) (s : ∀ i, S i) : ℝ :=
  ∏ i, σ i (s i)

    DefinitionConditional regret#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.CorrelatedEquilibrium.condRegret

    After rounds with realised profiles \(s(1), \dots, s(T)\), the conditional regret of player \(i\) for \(j \to k\) is the total cost it would have saved by playing \(k\) in every round in which it played \(j\): $\(\sum_{t \le T :\, s_i(t) = j} \bigl(c_i(s(t)) - c_i(k, s_{-i}(t))\bigr).\)$ Divided by \(T\) this is Hart and Mas-Colell's \(D_T(j, k)\) in cost form; its positive part is the regret \(R_T(j, k)\) of (2.1c).

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.CorrelatedEquilibrium.isCorrelatedEq_empirical_iff

    After \(T \ge 1\) rounds, the empirical distribution of play is a correlated \(\varepsilon\)-equilibrium if and only if every conditional regret is at most \(T\varepsilon\), that is, every average regret \(D_T(j, k)\) is at most \(\varepsilon\).     DefinitionRegret-invariant mixed action#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.CorrelatedEquilibrium.IsRegretInvariant

    Given nonnegative regrets \(\lambda(j, k)\) of a player for switching from \(j\) to \(k\), a mixed action \(q\) is regret-invariant if the regret flowing into each strategy equals the regret flowing out of it: $\(\sum_k q(k)\,\lambda(k, j) = q(j) \sum_k \lambda(j, k) \quad \text{for all } j .\)$ Equivalently, \(q\) is an invariant probability vector of the stochastic matrix with off-diagonal entries \(\lambda(j, k)/\mu\) for \(\mu\) large. Foster and Vohra's calibrated forecasts use the same conservation condition, their equation (2).

    def IsRegretInvariant {A : Type*} [Fintype A] (lam : A → A → ℝ) (q : A → ℝ) : Prop :=
  ∀ j, ∑ k, q k * lam k j = q j * ∑ k, lam j k

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.CorrelatedEquilibrium.sum_mul_sub_eq_zero_of_isRegretInvariant
