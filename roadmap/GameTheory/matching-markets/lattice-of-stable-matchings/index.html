    Lattice of stable matchings

    For two stable matchings of a marriage market, giving every man the partner he prefers among his two partners yields again a stable perfect matching, in which every woman receives the partner she likes less. Roth (1986, Lemma 2) proves the college-admissions version of this observation, uses it for the rural hospitals theorem, and attributes the marriage case to Conway as reported by Knuth (Mariages stables, 1976); this is the property that makes the set of stable matchings a lattice under the men's common order. The pages here prove the join and the opposition of interests in the marriage model with complete preference lists. The join also gives a proof of optimality that does not follow the algorithm: a stable matching minimising a potential is unchanged by the men's join with any stable matching, so it is man-optimal; the existence of some stable matching is taken from the Gale–Shapley theorem, whose proof runs deferred acceptance. The lattice axioms and the women's join (the same construction with the sides exchanged) are not formalized.

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.Matching.StableMatchingLattice.menJoinFun_injective

    If μ and ν are stable, no two distinct men prefer the same woman among their two partners.

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.Matching.StableMatchingLattice.isStable_menJoin

    Let μ and ν be stable perfect matchings. The perfect matching that gives every man the partner he prefers among μ(m) and ν(m) is stable.

    Proof: if a man m and a woman w block the join, let m' be her partner in the join, which is her partner in μ or in ν, say μ. Then m prefers w to his join partner, hence to μ(m), and w prefers m to m' = her μ-partner, so (m, w) blocks μ.

    This is the marriage case, with complete preference lists, of Roth (1986), Lemma 2, whose footnote attributes the property to Conway via Knuth (1976) (a secondary attribution; Knuth's book was not consulted).

    theorem isStable_menJoin {μ ν : PerfectMatching M W} (hμ : IsStable mp wp μ)
    (hν : IsStable mp wp ν) : IsStable mp wp (menJoin wp hμ hν)
    abbrev PerfectMatching (M W : Type*) := M ≃ W

    The man-optimal stable matching

    TheoremExistence of a man-optimal stable matching, through the men's join#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.Matching.StableMatchingLattice.exists_stable_forall_not_lt

    Let the men and the women form finite sets of the same size, with strict preferences. Some stable perfect matching μ is weakly preferred by every man to every stable perfect matching: for every stable ν and every man m, m does not prefer ν(m) to μ(m).

    This is Gale and Shapley's Theorem 2 and Roth's Theorem 2 (men's side). The sources prove it by showing that deferred acceptance never lets a man be rejected by a woman he could have in a stable matching. Here the optimality step does not follow the algorithm, while the existence of some stable matching is taken from the Gale–Shapley theorem, whose proof runs deferred acceptance: among the stable matchings take one, μ, that minimises the total number of women the men prefer to their partners. For any stable ν, the men's join of μ and ν is stable and weakly better for every man; if it were strictly better for one man, it would lower the total, so it equals μ, and no man prefers ν(m) to μ(m).

    theorem exists_stable_forall_not_lt (hCard : Fintype.card M = Fintype.card W) :
    ∃ μ : PerfectMatching M W, IsStable mp wp μ ∧
      ∀ ν, IsStable mp wp ν → ∀ m, ¬ (mp m).lt (ν m) (μ m)
    abbrev PerfectMatching (M W : Type*) := M ≃ W
