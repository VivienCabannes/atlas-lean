    Stable matching mechanisms and incentives

    A matching mechanism maps every stated preference profile of a marriage market to a perfect matching. Roth (1982) asks which mechanisms can at once be stable (always return a stable matching for the stated profile) and give agents the incentive to state their true preferences. His answers: no stable mechanism makes truth-telling a dominant strategy for every agent (Theorem 3, proved here), but the mechanism returning the man-optimal stable matching makes it dominant for every man (Theorem 5, wanted), and that matching is weakly Pareto optimal for the men among all matchings (Theorem 6, wanted). The model is Roth's: two sides of equal size, complete strict preferences, perfect matchings.

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.Matching.StableMechanism.MatchingMechanism

    A matching mechanism for men M and women W assigns a perfect matching between M and W to every stated profile of men's strict preferences over W and women's strict preferences over M. Roth calls it a matching procedure and identifies procedures that yield the same outcomes (Roth 1982, §4, remark after Corollary 5.1), which is why a mechanism is a function of the stated profile.

    DefinitionStrategy-proof matching mechanism#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.Matching.StableMechanism.IsStrategyProof

    A matching mechanism h is strategy-proof when truthful revelation is a dominant strategy for every man and every woman: for every stated profile and every agent, replacing that agent's preference by any other strict order never yields a partner that the agent prefers, under the original preference, to the partner obtained by stating it.

    Unlike a social choice function, a matching mechanism gives each agent a private outcome (a partner), and each agent judges outcomes by its preference over the other side.

    def IsStrategyProof [DecidableEq M] [DecidableEq W] (h : MatchingMechanism M W) : Prop :=
  (∀ m, IsTruthfulDominantForMan h m) ∧ ∀ w, IsTruthfulDominantForWoman h w
    abbrev MatchingMechanism (M W : Type*) := MPrefs M W → WPrefs M W → PerfectMatching M W

    Roth's impossibility theorem

    TheoremRoth's impossibility theorem#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.Matching.StableMechanism.not_exists_stable_strategyProof

    No stable matching mechanism makes truthful revelation a dominant strategy for all agents. Formally, in the market with three men and three women (Fin 3 on each side), there is no matching mechanism h such that h(P) is stable for every stated profile P and, for every man and every woman, no misreport ever yields a partner preferred under the agent's true preference.

    This is exactly Roth's Theorem 3, which Roth also proves by exhibiting one market: the theorem asserts that some market defeats every stable mechanism, not that every mechanism is manipulable at every profile. The proof is the one of the source: at Roth's profile, any stable mechanism gives either man m₁ or woman w₁ a profitable misreport. Larger markets are not treated here.

    The impossibility rests on stability: a constant mechanism is strategy-proof, and Roth's Theorem 4 (serial dictatorship) gives Pareto-efficient strategy-proof mechanisms.

    theorem not_exists_stable_strategyProof :
    ¬ ∃ h : MatchingMechanism (Fin 3) (Fin 3), IsStableMechanism h ∧ IsStrategyProof h

    TheoremIn Roth's market every stable mechanism is manipulable#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.Matching.StableMechanism.roth_stable_mechanism_manipulable

    Let h be a matching mechanism on three men and three women whose outcome is stable for every stated profile. At Roth's profile P, either man m₁ obtains a partner he truly prefers by reporting w₂ ≻ w₃ ≻ w₁, or woman w₁ obtains a partner she truly prefers by reporting m₁ ≻ m₂ ≻ m₃.

    Proof, following Roth. If h(P) matches m₁ with w₁, then m₁ reports w₂ ≻ w₃ ≻ w₁; the only stable matching of the resulting profile is x, which gives him w₂, his true first choice. Otherwise w₁ is not matched with m₁, her true first choice; she reports m₁ ≻ m₂ ≻ m₃, and the only stable matching of the resulting profile is y, which gives her m₁. Roth phrases the case split as h(P) = x or h(P) = y; the Lean proof splits on m₁'s partner directly and does not need the description of the stable set of P.

    theorem roth_stable_mechanism_manipulable (h : MatchingMechanism (Fin 3) (Fin 3))
    (hh : IsStableMechanism h) :
    (rothMenPrefs 0).lt (h (Function.update rothMenPrefs 0 rothManMisreport) rothWomenPrefs 0)
        (h rothMenPrefs rothWomenPrefs 0) ∨
      (rothWomenPrefs 0).lt
        ((h rothMenPrefs (Function.update rothWomenPrefs 0 rothWomanMisreport)).symm 0)
        ((h rothMenPrefs rothWomenPrefs).symm 0)

    MathlibExt.GameTheory.Matching.StableMechanismIncentivesWanted.isTruthfulDominantForMan_of_isManOptimalStable

    Let h be a matching mechanism that returns, for every stated profile, the man-optimal stable matching of that profile. Then truthful revelation is a dominant strategy for every man: no man can obtain a partner he truly prefers by misreporting, whatever the others state.

    This is the men's side of Roth's Theorem 5, which Roth states for either side; the women's side follows by exchanging the roles. Roth's footnote 4 notes that related conclusions were developed independently by Dubins and Freedman (their paper, Amer. Math. Monthly 88 (1981), was not consulted for this page). The statement is recorded as wanted: Roth's proof follows the men-proposing algorithm step by step (§5, Lemmas 1 and 2).

    The hypothesis is satisfiable for markets of every size n with n men and n women: deferred acceptance is such a mechanism.
