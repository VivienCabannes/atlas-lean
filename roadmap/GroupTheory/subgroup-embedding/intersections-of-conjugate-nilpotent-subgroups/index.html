    DefinitionTriple intersections with conjugates#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.MinimalIntersectionsFittingKourovka20Problem122Wanted.tripleIntersections

    For subgroups \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) of a group \(G\), this is the set of all subgroups of \(G\) of the form \(A \cap xBx^{-1} \cap yCy^{-1}\) with \(x, y \in G\), that is, the intersections of \(A\) with a conjugate of \(B\) and a conjugate of \(C\).

    def tripleIntersections {G : Type} [Group G] (A B C : Subgroup G) : Set (Subgroup G) :=
  {D | ∃ x y : G,
    D = A ⊓ B.map (MulAut.conj x).toMonoidHom ⊓ C.map (MulAut.conj y).toMonoidHom}

    DefinitionSubgroup generated by the triple intersections of least order#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.MinimalIntersectionsFittingKourovka20Problem122Wanted.minimalOrderIntersectionJoin

    For subgroups \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) of a group \(G\), the subgroup \(\min_G(A, B, C)\) is the subgroup of \(G\) generated by those intersections \(A \cap xBx^{-1} \cap yCy^{-1}\) (\(x, y \in G\)) whose order is at most the order of every such intersection.

    Orders are taken as natural numbers, so an infinite subgroup counts as having order \(0\); the notion is intended for finite \(G\).

    def minimalOrderIntersectionJoin {G : Type} [Group G] (A B C : Subgroup G) : Subgroup G :=
  sSup {D | D ∈ tripleIntersections A B C ∧
    ∀ E ∈ tripleIntersections A B C, Nat.card D ≤ Nat.card E}

    DefinitionNilpotent injector#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.NilpotentInjectorConjugatesKourovka19Problem37Wanted.IsNilpotentInjector

    A subgroup \(H\) of a group \(G\) is a nilpotent injector when, for every subnormal subgroup \(K\) of \(G\), the intersection \(H \cap K\) is nilpotent and is a maximal nilpotent subgroup of \(K\): every nilpotent subgroup \(L\) of \(G\) with \(H \cap K \le L \le K\) equals \(H \cap K\).

    def IsNilpotentInjector {G : Type*} [Group G] (H : Subgroup G) : Prop :=
  ∀ K : Subgroup G, K.IsSubnormal → Group.IsNilpotent ↥(H ⊓ K) ∧
    ∀ L : Subgroup G, L ≤ K → Group.IsNilpotent L → H ⊓ K ≤ L → L = H ⊓ K

    For every finite group \(G\) and every nilpotent injector \(H\) of \(G\) there are elements \(g_1, g_2, g_3 \in G\), not necessarily distinct, with \(g_1 H g_1^{-1} \cap g_2 H g_2^{-1} \cap g_3 H g_3^{-1} = F(G)\), where \(F(G)\) is the Fitting subgroup of \(G\).

    This answers Kourovka Problem 19.37(b) positively; it is due to Zenkov and uses the classification of finite simple groups, the soluble case being due to Passman and Mann.
