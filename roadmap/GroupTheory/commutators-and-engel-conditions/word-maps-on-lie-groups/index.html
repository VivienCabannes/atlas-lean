    TheoremSome nontrivial word map on \(\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})\) is not surjective (Kourovka 16.68(a))#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.WordMapsSurjectivityKourovka16Problem68Wanted.psl2RealWordMapsSurjective_refuted

    There is a nontrivial element \(w\) of the free group \(F_2\) on two generators whose word map \(\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R}) \times \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R}) \to \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})\), \((x, y) \mapsto w(x, y)\), is not surjective. That is, Mycielski's question has a negative answer for \(\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})\).

    Nilradical (Theorem 2) gives an explicit nontrivial word \(W\) (cyclically reduced length 44) with \(\operatorname{tr} W(A, B) > 7/4\) for all \(A, B \in \mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})\); a nonidentity involution of \(\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})\) lifts to a matrix of trace \(0\), so the word map of \(W\) misses it. The Kourovka Notebook (21st issue) records the negative answer for \(\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})\) (AI solution confirmed by A. Thom).
