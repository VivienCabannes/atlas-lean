    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.EngelElementLengthPolynomialKourovka11Problem88Wanted.engelComm

    For elements \(h\) and \(g\) of a group and a natural number \(l\), the iterated commutator \([h, g; l]\) is defined by \([h, g; 0] = h\) and \([h, g; l + 1] = [[h, g; l], g]\), where \([x, y] = x^{-1} y^{-1} x y\); in particular \([h, g; 1] = [h, g]\).

    def engelComm {G : Type*} [Group G] (h g : G) : ℕ → G
  | 0 => h
  | l + 1 => kcomm (engelComm h g l) g

    DefinitionEngel length#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.EngelElementLengthPolynomialKourovka11Problem88Wanted.engelLength

    For an element \(g\) of a group \(G\), the Engel length \(l(g)\) is the least \(k \ge 1\) such that \([h, g; k] = 1\) for every \(h \in G\), where \([h, g; 0] = h\), \([h, g; i + 1] = [[h, g; i], g]\) and \([x, y] = x^{-1} y^{-1} x y\). If no such \(k\) exists, that is, if \(g\) is not an Engel element, then \(l(g) = 0\).

    noncomputable def engelLength {G : Type*} [Group G] (g : G) : ℕ :=
  sInf {l | 1 ≤ l ∧ ∀ h : G, engelComm h g l = 1}
    def engelComm {G : Type*} [Group G] (h g : G) : ℕ → G
  | 0 => h
  | l + 1 => kcomm (engelComm h g l) g

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.EngelElementLengthPolynomialKourovka11Problem88Wanted.engelLengthPolynomialBound

    The statement that every group \(G\) has a polynomial \(\varphi(x, y)\) in two variables with natural-number coefficients such that \(l(uv) \le \varphi(l(u), l(v))\) for all \(u, v \in G\) for which \(u\), \(v\) and \(uv\) are Engel elements. Here \(g \in G\) is an Engel element if there is a \(k \ge 1\) with \([h, g; k] = 1\) for all \(h \in G\), and its Engel length \(l(g)\) is the least such \(k\), where \([h, g; 0] = h\), \([h, g; i + 1] = [[h, g; i], g]\) and \([x, y] = x^{-1} y^{-1} x y\).

    Natural coefficients lose nothing: on \(\mathbb{N}^2\) every real polynomial is bounded by one with natural coefficients.

    TheoremEngel lengths of products admit no polynomial bound (Kourovka 11.88)#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.EngelElementLengthPolynomialKourovka11Problem88Wanted.engelLengthPolynomialBound_refuted

    There is a group \(G\) such that for every polynomial \(\varphi(x, y)\) in two variables with natural-number coefficients there are \(u, v \in G\) such that \(u\), \(v\) and \(uv\) are Engel elements and \(l(uv) > \varphi(l(u), l(v))\). Here \(g \in G\) is an Engel element if there is a \(k \ge 1\) with \([h, g; k] = 1\) for all \(h \in G\), and its Engel length \(l(g)\) is the least such \(k\), where \([h, g; 0] = h\), \([h, g; i + 1] = [[h, g; i], g]\) and \([x, y] = x^{-1} y^{-1} x y\).

    For a prime \(p\), Dolbak builds finite \(p\)-groups \(G_n\) (wreath products of a cyclic group of order \(p\) by groups \(K_n\)) with elements \(c_n\) and \(c_n^{-1} b_0\) of Engel length at most \((n + 1)p - n + 1\) whose product \(b_0\) has Engel length at least \(p^n\). He takes the direct product of the \(G_n\) in the restricted sense (the direct sum, whose elements have finite support). It is a single Engel group: an element of finite support has Engel length at most the largest Engel length of its coordinates, and each \(G_n\) is a finite \(p\)-group, hence nilpotent. (The unrestricted product is not Engel: the element with \(b_0\) in every coordinate has unbounded Engel length.) Let \(u_n\) and \(v_n\) be \(c_n\) and \(c_n^{-1} b_0\) in coordinate \(n\) and \(1\) elsewhere: they are Engel elements of length at most \((n + 1)p - n + 1\), and \(u_n v_n\) has length at least \(p^n\), which exceeds every polynomial in the lengths of the factors (Theorem 1: there are Engel groups in which the Engel length of a product of two elements is bounded by no polynomial in the Engel lengths of the factors). The refutation of this statement uses only that \(u_n\), \(v_n\) and \(u_n v_n\) are Engel elements of one group. The Kourovka Archive answers 11.88 "No, it does not".

    DefinitionEngel element#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.EngelElementSimpleGroupKourovka16Problem17Wanted.IsEngelElement

    An element \(g\) of a group \(G\) is an Engel element if for every \(h \in G\) there is an \(n \ge 0\) with \(h_n = 1\), where \(h_0 = h\) and \(h_{i + 1} = [h_i, g]\) with \([x, g] = x^{-1} g^{-1} x g\); that is, some iterated commutator \([h, g, \dots, g]\) is trivial.

    def IsEngelElement {G : Type*} [Group G] (g : G) : Prop :=
  ∀ h : G, ∃ n : ℕ, (fun x => x⁻¹ * g⁻¹ * x * g)^[n] h = 1

    There is a non-abelian simple group \(G\) (one with elements \(a\), \(b\) such that \(ab \ne ba\)) that contains an Engel element \(g \ne 1\). Here \(g\) is an Engel element if for every \(h \in G\) some iterated commutator \([h, g, \dots, g]\) is trivial, where \([x, g] = x^{-1} g^{-1} x g\).

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.RightEngelPowerUpperCentralKourovka17Problem12Wanted.IsRightEngel

    For a natural number \(n\), an element \(a\) of a group \(G\) is a right \(n\)-Engel element when \([a, {}_n x] = 1\) for every \(x \in G\), where \([y, x] = y^{-1} x^{-1} y x\) and \([a, {}_n x] = [\dots[[a, x], x], \dots, x]\) is the left-normed commutator with \(n\) copies of \(x\) (so \([a, {}_0 x] = a\)).

    def IsRightEngel {G : Type*} [Group G] (n : ℕ) (a : G) : Prop :=
  ∀ x : G, (fun y => y⁻¹ * x⁻¹ * y * x)^[n] a = 1

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.RightEngelPowerUpperCentralKourovka17Problem12Wanted.rightEngelPowerUpperCentral_holds

    There are functions \(e, c \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\) with \(e(n) > 0\) for every \(n\) such that, for every natural number \(n\), every nilpotent group \(G\) and every normal subgroup \(H\) of \(G\) all of whose elements are right \(n\)-Engel elements of \(G\), the subgroup \(H^{e(n)}\) generated by the \(e(n)\)-th powers of elements of \(H\) is contained in the \(c(n)\)-th term \(\zeta_{c(n)}(G)\) of the upper central series of \(G\).

    Crosby and Traustason prove that there are positive integers \(e(n)\), \(c(n)\) such that \([H^{e(n)}, {}_{c(n)} G] = 1\), that is \(H^{e(n)} \le \zeta_{c(n)}(G)\), whenever \(H\) is a normal subgroup of \(G\) consisting of right \(n\)-Engel elements and lying in some term of the upper central series; in a nilpotent group every subgroup does. The Kourovka Archive answers 17.12 "Yes, there are".
