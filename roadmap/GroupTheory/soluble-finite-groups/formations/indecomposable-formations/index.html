    DefinitionFormation of finite groups (Kourovka 10.72)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.PGroupFormationIndecomposableKourovka10Problem72Wanted.IsFormation

    A class \(X\) of finite groups is a formation when it is closed under isomorphisms, contains the trivial group, contains the quotient \(G/N\) of each of its members \(G\) by every normal subgroup \(N\), and contains \(G/(N_1 \cap N_2)\) whenever \(N_1\) and \(N_2\) are normal subgroups of a finite group \(G\) with \(G/N_1\) and \(G/N_2\) in \(X\). The last condition is closure under subdirect products.

    def IsFormation (X : GroupClass) : Prop :=
  (∀ (G H : Type) [Group G] [Finite G] [Group H] [Finite H], (G ≃* H) → X G → X H) ∧ X Unit ∧
  (∀ (G : Type) [Group G] [Finite G] (N : Subgroup G) [N.Normal], X G → X (G ⧸ N)) ∧
  ∀ (G : Type) [Group G] [Finite G] (N₁ N₂ : Subgroup G) [N₁.Normal] [N₂.Normal],
    X (G ⧸ N₁) → X (G ⧸ N₂) → X (G ⧸ (N₁ ⊓ N₂))

    DefinitionResidual of a finite group with respect to a class (Kourovka 10.72)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.PGroupFormationIndecomposableKourovka10Problem72Wanted.residual

    For a class \(X\) of finite groups and a finite group \(G\), the \(X\)-residual of \(G\) is the intersection of all normal subgroups \(N\) of \(G\) with \(G/N \in X\). When \(X\) is a formation, it is the smallest such \(N\).

    DefinitionIndecomposability of the formation of finite \(p\)-groups (Kourovka 10.72)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.PGroupFormationIndecomposableKourovka10Problem72Wanted.pGroupFormationIndecomposable

    The statement that for every prime \(p\), the class \(\mathfrak{N}_p\) of all finite \(p\)-groups is not the Gaschütz product \(\mathfrak{F} \circ \mathfrak{G}\) of two formations \(\mathfrak{F}\) and \(\mathfrak{G}\) that consist of \(p\)-groups, each contain a nontrivial group and each omit some finite \(p\)-group. Here a finite group \(G\) lies in \(\mathfrak{F} \circ \mathfrak{G}\) when its \(\mathfrak{G}\)-residual lies in \(\mathfrak{F}\).

    Omitting some finite \(p\)-group is part of the reading of "non-trivial": for all formations \(\mathfrak{F}, \mathfrak{G}\) consisting of \(p\)-groups, \(\mathfrak{N}_p \circ \mathfrak{G} = \mathfrak{F} \circ \mathfrak{N}_p = \mathfrak{N}_p\).

    def pGroupFormationIndecomposable : Prop :=
  ∀ p : ℕ, p.Prime → ∀ F H : GroupClass, IsFormation F → IsFormation H →
    (∀ (G : Type) [Group G] [Finite G], F G → IsPGroup p G) →
    (∀ (G : Type) [Group G] [Finite G], H G → IsPGroup p G) →
    (∃ (G : Type) (_ : Group G) (_ : Finite G), Nontrivial G ∧ F G) →
    (∃ (G : Type) (_ : Group G) (_ : Finite G), Nontrivial G ∧ H G) →
    (∃ (G : Type) (_ : Group G) (_ : Finite G), IsPGroup p G ∧ ¬ F G) →
    (∃ (G : Type) (_ : Group G) (_ : Finite G), IsPGroup p G ∧ ¬ H G) →
    ¬ ∀ (G : Type) [Group G] [Finite G], IsPGroup p G ↔ F (residual H G)

    DefinitionGroups with a subnormal series whose factors are all isomorphic to a given group#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.SimpleCompositionFactorFormationKourovka12Problem97Wanted.AllCompositionFactorsIso

    For groups \(T\) and \(G\), the group \(G\) lies in the class \(\mathfrak{F}_T\) when it has a subnormal series \(1 = H_0 \le H_1 \le \dots \le H_n = G\), each \(H_i\) normal in \(H_{i+1}\), all of whose factors \(H_{i+1}/H_i\) are isomorphic to \(T\). For simple \(T\) these are the groups all of whose composition factors are isomorphic to \(T\).

    def AllCompositionFactorsIso (T : Type) [Group T] (G : Type) [Group G] : Prop :=
  ∃ (n : ℕ) (H : Fin (n + 1) → Subgroup G), H 0 = ⊥ ∧ H (Fin.last n) = ⊤ ∧
    ∀ i : Fin n, H i.castSucc ≤ H i.succ ∧
      ∃ _ : ((H i.castSucc).subgroupOf (H i.succ)).Normal,
        Nonempty ((H i.succ ⧸ (H i.castSucc).subgroupOf (H i.succ)) ≃* T)

    For a group \(T\), write \(\mathfrak{F}_T\) for the class of groups having a subnormal series all of whose factors are isomorphic to \(T\). In other words, \(\mathfrak{F}_T\) is not the Gaschütz product \(\mathfrak{M}\mathfrak{H} = \{G : G^{\mathfrak{H}} \in \mathfrak{M}\}\) of two nontrivial proper subformations.
