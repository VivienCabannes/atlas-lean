    StructureHereditary formations of finite soluble groups#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.HereditaryFormationShmidtLocalKourovka11Problem91Wanted.IsHereditarySolubleFormation

    A class \(\mathfrak F\) of groups is a hereditary formation of finite soluble groups if every group in \(\mathfrak F\) is finite and soluble; \(\mathfrak F\) is closed under isomorphisms, under taking subgroups and under taking quotients by normal subgroups; and for every finite group \(G\) with normal subgroups \(M\) and \(N\) such that \(G/M\) and \(G/N\) lie in \(\mathfrak F\), also \(G/(M \cap N)\) lies in \(\mathfrak F\).

    structure IsHereditarySolubleFormation (F : GroupClass) : Prop where
  finite_solvable : ∀ (G : Type) [Group G], F G → Finite G ∧ Group.IsSolvable G
  of_mulEquiv : ∀ (G H : Type) [Group G] [Group H], (G ≃* H) → F G → F H
  subgroup : ∀ (G : Type) [Group G] (H : Subgroup G), F G → F H
  quotient : ∀ (G : Type) [Group G] (N : Subgroup G) [N.Normal], F G → F (G ⧸ N)
  subdirect : ∀ (G : Type) [Group G] [Finite G] (M N : Subgroup G) [M.Normal] [N.Normal],
    F (G ⧸ M) → F (G ⧸ N) → F (G ⧸ (M ⊓ N))
    abbrev GroupClass : Type 1 := ∀ (G : Type) [Group G], Prop

    DefinitionSaturated classes of groups#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.HereditaryFormationShmidtLocalKourovka11Problem91Wanted.IsSaturated

    A class \(\mathfrak F\) of groups is saturated if every finite soluble group \(G\) such that \(G/\Phi(G)\) lies in \(\mathfrak F\), where \(\Phi(G)\) is the Frattini subgroup of \(G\), itself lies in \(\mathfrak F\).

    By the Gaschütz–Lubeseder theorem, a nonempty formation of finite soluble groups is saturated if and only if it is local.

    def IsSaturated (F : GroupClass) : Prop :=
  ∀ (G : Type) [Group G] [Finite G], Group.IsSolvable G → F (G ⧸ frattini G) → F G

    DefinitionShmidt groups#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.HereditaryFormationShmidtLocalKourovka11Problem91Wanted.IsShmidtGroup

    A group \(G\) is a Shmidt group if it is finite and not nilpotent, but every proper subgroup of \(G\) is nilpotent.

    def IsShmidtGroup (G : Type) [Group G] : Prop :=
  Finite G ∧ ¬ Group.IsNilpotent G ∧ ∀ H : Subgroup G, H ≠ ⊤ → Group.IsNilpotent H

    DefinitionKourovka Problem 11.91 (statement)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.HereditaryFormationShmidtLocalKourovka11Problem91Wanted.hereditaryFormationLocalOfShmidt

    The statement that every hereditary formation \(\mathfrak F\) of finite soluble groups such that every finite soluble non-simple minimal non-\(\mathfrak F\)-group (a group outside \(\mathfrak F\) all of whose proper subgroups lie in \(\mathfrak F\)) is a Shmidt group is saturated, that is, every finite soluble group \(G\) whose quotient \(G/\Phi(G)\) by its Frattini subgroup \(\Phi(G)\) lies in \(\mathfrak F\) itself lies in \(\mathfrak F\).

    Under this hypothesis \(\mathfrak F\) contains the trivial group, so by the Gaschütz–Lubeseder theorem saturated is the same as local, the property the problem (V. N. Semenchuk) asks for.

    def hereditaryFormationLocalOfShmidt : Prop :=
  ∀ F : GroupClass, IsHereditarySolubleFormation F →
    (∀ (G : Type) [Group G] [Finite G], Group.IsSolvable G → ¬ IsSimpleGroup G → IsMinimalNon F G →
      IsShmidtGroup G) →
    IsSaturated F
    abbrev GroupClass : Type 1 := ∀ (G : Type) [Group G], Prop

    TheoremSkiba's theorem (Kourovka Problem 11.91)#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.HereditaryFormationShmidtLocalKourovka11Problem91Wanted.hereditaryFormationLocalOfShmidt_holds

    Let \(\mathfrak F\) be a hereditary formation of finite soluble groups: every group in \(\mathfrak F\) is finite and soluble, \(\mathfrak F\) is closed under isomorphisms, subgroups and quotients, and whenever \(M\) and \(N\) are normal subgroups of a finite group \(G\) with \(G/M\) and \(G/N\) in \(\mathfrak F\), also \(G/(M \cap N)\) lies in \(\mathfrak F\). Suppose that every finite soluble group that is not simple and is a minimal non-\(\mathfrak F\)-group (it does not lie in \(\mathfrak F\), but all of its proper subgroups do) is a Shmidt group, that is, a finite non-nilpotent group all of whose proper subgroups are nilpotent. Then \(\mathfrak F\) is saturated: every finite soluble group \(G\) such that \(G/\Phi(G)\) lies in \(\mathfrak F\), where \(\Phi(G)\) is the Frattini subgroup of \(G\), itself lies in \(\mathfrak F\).

    The hypothesis forces the trivial group into \(\mathfrak F\), and by the Gaschütz–Lubeseder theorem a nonempty saturated formation of finite soluble groups is local, so \(\mathfrak F\) is local.
