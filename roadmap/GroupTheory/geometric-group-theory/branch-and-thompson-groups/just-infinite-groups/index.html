    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.JustInfiniteIntermediateGrowthBranchKourovka15Problem17Wanted.TreeVertex

    For a sequence \(m : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\) of branching indices, the vertices of the spherically homogeneous rooted tree with branching sequence \(m\) are the finite words \(x_0 x_1 \cdots x_{\ell-1}\) of natural numbers with \(x_i < m(i)\) for every \(i < \ell\). The length of a word is its level and its prefixes are its ancestors.

    def TreeVertex (m : ℕ → ℕ) : Type :=

    DefinitionRigid vertex stabilizer#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.JustInfiniteIntermediateGrowthBranchKourovka15Problem17Wanted.rigidVertexStabilizer

    Let \(\rho\) be a homomorphism from a group \(G\) to the group of permutations of the vertices of the rooted tree with branching sequence \(m\), and let \(v\) be a vertex. The rigid vertex stabilizer of \(v\) is the set of elements \(g \in G\) such that \(\rho(g)\) fixes every vertex that does not have \(v\) as a prefix, that is, the elements whose support (the set of vertices they move) lies in the subtree rooted at \(v\).

    def rigidVertexStabilizer {m : ℕ → ℕ} {G : Type*} [Group G] (ρ : G →* Equiv.Perm (TreeVertex m))
    (v : TreeVertex m) : Set G :=
  {g | ∀ w : TreeVertex m, ¬ v.1 <+: w.1 → ρ g w = w}
    def TreeVertex (m : ℕ → ℕ) : Type :=
  {l : List ℕ // ∀ (i : ℕ) (h : i < l.length), l[i] < m i}

    DefinitionBranch groups#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.JustInfiniteIntermediateGrowthBranchKourovka15Problem17Wanted.IsBranchGroup

    A group \(G\) is a branch group (Kourovka 15.12) if there are a sequence \(m : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\) with \(m(i) \ge 2\) for all \(i\) and a homomorphism \(\rho\) from \(G\) to the group of permutations of the vertices of the rooted tree with branching sequence \(m\) such that \(\rho\) is injective; every \(\rho(g)\) is a tree automorphism, that is, for all vertices \(v\) and \(w\), \(v\) is a prefix of \(w\) if and only if \(\rho(g)(v)\) is a prefix of \(\rho(g)(w)\); the action is level-transitive, that is, for any two vertices \(v\) and \(w\) of the same level there is \(g \in G\) with \(\rho(g)(v) = w\); and for every \(n\) the \(n\)-th rigid level stabilizer has finite index in \(G\).

    def IsBranchGroup (G : Type*) [Group G] : Prop :=
  ∃ (m : ℕ → ℕ) (_ : ∀ i, 2 ≤ m i) (ρ : G →* Equiv.Perm (TreeVertex m)),
    Function.Injective ρ ∧
    (∀ (g : G) (v w : TreeVertex m), v.1 <+: w.1 ↔ (ρ g v).1 <+: (ρ g w).1) ∧
    (∀ v w : TreeVertex m, v.1.length = w.1.length → ∃ g : G, ρ g v = w) ∧
    ∀ n : ℕ, (rigidLevelStabilizer ρ n).FiniteIndex

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.JustInfiniteIntermediateGrowthBranchKourovka15Problem17Wanted.growthRate

    Let \(P\) be a generating family of a group \(G\) indexed by \(\{0, \dots, k-1\}\), and let \(\beta_P(n)\) be the number of elements of \(G\) whose word length with respect to \(P\) is at most \(n\), that is, which are products of at most \(n\) members of \(P\) and their inverses. The growth rate of \(P\) is the real number \(\omega(G, P) = \limsup_{n \to \infty} \beta_P(n)^{1/n}\).

    TheoremJust infinite groups of intermediate growth need not be branch groups#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.JustInfiniteIntermediateGrowthBranchKourovka15Problem17Wanted.justInfiniteIntermediateGrowthIsBranch_refuted

    It is not the case that every finitely generated just infinite group of intermediate growth is a branch group: the Kourovka Archive answers Problem 15.17 "No, not every".

    Nekrashevych constructed infinite finitely generated simple groups of intermediate growth. Such a group is just infinite, and it is not a branch group: its rigid level stabilizers, nontrivial normal subgroups of finite index, would be the whole group, which they split as a direct product of at least two nontrivial factors.
