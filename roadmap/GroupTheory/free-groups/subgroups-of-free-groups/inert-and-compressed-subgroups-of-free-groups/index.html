    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.EndomorphismFixedSubgroupInertKourovka19Problem105Wanted.endomorphismFixedSubgroupInert_holds

    Let \(n \in \mathbb{N}\), let \(F_n\) be the free group on \(n\) generators, and for a subgroup \(H \le F_n\) let \(r(H)\) be its rank, the least size of a finite generating set of \(H\), or \(\infty\) if \(H\) is not finitely generated.     TheoremInertness is decidable in free groups of finite rank (Kourovka Problem 19.104)#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.GroupTheory.InertSubgroupDecidableKourovka19Problem104Wanted.inertSubgroupDecidable_holds

    For every natural number \(n\), let \(F_n\) be the free group on generators \(x_0, \dots, x_{n-1}\), and give a finitely generated subgroup of \(F_n\) by a finite list of generating words in the letters \(x_i^{\pm 1}\). Then there is an algorithm deciding, for such a list, whether the subgroup \(H\) it generates is inert, that is, whether \(r(H \cap K) \le r(K)\) for every subgroup \(K\) of \(F_n\). Here the rank \(r(L)\) of a subgroup \(L\) is the least size of a finite generating set of \(L\), and \(r(L) = \infty\) when \(L\) is not finitely generated; "an algorithm" means that the predicate on lists of words is computable.

    Jaikin-Zapirain proved that a finitely generated subgroup of a free group is inert if and only if it is compressed, and compressedness is known to be decidable.
