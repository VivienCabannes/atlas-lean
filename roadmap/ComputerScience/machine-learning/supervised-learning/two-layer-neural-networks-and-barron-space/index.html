    Two-layer neural networks and Barron space

    Two-layer ReLU networks \(f(x) = \sum_k a_k \max(\langle w_k, x \rangle, 0)\), measured by the path norm \(\sum_k \lvert a_k \rvert \lVert w_k \rVert_1\) rather than by their width, in the approach of W. E, C. Ma and L. Wu: the Barron space is the function space naturally attached to these networks, on which approximation and estimation errors scale like Monte Carlo rates, \(m^{-1/2}\) in the width and \(n^{-1/2}\) in the sample size, independently of the input dimension \(d\). The empirical Rademacher complexity of a path-norm ball is at most \(2 Q \sqrt{2 \log(2d) / n}\), proved here from the Rademacher calculus (homogeneity, contraction and Massart's lemma), with and without a bias term; with a bias the proof gives \(\log(2(d+1))\). The direct approximation theorem, approximation of every Barron function by width-\(m\) networks with squared error \(3 \lVert f \rVert_B^2 / m\) and path norm \(2 \lVert f \rVert_B\), is proved by Monte Carlo sampling of the neurons; the inverse approximation theorem, which together with it identifies the Barron space with the functions approximable by networks of bounded path norm, and the a priori estimate of the population risk of the path-norm regularized estimator, which combines approximation and estimation into a width-independent \(O(n^{-1/2})\) bound, are recorded as wanted statements.

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.NeuralNetworks.IsBarronRepresentation

    A measure ρ on the parameters (a, b, c) ∈ ℝ × ℝ^d × ℝ represents f on the cube when ρ is a probability measure and, for every x ∈ [0, 1]^d, the neuron output is ρ-integrable with f(x) = ∫ a σ(⟨b, x⟩ + c) dρ (E, Ma and Wu, The Barron space and the flow-induced function spaces for neural network models, equation (1)).

    DefinitionBarron norm#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.NeuralNetworks.barronNorm

    For \(f : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}\), the Barron norm is

    \[\lVert f \rVert_B = \inf_\rho \Big( \mathbb{E}_{(a, b, c) \sim \rho}\, \lvert a \rvert^2 (\lVert b \rVert_1 + \lvert c \rvert)^2 \Big)^{1/2} \in [0, \infty],\]

    the infimum over the Barron representations \(\rho\) of \(f\) on the cube \([0, 1]^d\), that is, the probability measures on \(\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}\) with \(f(x) = \mathbb{E}_\rho[a \max(\langle b, x \rangle + c, 0)]\) for every \(x\) in the cube; it is \(\infty\) when there is no representation. This is the \(p = 2\) case of the norms \(\lVert f \rVert_{B_p} = \inf_\rho (\mathbb{E}_\rho \lvert a \rvert^p (\lVert b \rVert_1 + \lvert c \rvert)^p)^{1/p}\) of the source; for the ReLU all \(p \in [1, \infty]\) give the same value (the source's Proposition 1, not formalized here). The Barron space is the set of functions of finite Barron norm.

    noncomputable def barronNorm (f : (Fin d → ℝ) → ℝ) : ENNReal :=
  ⨅ (ρ : Measure (ℝ × (Fin d → ℝ) × ℝ)) (_ : IsBarronRepresentation f ρ),
    (∫⁻ θ, ‖θ.1‖ₑ ^ 2 * (∑ j, ‖θ.2.1 j‖ₑ + ‖θ.2.2‖ₑ) ^ 2 ∂ρ) ^ (1 / 2 : ℝ)

    TheoremDirect approximation theorem for the Barron space#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.NeuralNetworks.barron_direct_approximation

    Let \(f\) have finite Barron norm \(\lVert f \rVert_B\), let \(\mu\) be a probability measure carried by the cube \([0, 1]^d\), and let \(m \ge 1\). Then there are neuron parameters \((a_k, b_k, c_k) \in \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}\), \(k = 1, \dots, m\), such that the two-layer network \(f_m(x) = \frac1m \sum_{k=1}^m a_k \max(\langle b_k, x \rangle + c_k, 0)\) satisfies

    \[\lVert f - f_m \rVert_{L^2(\mu)}^2 \le \frac{3 \lVert f \rVert_B^2}{m} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \frac1m \sum_{k=1}^m \lvert a_k \rvert (\lVert b_k \rVert_1 + \lvert c_k \rvert) \le 2 \lVert f \rVert_B.\]

    The rate \(m^{-1/2}\) is the Monte Carlo rate and does not depend on \(d\), and the path norm of the approximant is controlled independently of the width.

    Proof (as in the source, with one simplification): take a representation \(\rho\) of \(f\) whose second moment \(M_2 = \mathbb{E}_\rho[a^2 (\lVert b \rVert_1 + \lvert c \rvert)^2]\) is below \(1.21 \lVert f \rVert_B^2\), and sample the \(m\) neurons i.i.d. from \(\rho\). For each \(x\) in the cube the expected squared error is the variance of an i.i.d. sample mean, at most \(M_2 / m\), and Tonelli's theorem integrates this over \(\mu\); the expected path norm is \(\mathbb{E}_\rho \lvert a \rvert (\lVert b \rVert_1 + \lvert c \rvert) \le \sqrt{M_2}\) by Cauchy–Schwarz. The source applies Markov's inequality to the two quantities separately; the formal proof instead averages the single functional \(\mathrm{error} / (3 \lVert f \rVert_B^2 / m) + \mathrm{path\ norm} / (2 \lVert f \rVert_B)\), whose mean is below \(1.21/3 + 1.1/2 < 1\), so that some sample makes it smaller than \(1\). A function of Barron norm \(0\) vanishes on the cube and is approximated by the zero network.

    theorem barron_direct_approximation {d : ℕ} (f : (Fin d → ℝ) → ℝ)
    (hf : barronNorm f < ⊤) (μ : Measure (Fin d → ℝ)) [IsProbabilityMeasure μ]
    (hμ : μ (unitCube d)ᶜ = 0) {m : ℕ} (hm : 0 < m) :
    ∃ θ : Fin m → ℝ × (Fin d → ℝ) × ℝ,
      ∫⁻ x, ‖f x - (m : ℝ)⁻¹ * ∑ k, reluNeuron (θ k) x‖ₑ ^ 2 ∂μ ≤
          3 * barronNorm f ^ 2 / m ∧
        (m : ENNReal)⁻¹ * ∑ k, ‖(θ k).1‖ₑ * (∑ j, ‖(θ k).2.1 j‖ₑ + ‖(θ k).2.2‖ₑ) ≤
          2 * barronNorm f

    TheoremInverse approximation theorem for the Barron space#●ready to prove

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.NeuralNetworks.barron_inverse_approximation

    Let \(f\) be continuous on the cube \([0, 1]^d\) and let \(Q \in \mathbb{R}\). Suppose that two-layer ReLU networks \(f_k(x) = \frac{1}{w_k} \sum_{i=1}^{w_k} a_i \max(\langle b_i, x \rangle + c_i, 0)\) of widths \(w_k \ge 1\), each with path norm \(\frac{1}{w_k} \sum_i \lvert a_i \rvert (\lVert b_i \rVert_1 + \lvert c_i \rvert) \le Q\), converge to \(f\) at every point of the cube. Then \(f\) admits a Barron representation on the cube, and \(\lVert f \rVert_B \le Q\).

    Together with the direct approximation theorem, this characterizes the Barron space as the set of functions approximable by two-layer networks with bounded path norm. Proof in the source: after normalizing \(\lVert b \rVert_1 + \lvert c \rvert = 1\) by homogeneity of the ReLU, each \(f_k\) is represented by a probability measure supported in the compact set \(\{\lvert a \rvert \le Q, \lVert b \rVert_1 + \lvert c \rvert \le 1\}\); Prokhorov's theorem gives a weakly convergent subsequence, whose limit represents \(f\).

    This is a wanted statement: the proof is not formalized.

    TheoremRademacher complexity of path-norm balls of two-layer ReLU networks#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.rademacherComplexity_pathNormBallValues_le

    Let \(d \ge 1\), let \(x_1, \dots, x_n \in \mathbb{R}^d\) have every coordinate in \([-1, 1]\), and let \(Q \ge 0\). The set \(A_Q \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n\) of value vectors \((f(x_1), \dots, f(x_n))\) of the two-layer ReLU networks \(f(x) = \sum_k a_k \max(\langle w_k, x \rangle, 0)\), of any width, with path norm \(\sum_k \lvert a_k \rvert \lVert w_k \rVert_1 \le Q\), has empirical Rademacher complexity

    \[R(A_Q) = \frac1n \mathbb{E}_\sigma \sup_{a \in A_Q} \sum_i \sigma_i a_i \le 2 Q \sqrt{\frac{2 \log(2d)}{n}}.\]

    The bound does not depend on the width of the networks, and depends on the dimension only through \(\log(2d)\). Proof: by positive homogeneity of the ReLU, each network is a combination of single neurons with \(\lVert u \rVert_1 \le 1\) with total weight at most \(Q\), and the two signs of the outer weights cost the factor \(2\) (step 1); the contraction lemma removes the ReLU (step 2); a linear functional over the \(\ell^1\) unit ball is at most its largest value at the \(2d\) vectors \(\pm e_j\) (step 3); and Massart's lemma bounds the complexity of these \(2d\) vectors, each of Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt n\) on the sample (step 4).

    theorem rademacherComplexity_pathNormBallValues_le (hd : 0 < d) {x : Fin n → Fin d → ℝ}
    (hx : ∀ i j, |x i j| ≤ 1) {Q : ℝ} (hQ : 0 ≤ Q) :
    rademacherComplexity (pathNormBallValues x Q) ≤ 2 * Q * √(2 * log (2 * d) / n)
    noncomputable def rademacherComplexity (A : Set (Fin n → ℝ)) : ℝ :=
  (n : ℝ)⁻¹ * (((2 : ℝ) ^ n)⁻¹ * ∑ ε : Fin n → Bool, supCorr A ε)

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.twoLayer_a_priori_estimate

    There is an absolute constant \(C\) such that the following holds. Let \(\log(2d) \ge 1\), \(n \ge 1\) and \(m \ge 1\); let \(\rho_x\) be a probability measure carried by \(X = [-1, 1]^d\); let \(f^*\) be measurable with values in \([0, 1]\) on \(X\) and \(f^*(x) = \int a(w) \max(\langle w, x \rangle, 0) \, d\pi(w)\) on \(X\), for a probability measure \(\pi\) carried by the \(\ell^1\) unit sphere and a function \(a\) with \(\int a^2 \, d\pi \le \gamma^2\), \(\gamma \ge 0\). Let \(\lambda \ge 4 \sqrt{2 \log(2d) / n}\) and \(\delta > 0\). Then with probability at least \(1 - \delta\) over an i.i.d. sample \(x_1, \dots, x_n \sim \rho_x\), every minimizer \(\hat\theta\) over width-\(m\) networks \(f(x; \theta) = \sum_k a_k \max(\langle w_k, x \rangle, 0)\) of the regularized empirical risk

    \[J_\lambda(\theta) = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^n \tfrac12 \big(T f(x_i; \theta) - f^*(x_i)\big)^2 + \lambda \big(\lVert \theta \rVert_P + 1\big), \qquad T t = \max(\min(t, 1), 0),\]

    satisfies

    \[\mathbb{E}_{x \sim \rho_x} \big|T f(x; \hat\theta) - f^*(x)\big|^2 \le C \Big( \frac{\gamma^2}{m} + \lambda \max(1, \gamma) + \frac{\max(1, \gamma) + \sqrt{\log(n / \delta)}}{\sqrt n} \Big).\]

    Taking the infimum over representations gives the source's form with \(\gamma_2(f^*)\), the Barron norm of \(f^*\) in the source's normalization (no bias, data in \([-1, 1]^d\)). The bound is a priori (it involves only the norm of the target), and its estimation part does not depend on the width: with \(\lambda \asymp \lambda_n\) and \(m \ge \sqrt n\) it is \(O(n^{-1/2})\) up to logarithms, in the over-parametrized regime included. Measurability of \(f^*\) is an added regularity assumption (on \(X\) the representation already makes \(f^*\) continuous). As in the Rademacher risk bound, "with probability at least \(1 - \delta\)" is stated with the outer measure of the product measure, since the event need not be measurable.

    Related topics

    • Rademacher complexity: the calculus (scaling, contraction, Massart's finite-class lemma) from which the bound for path-norm balls is assembled, and the risk bound that turns it into a generalization guarantee.
    • Generalization bounds and concentration: the dimension-free bound for balls of linear predictors in a Hilbert space, the \(\ell^2\) counterpart of the \(\ell^1\) geometry of the path norm.
    • Universal approximation theorem: the qualitative density of two-layer networks, which gives no rate; the Barron space is where a dimension-free rate holds.
    • Positive-definite kernels: random-feature and kernel models fix the inner weights; E, Ma and Wu describe the Barron space as the union of the reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces of the kernels \(k_\pi(x, x') = \mathbb{E}_{w \sim \pi} \sigma(\langle w, x \rangle) \sigma(\langle w, x' \rangle)\).
    • Backpropagation: the gradient computation used to train these networks.
