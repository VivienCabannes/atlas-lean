    Let \((\Omega, \mu)\) be a probability space, with expectation \(\mathbb{E}\) and variance \(\operatorname{Var}\) taken with respect to \(\mu\). If \(Y\) and \(\hat Y\) are independent square-integrable real random variables on \(\Omega\), then \(\mathbb{E}[(Y - \hat Y)^2] = \operatorname{Var}(Y) + \operatorname{Var}(\hat Y) + (\mathbb{E}[Y] - \mathbb{E}[\hat Y])^2\).

    Let \((\Omega, \mu)\) be a probability space, with expectation \(\mathbb{E}\) and variance \(\operatorname{Var}\) taken with respect to \(\mu\). Let \(f\) be a real number, and let \(\varepsilon\) (the noise) and \(\hat Y\) (the predictor) be square-integrable real random variables on \(\Omega\) such that \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon] = 0\) and \(\varepsilon\) is independent of \(\hat Y\). Then the expected squared error of \(\hat Y\) for the response \(\varepsilon + f\) is the noise variance plus the squared bias plus the variance of the predictor: \(\mathbb{E}[(\varepsilon + f - \hat Y)^2] = \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon) + (f - \mathbb{E}[\hat Y])^2 + \operatorname{Var}(\hat Y)\).
