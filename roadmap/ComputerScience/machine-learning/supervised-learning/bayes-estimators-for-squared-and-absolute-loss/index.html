    TheoremThe mean minimizes the expected squared loss#✓fully proved

    Cslib.MachineLearning.OptimalPredictors.lintegral_sqLoss_integral_le

    Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\mathbb{R}\) with mean \(m = \int t \, d\nu(t)\) (taken to be \(0\) when \(\nu\) has no finite first moment). Then for every \(y \in \mathbb{R}\),

    \[\int (\theta - m)^2 \, d\nu(\theta) \le \int (\theta - y)^2 \, d\nu(\theta),\]

    where both integrals are taken in \([0, \infty]\).

    When the right side is infinite the inequality is trivial; otherwise \(\nu\) has a second moment and the variance decomposition applies.

    theorem lintegral_sqLoss_integral_le (ν : Measure ℝ) [IsProbabilityMeasure ν] (y : ℝ) :
    ∫⁻ θ, sqLoss θ (∫ t, t ∂ν) ∂ν ≤ ∫⁻ θ, sqLoss θ y ∂ν

    DefinitionPosterior mean#✓fully proved

    Cslib.MachineLearning.OptimalPredictors.posteriorMean

    Let \(P\) be a finite kernel from \(\mathbb{R}\) to a measurable space \(\mathcal{X}\), let \(\pi\) be a finite measure on \(\mathbb{R}\), and write \(P^\dagger\pi\) for the posterior kernel from \(\mathcal{X}\) to \(\mathbb{R}\). The posterior mean is the function on \(\mathcal{X}\) sending \(x\) to \(\int \theta \, d(P^\dagger\pi)(x)(\theta)\), the mean of the posterior distribution given the observation \(x\) (taken to be \(0\) when that distribution has no finite first moment).

    Cslib.MachineLearning.OptimalPredictors.IsPosteriorMean

    Let \(P\) be a finite kernel from \(\mathbb{R}\) to a measurable space \(\mathcal{X}\), sending \(\theta\) to the measure \(P_\theta\), let \(\pi\) be a finite measure on \(\mathbb{R}\), and write \(P^\dagger\pi\) for the posterior kernel from \(\mathcal{X}\) to \(\mathbb{R}\).
