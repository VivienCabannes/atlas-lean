    Natural policy gradient

    Softmax policies and the natural policy gradient iteration, which for softmax policies is multiplicative weights on the advantages in every state, with its improvement lower bound and its dimension-free global convergence rate (Agarwal, Kakade, Lee and Mahajan 2021, Section 5.3).

    Natural policy gradient

    DefinitionNatural policy gradient iterates#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.npgLogits

    Let \(M\) be a Markov decision process with a finite set of states \(S\), a finite set of actions \(A\), real rewards \(r(s,a)\) and transition probabilities \(P(s,a,s')\) that are nonnegative and sum to \(1\) over \(s'\), and let \(0 \le \gamma < 1\). For a step size \(\eta\), natural policy gradient with softmax policies starts from \(\theta_0 = 0\) and updates

    \[\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t + \frac{\eta}{1-\gamma} A^{\pi_{\theta_t}},\]

    equivalently \(\pi_{t+1}(a \mid s) \propto \pi_t(a \mid s) \exp\bigl(\eta A^{\pi_t}(s,a)/(1-\gamma)\bigr)\): multiplicative weights in every state, with the advantages as gains, as in the Hedge algorithm. This is the closed form that the Fisher-preconditioned update \(\theta \leftarrow \theta + \eta F(\theta)^\dagger \nabla V^{\pi_\theta}\) takes for softmax policies on the states reachable from the start distribution \(\rho\) (AKLM, Lemma 5.1 and footnote 6), up to a state-dependent offset that the softmax ignores (AJKS Lemma 12.6). Here the soft policy iteration is applied at every state; the derivation from the Fisher update is not formalized, and the iteration is defined by its closed form.

    noncomputable def npgLogits (η : ℝ) : ℕ → S → A → ℝ
  | 0 => 0
  | t + 1 => fun s a =>
      npgLogits η t s a + η / (1 - γ) * advantage hM hγ0 hγ1 (softmax (npgLogits η t)) s a
    noncomputable def advantage (π : StochasticPolicy S A) (s : S) (a : A) : ℝ :=
  stochQValue hM hγ0 hγ1 π s a - stochValue M γ hM hγ0 hγ1 π s

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.optimalValue_sub_stochValue_npgPolicy_le

    Let \(M\) be a Markov decision process with a finite set of states \(S\), a finite set of actions \(A\), real rewards \(r(s,a)\) and transition probabilities \(P(s,a,s')\) that are nonnegative and sum to \(1\) over \(s'\), and let \(0 \le \gamma < 1\). Assume all rewards lie in \([0, 1]\) and \(A\) is nonempty, and run natural policy gradient with softmax policies from \(\theta_0 = 0\) with step size \(\eta > 0\). For every \(T \ge 1\) and every state \(s_0\),

    \[V^*(s_0) - V^{\pi_T}(s_0) \le \frac{\log |A|}{\eta T} + \frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^2 T}.\]

    Averaging over a start distribution \(\rho\) gives the published form \(V^{\pi_T}(\rho) \ge V^*(\rho) - \log|A|/(\eta T) - 1/((1-\gamma)^2 T)\). The rate depends on neither the number of states nor a distribution mismatch coefficient, although \(V^{\pi_\theta}\) is not concave in \(\theta\). The proof combines the performance difference lemma against an optimal deterministic policy with the regret analysis of multiplicative weights, as for the Hedge algorithm, run in every state.
