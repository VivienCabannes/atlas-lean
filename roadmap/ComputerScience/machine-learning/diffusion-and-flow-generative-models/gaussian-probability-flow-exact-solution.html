    Exact probability-flow solution for Gaussian data

    Setting (variance-exploding, as in Wang and Vastola, who follow the EDM formulation): data \(X_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)\) in \(\mathbb{R}^D\) with \(\Sigma\) real symmetric positive semidefinite, possibly singular, and the continuous densities \(p(\cdot\,; \sigma)\) of the laws of \(X_0 + \sigma \varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\) independent of \(X_0\). Let \(J\) be a closed bounded interval and let \(t \mapsto \sigma_t\) be continuously differentiable on \(J\) with \(\sigma_t > 0\) for every \(t \in J\), endpoints included; \(\sigma\) need not be monotone. Write \(C_t = \Sigma + \sigma_t^2 I\), which is positive definite. With the score of the Gaussian marginal (first page under Informally uses), \(\nabla \log p(x; \sigma_t) = -C_t^{-1} (x - \mu)\), the probability-flow ODE \(\dot{x} = -\dot{\sigma}_t \sigma_t \nabla \log p(x; \sigma_t)\) becomes the linear system

    \[\dot{x}(t) = A(t) \bigl(x(t) - \mu\bigr), \qquad A(t) = \dot{\sigma}_t \sigma_t C_t^{-1}.\]

    \(A\) is continuous on \(J\), and the matrices \(A(t)\), being functions of \(\Sigma\), commute with one another.

    Statement: for all \(s, t \in J\) and \(x \in \mathbb{R}^D\), the unique solution on \(J\) with \(x(s) = x\) takes at time \(t\) the value

    \[X_{s,t}(x) = \mu + C_t^{1/2} C_s^{-1/2} (x - \mu),\]

    with positive definite square roots. In a real orthonormal eigenbasis \(u_1, \dots, u_D\) of \(\Sigma\), with \(\Sigma u_k = \lambda_k u_k\) and \(\lambda_k \ge 0\), zero eigenvalues included,

    \[X_{s,t}(x) = \mu + \sum_{k=1}^{D} \psi_k(s, t) \langle u_k, x - \mu \rangle u_k, \qquad \psi_k(s, t) = \sqrt{\frac{\lambda_k + \sigma_t^2}{\lambda_k + \sigma_s^2}},\]

    and a direction with \(\lambda_k = 0\) has the factor \(\sigma_t / \sigma_s\). The formula covers both orientations, \(t < s\) and \(t > s\); when \(\sigma\) increases with \(t\), sampling runs from \(s\) down to \(t < s\), as in Wang and Vastola's backward integration from a large noise level. With Wang and Vastola's initial level \(\sigma_T\) in the role of \(\sigma_s\), it is their closed-form solution.

    Proof: in the eigenbasis, \(c_k(t) = \langle u_k, x(t) - \mu \rangle\) satisfies \(\dot{c}_k = \frac{\dot{\sigma}_t \sigma_t}{\lambda_k + \sigma_t^2} c_k = \tfrac{1}{2} \frac{d}{dt} \log(\lambda_k + \sigma_t^2) \, c_k\), so \(c_k(t) = \psi_k(s, t) c_k(s)\); equivalently, \(\frac{d}{dt} C_t^{1/2} = \tfrac{1}{2} \dot{C}_t C_t^{-1/2}\) because the \(C_t\) commute, which shows directly that \(X_{s,t}(x)\) solves the system. Uniqueness: extend \(A\) continuously from \(J\) to \(\mathbb{R}\), for instance by the constant values \(A(a)\) before the left endpoint \(a\) and \(A(b)\) after the right endpoint \(b\). A solution on \(J\) continues, by solving the extended system from \(a\) and from \(b\), to a solution of the extended system on \(\mathbb{R}\); the one-sided derivatives agree at \(a\) and \(b\) because \(A\) is continuous. The fundamental-matrix theorem, applied to \(x - \mu\), gives uniqueness on \(\mathbb{R}\), hence on \(J\).

    Singular covariance: if \(\Sigma\) is singular, the noise level \(0\) is only a limit. In a direction with \(\lambda_k = 0\) the coefficient is \(\dot{\sigma}_t / \sigma_t\), which is not integrable as \(\sigma_t \to 0^+\), so \(\sigma = 0\) is outside the domain of the system and the uniqueness argument does not apply there. As \(\sigma_t \to 0^+\) with \(s\) fixed, \(X_{s,t}(x)\) converges to \(\mu + \sum_{\lambda_k > 0} \sqrt{\lambda_k / (\lambda_k + \sigma_s^2)} \langle u_k, x - \mu \rangle u_k\), Wang and Vastola's noise-to-sample map. This limit map has rank \(\operatorname{rank} \Sigma\), so it is not invertible when \(\Sigma\) is singular, and it carries \(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma + \sigma_s^2 I)\) to the degenerate \(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)\). Solutions starting at noise level \(0\) are not unique: for \(\sigma_t = t\) the equation \(\dot{c} = c / t\) of a \(\lambda_k = 0\) direction has the solutions \(c(t) = K t\) for every \(K\), all with limit \(0\) at \(t = 0\). If \(\Sigma\) is positive definite, \(A\) stays continuous where \(\sigma_t = 0\), and such an endpoint is regular.

    Affine noising paths (derived): let \(X_t = \alpha_t X_0 + \sigma_t \varepsilon\) with \(\alpha\) and \(\sigma\) continuously differentiable on \(J\), and suppose \(C_t = \alpha_t^2 \Sigma + \sigma_t^2 I\) is positive definite for every \(t \in J\), as it is when \(\sigma_t > 0\) on \(J\). The variance-exploding setting above is \(\alpha \equiv 1\); a variance-preserving schedule has \(\alpha_t^2 + \sigma_t^2 = 1\). The marginal velocity \(v(z, t) = \mathbb{E}[\dot{\alpha}_t X_0 + \dot{\sigma}_t \varepsilon \mid X_t = z]\) is \(v(z, t) = \dot{\alpha}_t \mu + \tfrac{1}{2} \dot{C}_t C_t^{-1} (z - \alpha_t \mu)\), and the flow of \(\dot{z} = v(z, t)\) is \(X_{s,t}(z) = \alpha_t \mu + C_t^{1/2} C_s^{-1/2} (z - \alpha_s \mu)\), with spectral factors \(\sqrt{(\alpha_t^2 \lambda_k + \sigma_t^2) / (\alpha_s^2 \lambda_k + \sigma_s^2)}\). For a variance-preserving schedule with \(\alpha_t > 0\), \(v\) is the Gaussian-score probability-flow ODE of Wang and Vastola's appendix on the VP formulation, and for \(\alpha > 0\) the flow is their scaled solution, which they obtain by substituting \(x_t \mapsto x_t / \alpha_t\) and \(\sigma_t \mapsto \sigma_t / \alpha_t\). The formula here also covers a time where \(\alpha_t = 0\), the pure-noise end of a path such as \(\alpha_t = 1 - t\), \(\sigma_t = t\) at \(t = 1\), where that substitution is undefined.

    Further properties (derived), for \(s, t, \tau \in J\) in the variance-exploding setting:

    • (i) Composition: \(C_\tau^{1/2} C_t^{-1/2} C_t^{1/2} C_s^{-1/2} = C_\tau^{1/2} C_s^{-1/2}\), so \(X_{t,\tau} \circ X_{s,t} = X_{s,\tau}\) and \(X_{t,s}\) is the inverse of \(X_{s,t}\); in the eigenbasis, \(\psi_k(t, \tau) \psi_k(s, t) = \psi_k(s, \tau)\). This illustrates the composition property of flow maps in the indexing of Boffi, Albergo and Vanden-Eijnden, where \(X_{s,t}\) sends the state at time \(s\) to the state at time \(t\); their framework is stated for interpolant time in \([0, 1]\), and the Gaussian example here is derived, not taken from their paper.
    • (ii) Transport of marginals: with \(\Phi = C_t^{1/2} C_s^{-1/2}\), which is symmetric, \(\Phi C_s \Phi^{\mathsf{T}} = C_t\), so \(X_{s,t}\) carries \(\mathcal{N}(\mu, C_s)\) to \(\mathcal{N}(\mu, C_t)\): centre at \(\mu\), apply the linear map \(\Phi\), and translate back.
    • (iii) Volume: \(\det \partial X_{s,t} = \det \Phi = \prod_{k=1}^{D} \psi_k(s, t) = \exp \int_s^t \operatorname{tr} A(r) \, dr\), with the oriented integral, consistent with Liouville's formula for the fundamental matrix of the system.
