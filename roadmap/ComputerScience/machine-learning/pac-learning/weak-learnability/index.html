    Weak learnability

    Kearns and Valiant's weak PAC learnability, accuracy only slightly better than random guessing at every confidence, in CSLib's PAC vocabulary, and Schapire's theorem that it is equivalent to PAC learnability (recorded in its sample-complexity form for the realizable case).

    DefinitionWeak PAC learnability#✓fully proved

    Cslib.MachineLearning.PACLearning.IsWeakPACLearnable

    A concept class \(C\) is weakly PAC learnable over a family \(\mathcal{D}\) of distributions if there is an accuracy \(\varepsilon \in (0, 1)\) with \(\varepsilon < 1/2\) such that for every confidence \(\delta \in (0, 1)\) some sample size \(m\) admits a learner that, for every \(D \in \mathcal{D}\), outputs with probability at least \(1 - \delta\) over \(m\) i.i.d. examples a hypothesis of error at most \(\operatorname{opt}_C(D) + \varepsilon\) (CSLib's IsPACLearnerFor m ε δ C 𝒟).

    Because CSLib's learners are measured against \(\operatorname{opt}_C(D)\), this matches Kearns and Valiant's notion only when every distribution in \(\mathcal{D}\) is realizable (\(\operatorname{opt}_C(D) = 0\)), as for the realizable family used in the wanted converse; over other distributions it is an agnostic variant. On realizable distributions it is Kearns and Valiant's notion, as stated by Schapire, without its computational part. Schapire's definition also requires the learner to run in polynomial time and lets the advantage \(1/2 - \varepsilon\) shrink as an inverse polynomial in the size parameters; CSLib's learners count examples only.

    def IsWeakPACLearnable (C : ConceptClass α β) (𝒟 : Set (Measure (α × β))) : Prop :=
  ∃ ε : Set.Ioo (0 : ℝ≥0) 1, ε.val < 1 / 2 ∧
    ∀ δ : Set.Ioo (0 : ℝ≥0) 1, ∃ m, IsPACLearnerFor m ε δ C 𝒟

    TheoremPAC learnability implies weak PAC learnability#✓fully proved

    Cslib.MachineLearning.PACLearning.IsPACLearnable.toIsWeakPACLearnable

    If a concept class is PAC learnable over a family of distributions, it is weakly PAC learnable over the same family: take the accuracy \(\varepsilon = 1/4\). This is the direction of Schapire's Theorem 1 that he calls trivial.

    TheoremWeak learnability implies PAC learnability#●ready to prove

    Cslib.MachineLearning.PACLearning.IsWeakPACLearnable.isPACLearnable_realizableFamily

    Let \(X\) be a countable instance space with measurable singletons and \(C\) a class of hypotheses \(X \to \{0, 1\}\). If \(C\) is weakly PAC learnable over its realizable distributions (those under which some concept of \(C\) has error \(0\)), then \(C\) is PAC learnable over them.

    This is the sample-complexity form of Schapire's Theorem 1, the equivalence of weak and strong learnability. Schapire's theorem is about polynomial-time learners and needs his recursive majority-of-three construction, which filters the example distribution so that the weak learner is forced onto the parts it has not yet learned.

    This statement is recorded as wanted: its proof is not formalized.
