    Additive noise models and causal identifiability

    Observational data identify a causal graph in general only up to Markov equivalence: with two variables, \(X \to Y\) and \(Y \to X\) cannot be told apart from the joint law without assumptions on the mechanisms. Restricting the structural equations changes this. In an additive noise model \(Y = f(X) + N\) with \(N\) independent of \(X\), the causal direction is identifiable unless the model is of a special form; for linear \(f\) the exception is exactly the Gaussian case, where a backward model \(X = \psi Y + \tilde N\) with \(\tilde N\) independent of \(Y\) exists (LiNGAM, Shimizu et al. 2006; Peters, Mooij, Janzing and Schölkopf 2014, Proposition 13), and for nonlinear \(f\) identifiability holds generically (Hoyer et al. 2009). These results are the basis of causal discovery methods that regress each variable on the others and test whether the residuals are independent of the regressors.

    The linear Gaussian case is not identifiable

    TheoremA linear Gaussian additive noise model has a backward model#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Probability.LinearGaussianBackwardModel.exists_indepFun_backward_of_hasGaussianLaw

    A linear Gaussian additive noise model has a backward model (Peters, Mooij, Janzing and Schölkopf, Causal discovery with continuous additive noise models, JMLR 15 (2014), Example 12 and the "if" direction of Proposition 13). Let X and N be independent random variables with Gaussian laws (possibly degenerate) and let Y = φ X + N. Then there is ψ ∈ ℝ such that Ñ = X - ψ Y is independent of Y; one can take ψ = Cov(X, Y) / Var(Y).

    The proof follows Example 12 of Peters et al.: the pair \((X, N)\) is jointly Gaussian because its coordinates are independent Gaussians, so \((X - \psi Y, Y)\), a linear image of it, is jointly Gaussian too, and for \(\psi = \mathrm{Cov}(X, Y) / \mathrm{Var}(Y) = \varphi \mathrm{Var}(X) / (\varphi^2 \mathrm{Var}(X) + \mathrm{Var}(N))\) its coordinates are uncorrelated, hence independent. When \(\mathrm{Var}(Y) = 0\) both coordinates are degenerate and any \(\psi\) works. The joint law of \((X, Y)\) therefore admits the two structural descriptions \(X \to Y\) and \(Y \to X\) with independent noise: in the linear Gaussian case the causal direction is not identifiable from observational data.

    theorem exists_indepFun_backward_of_hasGaussianLaw (hX : HasGaussianLaw X P)
    (hN : HasGaussianLaw N P) (hXN : IndepFun X N P) (φ : ℝ) :
    ∃ ψ : ℝ, IndepFun (fun ω => X ω - ψ * (φ * X ω + N ω)) (fun ω => φ * X ω + N ω) P

    Linear additive noise models with Gaussian laws are reversible, the laws being written as gaussianReal (Peters, Mooij, Janzing and Schölkopf, Causal discovery with continuous additive noise models, JMLR 15 (2014), the "if" direction of Proposition 13). If X and N are independent and measurable with X ~ 𝒩(m₁, v₁) and N ~ 𝒩(m₂, v₂), then for every φ there is ψ such that X - ψ (φ X + N) is independent of φ X + N.

    Non-Gaussian noise identifies the direction

    TheoremA reversible linear additive noise model is Gaussian#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.ComputerScience.LinearNonGaussianIdentifiabilityWanted.gaussian_of_indepFun_backward

    A reversible linear additive noise model is Gaussian (Peters, Mooij, Janzing and Schölkopf, Causal discovery with continuous additive noise models, JMLR 15 (2014), the "only if" direction of Proposition 13, after Shimizu et al. 2006). Let X and N be independent real random variables, neither almost surely constant, and let Y = φ X + N with φ ≠ 0. If there is ψ ∈ ℝ such that Ñ = X - ψ Y is independent of Y, then X and N have nondegenerate Gaussian laws.

    In causal terms: from the joint law of \((X, Y)\) alone, the direction \(X \to Y\) of a linear structural equation with independent noise can be recovered unless both the cause and the noise are Gaussian; together with the converse (the linear Gaussian case) this is the characterization of Proposition 13. Peters et al. present it (Section 2.3) among the restrictions of structural equation models that make the graph identifiable, before turning to nonlinear additive noise models, whose bivariate identifiability (Hoyer et al. 2009) is their Theorem 20. The proof they point to uses the Darmois–Skitovich theorem: if two linear combinations of independent random variables, all of whose coefficients are nonzero, are independent, then the variables are Gaussian.

    The statement assumes \(X\) and \(N\) not almost surely constant; Proposition 13 tacitly works with nondegenerate variables. The assumption is needed for both: if \(X\) is almost surely constant, \(\psi = 0\) gives a backward model for every law of \(N\), and if \(N\) is almost surely constant, \(\psi = 1/\varphi\) makes \(X - \psi Y = -N/\varphi\) constant, a backward model for every law of \(X\).

    (hNc : ∀ c : ℝ, ¬ N =ᵐ[P] fun _ => c) {φ : ℝ} (hφ : φ ≠ 0) {ψ : ℝ}
    (hback : IndepFun (fun ω => X ω - ψ * (φ * X ω + N ω)) (fun ω => φ * X ω + N ω) P) :

    Nonlinear additive noise models

    TheoremReversible additive noise models satisfy the identifiability differential equation#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.ComputerScience.AdditiveNoiseModelIdentifiabilityWanted.identifiability_ode_of_reversible

    Reversible additive noise models satisfy the identifiability differential equation (Peters, Mooij, Janzing and Schölkopf, Causal discovery with continuous additive noise models, JMLR 15 (2014), equation (7), Condition 19 and the proof of Theorem 20 in Appendix A.5, after Hoyer et al. 2009). Let f, g, ν, ξ, ν̃, η : ℝ → ℝ be three times continuously differentiable, where ν, ξ are the log-densities of the noise and of the cause of the model Y = f(X) + N and ν̃, η those of a backward model X = g(Y) + Ñ, and suppose both models give the same joint log-density, ν(y - f(x)) + ξ(x) = ν̃(x - g(y)) + η(y) for all x, y. Then at every point with ν''(y - f(x)) f'(x) ≠ 0, ξ''' = ξ'' (-ν''' f' / ν'' + f'' / f') - 2 ν'' f'' f' + ν' f''' + ν' ν''' f'' f' / ν'' - ν' (f'')² / f', where ν and its derivatives are evaluated at y - f(x) and ξ, f and their derivatives at x.

    Condition 19 of Peters et al. calls the triple \((f, p_X, p_N)\) identifiable when it does not satisfy this equation at all points where \(\nu''(y - f(x)) f'(x) \neq 0\); the statement here is the core of the proof of Theorem 20: a model satisfying Condition 19 has no backward additive noise model with three times continuously differentiable ingredients. For fixed \(f\) and noise law, the laws of \(X\) violating Condition 19 lie in a three-dimensional family (their Proposition 21), so nonlinear additive noise models are generically identifiable. For Gaussian \(X\) and \(N\) the equation forces \(f\) to be linear (their Corollary 22), the reversible case of the linear Gaussian model.
