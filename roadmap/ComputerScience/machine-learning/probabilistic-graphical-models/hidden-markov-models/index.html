    Cslib.MachineLearning.Sequence.HiddenMarkov.HMM

    A hidden Markov model with a set of states \(S\), a set of observations \(O\) and weights in \(R\) consists of an initial weight \(\pi(s) \in R\) for each state \(s\), a transition weight \(a(s, s') \in R\) for each pair of states, the weight of a transition from \(s\) to \(s'\), and an emission weight \(e(s, o) \in R\) for each state \(s\) and observation \(o\), the weight of emitting \(o\) from \(s\).

    No structure on \(R\) and no normalization of the weights is required.

    structure HMM (S O R : Type*) where
  /-- The initial weight of each state. -/
  init : S → R
  /-- The weight of a transition from the first state to the second. -/
  trans : S → S → R
  /-- The weight of emitting an observation from a state. -/
  emit : S → O → R

    Cslib.MachineLearning.Sequence.HiddenMarkov.viterbi

    Let \(h\) be a hidden Markov model with a finite nonempty set of states \(S\), initial weights \(\pi(s)\), transition weights \(a(s', s)\) and emission weights \(b(s, o)\) in a linearly ordered commutative semiring \(R\), and let \(x_0, x_1, \dots\) be a sequence of observations. The Viterbi variables \(V_t(s)\), for \(t \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(s \in S\), are defined by \(V_0(s) = \pi(s) \, b(s, x_0)\) and

    \[V_{t+1}(s) = \Big(\max_{s' \in S} V_t(s') \, a(s', s)\Big) \, b(s, x_{t+1}).\]

    When all weights are nonnegative, \(V_t(s)\) is the largest joint weight of a state path of length \(t + 1\) ending in \(s\) and the observations \(x_0, \dots, x_t\).

    def viterbi : ℕ → S → R
  | 0, s => h.init s * h.emit s (x 0)
  | t + 1, s => Finset.univ.sup' Finset.univ_nonempty (fun s' => viterbi t s' * h.trans s' s) *
      h.emit s (x (t + 1))

    A hidden Markov model \(h\) with weights in a commutative semiring with a linear order has nonnegative weights when all of its initial weights, transition weights and emission weights are \(\ge 0\): for all states \(s, s'\) and every observation \(o\), the initial weight of \(s\), the weight of the transition from \(s\) to \(s'\), and the weight of emitting \(o\) from \(s\) are nonnegative.
