    Structural causal models and counterfactuals

    A Markovian structural causal model assigns to every variable an autonomous mechanism X_k = f_k(X_{pa k}, U_k) driven by mutually independent disturbances. Its law factorizes over its causal diagram (the causal Markov condition), an intervention do(X = x) replaces the mechanisms of the intervened variables by constants (the submodel M_x, whose law is the truncated factorization of the causal Bayesian network), and a counterfactual Y_x(u) is the solution of that submodel for the unit u. These are the three rungs of Pearl's hierarchy: association, intervention, counterfactual.

    TheoremThe causal Markov condition#✓fully proved

    Cslib.MachineLearning.GraphicalModels.SCM.law_eq_joint

    The causal Markov condition (Pearl, Statistics Surveys 2009, Theorem 1, eq. (15); Pearl and Verma 1991): the law of a Markovian structural causal model factorizes over its causal diagram: it is the joint of s.toBayesNet, the product over the nodes of the probability that the node's equation produces its value from the values of its parents.

    The proof writes the indicator of solve u = x as the product over the nodes of the indicators that x satisfies each structural equation (solve_eq_iff), and then exchanges the sum over the independent disturbances with the product over the nodes.

    theorem law_eq_joint [Fintype β] [DecidableEq α] [Nonempty α] (x : Fin n → α) :
    s.law x = s.toBayesNet.joint x

    DefinitionThe submodel of the intervention do(X_W = w)#✓fully proved

    Cslib.MachineLearning.GraphicalModels.SCM.doSet

    The submodel of the intervention do(X_W = w) (Pearl 1995, Definition 2; Pearl, Statistics Surveys 2009, eq. (7)): the equation of every node j ∈ W is replaced by the constant X j = w j (so j loses its parents), and every other equation is kept.

    def doSet (W : Finset (Fin n)) (w : Fin n → α) : SCM α β n where
  pa j := if j ∈ W then ∅ else s.pa j
  noise := s.noise
  mech j z e := if h : j ∈ W then w j else s.mech j (fun i => z ⟨i, by simp [h]⟩) e

    Cslib.MachineLearning.GraphicalModels.SCM.law_doSet

    Interventions are truncated factorizations (Pearl, Statistics Surveys 2009, Corollary 1, eq. (17); Pearl 1995, eq. (5)): the law of the submodel of do(X_W = w) is the joint of the network of the model with the arrows into W removed and point masses at w on W.

    TheoremThe probability of necessity under monotonicity#✓fully proved

    Cslib.MachineLearning.GraphicalModels.SCM.cfProb_necessity

    The probability of necessity under monotonicity (Pearl, Statistics Surveys 2009, eq. (53) and Theorem 3): in a model whose variables are all binary (the framework uses one value type), for the treatment X_i and the outcome X_j, the probability of necessity PN = P(Y_{x'} = y' | X = x, Y = y) (with x, y = true and x', y' = false) is identified from the observational law and the interventional law of do(X_i = false) when Y is monotonic in X (Y_{x'}(u) = true implies Y_x(u) = true): PN = (P(y) - P(y | do(x'))) / P(x, y). SCM.cfProb_necessity_eq rewrites this as the right-hand side of eq. (54). Pearl needs only the treatment and the outcome to be binary.

    The probability of necessity is a counterfactual (third-rung) quantity: it conditions on the factual treatment and outcome and asks about the outcome under the opposite treatment. It is not identified in general; monotonicity, together with the interventional law of do(x'), makes it identifiable. The proof is pointwise in the disturbances: by consistency, a unit with X = x has Y = Y_x, and a unit with X = x' has Y = Y_{x'}, so under monotonicity the indicator of {X = x, Y = y, Y_{x'} = y'} equals that of {Y = y} minus that of {Y_{x'} = y}.

    theorem cfProb_necessity [Fintype β] (s : SCM Bool β n) (i j : Fin n)
    (hmono : ∀ u, s.counterfactual {i} (fun _ => false) u j = true →
      s.counterfactual {i} (fun _ => true) u j = true) :
    s.cfProb {i} (fun _ => false) (fun x => x i = true ∧ x j = true) (fun x => x j = false) =
      ((∑ x with x j = true, s.law x) - ∑ x with x j = true, (s.doSet {i} fun _ => false).law x) /
        ∑ x with x i = true ∧ x j = true, s.law x

    TheoremPearl's formula for the probability of necessity#✓fully proved

    Cslib.MachineLearning.GraphicalModels.SCM.cfProb_necessity_eq

    Pearl's formula for the probability of necessity (Pearl, Statistics Surveys 2009, Theorem 3, eq. (54)): under monotonicity, and when P(x, y) > 0, PN = (P(y|x) - P(y|x')) / P(y|x) + (P(y|x') - P(y | do(x'))) / P(x, y): the excess risk ratio plus a correction for confounding.

    theorem cfProb_necessity_eq [Fintype β] (s : SCM Bool β n) (i j : Fin n)
