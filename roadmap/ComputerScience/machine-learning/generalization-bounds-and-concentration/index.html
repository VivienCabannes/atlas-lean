    Generalization bounds and concentration

    Uniform deviation bounds between the empirical and the expected risk, obtained from concentration inequalities, and their use for empirical risk minimization: if every deviation is at most \(s\), the empirical risk minimizer is within \(2 s\) of the best predictor of the class. For a finite class, Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound give a deviation of order \(\sqrt{\log |F| / n}\); for a general class, McDiarmid's bounded-differences inequality concentrates the supremum of the deviations around its mean, and Rademacher averages control that mean, dimension free for balls of linear predictors. The two pages marked as expositions are AI-authored expositions of published results without Lean formalization; each names what it relies on, and how, under Informally uses. Algorithmic stability gives a complementary route that involves no complexity of the class: if replacing one example changes the loss of the learned predictor by at most \(\varepsilon\) (uniform stability), the expected generalization gap is at most \(\varepsilon\) (Hardt, Recht and Singer 2016, Theorem 2.2, after Bousquet and Elisseeff 2002), and stochastic gradient descent on convex, smooth, Lipschitz losses is uniformly stable (their Theorem 3.8). The Rademacher complexity topic is formalized: the structural calculus of the empirical Rademacher complexity, Massart's finite-class lemma, the bound for classes of finite VC dimension through the Sauer–Shelah lemma, and the contraction lemma, with the Rademacher risk bound as a wanted statement.

    The PAC-Bayes bounds are formalized in Lean: Catoni's bound for bounded losses holds with high probability simultaneously for every posterior, through the Donsker–Varadhan change of measure.

    DefinitionExpected risk#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.risk

    Let \(\nu\) be a probability distribution on a space \(\mathcal{Z}\) of examples \(z = (x, y)\), and let \(L(f, z)\) be the loss \(\ell(y, f(x))\) of a predictor \(f\) at the example \(z\). The expected risk of \(f\) is

    \[ R(f) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \nu}\big[\ell(y, f(x))\big] = \int L(f, z) \, d\nu(z). \]

    Predictors are abstract: only the loss map \(L\) enters.

    noncomputable def risk (L : F → 𝒵 → ℝ) (ν : Measure 𝒵) (f : F) : ℝ :=
  ∫ z, L f z ∂ν

    DefinitionEmpirical risk#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.empiricalRisk

    For a sample \(z_1, \dots, z_n\) of examples and a loss \(L(f, z) = \ell(y, f(x))\), the empirical risk of a predictor \(f\) is the average loss on the sample,

    \[ \hat R(f) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n L(f, z_i). \]
    noncomputable def empiricalRisk {n : ℕ} (L : F → 𝒵 → ℝ) (z : Fin n → 𝒵) (f : F) : ℝ :=
  (1 / n : ℝ) * ∑ i, L f (z i)

    TheoremMeasurability of the empirical risk#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.measurable_empiricalRisk

    If the sample maps \(\omega \mapsto z_i(\omega)\) and the loss \(z \mapsto L(f, z)\) are measurable, then for each predictor \(f\) the empirical risk \(\omega \mapsto \hat R(f)\) on the random sample is measurable. It makes the deviation events of the uniform deviation bounds measurable.

    theorem measurable_empiricalRisk {n : ℕ} {Z : Fin n → Ω → 𝒵} (hZ : ∀ i, Measurable (Z i))
    {L : F → 𝒵 → ℝ} (hL : ∀ f, Measurable (L f)) (f : F) :
    Measurable fun ω => empiricalRisk L (fun i => Z i ω) f
    noncomputable def empiricalRisk {n : ℕ} (L : F → 𝒵 → ℝ) (z : Fin n → 𝒵) (f : F) : ℝ :=

    TheoremEmpirical risk minimization under a uniform deviation bound#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.risk_le_add_two_mul_of_forall_abs_sub_le

    Let \(R\) and \(\hat R\) be real functions on a class of predictors, let \(\hat f\) minimize \(\hat R\) over the class, and suppose \(|\hat R(f) - R(f)| \le s\) for every predictor \(f\) of the class. Then for every \(f\) in the class,

    \[ R(\hat f) \le R(f) + 2 s. \]

    Proof: \(R(\hat f) \le \hat R(\hat f) + s \le \hat R(f) + s \le R(f) + 2 s\).

    With the expected and empirical risks, this reduces the estimation error of empirical risk minimization to a uniform deviation bound, the starting point of every bound in this topic.

    theorem risk_le_add_two_mul_of_forall_abs_sub_le {R Rhat : F → ℝ} {fhat : F}
    (hmin : ∀ f, Rhat fhat ≤ Rhat f) {s : ℝ} (hdev : ∀ f, |Rhat f - R f| ≤ s) (f : F) :
    R fhat ≤ R f + 2 * s

    TheoremUniform deviation of the empirical risk over a finite class#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.measureReal_exists_abs_empiricalRisk_sub_risk_ge_le

    Let \(z_1, \dots, z_n\), with \(n \ge 1\), be independent examples with common law \(\nu\), let \(F\) be a finite class of predictors, and let the loss satisfy \(L(f, z) \in [0, \ell_\infty]\) for \(\nu\)-almost every \(z\), with \(z \mapsto L(f, z)\) measurable for each \(f\). Then for every \(t \ge 0\),

    \[ \mathbb{P}\big(\exists f \in F,\ |\hat R(f) - R(f)| \ge t\big) \le 2 |F| \exp\Big(-\frac{2 n t^2}{\ell_\infty^2}\Big), \]

    with a quotient by \(0\) read as \(0\). Proof: for each \(f\) the losses \(L(f, z_i)\) are independent, lie in \([0, \ell_\infty]\) and have mean \(R(f)\), so the two-sided Hoeffding inequality bounds each deviation event; a union bound over the \(|F|\) predictors gives the result.

    This is the formal counterpart of the exposition Uniform deviation for a finite class, for an arbitrary bounded loss and with the non-strict event.

    theorem measureReal_exists_abs_empiricalRisk_sub_risk_ge_le {μ : Measure Ω}
    [IsProbabilityMeasure μ] [Fintype F] {ν : Measure 𝒵} {n : ℕ} (hn : 0 < n)
    {Z : Fin n → Ω → 𝒵} (h_indep : iIndepFun Z μ) (hZ : ∀ i, Measurable (Z i))
    (hlaw : ∀ i, μ.map (Z i) = ν) {L : F → 𝒵 → ℝ} (hL : ∀ f, Measurable (L f)) {ℓ : ℝ}
    (hbound : ∀ f, ∀ᵐ z ∂ν, L f z ∈ Set.Icc 0 ℓ) {t : ℝ} (ht : 0 ≤ t) :
    μ.real {ω | ∃ f, t ≤ |empiricalRisk L (fun i => Z i ω) f - risk L ν f|} ≤
      2 * Fintype.card F * exp (-2 * n * t ^ 2 / ℓ ^ 2)
    noncomputable def empiricalRisk {n : ℕ} (L : F → 𝒵 → ℝ) (z : Fin n → 𝒵) (f : F) : ℝ :=
  (1 / n : ℝ) * ∑ i, L f (z i)

    TheoremUniform deviation over a finite class, high-probability form#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.one_sub_le_measureReal_forall_abs_empiricalRisk_sub_risk_lt

    Under the hypotheses of the uniform deviation bound for a finite class, with \(F\) nonempty and \(\ell_\infty > 0\), for every \(\delta \in (0, 1]\), with probability at least \(1 - \delta\),

    \[ |\hat R(f) - R(f)| \lt \ell_\infty \sqrt{\frac{\log(2 |F| / \delta)}{2 n}} \quad \text{for every } f \in F. \]

    Proof: for \(t = \ell_\infty \sqrt{\log(2 |F| / \delta) / (2 n)}\) the tail bound \(2 |F| \exp(-2 n t^2 / \ell_\infty^2)\) equals \(\delta\); pass to the complementary event.

    Combined with empirical risk minimization, it gives \(R(\hat f) \le \min_{f \in F} R(f) + 2 \ell_\infty \sqrt{\log(2 |F| / \delta) / (2 n)}\) with probability at least \(1 - \delta\).

    theorem one_sub_le_measureReal_forall_abs_empiricalRisk_sub_risk_lt {μ : Measure Ω}
    [IsProbabilityMeasure μ] [Fintype F] [Nonempty F] {ν : Measure 𝒵} {n : ℕ} (hn : 0 < n)
    {Z : Fin n → Ω → 𝒵} (h_indep : iIndepFun Z μ) (hZ : ∀ i, Measurable (Z i))
    (hlaw : ∀ i, μ.map (Z i) = ν) {L : F → 𝒵 → ℝ} (hL : ∀ f, Measurable (L f)) {ℓ : ℝ}
    (hℓ : 0 < ℓ) (hbound : ∀ f, ∀ᵐ z ∂ν, L f z ∈ Set.Icc 0 ℓ) {δ : ℝ} (hδ : δ ∈ Set.Ioc 0 1) :
    1 - δ ≤ μ.real {ω | ∀ f, |empiricalRisk L (fun i => Z i ω) f - risk L ν f| <
      ℓ * √(log (2 * Fintype.card F / δ) / (2 * n))}
    noncomputable def empiricalRisk {n : ℕ} (L : F → 𝒵 → ℝ) (z : Fin n → 𝒵) (f : F) : ℝ :=
  (1 / n : ℝ) * ∑ i, L f (z i)

    TheoremRademacher average of a ball of linear predictors#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.rademacher_average_linear_ball_le

    Let \(\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_n\) be vectors of a real inner product space (the features \(\varphi(x_i)\) of a sample) and let \(D \ge 0\). For independent uniform signs \(\varepsilon_1, \dots, \varepsilon_n \in \{-1, 1\}\) (the expectation being the average over the \(2^n\) sign patterns),

    \[ \mathbb{E}_\varepsilon \sup_{\|\theta\| \le D} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i \langle \theta, \varphi_i \rangle \le \frac{D}{n} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n \|\varphi_i\|^2}. \]

    The left side is the empirical Rademacher complexity, on the sample, of the linear predictors \(x \mapsto \langle \theta, \varphi(x) \rangle\) with \(\|\theta\| \le D\). Proof: \(\frac{1}{n} \sum_i \varepsilon_i \langle \theta, \varphi_i \rangle = \frac{1}{n} \langle \theta, \sum_i \varepsilon_i \varphi_i \rangle \le \frac{D}{n} \|\sum_i \varepsilon_i \varphi_i\|\) by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and the average of \(\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i \varphi_i\|\) over the signs is at most \(\sqrt{\sum_i \|\varphi_i\|^2}\) because Hilbert spaces have Rademacher type \(2\).

    When \(\|\varphi(x)\| \le R\) the bound is \(D R / \sqrt{n}\), independent of the dimension of the feature space. It therefore covers balls of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, where \(\varphi(x)\) is the kernel function at \(x\) and \(\sum_i \|\varphi(x_i)\|^2 = \sum_i k(x_i, x_i)\) is the trace of the kernel matrix. With symmetrization and the contraction principle for a \(G\)-Lipschitz loss, it gives the estimation error \(4 G R D / \sqrt{n}\) of constrained empirical risk minimization over linear predictors.

    theorem rademacher_average_linear_ball_le {E : Type*} [NormedAddCommGroup E]
    [InnerProductSpace ℝ E] {n : ℕ} (φ : Fin n → E) {D : ℝ} (hD : 0 ≤ D) :
    ((2 : ℝ) ^ n)⁻¹ * ∑ σ : Fin n → Bool,
        (⨆ θ : {θ : E // ‖θ‖ ≤ D}, (1 / n : ℝ) * ∑ i, rademacherSign ℝ σ i * ⟪(θ : E), φ i⟫) ≤
      D / n * √(∑ i, ‖φ i‖ ^ 2)
    def rademacherSign (𝕜 : Type*) [RCLike 𝕜] {n : ℕ} (ε : Fin n → Bool) (i : Fin n) : 𝕜 :=
  if ε i then 1 else -1

    Related topics

    • Hedge algorithm: regret bounds, the online counterpart of uniform deviation bounds; the \(\sqrt{T \log n}\) regret of Hedge mirrors the \(\sqrt{\log \lvert \mathcal{F} \rvert / n}\) deviation of a finite class.
    • Effective ranks and benign overfitting: the excess risk of the minimum-norm interpolating estimator is analysed through the spectrum of the covariance rather than through a uniform deviation bound over the class.
    • Hoeffding's inequality: the per-predictor concentration step.
    • McDiarmid's inequality: concentration of the supremum of the deviations.
    • Rademacher type and cotype: Rademacher averages in Hilbert spaces, behind the bound for linear predictors.
    • Positive-definite kernels: feature maps of kernels, whose balls are balls of linear predictors.
    • Gradient descent: the subgradient method and stochastic gradient descent, whose \(1/\sqrt{t}\) rate matches the \(1/\sqrt{n}\) estimation error.
    • Sub-exponential variables and Bernstein's inequality
    • Uniform laws and Rademacher complexity
    • Matrix concentration inequalities
    • Differential privacy: when the hypothesis or query is chosen by looking at the data, a union bound over a fixed class no longer applies; if the choice is differentially private, the empirical mean of the chosen query still concentrates (Dwork, Feldman, Hardt, Pitassi, Reingold and Roth 2015, recorded there as a wanted transfer theorem).
    • PAC learning: the realizable case, where a consistent learner over a finite class needs only \(\log(|H|/\delta)/\varepsilon\) examples and no concentration inequality.
    • Two-layer neural networks and Barron space: Rademacher bounds for path-norm balls of two-layer networks and the a priori estimate of the population risk of the path-norm regularized estimator.
    • Finite-sample expressivity of ReLU networks: two-layer ReLU networks with \(2n + d\) weights fit every labelling of \(n\) distinct points, so uniform deviation bounds over such a class cannot explain generalization at that sample size.
