    Algorithmic stability

    An algorithm is uniformly stable if replacing one example of its training sample changes the loss of its output at every test example by at most \(\varepsilon\). Uniform stability bounds the expected generalization gap by \(\varepsilon\), with no reference to the size or complexity of the class of predictors the algorithm may output (Bousquet and Elisseeff 2002; Hardt, Recht and Singer 2016, Theorem 2.2). The proof is an exchange argument: replacing a training example by a fresh one does not change the law of the sample. The generalization bound is formalized for a bounded, jointly measurable loss.

    DefinitionUniform stability#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.IsUniformlyStable

    Let \(A\) be an algorithm that maps a sample \(S = (z_1, \dots, z_n) \in \mathcal{Z}^n\) to a predictor \(A(S)\), and let \(L(h, z)\) be the loss of a predictor \(h\) at an example \(z\). For \(i \in \{1, \dots, n\}\) and \(z' \in \mathcal{Z}\), write \(S^{(i, z')}\) for the sample \(S\) with its \(i\)-th example replaced by \(z'\). The algorithm is \(\varepsilon\)-uniformly stable if for every sample \(S\), index \(i\), replacement \(z'\) and test example \(z\),

    \[ L\big(A(S^{(i, z')}), z\big) - L\big(A(S), z\big) \le \varepsilon . \]

    The pairs \((S, S^{(i, z')})\) are exactly the pairs of samples that differ in at most one example. Since \(S\) is itself \((S^{(i, z')})^{(i, z_i)}\), the one-sided bound of the definition also holds with the two samples exchanged, so \(\lvert L(A(S^{(i, z')}), z) - L(A(S), z)\rvert \le \varepsilon\).

    The Lean definition covers algorithms without internal randomness. For a randomized algorithm, Definition 2.1 of the source takes the expectation over the internal randomness inside the supremum; this is the deterministic definition applied to the expected loss \((S, z) \mapsto \mathbb{E}_A L(A(S), z)\), which is how the stability of SGM is stated.

    def IsUniformlyStable {n : ℕ} (L : H → 𝒵 → ℝ) (A : (Fin n → 𝒵) → H) (ε : ℝ) : Prop :=
  ∀ (S : Fin n → 𝒵) (i : Fin n) (z' z : 𝒵), L (A (Function.update S i z')) z - L (A S) z ≤ ε

    TheoremReplace-one sampling preserves the law of an i.i.d. sample#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.map_update_prod_pi

    Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\mathcal{Z}\), let \(S = (z_1, \dots, z_n) \sim \nu^{\otimes n}\) and \(z \sim \nu\) be independent, and let \(S^{(i, z)}\) be the sample \(S\) with its \(i\)-th example replaced by \(z\). Then \(S^{(i, z)}\) has law \(\nu^{\otimes n}\): the image of \(\nu^{\otimes n} \otimes \nu\) under \((S, z) \mapsto S^{(i, z)}\) is \(\nu^{\otimes n}\).

    Proof: on a box \(\prod_j B_j\), the preimage is \(\big(\prod_{j \ne i} B_j \times \mathcal{Z}\big) \times B_i\), of measure \(\prod_{j \ne i} \nu(B_j) \cdot \nu(\mathcal{Z}) \cdot \nu(B_i) = \prod_j \nu(B_j)\), and boxes determine a product measure.

    This is the exchange step of the stability argument: the loss of \(A(S)\) at the training example \(z_i\) has the same law as the loss of \(A(S^{(i, z)})\) at the fresh example \(z\).

    theorem map_update_prod_pi {n : ℕ} (ν : Measure 𝒵) [IsProbabilityMeasure ν] (i : Fin n) :
    ((Measure.pi fun _ : Fin n => ν).prod ν).map (fun p => Function.update p.1 i p.2) =
      Measure.pi fun _ : Fin n => ν

    TheoremUniform stability implies generalization in expectation#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.abs_integral_empiricalRisk_sub_risk_le

    Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on the example space \(\mathcal{Z}\), let \(S = (z_1, \dots, z_n) \sim \nu^{\otimes n}\) with \(n \ge 1\), and let \(A\) be an \(\varepsilon\)-uniformly stable algorithm for a loss \(L\). Assume that \((S, z) \mapsto L(A(S), z)\) is jointly measurable and bounded. Then

    \[ \Big\lvert\, \mathbb{E}_S\big[\hat R_S(A(S)) - R(A(S))\big] \Big\rvert \le \varepsilon, \]

    where \(\hat R_S(h) = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^n L(h, z_i)\) is the empirical risk and \(R(h) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \nu} L(h, z)\) the expected risk.

    Proof: by replace-one sampling, \(\mathbb{E}\, L(A(S), z_i) = \mathbb{E}\, L(A(S^{(i, z)}), z)\) for an independent \(z \sim \nu\), while \(\mathbb{E}\, R(A(S)) = \mathbb{E}\, L(A(S), z)\) by Fubini. Each of the \(n\) differences \(L(A(S^{(i, z)}), z) - L(A(S), z)\) lies in \([-\varepsilon, \varepsilon]\) by stability, and averaging over \(i\) gives the bound.

    The source states the theorem without regularity hypotheses. Joint measurability makes the loss at a training example, \(S \mapsto L(A(S), z_i)\), measurable, and boundedness makes every expectation in the proof finite; both are added here. The bound holds in expectation only.

    Unlike the uniform deviation bounds of the parent topic and Rademacher complexity, the bound involves no complexity of a class of predictors: only the sensitivity of the algorithm to one example. Combined with the uniform stability of stochastic gradient descent, it bounds the generalization gap of SGD by \(2L^2/n\) times the sum of its step sizes, for a loss bounded at the starting point.

    theorem abs_integral_empiricalRisk_sub_risk_le {ν : Measure 𝒵} [IsProbabilityMeasure ν] {n : ℕ}
    (hn : 0 < n) {L : H → 𝒵 → ℝ} {A : (Fin n → 𝒵) → H}
    (hmeas : Measurable fun p : (Fin n → 𝒵) × 𝒵 => L (A p.1) p.2) {M : ℝ}
    (hbdd : ∀ S z, |L (A S) z| ≤ M) {ε : ℝ} (hstab : IsUniformlyStable L A ε) :
    |∫ S, (empiricalRisk L S (A S) - risk L ν (A S)) ∂(Measure.pi fun _ : Fin n => ν)| ≤ ε
    def IsUniformlyStable {n : ℕ} (L : H → 𝒵 → ℝ) (A : (Fin n → 𝒵) → H) (ε : ℝ) : Prop :=
  ∀ (S : Fin n → 𝒵) (i : Fin n) (z' z : 𝒵), L (A (Function.update S i z')) z - L (A S) z ≤ ε

    Stability of stochastic gradient descent

    Stochastic gradient descent on convex, \(\beta\)-smooth, \(L\)-Lipschitz losses with steps \(\alpha_t \le 2/\beta\) is uniformly stable with \(\varepsilon \le (2L^2/n)\sum_t \alpha_t\) (Hardt, Recht and Singer, Theorem 3.8). The proof couples the runs on two samples that differ in one example through the same random indices: a step on a shared example applies the same non-expansive gradient update to both runs, and a step on the differing example, which happens with probability \(1/n\), moves them apart by at most \(2\alpha_t L\). With the generalization bound above, applied to a loss bounded at the starting point (each step moves the iterate by at most \(\alpha_t L\), so the losses along the run stay bounded), the expected generalization gap of SGD is at most \(2L^2/n\) times its total step length, whatever the number of passes over the data: training faster generalizes better.

    DefinitionIterates of the stochastic gradient method#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.sgmIterate

    Let \(\ell(w, z)\) be the loss of a parameter \(w\) in a real Hilbert space at an example \(z\), let \(S = (z_1, \dots, z_n)\) be a sample, \((\alpha_t)_{t \ge 0}\) step sizes and \(\iota : \mathbb{N} \to \{1, \dots, n\}\) an index sequence. The stochastic gradient method (SGM) starts at \(w_0\) and updates

    \[ w_{t+1} = w_t - \alpha_t\, \nabla_w \ell(w_t, z_{\iota(t)}) . \]

    Each step is the gradient update of the loss at one example of the sample; the index sequence is random in the algorithm (random selection rule) and fixed here.

    noncomputable def sgmIterate {n : ℕ} (ℓ : E → 𝒵 → ℝ) (α : ℕ → ℝ) (w₀ : E) (S : Fin n → 𝒵)
    (ι : ℕ → Fin n) : ℕ → E
  | 0 => w₀
  | t + 1 => sgmIterate ℓ α w₀ S ι t -
      α t • gradient (fun w => ℓ w (S (ι t))) (sgmIterate ℓ α w₀ S ι t)

    DefinitionRandom selection rule#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.indexMeasure

    The random selection rule of the stochastic gradient method draws the indices \(i_0, \dots, i_{T-1}\) independently and uniformly from \(\{1, \dots, n\}\). Its law is the product of \(T\) uniform distributions on \(\{1, \dots, n\}\), a probability measure on the index sequences \(\kappa : \{0, \dots, T-1\} \to \{1, \dots, n\}\) when \(n \ge 1\). Under it, each step selects a given index with probability \(1/n\).

    The source also analyses the random permutation rule (one pass over a random permutation of the sample); it is not formalized here.

    noncomputable def indexMeasure (n T : ℕ) : Measure (Fin T → Fin n) :=
  Measure.pi fun _ : Fin T => uniformOn (Set.univ : Set (Fin n))

    TheoremGrowth recursion for two bounded updates#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.norm_sub_le_min_mul_add_two_mul

    Let \(G\) and \(G'\) be maps of a normed space into itself. Suppose \(G\) is \(\eta\)-expansive, \(\lVert G(v) - G(w)\rVert \le \eta \lVert v - w\rVert\), and that both are \(\sigma\)-bounded, \(\lVert w - G(w)\rVert \le \sigma\) and \(\lVert w - G'(w)\rVert \le \sigma\) for all \(w\). Then for all \(v, w\),

    \[ \lVert G(v) - G'(w) \rVert \le \min(\eta, 1)\, \lVert v - w \rVert + 2\sigma . \]

    Proof: \(G(v) - G'(w) = (v - w) - (v - G(v)) + (w - G'(w))\) gives the bound with \(1\), and \(G(v) - G'(w) = (G(v) - G(w)) - (w - G(w)) + (w - G'(w))\) gives the bound with \(\eta\).

    Along two runs \(w_{t+1} = G_t(w_t)\), \(w'_{t+1} = G'_t(w'_t)\) from the same start, the distance \(\delta_t = \lVert w_t - w'_t\rVert\) thus satisfies \(\delta_{t+1} \le \eta\, \delta_t\) when \(G_t = G'_t\), and \(\delta_{t+1} \le \min(\eta, 1)\,\delta_t + 2\sigma\) otherwise. This recursion is the engine of the stability of SGM.

    theorem norm_sub_le_min_mul_add_two_mul {G G' : E → E} {η σ : ℝ}
    (hG : ∀ v w, ‖G v - G w‖ ≤ η * ‖v - w‖) (hσ : ∀ w, ‖w - G w‖ ≤ σ)
    (hσ' : ∀ w, ‖w - G' w‖ ≤ σ) (v w : E) :
    ‖G v - G' w‖ ≤ min η 1 * ‖v - w‖ + 2 * σ

    TheoremCoupled distance of SGM along a fixed index sequence#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.norm_sgmIterate_sub_le

    Let every loss \(\ell(\cdot, z)\) be convex, differentiable and \(L\)-Lipschitz on a real Hilbert space, with \(\beta\)-Lipschitz gradient, and let \(0 \le \alpha_t \le 2/\beta\). Let \(S\) and \(S'\) be samples that differ only in their \(k\)-th example, and run the stochastic gradient method from the same \(w_0\) on \(S\) and on \(S'\) with the same index sequence \(\iota\). Then after \(t\) steps

    \[ \lVert w_t - w'_t \rVert \le \sum_{s < t,\ \iota(s) = k} 2\, \alpha_s L . \]

    Proof: by induction with the growth recursion. When \(\iota(s) \ne k\), both runs apply the same gradient update, which is non-expansive; when \(\iota(s) = k\), both updates are \(\alpha_s L\)-bounded and the distance grows by at most \(2\alpha_s L\).

    (hdiff : ∀ z, Differentiable ℝ fun w => ℓ w z)

    (hlip : ∀ z, LipschitzWith L fun w => ℓ w z) {α : ℕ → ℝ} (hα0 : ∀ t, 0 ≤ α t)

    TheoremUniform stability of SGM for convex, smooth, Lipschitz losses#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.integral_abs_sgmOutput_sub_le

    Let every loss \(\ell(\cdot, z)\) be convex, differentiable and \(L\)-Lipschitz on a real Hilbert space, with \(\beta\)-Lipschitz gradient (\(\beta > 0\)), and let the steps satisfy \(0 \le \alpha_t \le 2/\beta\). Let \(S\) and \(S'\) be samples of size \(n \ge 1\) that differ in one example, and let \(w_T\) and \(w'_T\) be the outputs of \(T\) steps of the stochastic gradient method from the same \(w_0\), with the same indices drawn by the random selection rule. Then for every test example \(z\),

    \[ \mathbb{E}\, \big\lvert \ell(w_T, z) - \ell(w'_T, z) \big\rvert \le \frac{2 L^2}{n} \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \alpha_t . \]

    Proof: since \(\ell(\cdot, z)\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz, the left side is at most \(L\, \mathbb{E}\lVert w_T - w'_T\rVert\). Along each index sequence the coupled distance is at most \(\sum_t 2\alpha_t L \cdot \mathbf{1}[\text{step } t \text{ selects the differing example}]\), and each step selects it with probability \(1/n\).

    The bound does not depend on the number of passes over the data, only on the total step length \(\sum_t \alpha_t\): SGD that is stopped early, or run with decreasing steps, is stable. Combined with the generalization bound for stable algorithms, this gives the generalization bound for SGM, for a loss bounded at the starting point, the "train faster, generalize better" principle. It complements the optimization rate of stochastic gradient descent: the optimization error decreases and the stability bound increases with \(\sum_t \alpha_t\).

    The source states the theorem for steps \(\alpha_t > 0\) indexed from \(1\) to \(T\); here the steps are indexed from \(0\) and may vanish. Its strongly convex (Theorem 3.9) and non-convex (Theorem 3.12) versions are not formalized here.

    (hα : ∀ t, α t ≤ 2 / β) (w₀ : E) (T : ℕ) (S : Fin n → 𝒵) (k : Fin n) (z' z : 𝒵) :
    ∫ κ, |ℓ (sgmOutput ℓ α w₀ S T κ) z - ℓ (sgmOutput ℓ α w₀ (Function.update S k z') T κ) z|
        ∂(indexMeasure n T) ≤
      2 * (L : ℝ) ^ 2 / n * ∑ t ∈ Finset.range T, α t

    TheoremSGM is uniformly stable#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.isUniformlyStable_sgm

    Under the hypotheses of the stability bound for SGM, the stochastic gradient method is \(\varepsilon\)-uniformly stable with

    \[ \varepsilon = \frac{2 L^2}{n} \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \alpha_t , \]

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.norm_sgmIterate_sub_start_le

    Let every loss \(\ell(\cdot, z)\) be \(L\)-Lipschitz on a real Hilbert space and let the steps satisfy \(\alpha_t \ge 0\). Then along every index sequence the iterates of the stochastic gradient method satisfy

    \[ \lVert w_t - w_0 \rVert \le L \sum_{s < t} \alpha_s . \]

    Proof: each step \(w_{s+1} = w_s - \alpha_s \nabla_w \ell(w_s, z_{i_s})\) moves the iterate by at most \(\alpha_s L\), since the gradient update of a Lipschitz function is \(\alpha L\)-bounded; sum over the steps.

    Consequently, if the loss is bounded at the start, \(\lvert \ell(w_0, z)\rvert \le M\), then every loss along the run satisfies \(\lvert \ell(w_t, z)\rvert \le M + L^2 \sum_{s<t} \alpha_s\), which is what the generalization bound for SGM needs.

    CSLibExt.MachineLearning.GeneralizationBounds.abs_integral_empiricalRisk_sub_risk_sgm_le

    Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on the example space and \(S \sim \nu^{\otimes n}\) an i.i.d. sample of size \(n \ge 1\). Let every loss \(\ell(\cdot, z)\) be convex, differentiable and \(L\)-Lipschitz on a real Hilbert space, with \(\beta\)-Lipschitz gradient, let \(0 \le \alpha_t \le 2/\beta\), and let \(w_T\) be the output of \(T\) steps of the stochastic gradient method with the random selection rule. Assume moreover that the loss is bounded at the starting point, \(\lvert \ell(w_0, z)\rvert \le M\) for every \(z\), and that, for every index sequence \(\kappa\), \((S, z) \mapsto \ell(w_T(S, \kappa), z)\) is jointly measurable. Then

    \[ \Big\lvert\, \mathbb{E}_{S, \kappa}\big[\hat R_S(w_T) - R(w_T)\big] \Big\rvert \le \frac{2 L^2}{n} \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \alpha_t , \]

    with \(\hat R_S(w) = \frac1n \sum_i \ell(w, z_i)\) and \(R(w) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \nu}\, \ell(w, z)\). In Lean the expectation over \(\kappa\) is taken inside the empirical and expected risks, as in the uniform stability of SGM.

    Proof: SGM is uniformly stable, and uniform stability implies generalization in expectation. That theorem needs a bounded loss: since each step moves the iterate by at most \(\alpha_t L\), the run stays within \(L \sum_t \alpha_t\) of \(w_0\), so every loss along the run is at most \(M + L^2 \sum_{t<T} \alpha_t\) in absolute value. The bound at \(w_0\) and the measurability make the expectations defined; the source does not state them. A bound on the whole space would not do: a convex function bounded on the whole space is constant.
