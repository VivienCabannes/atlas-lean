    Langevin Monte Carlo

    Langevin Monte Carlo (the unadjusted Langevin algorithm) samples approximately from a density \(\pi \propto e^{-f}\) on \(\mathbb{R}^p\) by gradient descent plus Gaussian noise, \(\vartheta_{k+1} = \vartheta_k - h \nabla f(\vartheta_k) + \sqrt{2h}\,\xi_{k+1}\). It is the Euler discretization of the Langevin diffusion \(dL_t = -\nabla f(L_t)\,dt + \sqrt 2\, dW_t\), whose law is the Wasserstein gradient flow of the relative entropy \(\mathrm{KL}(\cdot \,\|\, \pi)\) (Jordan, Kinderlehrer and Otto). In this view sampling is optimization over probability measures: for a strongly log-concave target the iterates contract in \(W_2\) at the gradient-descent rate \(1 - mh\), up to a discretization bias of order \(\sqrt{hp}\) (Dalalyan; Durmus and Moulines), and convergence in relative entropy follows the same gradient-flow analysis (Cheng and Bartlett).

    MathlibExt.Probability.LangevinMonteCarlo.lmcStep

    Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real inner product space, \(f : E \to \mathbb{R}\), \(h \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(\mu\) a measure on \(E\). The Langevin Monte Carlo step sends \(\mu\) to

    \[ \mathrm{lmcStep}(f, h, \mu) = (\mu \otimes \mathcal N(0, I))\circ T^{-1}, \qquad T(\vartheta, \xi) = \vartheta - h\nabla f(\vartheta) + \sqrt{2h}\,\xi : \]

    if \(\vartheta \sim \mu\) and \(\xi\) is an independent standard Gaussian vector, it is the law of \(\vartheta - h\nabla f(\vartheta) + \sqrt{2h}\,\xi\). Iterating it from the law \(\nu_0\) of \(\vartheta_0\) gives the laws \(\nu_k\) of the iterates of the update rule \(\vartheta_{k+1} = \vartheta_k - h\nabla f(\vartheta_k) + \sqrt{2h}\,\xi_{k+1}\) with i.i.d. standard Gaussian \(\xi_k\) independent of \(\vartheta_0\).

    noncomputable def lmcStep (f : E → ℝ) (h : ℝ) (μ : Measure E) : Measure E :=
  (μ.prod (stdGaussian E)).map
    (fun q : E × E => q.1 - h • gradient f q.1 + Real.sqrt (2 * h) • q.2)

    MathlibExt.Probability.LangevinMonteCarlo.lmcStep_dirac

    Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real inner product space, \(f : E \to \mathbb{R}\) with continuous gradient, \(h \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(x \in E\). Then

    \[ \mathrm{lmcStep}(f, h, \delta_x) = \mathcal N(0, I) \circ \bigl(\xi \mapsto x - h\nabla f(x) + \sqrt{2h}\,\xi\bigr)^{-1}, \]

    which for \(h \ge 0\) is the Gaussian law \(\mathcal N(x - h\nabla f(x),\, 2h\, I)\): the transition kernel of the algorithm is a gradient-descent step followed by Gaussian noise of variance \(2h\).

    theorem lmcStep_dirac {f : E → ℝ} (hf : Continuous (gradient f)) (h : ℝ) (x : E) :
    lmcStep f h (Measure.dirac x) =
      (stdGaussian E).map (fun ξ : E => x - h • gradient f x + Real.sqrt (2 * h) • ξ)
    noncomputable def lmcStep (f : E → ℝ) (h : ℝ) (μ : Measure E) : Measure E :=

    MathlibExt.Probability.LangevinMonteCarloWanted.wasserstein_lmc_le

    Let \(p \ge 1\) and \(f : \mathbb{R}^p \to \mathbb{R}\) be differentiable with, for some \(m, M > 0\) and all \(\theta, \theta'\),

    \[ f(\theta) - f(\theta') - \nabla f(\theta')^\top(\theta - \theta') \ge \tfrac m2\|\theta - \theta'\|^2, \qquad \|\nabla f(\theta) - \nabla f(\theta')\| \le M\|\theta - \theta'\| , \]

    and let \(\pi\) have density \(e^{-f}/\int e^{-f}\). Let \(0 < h \le 2/(m+M)\) and let \(\nu_k\) be the law of the \(k\)-th Langevin Monte Carlo iterate, started from a law \(\nu_0\) with finite second moment. Then for every \(K \ge 0\),

    \[ W_2(\nu_K, \pi) \le (1 - mh)^K\, W_2(\nu_0, \pi) + 1.82\,\frac{M}{m}\,(hp)^{1/2} . \]

    The first term is the contraction of gradient descent on the \(m\)-strongly convex, \(M\)-smooth \(f\); the second is the bias of the Euler discretization of the Langevin diffusion, which does not vanish as \(K \to \infty\). To reach \(W_2(\nu_K, \pi) \le \varepsilon\) it suffices to take \(h \asymp m^2\varepsilon^2/(M^2 p)\) and \(K \asymp (p/\varepsilon^2)\log(p/\varepsilon)\) iterations. The finite second moment of \(\nu_0\), implicit in the source, rules out the indeterminate product \(0\cdot\infty\) when \(mh = 1\).

    Background
    Background
    • Wasserstein distance: the metric \(W_2\) in which the bound is stated.
    • Linear convergence of gradient descent for strongly convex functions: the noiseless analogue; Dalalyan recovers \(\|\theta_K - \theta^\star\| \le (1 - m/M)^K\|\theta_0 - \theta^\star\|\) from this bound in the zero-temperature limit (Section 3 of the source).
    • Convergence of the JKO scheme to the Fokker–Planck equation: the law of the Langevin diffusion is the Wasserstein gradient flow of the free energy, of which this algorithm is a discretization.
    • Talagrand's transport inequality: strongly log-concave targets satisfy \(W_2^2 \le (2/m)\,\mathrm{KL}\), which converts the relative-entropy bounds of Cheng and Bartlett into \(W_2\) bounds.
    Sources
    Sources

    • A. Durmus and É. Moulines, High-dimensional Bayesian inference via the unadjusted Langevin algorithm, arXiv:1605.01559 (Bernoulli 25 (2019)): the earlier Wasserstein bound, recalled as (6) in Dalalyan's paper.

    Related topics

    • Markov chain Monte Carlo: exact-invariance samplers (Metropolis–Hastings, Gibbs) on finite spaces; Langevin Monte Carlo has no accept/reject step, so its invariant law is only close to the target.
    • Wasserstein gradient flows and the JKO scheme: the Langevin diffusion as the \(W_2\) gradient flow of the free energy.
    • Transport-cost inequalities: the log-Sobolev and Talagrand inequalities that give exponential convergence of the diffusion in relative entropy and in \(W_2\).
    • Accelerated gradient methods: the optimization side of the same continuous-time view of algorithms.
    • Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process: the Langevin diffusion for a quadratic potential, the Gaussian case in which the target of the algorithm is a Gaussian.
    • Fokker–Planck equation: the equation of the law of the Langevin diffusion, whose free energy decreases along the flow.
