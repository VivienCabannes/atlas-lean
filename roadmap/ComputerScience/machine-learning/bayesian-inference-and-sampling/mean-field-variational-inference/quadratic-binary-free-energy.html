    Quadratic binary mean-field free energy

    Let \(d \ge 1\), let \(U\) be a real symmetric \(d \times d\) matrix with operator norm \(\|U\| \le a < 1\) for the Euclidean norm, and let \(h \in \mathbb{R}^d\). For \(t \in [-1, 1]\) let

    \[H(t) = -\frac{1 + t}{2} \log \frac{1 + t}{2} - \frac{1 - t}{2} \log \frac{1 - t}{2},\]

    with \(0 \log 0 = 0\), be the entropy of a \(\pm 1\) variable with mean \(t\); it is continuous on \([-1, 1]\) and \(H(\pm 1) = 0\). On the closed cube \([-1, 1]^d\) define

    \[F(m) = -\sum_{i=1}^{d} H(m_i) - \tfrac{1}{2} \langle m, U m \rangle - \langle h, m \rangle .\]

    Then:

    • (a) On the open cube \((-1, 1)^d\), \(F\) is smooth and \(\nabla F(m) = \operatorname{artanh}(m) - U m - h\), with \(\operatorname{artanh}\) applied coordinatewise.
    • (b) On the open cube, \(\nabla^2 F(m) = \operatorname{diag}\bigl((1 - m_i^2)^{-1}\bigr) - U \succeq (1 - a) I\), so \(F\) is strongly convex there.
    • (c) \(F\) has exactly one critical point in the open cube. It is the unique fixed point \(m^\star\) of \(m \mapsto \tanh(U m + h)\) and the unique minimizer of \(F\) over the open cube.

    Proof: (a) For \(t \in (-1, 1)\), \(H'(t) = -\tfrac{1}{2} \log \frac{1 + t}{1 - t} = -\operatorname{artanh}(t)\). The gradient of \(\tfrac{1}{2} \langle m, U m \rangle\) is \(\tfrac{1}{2} (U + U^{\mathsf{T}}) m = U m\) because \(U\) is symmetric; without symmetry the gradient equation would involve \(\tfrac{1}{2} (U + U^{\mathsf{T}})\) instead. (b) \(\operatorname{artanh}'(t) = (1 - t^2)^{-1} \ge 1\) on \((-1, 1)\), and \(U \preceq \|U\| I \preceq a I\) because \(U\) is symmetric, so \(\nabla^2 F(m) \succeq I - a I\). (c) By (a), \(m\) in the open cube is a critical point exactly when \(\operatorname{artanh}(m) = U m + h\), which holds exactly when \(m\) is a fixed point of \(T(m) = \tanh(U m + h)\). Since \(\tanh\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz and \(\|U\| \le a < 1\), \(T\) is a contraction of \(\mathbb{R}^d\) with constant \(a\). Banach's fixed-point theorem on the complete space \(\mathbb{R}^d\) gives a unique fixed point, and it lies in the open cube because every value of \(\tanh\) does; equivalently, \(T\) maps the complete closed cube into its interior, so it is a contraction of the closed cube with the same fixed point. A strongly convex function on the convex open cube attains its minimum over the cube at any critical point, and only there. No coercivity of \(F\) is used: \(F\) stays bounded near the boundary of the cube; only its gradient diverges.

    This page does not claim that \(m^\star\) minimizes \(F\) over the closed cube; that would need a separate argument.

    Ising product law (derived). Let \(A\) be a real symmetric \(d \times d\) matrix, let \(p(\sigma) \propto \exp\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2} \sigma^{\mathsf{T}} A \sigma + h^{\mathsf{T}} \sigma\bigr)\) on \(\{-1, 1\}^d\), and let \(q\) be a product law on \(\{-1, 1\}^d\) with means \(m_i\). Under \(q\), \(\mathbb{E}[\sigma_i \sigma_j] = m_i m_j\) for \(i \ne j\) but \(\mathbb{E}[\sigma_i^2] = 1\). Hence \(\mathbb{E}_q\bigl[\tfrac{1}{2} \sigma^{\mathsf{T}} A \sigma\bigr] = \tfrac{1}{2} m^{\mathsf{T}} A m + \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_i A_{ii} (1 - m_i^2)\), and the evidence lower bound of \(q\) against the unnormalized weights satisfies

    \[-\mathrm{ELBO}(q) = F_A(m) - \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^{d} A_{ii} (1 - m_i^2) = F_{A - \operatorname{diag}(A)}(m) - \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^{d} A_{ii},\]

    where \(F_U\) denotes \(F\) with coupling \(U\). So the product-law stationarity equation is \(\operatorname{artanh}(m) = (A - \operatorname{diag}(A)) m + h\), and (c) applies when \(\|A - \operatorname{diag}(A)\| \le a < 1\). The diagonal correction belongs to this link only; the statements (a)–(c) hold for any symmetric \(U\) with \(\|U\| \le a < 1\), whatever its diagonal. The two-factor coordinate ascent on this page's topic is a different setting and is not used here.

    This page is an AI-authored exposition. The free energy, its gradient and Hessian, its minimizer and the Ising link have no Lean formalization in this library; the fixed-point statements in (c) are formalized, as listed below.

    Informally uses

    • Fixed points of the mean-field map are the solutions of artanh m = U m + h: mathematical prerequisite, the equivalence of the critical-point equation in (c) with the fixed-point equation, for \(U\) viewed as a linear map on \(\mathbb{R}^d\) with the Euclidean norm.
    • Existence and uniqueness of the mean-field fixed point: mathematical prerequisite, the unique fixed point in (c).
    • The mean-field map is a contraction for a coupling of norm below one: mathematical prerequisite, the contraction in (c).
