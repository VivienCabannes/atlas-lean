    DefinitionAlternating breadth-first layers#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.BipartiteMatching.layers

    Let \(\mu\) give each right vertex \(j\) of a bipartite graph at most one partner \(\mu(j)\) among the left vertices. The layers are pairs \((Z_k, R_k)\) of a set \(Z_k\) of left vertices and a set \(R_k\) of right vertices, defined by recursion on \(k\): \(Z_0\) is the set of unmatched left vertices (those that are the partner of no right vertex) and \(R_0 = \emptyset\); \(R_{k+1}\) is the set of right vertices \(j\) adjacent to some \(i \in Z_k\) with \(\mu(j) \ne i\), and \(Z_{k+1}\) is \(Z_k\) together with the left vertices \(\mu(j)\) for \(j \in R_{k+1}\).

    The layers are cumulative on both sides: \(Z_k\) holds the left vertices reached within \(k\) rounds and \(R_k\) the right vertices reached within \(k\) rounds. The round where a vertex is first reached is its breadth-first level.

    def layers (mate : Fin b → Option (Fin a)) : ℕ → Finset (Fin a) × Finset (Fin b)
  | 0 => (Finset.univ.filter fun i => Free mate i, ∅)
  | k + 1 =>
      let Z := (layers mate k).1
      let R := Finset.univ.filter fun j => ∃ i ∈ Z, j ∈ adj i ∧ mate j ≠ some i
      (Z ∪ Finset.univ.filter (fun i => ∃ j ∈ R, mate j = some i), R)

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.BipartiteMatching.pathTo

    Given the adjacency lists \(\mathrm{adj}\) of a bipartite graph, a partner assignment \(\mathrm{mate}\), a level \(k\) and a right vertex \(j\), the walk back from \(j\) at level \(k\) is a list of pairs defined by recursion on \(k\). At level \(0\) it is \([(i, j)]\) for the least unmatched left vertex \(i\) with \(j \in \mathrm{adj}(i)\) and \(\mathrm{mate}(j) \ne i\), and empty if there is none. At level \(k + 1\), if \(j\) is reached within \(k + 1\) rounds of the alternating layered search, it is the walk back from \(j\) at level \(k\). Otherwise take the least left vertex \(i\) reached within \(k + 1\) rounds with \(j \in \mathrm{adj}(i)\) and \(\mathrm{mate}(j) \ne i\) (the walk is empty if there is none): if no right vertex has partner \(i\) the walk is \([(i, j)]\), and otherwise it is \((i, j)\) followed by the walk back at level \(k\) from the least right vertex \(j'\) with \(\mathrm{mate}(j') = i\).

    When \(j\) is reached within \(k + 1\) rounds, the walk descends to the round where \(j\) is first reached and lists the pairs to match, \(j\) first.
