    Zero-knowledge proof of quadratic residuosity

    The first zero-knowledge proof system (Goldwasser, Micali and Rackoff, Section 5). To prove that \(y\) is a quadratic residue modulo \(x\) without revealing a square root, the prover sends a random quadratic residue \(u\); the verifier sends a random bit; the prover answers with a square root of \(u\) (bit \(0\)) or of \(u y\) (bit \(1\)); the verifier checks the square; this is repeated \(m\) times. A cheating prover for a non-residue cannot have both \(u\) and \(u y\) be squares, so it fails each round with probability at least \(\tfrac12\) (Claim 1); and the protocol is perfectly zero knowledge (Theorem 1).

    Here the protocol is stated in any finite commutative group (the units modulo \(x\) in the paper), as a Sigma protocol in the framework of zero-knowledge proofs, with soundness error \(2^{-m}\), perfect special honest-verifier zero knowledge with the paper's simulator, and, against an arbitrary verifier in one round, a rewinding simulator whose \(k\) attempts reproduce every test of the verifier's view within \(2^{-k}\). Not formalized: the composition of the simulation over \(m\) rounds, perfect simulation in expected polynomial time, and the proof for quadratic non-residuosity (Section 6).

    DefinitionThe quadratic-residuosity protocol as a Sigma protocol#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Crypto.Identification.QR.sigma

    In a commutative group \(G\): a statement is an element \(y\) and a witness is an \(s\) with \(s^2 = y\). The prover draws \(r\) and commits to \(u = r^2\); on the bit \(0\) it answers \(r\) and on the bit \(1\) it answers \(r s\); the verifier accepts an answer \(w\) when \(w^2 = u\) (bit \(0\)) or \(w^2 = u y\) (bit \(1\)).

    def sigma : SigmaProtocol G G G G Bool G where
  Rel y s := s ^ 2 = y
  commit _ _ r := r ^ 2
  respond _ s r b := if b then r * s else r
  verify y u b w := decide (w ^ 2 = if b then u * y else u)

    For the statement \(y\) and the bit \(b\): draw a uniform \(w\) and output the commitment \(w^2\) (bit \(0\)) or \(w^2 y^{-1}\) (bit \(1\)) together with the answer \(w\).

    TheoremZero knowledge of the quadratic-residuosity protocol against an arbitrary verifier, one round#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Crypto.Identification.QR.abs_winProb_simulatorK_sub_le_half_pow

    Let \(s^2 = y\). For every verifier whose bit is any function of the commitment \(u\) and of its coins, every test \(T\) of its view and every \(k\), the rewinding simulator with \(k\) attempts, which uses GMR's simulator and never sees \(s\), and the real interaction produce views passing \(T\) with probabilities that differ by at most \(2^{-k}\).

    Goldwasser, Micali and Rackoff's Theorem 1 asserts more: perfect zero knowledge of the \(m\)-round protocol, with a simulator that repeats attempts until success.

    theorem abs_winProb_simulatorK_sub_le_half_pow {R : Type*} [Fintype R] [Nonempty R] {y s : G}
    (hs : s ^ 2 = y) (Vstar : G → R → Bool) (T : R × G × G → Bool) (k : ℕ) :
    |winProb ((SigmaProtocol.simulatorK simAt y Vstar k).map fun o => o.elim false T) -
        winProb ((sigma.realViewDist y s Vstar).map T)| ≤ 1 / 2 ^ k
    noncomputable def winProb (G : PMF Bool) : ℝ := (G true).toReal
