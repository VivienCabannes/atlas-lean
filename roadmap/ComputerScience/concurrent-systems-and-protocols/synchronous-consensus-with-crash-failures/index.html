    Synchronous consensus with crash failures

    Consensus in synchronous rounds when processes may crash. The model on these pages: lock-step rounds in which every process sends one message to every process; crash (stopping) failures, a process crashing in some round delivering its messages of that round to an arbitrary subset of the processes and nothing afterwards; a complete network of reliable links on which a receiver knows the sender; deterministic protocols deciding after a fixed number of rounds. Consensus asks for agreement among correct processes and validity (a decided value is some process's input). Tolerating \(f\) crashes needs \(f + 1\) rounds when there are at least \(f + 2\) processes.

    StructureSynchronous protocol with crash failures#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Distributed.CrashConsensus.CrashProtocol

    A protocol for the processes G with inputs in V, local states σ and messages M. Process i starts in state init i x from its input x; in round r process j in state s sends send r j s i to process i, and process i in state s moves to recv r i s msg, where msg j is the message it received from j, or none if it received none; at the end process i decides decide i s.

    Model. Synchronous lock-step rounds over a complete network of reliable links; a receiver sees, for each sender, either its message or nothing, and knows the sender. Crashes are given separately by a crash pattern. Protocols are deterministic, with one state type for all processes.

    structure CrashProtocol (G V σ M : Type*) where
  /-- The initial state of each process, given its input. -/
  init : G → V → σ
  /-- `send r j s i` is the message process `j`, in state `s`, sends to `i` in round `r`. -/
  send : ℕ → G → σ → G → M
  /-- `recv r i s msg` is the state of process `i` after round `r`, from its state `s` and the
  message `msg j` it received from each process `j` (`none` if it received none). -/
  recv : ℕ → G → σ → (G → Option M) → σ
  /-- `decide i s` is the value process `i` decides in its final state `s`. -/
  decide : G → σ → V

    AbbreviationCrash pattern#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Distributed.CrashConsensus.CrashPattern

    π j = some (ρ, D) when process j crashes in round ρ, its messages of that round reaching exactly the processes of D; π j = none when j is correct.

    This is the crash model of Aguilera and Toueg (1999, §1): a process that crashes in a round sends a subset of the messages it intends to send in that round and does not execute later rounds. A crash in a round \(\rho \ge T\), after the decisions, makes the process faulty without changing anything it sends before the end.

    Q.state x π r i is the state of process i after r rounds of Q with inputs x and crash pattern π.

    def state (x : G → V) (π : CrashPattern G) : ℕ → G → σ
  | 0, i => Q.init i (x i)
  | r + 1, i => Q.recv r i (state x π r i) fun j =>
      if π.delivers r j i then some (Q.send r j (state x π r j) i) else none

    DefinitionSolving consensus with crash failures#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Distributed.CrashConsensus.CrashProtocol.Solves

    Q solves consensus tolerating f crashes in T rounds: for all inputs and every crash pattern with at most f faulty processes, no two correct processes decide differently (agreement), and the value decided by a correct process is the input of some process (validity).

    Termination is built in: every correct process decides at the end of round \(T\). Faulty processes are not required to decide or to agree (agreement is not uniform).

    def Solves [Fintype G] (f T : ℕ) : Prop :=
  ∀ (x : G → V) (π : CrashPattern G), π.faulty.card ≤ f →
    (∀ i j, π i = none → π j = none → Q.decision x π T i = Q.decision x π T j) ∧
      ∀ i, π i = none → ∃ j, x j = Q.decision x π T i
    abbrev CrashPattern (G : Type*) := G → Option (ℕ × Finset G)

    Cslib.Distributed.CrashConsensus.CrashProtocol.not_solves_zero

    With at least two processes and at least two values, no protocol solves consensus in zero rounds, even when no process crashes: each process would decide a function of its own input, validity forces that function to be the identity, and two processes with different inputs then disagree.     Cslib.Distributed.CrashConsensus.CrashProtocol.SolvesOnePerRound

    Q solves consensus in T rounds against every admissible crash pattern for T rounds (crashes before round T, at most one per round): no two correct processes decide differently, and the value decided by a correct process is the input of some process.     TheoremConsensus against \(T\) crashes, at most one per round, needs more than \(T\) rounds#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Distributed.CrashConsensus.CrashProtocol.not_solvesOnePerRound

    If there are at least \(T + 2\) processes and at least two values, no deterministic synchronous protocol solves consensus in \(T\) rounds against the crash patterns whose crashes happen before round \(T\), at most one per round. This is Theorem 1 of Aguilera and Toueg (1999) with \(t = T\): "Consider a synchronous round-based system S with n processes and at most t crash failures such that at most one process crashes in each round. If n > t + 1 then there is no algorithm that solves consensus in t rounds in S."

    Relation to the source. Aguilera and Toueg's system also allows crashes in rounds \(T\) and later, which change nothing before the decisions; the hypothesis here quantifies only over crashes before round \(T\), so it is weaker than theirs and the theorem is at least as strong as their Theorem 1. With a single process the hypothesis on \(n\) fails and consensus is solvable (see the test module).

    Model. Synchronous rounds; crash failures as in Crash pattern; a complete network of reliable links with known senders; deterministic protocols deciding at the end of round \(T\).

    Proof. The bivalency proof of Aguilera and Toueg. If \(T = 0\) the argument is that of Consensus needs a round. Otherwise: every partial run up to round \(T - 1\) is univalent (their Lemma 2); some initial configuration is bivalent (their Lemma 3, the chain argument of Fischer, Lynch and Paterson with a process crashed at the start); and a bivalent partial run up to a round \(k < T - 1\) has a bivalent one-round extension (their Lemma 4, crashing a process in round \(k\) and, to hide who heard it, another one silently in round \(k + 1\)). Together they give a bivalent partial run up to round \(T - 1\), a contradiction.

    theorem not_solvesOnePerRound [Fintype G] [Nontrivial V] (Q : CrashProtocol G V σ M) {T : ℕ}
    (hG : T + 2 ≤ Fintype.card G) : ¬ Q.SolvesOnePerRound T

    TheoremConsensus with \(f\) crash failures needs \(f + 1\) rounds#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Distributed.CrashConsensus.CrashProtocol.f_add_one_le_of_solves

    If there are at least \(f + 2\) processes and at least two values, and a deterministic synchronous protocol solves consensus (agreement and validity, every correct process deciding at the end of round \(T\)) tolerating \(f\) crash failures, then \(T \ge f + 1\).

    Model. Synchronous rounds; crash failures (a process crashing in round \(\rho\) delivers its round-\(\rho\) messages to an arbitrary subset and nothing later); a complete network of reliable links with known senders; deterministic protocols. Validity is that of Aguilera and Toueg: a value decided by a correct process is the input of some process.

    Relation to the source. Aguilera and Toueg (1999, Theorem 1) state the bound for systems in which at most one process crashes per round, which is stronger than this statement, where the adversary may crash several processes in a round. Their form is proved as Consensus against \(T\) crashes, at most one per round, needs more than \(T\) rounds, and this statement follows from it. The hypothesis \(n \ge f + 2\) is theirs (\(n > t + 1\)); it provides two correct processes in their Lemma 2. Lynch's survey A hundred impossibility proofs for distributed computing (PODC 1989) recounts the history of the bound for stopping faults (unpublished notes of Hadzilacos, of Fischer and Lamport, and of Merritt, then Dwork and Moses); the Byzantine version is Theorem 5 of Fischer and Lynch (1982).

    Proof. If \(T \le f\), the protocol solves consensus in particular against the patterns with at most one crash per round before round \(T\), which is impossible by the bivalency proof of Aguilera and Toueg. Their proof: if \(T = 0\) the argument is that of Consensus needs a round; otherwise every partial run up to round \(T - 1\) is univalent (their Lemma 2); some initial configuration is bivalent (their Lemma 3, the chain argument of Fischer, Lynch and Paterson with a process crashed at the start); and a bivalent partial run up to a round \(k < T - 1\) has a bivalent one-round extension (their Lemma 4, crashing a process in round \(k\) and, to hide who heard it, another one silently in round \(k + 1\)). Together they give a bivalent partial run up to round \(T - 1\), a contradiction.
