    Input/output automata

    The input/output automaton model of Lynch and Tuttle: a labelled transition system whose actions are split into inputs, outputs and internal actions, and which accepts every input in every state. Automata compose by synchronizing on shared actions, each output being controlled by one component only. The finite schedules and behaviours (traces) of a composition are exactly the sequences whose projections are schedules and behaviours of the components, which makes trace semantics compositional. These pages treat the composition of two automata and finite executions; the composition of countable families, fairness (the partition of locally controlled actions) and infinite executions are not formalized.

    Cslib.IOAutomaton

    An action signature, a nonempty set of start states and a labelled transition system whose transitions are labelled by actions of the signature and which is input-enabled: every input action is enabled in every state (Lynch and Tuttle, 1989, §3.1). Input-enabledness expresses that an automaton cannot block its environment: it controls only its outputs and internal actions.

    Relation to the source. Lynch and Tuttle's automata also carry a partition of the locally controlled (output and internal) actions, used to define fair executions. It is not included, so nothing on these pages concerns fairness.

    structure IOAutomaton (α σ : Type*) where
  /-- The action signature. -/
  sig : ActionSig α
  /-- The start states. -/
  start : Set σ
  /-- The transitions. -/
  lts : LTS σ α
  start_nonempty : start.Nonempty
  mem_acts_of_tr : ∀ s a s', lts.Tr s a s' → a ∈ sig.acts
  input_enabled : ∀ s, ∀ a ∈ sig.inp, ∃ s', lts.Tr s a s'

    DefinitionComposition of I/O automata#✓fully proved

    Cslib.IOAutomaton.compose

    The composition of two strongly compatible automata (Lynch and Tuttle, 1989, §3.2, for two automata). Its states are pairs of states. An action of the composition moves each component that has it in its signature by one of its transitions, and leaves the other component unchanged; a shared action is therefore taken simultaneously by both components, which is how an output of one is received as an input by the other. Its signature is the composition of the two signatures, and it is again input-enabled.

    Relation to the source. Lynch and Tuttle compose countable families; only pairs are formalized. Composition does not hide the actions shared by the components: they stay outputs of the composition, as in the source, where hiding is a separate operation (not formalized).

    def compose (A₁ : IOAutomaton α σ₁) (A₂ : IOAutomaton α σ₂)
    (h : A₁.sig.StronglyCompatible A₂.sig) : IOAutomaton α (σ₁ × σ₂) where
  sig := A₁.sig.compose A₂.sig h
  start := A₁.start ×ˢ A₂.start
  lts := ⟨fun s a t => a ∈ A₁.sig.acts ∪ A₂.sig.acts ∧
    A₁.MoveOrStay s.1 a t.1 ∧ A₂.MoveOrStay s.2 a t.2⟩
  start_nonempty := A₁.start_nonempty.prod A₂.start_nonempty
  mem_acts_of_tr := by
    rintro s a t ⟨ha | ha, -⟩
    · rcases ha with (hi | ho) | hn
      · by_cases hc : a ∈ A₁.sig.out ∪ A₂.sig.out
        · exact Or.inl (Or.inr hc)
        · exact Or.inl (Or.inl ⟨Or.inl hi, hc⟩)
      · exact Or.inl (Or.inr (Or.inl ho))
      · exact Or.inr (Or.inl hn)
    · rcases ha with (hi | ho) | hn
      · by_cases hc : a ∈ A₁.sig.out ∪ A₂.sig.out
        · exact Or.inl (Or.inr hc)
        · exact Or.inl (Or.inl ⟨Or.inr hi, hc⟩)
      · exact Or.inl (Or.inr (Or.inr ho))
      · exact Or.inr (Or.inr hn)
  input_enabled := by
    rintro ⟨s₁, s₂⟩ a ⟨hi, hno⟩
    -- An input of the composition that is an action of a component is an input of it.
    have key : ∀ {S : ActionSig α}, a ∈ S.acts → a ∉ S.out → a ∉ S.int → a ∈ S.inp := by
      rintro S ((h | h) | h) h' h''
      exacts [h, absurd h h', absurd h h'']
    have hni₁ : a ∉ A₁.sig.int := fun hn => by
      rcases hi with hi | hi
      · exact Set.disjoint_left.1 A₁.sig.disjoint_inp_int hi hn
      · exact Set.disjoint_left.1 h.disjoint_int_left hn (Or.inl (Or.inl hi))
    have hni₂ : a ∉ A₂.sig.int := fun hn => by
      rcases hi with hi | hi
      · exact Set.disjoint_left.1 h.disjoint_int_right hn (Or.inl (Or.inl hi))
      · exact Set.disjoint_left.1 A₂.sig.disjoint_inp_int hi hn
    have e₁ : ∃ t₁, A₁.MoveOrStay s₁ a t₁ := by
      by_cases ha : a ∈ A₁.sig.acts
      · obtain ⟨t₁, ht₁⟩ := A₁.input_enabled s₁ a (key ha (fun ho => hno (Or.inl ho)) hni₁)
        exact ⟨t₁, fun _ => ht₁, fun h' => absurd ha h'⟩
      · exact ⟨s₁, fun h' => absurd h' ha, fun _ => rfl⟩
    have e₂ : ∃ t₂, A₂.MoveOrStay s₂ a t₂ := by
      by_cases ha : a ∈ A₂.sig.acts
      · obtain ⟨t₂, ht₂⟩ := A₂.input_enabled s₂ a (key ha (fun ho => hno (Or.inr ho)) hni₂)
        exact ⟨t₂, fun _ => ht₂, fun h' => absurd ha h'⟩
      · exact ⟨s₂, fun h' => absurd h' ha, fun _ => rfl⟩
    obtain ⟨t₁, ht₁⟩ := e₁
    obtain ⟨t₂, ht₂⟩ := e₂
    refine ⟨(t₁, t₂), ?_, ht₁, ht₂⟩
    rcases hi with hi | hi
    · exact Or.inl (Or.inl (Or.inl hi))
    · exact Or.inr (Or.inl (Or.inl hi))

    TheoremPasting behaviours of the components of a composition#✓fully proved

    Cslib.IOAutomaton.beh_of_proj

    A finite sequence \(\beta\) of external actions of a composition whose projections onto the two components are behaviours of the components is a behaviour of the composition (Lynch and Tuttle, 1989, Proposition 3, which they state for schedules and then "when acts() and scheds() are replaced by ext() and behs()"; here for two automata and finite behaviours). With the projection of behaviours, the finite behaviours of a composition are exactly the sequences of its external actions whose projections are behaviours of the components: trace semantics is compositional.

    Proof. Take executions of the components with the two behaviours and interleave them: a leading internal action of either component is taken alone, which is possible because internal actions of one component are not actions of the other (strong compatibility); otherwise the next action of \(\beta\) is taken by every component that has it. The induction is on the total length of \(\beta\) and the two executions.

    theorem beh_of_proj {β : List α} (hβ : ∀ a ∈ β, a ∈ (A₁.compose A₂ h).sig.ext)
    (h₁ : A₁.Beh (A₁.sig.proj β)) (h₂ : A₂.Beh (A₂.sig.proj β)) :
    (A₁.compose A₂ h).Beh β
