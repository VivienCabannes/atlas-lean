    Let \(W\) be a linearly ordered type, let \(W \cup \{\top\}\) be \(W\) with a new greatest element \(\top\) adjoined, and let \(n\) be a natural number. An indexed minimum queue over the identities \(0, \dots, n-1\) consists of a binary min-heap whose entries are pairs \((k, v)\) of a key \(k \in W \cup \{\top\}\) and an identity \(v < n\), ordered lexicographically (first by key, then by identity), together with a position vector assigning to each identity \(v < n\) either nothing or an index \(i < n\). It is required that the heap has at most \(n\) entries and that, for all identities \(v < n\) and indices \(i < n\), the position of \(v\) is \(i\) exactly when \(i\) is a slot of the heap whose entry has identity \(v\).

    Let \(W\) be a linearly ordered type and let \(W \cup \{\top\}\) be \(W\) with a new greatest element \(\top\) adjoined.     DefinitionExtract-min in an indexed minimum queue#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.IndexedMinQueue.extractMin

    Costs are counted as five event counts: comparisons, swaps, reads of the position vector, writes to the position vector, and entry appends, pops or replacements. Given an indexed minimum queue \(q\), extract-min fails (returns nothing) exactly when the heap of \(q\) is empty, at zero cost. Otherwise let \((k, v)\) be the root entry of the heap. If the heap has one entry, extract-min returns \((k, v)\) together with the queue whose heap is empty and in which \(v\) has no position, at the cost of one position write and one entry pop. If the heap has at least two entries, the root is swapped with the last entry and the positions of both are updated, the last slot is removed, the position of \(v\) is cleared, and the new root is sifted down: while it has a child, let \(c\) be its smaller child (the left one on ties); if \(c\) is smaller than it, the two are swapped and the positions of both are updated, otherwise the sift stops. It returns \((k, v)\) together with the resulting queue, at the cost of one swap, three position writes and one entry pop, plus, at each sift-down step, one comparison between the two children when both exist, one comparison with \(c\), and one swap and two position writes when a swap is made.
