    Randomized rounding for MAX-SAT

    Goemans and Williamson (SIAM J. Discrete Math. 7(4) (1994)) give a \(\frac34\)-approximation for weighted MAX SAT from two simple randomized algorithms. Johnson's assignment sets each variable true with probability \(\frac12\) and satisfies a \(k\)-literal clause with probability \(1 - 2^{-k}\). Randomized rounding solves the linear relaxation (LP), with variables \(y_i\) for the literals and \(z_j\) for the clauses, and sets \(x_i\) true with probability \(y_i\); by the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality it satisfies clause \(j\) with probability at least \(\beta_k z_j\), \(\beta_k = 1 - (1 - 1/k)^k\) (Lemma 3.1). Since \((1 - 2^{-k}) + \beta_k \ge \frac32\) for every \(k\), the better of the two has expected weight at least \(\frac34 Z^*_{LP} \ge \frac34 \mathrm{OPT}\) (Theorem 4.1).

    Proved here, for clauses given as the repository's CNF clauses and satisfaction probabilities in closed form: Johnson's bound, Lemma 3.1, Theorem 4.1, and the comparison with the MAX SAT optimum. The closed forms are the true probabilities, and these results the paper's probabilistic statements, for clauses whose literals use distinct variables. A CNF clause may repeat a variable, and then the closed form is not a probability: for \(x_0 \vee x_0\) with \(p_0 = \frac12\) it is \(\frac34\), not \(\frac12\). Not formalized: the probability space, derandomization, and the other \(\frac34\) algorithms of §5 (rounding with a function \(f(y_i)\)).

    DefinitionSatisfaction probability of a clause#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.Algorithms.Approximation.MaxSat.clauseSatProb

    For a clause \(C\) (a list of literals, as in the repository's CNF clauses) and probabilities \(p_i\),

    \[1 - \prod_{\ell \in C} \left(1 - \Pr[\ell \text{ true}]\right) = 1 - \prod_{i \in P}(1 - p_i) \prod_{i \in N} p_i .\]

    When the literals of \(C\) use distinct variables and the variables are set independently, this is the probability that \(C\) is satisfied. The library works with this closed form; the probability space itself is not formalized, and for clauses that repeat a variable the expression is not a probability.

    noncomputable def clauseSatProb (p : ℕ → ℝ) (C : CNFClause) : ℝ :=
  1 - (C.map fun l => 1 - literalProb p l).prod
    abbrev CNFClause := List (Nat × Bool)

    DefinitionFeasible solutions of the MAX SAT linear relaxation#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.Algorithms.Approximation.MaxSat.LPFeasible

    For clauses \(C_1, \ldots, C_m\), a pair \((y, z)\) is feasible for (LP) when \(0 \le y_i \le 1\) for every variable, \(0 \le z_j \le 1\) and \(z_j \le \sum_{i \in P_j} y_i + \sum_{i \in N_j}(1 - y_i)\) for every clause.     TheoremGoemans–Williamson Lemma 3.1#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.Algorithms.Approximation.MaxSat.roundingFactor_mul_le_clauseSatProb

    Let \(C\) be a clause with \(k\) literals, let \(0 \le y_i \le 1\) on its variables and \(0 \le z \le \min\left(1, \sum_{i \in P} y_i + \sum_{i \in N}(1 - y_i)\right)\). Then the closed-form satisfaction probability of \(C\) under \(p_i = y_i\) is at least \(\beta_k z\). For a clause whose literals use distinct variables, as in the paper, this says that randomized rounding, which sets \(x_i\) true with probability \(y_i\), satisfies \(C\) with probability at least \(\beta_k z\). The proof is the paper's: the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality applied to the factors \(1 - \Pr[\ell \text{ true}]\) gives \(1 - (1 - z/k)^k\), and convexity of \(t \mapsto t^k\) gives \(1 - (1 - z/k)^k \ge \beta_k z\) on \([0, 1]\). The paper's reduction to unnegated variables is not needed: negated literals enter through \(1 - y_i\) directly. The inequality holds for every clause, including one that repeats a variable, but for such a clause the closed form is not a probability (and the relaxation counts the repeated literal twice), so the probabilistic reading needs distinct variables.

    theorem roundingFactor_mul_le_clauseSatProb {y : ℕ → ℝ} {C : CNFClause} {z : ℝ}
    (hy : ∀ l ∈ C, 0 ≤ y l.1 ∧ y l.1 ≤ 1) (hz0 : 0 ≤ z) (hz1 : z ≤ 1)
    (hz : z ≤ lpClauseBound y C) :
    roundingFactor C.length * z ≤ clauseSatProb y C
    abbrev CNFClause := List (Nat × Bool)

    TheoremThe per-clause inequality behind the 3/4 guarantee#✓fully proved

    CSLibExt.Algorithms.Approximation.MaxSat.three_halves_le_johnson_add_roundingFactor

    For every \(k \ge 1\),

    \[\left(1 - 2^{-k}\right) + \beta_k \ge \frac32 .\]

    Equality holds for \(k = 1\) (\(\frac12 + 1\)) and \(k = 2\) (\(\frac34 + \frac34\)). For \(k \ge 3\), \(1 - 2^{-k} \ge \frac78\) and \((1 - 1/k)^k \le e^{-1} \le \frac38\), so the sum is at least \(\frac32\). Johnson's assignment is strong on long clauses and randomized rounding on short ones; this inequality is why their average never loses more than a quarter.

    theorem three_halves_le_johnson_add_roundingFactor {k : ℕ} (hk : 0 < k) :
    (3 / 2 : ℝ) ≤ (1 - (1 / 2 : ℝ) ^ k) + roundingFactor k

    CSLibExt.Algorithms.Approximation.MaxSat.three_quarters_le_max_expectedWeight

    Let the weights be nonnegative and \((y, z)\) feasible for (LP). Let \(\hat W_1\) be the expected weight under \(p_i = 1/2\) and \(\hat W_2\) under \(p_i = y_i\). Then

    \[\max(\hat W_1, \hat W_2) \ge \frac{\hat W_1 + \hat W_2}{2} \ge \frac34 \sum_j w_j z_j .\]

    CSLibExt.Algorithms.Approximation.MaxSat.three_quarters_le_max_expectedWeight_of_optimal

    For nonnegative weights, if \((y, z)\) is an optimal solution of (LP), then for every truth assignment \(a\),

    \[\max(\hat W_1, \hat W_2) \ge \frac34 \sum_{j : a \models C_j} w_j .\]

    Here \(\hat W_1\), \(\hat W_2\) are the closed forms of the expected weights. For instances whose clauses use distinct variables, the paper's setting, they are expected weights, so the better of Johnson's assignment and randomized rounding has expected weight at least \(\frac34\) of the optimum. For clauses that repeat a variable this reading fails: for the single clause \(x_0 \vee x_0\) of weight \(1\), \(y_0 = \frac12\), \(z = 1\) is optimal for the relaxation (which counts the literal twice) and both closed forms equal \(\frac34\), while both roundings satisfy the clause with probability \(\frac12\). Derandomization by conditional probabilities, which turns this into a deterministic \(\frac34\)-approximation algorithm, is not formalized.
