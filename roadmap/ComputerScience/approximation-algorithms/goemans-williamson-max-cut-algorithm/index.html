    Goemans–Williamson MAX-CUT algorithm

    Goemans and Williamson (J. ACM 42(6) (1995)) relax MAX CUT, the problem of maximizing \(\frac12 \sum_{i<j} w_{ij}(1 - y_i y_j)\) over \(y \in \{-1, 1\}^V\), to the vector program (P) that maximizes \(\frac12 \sum_{i<j} w_{ij}(1 - v_i \cdot v_j)\) over unit vectors \(v_i\), a semidefinite program. They round an optimal solution by a random hyperplane through the origin: \(i \in S\) when \(v_i \cdot r \ge 0\). A hyperplane separates \(v_i\) and \(v_j\) with probability \(\arccos(v_i \cdot v_j)/\pi\) (Lemma 3.2), so the expected cut weight is \(\sum_{i<j} w_{ij} \arccos(v_i \cdot v_j)/\pi\) (Theorem 3.1). For nonnegative weights, comparing this term by term with the objective of (P) (Lemma 3.4) gives an expected cut of at least \(\alpha\) times the relaxation value, hence at least \(\alpha \cdot \mathrm{OPT}\), with \(\alpha = \min_{0<\theta\le\pi} \frac{2}{\pi} \frac{\theta}{1-\cos\theta} > 0.87856\) (Theorem 3.3, Lemma 3.5).

    Proved here, for a standard Gaussian direction \(r\) (which the paper notes gives the same cut as a uniform point on the sphere): the relaxation property (\(Z_P \ge\) every cut), Lemma 3.2, Theorem 3.1, the constant \(\alpha\) with Lemma 3.4 and a certified proof of Lemma 3.5, and Theorem 3.3, hence the guarantee \(\mathbb{E}[w(S, \bar S)] \ge \alpha \cdot \mathrm{OPT}\) for vectors attaining \(Z_P\). Also proved: (P) and its semidefinite form, \(\max \frac12\sum_{i<j} w_{ij}(1 - Y_{ij})\) over positive semidefinite \(Y\) with unit diagonal, have the same feasible values when vectors of any dimension are allowed (Gram matrices). Not formalized: the polynomial-time solution of the semidefinite program, and the extensions to MAX 2SAT and MAX DICUT in §7 of the paper.

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Optimization.GoemansWilliamson.quadObjective

    For weights \(w_{ij}\) and a vector \(y \in \mathbb{R}^V\),

    \[\frac{1}{2} \sum_{i<j} w_{ij} (1 - y_i y_j),\]

    DefinitionObjective of the vector relaxation#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Optimization.GoemansWilliamson.vectorObjective

    For vectors \(v_i \in \mathbb{R}^m\),

    \[\frac{1}{2} \sum_{i<j} w_{ij} (1 - v_i \cdot v_j),\]

    DefinitionValue of the vector relaxation#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Optimization.GoemansWilliamson.relaxationValue

    The supremum \(Z_P\) of the relaxation objective over families of unit vectors \(v_i \in \mathbb{R}^m\). Goemans and Williamson take \(m = n = |V|\); any \(m \ge n\) gives the same value, since \(n\) vectors span at most \(n\) dimensions.     MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Optimization.GoemansWilliamson.vectorObjective_cutSign_single

    Let the weights be nonnegative, \(n \ge 1\), \(\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}\), and let unit vectors \(v_i \in \mathbb{R}^n\) have relaxation objective at least \(Z_P - \varepsilon\). Then for every vertex set \(S'\) the random-hyperplane cut \(S = \{i : v_i \cdot r \ge 0\}\) satisfies

    \[\mathbb{E}[w(S, \bar S)] \ge \alpha \left(w(S', \bar{S'}) - \varepsilon\right).\]

    Goemans and Williamson state the guarantee in the slightly stronger form \(\alpha(Z_P - \varepsilon)\): a semidefinite-programming algorithm returns, in time polynomial in the input size and \(\log(1/\varepsilon)\), vectors of value greater than \(Z_P - \varepsilon\), and the cut then has expected weight at least \(\alpha(Z_P - \varepsilon) \ge \alpha(\mathrm{OPT} - \varepsilon)\). The Lean statement gives the consequence for every cut, \(\alpha(w(S', \bar{S'}) - \varepsilon)\), which with \(S'\) a maximum cut is \(\alpha(\mathrm{OPT} - \varepsilon)\). The algorithm is not formalized.
