    DefinitionShifting de Bruijn indices#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.TheoryOfComputation.CoCStrongNormalizationWanted.ProgramVerification.CoCStrongNormalization.lift

    For natural numbers \(d\) and \(c\), shifting a term \(t\) by \(d\) above the cutoff \(c\) adds \(d\) to every variable index \(i \ge c\) and leaves every index \(i < c\) unchanged. The cutoff goes up by one under each binder, that is, in the body of a \(\lambda\) and in the codomain of a \(\Pi\) (not in the type annotation or the domain), and sorts are unchanged. Thus the free variables of \(t\) with index at least \(c\) are increased by \(d\).

  | .srt s => .srt s
  | .app f a => .app (lift d c f) (lift d c a)
  | .lam A b => .lam (lift d c A) (lift d (c + 1) b)

    InductiveProduct rules of CCω#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.TheoryOfComputation.CoCStrongNormalizationWanted.ProgramVerification.CoCStrongNormalization.Rl

    The product rules say in which sort \(s_3\) a dependent product \(\Pi (x : A).\, B\) lives when \(A\) lives in the sort \(s_1\) and \(B\) lives in the sort \(s_2\).     InductiveTyping judgement of the calculus of constructions with universes#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.TheoryOfComputation.CoCStrongNormalizationWanted.ProgramVerification.CoCStrongNormalization.Typing

    A context \(\Gamma\) is a list of terms whose head is the type of the most recent variable; write \(\Gamma, A\) for \(\Gamma\) extended by \(A\) at the head. The judgement \(\Gamma \vdash t : A\) (\(t\) has type \(A\) in \(\Gamma\)) is defined together with well-formedness of contexts as the least relation closed under the following rules.

    • Sorts: if \(\Gamma\) is well formed and \(s : s'\) is an axiom, namely \(\mathrm{Prop} : \mathrm{Type}_0\) or \(\mathrm{Type}_i : \mathrm{Type}_{i+1}\), then \(\Gamma \vdash s : s'\).
    • Variables: if \(\Gamma\) is well formed and its entry at position \(i\) (counting from \(0\) at the head) is \(A\), then \(\Gamma \vdash \#i : A'\), where \(A'\) is \(A\) with every free variable raised by \(i + 1\).
    • Products: if \(\Gamma \vdash A : s_1\), \(\Gamma, A \vdash B : s_2\) and \((s_1, s_2, s_3)\) is a product rule, namely \((s, \mathrm{Prop}, \mathrm{Prop})\) for a sort \(s\), \((\mathrm{Type}_i, \mathrm{Type}_j, \mathrm{Type}_{\max(i, j)})\) or \((\mathrm{Prop}, \mathrm{Type}_i, \mathrm{Type}_i)\), then \(\Gamma \vdash \Pi (x : A).\, B : s_3\).
    • Abstractions: if \(\Gamma \vdash \Pi (x : A).\, B : s\) for some sort \(s\) and \(\Gamma, A \vdash b : B\), then \(\Gamma \vdash \lambda (x : A).\, b : \Pi (x : A).\, B\).
    • Applications: if \(\Gamma \vdash f : \Pi (x : A).\, B\) and \(\Gamma \vdash a : A\), then \(\Gamma \vdash f\,a : B[a]\), where \(B[a]\) replaces variable \(0\) of \(B\) by \(a\) and lowers the other free variables of \(B\) by one.
    • Conversion: if \(\Gamma \vdash t : A\), \(\Gamma \vdash B : s\) for some sort \(s\), and \(A\) and \(B\) are beta convertible, then \(\Gamma \vdash t : B\).
