    Rounding error analysis

    Rounding error analysis bounds the error of an algorithm run in floating point, from the model \(\mathrm{fl}(a \mathbin{\mathrm{op}} b) = (a \mathbin{\mathrm{op}} b)(1 + \delta)\), \(|\delta| \le u\), of floating-point rounding. Its central notion is backward stability: an algorithm is backward stable if its computed result is the exact result for slightly perturbed data. The error of a backward stable algorithm is then at most its backward error times the condition number of the problem.

    A sum of \(n\) rounded products is accurate to \(\gamma_n = nu/(1 - nu)\) relative to \(\sum_i |x_i y_i|\), so a floating-point matrix product satisfies \(|\mathrm{fl}(AB) - AB| \le \gamma_n |A|\,|B|\) entrywise. The same analysis applied to the Doolittle formulas shows that Gaussian elimination computes the exact LU factors of \(A + E\) with \(|E| \le \gamma_n |\hat L|\,|\hat U|\): with the two triangular solves, the computed solution of \(Ax = b\) solves \((A + F)\hat x = b\) with \(|F| \le (3\gamma_n + \gamma_n^2)|\hat L|\,|\hat U|\), so Gaussian elimination is backward stable when \(|\hat L|\,|\hat U|\) is not much larger than \(A\), which partial pivoting achieves in practice. For Cholesky factorization \(|L|\,|L^T|\) is always bounded by the largest diagonal entry of \(A\), so no pivoting is needed. The statements follow J. W. Demmel's Math 221 notes, which give the bounds to first order in the machine precision; the exact \(\gamma_n\) forms are those of N. J. Higham, Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms, 2nd ed., SIAM, 2002.

    Let \(u = 2^{-p}\) be the unit roundoff of \(p\)-bit round to nearest and let \(n u < 1\). The floating-point dot product \(\hat s\) of \(x\) and \(y\), computed by adding the rounded products \(\mathrm{fl}(x_i y_i)\) from left to right and rounding each addition, satisfies

    \[\Big|\hat s - \sum_{i<n} x_i y_i\Big| \le \gamma_n \sum_{i<n} |x_i y_i|, \qquad \gamma_n = \frac{n u}{1 - n u}.\]

    J. W. Demmel's Math 221 course (UC Berkeley, Lecture 5 notes, 2024, recalling homework 1.10) uses this bound, in its first-order form \(n u \sum |x_i y_i|\), for matrix multiplication and for the backward error analysis of LU factorization. The exact form with \(\gamma_n\) is the standard one of N. J. Higham, Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms, 2nd ed., SIAM, 2002. The relative error is small when there is little cancellation, that is when \(|\sum x_i y_i|\) is comparable to \(\sum |x_i y_i|\).

    Proof. By induction on \(n\). The first partial sum is the single rounding \(\mathrm{fl}(x_0 y_0)\), since adding it to \(0\) is exact, and \(u \le \gamma_1\). If the partial sum \(s_k\) of \(k \ge 1\) terms has error \(e_k\) with \(|e_k| \le \gamma_k T_k\), \(T_k = \sum_{i<k} |x_i y_i|\), then \(s_{k+1} = \mathrm{fl}(s_k + \mathrm{fl}(x_k y_k))\) has error at most \((u + (1 + u)\gamma_k) T_k + (2u + u^2)|x_k y_k| \le \gamma_{k+1} T_{k+1}\).

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.FloatingPoint.LUBackwardError.IsFlLU

    IsFlLU p A L U holds when \(L\) is unit lower triangular, \(U\) is upper triangular, and their entries satisfy Doolittle's formulas evaluated in \(p\)-bit floating point:

    \[U_{ij} = \mathrm{fl}\Big(A_{ij} - \sum_{k<i} L_{ik} U_{kj}\Big) \ (i \le j), \qquad L_{ij} = \mathrm{fl}\Big(\mathrm{fl}\Big(A_{ij} - \sum_{k<j} L_{ik} U_{kj}\Big) \Big/ U_{jj}\Big) \ (i > j),\]

    each sum being evaluated by subtracting the rounded products one at a time, every operation rounded. These are the operations of Gaussian elimination without pivoting, as in the proof of the backward error analysis of LU decomposition in J. W. Demmel's Math 221 course (UC Berkeley, Lecture 5 notes, 2024), which takes \(P = I\); with partial pivoting the same factors are computed for the row-permuted matrix. The recurrences determine \(L\) and \(U\) from \(A\) as long as the pivots \(U_{jj}\) are nonzero; the predicate form avoids writing out the well-founded recursion.

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.FloatingPoint.LUBackwardError.exists_mul_eq_add_abs_le_gamma

    Let \(\hat L\) and \(\hat U\) be the factors computed by Gaussian elimination without pivoting in \(p\)-bit floating point from an \(n \times n\) matrix \(A\), with nonzero pivots \(\hat U_{jj}\) for \(j < n - 1\) (the last pivot is never divided by), and let \(n u < 1\). Then

    \[\hat L \hat U = A + E, \qquad |E_{ij}| \le \gamma_n \sum_k |\hat L_{ik}|\,|\hat U_{kj}| \quad \text{for all } i, j.\]

    J. W. Demmel's Math 221 course (UC Berkeley, Lecture 5 notes, 2024) states this as "\(A - E = P L U\) where \(|E| \le n\,\varepsilon\, P |L|\,|U|\)", to first order in \(\varepsilon = u\) and with \(P = I\) in the proof; the exact \(\gamma_n\) form is the one of N. J. Higham, Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms, 2nd ed., SIAM, 2002, in the chapter on LU factorization. Gaussian elimination is therefore backward stable as long as \(\|\,|\hat L|\,|\hat U|\,\|\) is a modest multiple of \(\|A\|\); the ratio is the pivot growth factor, which partial pivoting keeps small in practice although it can reach \(2^{n-1}\). Adding the backward errors of the two triangular solves gives \((A + F)\hat x = b\) with \(|F| \le (3\gamma_n + \gamma_n^2)\,|\hat L|\,|\hat U|\) for the computed solution of \(Ax = b\), and then the condition number bound bounds its error.

    Proof. For an entry of \(\hat U\) (\(i \le j\)), \(E_{ij} = \hat U_{ij} + \sum_{k<i} \hat L_{ik}\hat U_{kj} - A_{ij}\), and the error of the floating-point \(c - \sum a_k b_k\), measured by the result, bounds it by \(\gamma_i(\sum_{k<i} |\hat L_{ik}\hat U_{kj}| + |\hat U_{ij}|)\). For an entry of \(\hat L\) (\(i > j\)) the division by the pivot adds one rounding, of relative size \(\gamma_1\) measured by the rounded value, and \((1 + \gamma_j)(1 + \gamma_1) \le 1 + \gamma_{j+1}\) absorbs it. Both indices are below \(n\), and \(\gamma\) is monotone.
