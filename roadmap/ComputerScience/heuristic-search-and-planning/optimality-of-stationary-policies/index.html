    Optimality of stationary policies

    Markov policies choose a decision rule at each stage, possibly a different one each time. Following Bertsekas (Abstract Dynamic Programming, 3rd ed., 2022, Section 1.2), the \(N\)-stage value of a Markov policy \(\pi = (\mu_0, \mu_1, \dots)\) is the composition \(T_{\mu_0} T_{\mu_1} \cdots T_{\mu_{N-1}} V\) of the policies' Bellman operators applied to a terminal value \(V\), and its value is the limit as \(N \to \infty\). For a finite discounted MDP the limit exists and does not depend on \(V\); no Markov policy beats the optimal value, and the stationary policy greedy for the optimal value attains it. So optimal policies may be taken stationary. Over a finite horizon \(N\) the same comparison shows that the DP algorithm is optimal: the value iterate \(T^N V\) is the best \(N\)-stage value, attained by the policy that acts greedily for \(T^{N-1-k} V\) at stage \(k\). Randomized and history-dependent policies are not treated here.

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.nStageValue

    Let \(M\) be a Markov decision process with finitely many states, rewards and transition probabilities in an ordered field, and let \(\gamma\) be a discount factor. A Markov policy is a sequence \(\pi = (\mu_0, \mu_1, \dots)\) of deterministic decision rules \(\mu_k : S \to A\), one per stage. Its \(N\)-stage value with terminal value \(V : S \to K\) is

    \[T_{\mu_0} \big(T_{\mu_1} \big(\cdots \big(T_{\mu_{N-1}} V\big)\cdots\big)\big),\]

    where \(T_\mu\) is the Bellman operator of a policy. For \(N = 0\) it is \(V\).

    def nStageValue (M : MDP S A K) (γ : K) : (ℕ → S → A) → ℕ → (S → K) → S → K

  | π, N + 1, V => policyBellman M γ (π 0) (nStageValue M γ (fun k => π (k + 1)) N V)

    The value of a Markov policy \(\pi\) is the limit, as \(N \to \infty\), of its N-stage values with terminal value \(0\).     TheoremA Markov policy does no better than value iteration over N stages#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.nStageValue_le_iterate

    Let \(M\) be a Markov decision process with finitely many states, finitely many actions (at least one), rewards and transition probabilities in an ordered field, with stochastic transitions, and let \(\gamma \ge 0\).     \[T_{\mu_0} \cdots T_{\mu_{N-1}} V \le T^N V \quad \text{pointwise}.\]

    DefinitionThe dynamic programming policy#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.dpPolicy

    Let \(M\) be a Markov decision process with finitely many states, a nonempty finite set of actions, rewards and transition probabilities in an ordered field, and a discount factor \(\gamma\). For a horizon \(N\) and a terminal value \(V\), the dynamic programming policy is the Markov policy that at stage \(k < N\) acts greedily for the value iterate \(T^{N-1-k} V\): it chooses, in each state, an action maximizing \(r(s,a) + \gamma \sum_{s'} p(s,a,s')\,(T^{N-1-k} V)(s')\). These are the minimizations of the DP algorithm, run backwards from the terminal value.

    noncomputable def dpPolicy [Fintype A] [Nonempty A] (M : MDP S A K) (γ : K) (N : ℕ)
    (V : S → K) : ℕ → S → A :=
  fun k => greedy M γ ((bellman M γ)^[N - 1 - k] V)

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.isGreatest_nStageValue

    Let \(M\) be a Markov decision process with finitely many states, a nonempty finite set of actions, rewards and stochastic transition probabilities in an ordered field, and let \(\gamma \ge 0\).     Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.dist_nStageValue_le

    Let \(M\) be a finite Markov decision process with real rewards and stochastic transitions, and let \(0 \le \gamma < 1\). For every Markov policy \(\pi\), every \(N\) and all terminal values \(V\), \(W\),

    \[\big\|T_{\mu_0} \cdots T_{\mu_{N-1}} V - T_{\mu_0} \cdots T_{\mu_{N-1}} W\big\|_\infty \le \gamma^N \|V - W\|_\infty.\]

    Each \(T_\mu\) is a \(\gamma\)-contraction in the sup norm, and a composition of \(N\) of them is a \(\gamma^N\)-contraction.

    theorem dist_nStageValue_le (π : ℕ → S → A) (N : ℕ) (V W : S → ℝ) :
    dist (nStageValue M γ π N V) (nStageValue M γ π N W) ≤ γ ^ N * dist V W

    TheoremOptimality of stationary policies#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.isGreatest_markovValue

    Let \(M\) be a Markov decision process with finitely many states, finitely many actions (at least one), real rewards and stochastic transition probabilities, and let \(0 \le \gamma < 1\). The optimal value \(V^*\), the unique solution of Bellman's equation \(TV = V\), is the greatest value of a Markov policy: every Markov policy \(\pi\) has value at most \(V^*\) in every state, and the stationary policy greedy for \(V^*\) has value exactly \(V^*\). Optimal Markov policies may therefore be taken stationary.

    This is the deterministic Markov-policy case, for finite discounted MDPs, of the results listed by Bertsekas for monotone contractive models: Bellman's equation has the unique solution \(J^*\), and a stationary policy \(\mu^*\) is optimal if and only if \(T_{\mu^*} J^* = T J^*\). Randomized and history-dependent policies are not covered; stationary randomized policies are compared with \(V^*\) in No stochastic policy beats the optimal value.

    theorem isGreatest_markovValue [Fintype A] [Nonempty A] :
    IsGreatest (Set.range (markovValue M γ)) (optimalValue M γ hM hγ0 hγ1)
