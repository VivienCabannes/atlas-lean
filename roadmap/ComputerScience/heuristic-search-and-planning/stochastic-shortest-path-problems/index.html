    Stochastic shortest path problems

    A stochastic shortest path (SSP) problem is an undiscounted Markov decision process with a cost-free absorbing termination state: the decision maker wants to reach termination at minimum expected total cost (Bertsekas, Abstract Dynamic Programming, 3rd ed., 2022, Example 1.2.6; Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis, Math. Oper. Res. 16, 1991). Only the nonterminal states are modelled: transitions among them are substochastic, and the missing probability mass is the probability of terminating. As elsewhere in this graph rewards are maximized, so Bertsekas's cost per stage is the negative of the reward.

    A stationary policy is proper if termination has positive probability within \(|S|\) stages from every state. A proper policy's Bellman operator is an \(|S|\)-stage contraction, so it has a unique fixed point, value iteration converges to it, and that fixed point is the policy's total expected reward until termination. Bellman's equation for the optimal value, under the strong SSP conditions (some policy is proper; every improper policy has infinite cost from some state), is recorded as a wanted theorem. A discounted problem is the SSP in which the system terminates with probability \(1-\gamma\) at every stage; there every policy is proper and SSP values are discounted values.

    DefinitionSubstochastic transitions#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.MDP.Substochastic

    A Markov decision process with real transition probabilities \(p(s, a, s')\) among its nonterminal states is substochastic if \(p(s, a, s') \ge 0\) for all \(s, a, s'\) and \(\sum_{s'} p(s, a, s') \le 1\) for all \(s, a\). The missing mass \(1 - \sum_{s'} p(s, a, s')\) is the probability of moving from \(s\) under action \(a\) to the termination state, which is cost-free and absorbing and is not modelled explicitly.

    def MDP.Substochastic (M : MDP S A ℝ) : Prop :=
  (∀ s a s', 0 ≤ M.prob s a s') ∧ ∀ s a, ∑ s', M.prob s a s' ≤ 1
    structure MDP (S A K : Type*) where

  prob : S → A → S → K

    DefinitionTransition matrix of a stationary policy#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.transitionMatrix

    For a Markov decision process with states \(S\) and a deterministic stationary policy \(\mu : S \to A\), the transition matrix of \(\mu\) is the \(S \times S\) matrix \(P_\mu\) with entries \(P_\mu(s, s') = p(s, \mu(s), s')\). In a stochastic shortest path problem \(S\) is the set of nonterminal states and \(P_\mu\) is substochastic.

    For a stochastic MDP, the same matrix is the policy kernel of the Dirac (deterministic) stochastic policy of \(\mu\): see The transition matrix of a deterministic policy is its policy kernel.

    DefinitionProper policy#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.MDP.Proper

    In a stochastic shortest path problem with nonterminal states \(S\), a deterministic stationary policy \(\mu\) is proper if, from every initial state, termination has positive probability within \(n = |S|\) stages: every row sum of \(P_\mu^{\,n}\) is less than \(1\), that is

    \[\rho_\mu = \max_{s \in S} \Pr(x_n \ne t \mid x_0 = s, \mu) = \max_{s \in S} \sum_{s'} P_\mu^{\,n}(s, s') < 1.\]

    A policy that is not proper is improper. With no nonterminal states every policy is proper.

    def MDP.Proper (M : MDP S A ℝ) (μ : S → A) : Prop :=
  ∀ s, ∑ s', (transitionMatrix M μ ^ Fintype.card S) s s' < 1

    DefinitionValue of a stationary policy in a stochastic shortest path problem#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.sspValue

    The value of a stationary policy \(\mu\) in a stochastic shortest path problem is the limit, as \(N \to \infty\), of its \(N\)-stage total rewards \(T_\mu^N 0\), where \(T_\mu\) is the undiscounted (\(\gamma = 1\)) Bellman operator of \(\mu\). For a proper policy of a substochastic MDP the limit exists, is the unique fixed point of \(T_\mu\), and equals the total expected reward until termination \(\sum_{k \ge 0} P_\mu^k r_\mu\). Bertsekas's cost of \(\mu\) is the negative of this value.

    noncomputable def sspValue (M : MDP S A ℝ) (μ : S → A) : S → ℝ :=
  limUnder atTop fun N => (policyBellman M 1 μ)^[N] 0

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.policyBellman_one_eq

    For a Markov decision process with finitely many states and real rewards, and a deterministic stationary policy \(\mu\), the undiscounted (\(\gamma = 1\)) Bellman operator of \(\mu\) is affine: \(T_\mu J = r_\mu + P_\mu J\), where \(r_\mu(s) = r(s, \mu(s))\) and \(P_\mu\) is the transition matrix of \(\mu\).

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.policyBellman_iterate_zero

    TheoremA proper policy's Bellman operator is an |S|-stage contraction#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.contractingWith_policyBellman_iterate

    Let \(M\) be a Markov decision process with a finite set \(S\) of nonterminal states, real rewards and substochastic transitions, and let \(\mu\) be a proper stationary policy. Then \(T_\mu^{|S|}\), the \(|S|\)-th iterate of the undiscounted Bellman operator of \(\mu\), is a contraction of \((S \to \mathbb{R}, \|\cdot\|_\infty)\): there is \(K < 1\) with

    \[\big\|T_\mu^{|S|} J - T_\mu^{|S|} J'\big\|_\infty \le K \|J - J'\|_\infty \quad \text{for all } J, J'.\]

    The proof takes for \(K\) the largest row sum \(\rho_\mu\) of \(P_\mu^{|S|}\), which is less than \(1\) because \(\mu\) is proper.

    Bertsekas notes that \(T_\mu\) is also a contraction for a suitable weighted sup norm; that form is not formalized here.

    theorem contractingWith_policyBellman_iterate (hM : M.Substochastic) {μ : S → A}
    (hμ : M.Proper μ) :
    ∃ K : NNReal, ContractingWith K (policyBellman M 1 μ)^[Fintype.card S]

    TheoremBellman's equation for stochastic shortest path problems#●ready to prove

    Cslib.Algorithms.Lean.MarkovDecision.bellman_ssp

    Let \(M\) be a Markov decision process with a finite set \(S\) of nonterminal states, finitely many actions (at least one, every action allowed in every state), real rewards and substochastic transitions, and write \(T\) for the undiscounted Bellman optimality operator and \(T_\mu\) for the undiscounted Bellman operator of a stationary policy \(\mu\). Assume the strong SSP conditions:

    1. some stationary policy is proper;
    2. for every improper stationary policy \(\mu\) there is a state \(s\) from which the \(N\)-stage total rewards \((T_\mu^N 0)(s)\) are unbounded below (the cost of \(\mu\) from \(s\) is \(+\infty\)).

    Then there is \(V^* : S \to \mathbb{R}\) such that

    • \(V^*\) is the unique solution of Bellman's equation \(T V^* = V^*\);
    • value iteration converges: \(T^k J \to V^*\) for every \(J\);
    • no stationary policy does better than \(V^*\): for every stationary policy \(\mu\), proper or improper, and every state \(s\), \(\liminf_{N\to\infty} (T_\mu^N 0)(s) \le V^*(s)\) (stated in Lean as: for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), frequently \((T_\mu^N 0)(s) \le V^*(s) + \varepsilon\)). This is the reward form of Bertsekas's \(J^*(s) \le J_\mu(s)\), where \(J_\mu(s)\) is the \(\limsup\) of the \(N\)-stage costs;
    • in particular every proper policy \(\mu\) has value \(J_\mu \le V^*\);
    • a stationary policy \(\mu\) satisfies \(T_\mu V^* = T V^*\) if and only if \(\mu\) is proper and \(J_\mu = V^*\), that is, if and only if \(\mu\) is optimal among stationary policies.

    Recorded as a wanted theorem: its proof is deferred.

    In Bertsekas's cost formulation: \(J^* = -V^*\) is the unique fixed point of \(T\), and \(\mu^*\) is optimal if and only if \(T_{\mu^*} J^* = T J^*\). The Lean statement compares \(V^*\) with every stationary policy, proper or improper, but not with non-stationary policies. The source's \(J^*\) is optimal over all policies; that comparison needs a model of general policies, which is not formalized.
