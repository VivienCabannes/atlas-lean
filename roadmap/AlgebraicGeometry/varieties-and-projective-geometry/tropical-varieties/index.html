    Tropical varieties

    Over a field with a valuation, a Laurent polynomial f tropicalizes to the piecewise-linear function trop(f)(w) = min_u (val(c_u) + u · w), and its tropical hypersurface is where this minimum is attained at least twice. Kapranov's theorem identifies it with the closure of the coordinatewise valuations of the zeros of f. The tropicalization of a subvariety of the torus is the intersection of the tropical hypersurfaces of all polynomials in its ideal, and the Fundamental Theorem of tropical algebraic geometry identifies it, in the same way, with the closure of the valuations of the points of the variety.

    TheoremKapranov's theorem#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.AlgebraicGeometry.TropicalVarietyWanted.tropicalHypersurface_eq_closure_valuationsOfZeros

    Let K be an algebraically closed field with a nontrivial valuation val : K → ℝ ∪ {∞} and f = ∑_u c_u x^u a nonzero Laurent polynomial in n variables. The tropical hypersurface of f, the set of w ∈ ℝⁿ where the minimum min_u (val(c_u) + u · w) is attained at least twice, equals the closure in ℝⁿ of the set of coordinatewise valuations (val(y_1), …, val(y_n)) of the zeros y ∈ (K^*)ⁿ of f.

    So a purely combinatorial object, computed from the valuations of the coefficients alone, records where the points of the classical hypersurface lie at the scale of the valuation. Maclagan and Sturmfels state the theorem as the coincidence of three sets, the second being described by initial forms; this entry records the equality of the first and the third. It is the base case of the Fundamental Theorem of tropical algebraic geometry, which replaces f by an arbitrary ideal of Laurent polynomials.

    The hypothesis f ≠ 0 is implicit in the source: for f = 0 the tropical hypersurface is empty while every point of the torus is a zero.

    theorem_wanted tropicalHypersurface_eq_closure_valuationsOfZeros [IsAlgClosed K]
    (v : AddValuation K (WithTop ℝ)) (_hv : ∃ x : K, x ≠ 0 ∧ v x ≠ 0)

    tropicalHypersurface v f = closure (valuationsOfZeros v f)

    MathlibExt.AlgebraicGeometry.TropicalVarietyWanted.tropicalVariety

    For an ideal I of Laurent polynomials with variety X = V(I) ⊆ (K^*)ⁿ, the intersection trop(X) = ⋂_{f ∈ I} trop(V(f)) of the tropical hypersurfaces of the polynomials in I (Maclagan–Sturmfels, draft 2009, Definition 3.2.1, equation (3.1)).     TheoremFundamental Theorem of Tropical Algebraic Geometry#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.AlgebraicGeometry.TropicalVarietyWanted.tropicalVariety_eq_closure_valuationsOfVariety

    Let K be an algebraically closed field with a nontrivial valuation val : K → ℝ ∪ {∞}, I an ideal of Laurent polynomials in n variables and X = V(I) its variety in the torus (K^*)ⁿ. The tropical variety trop(X), the intersection of the tropical hypersurfaces of the nonzero polynomials in I, equals the closure in ℝⁿ of the set {(val(y_1), …, val(y_n)) : y ∈ X} of coordinatewise valuations of the points of X.

    Maclagan and Sturmfels state the theorem as the coincidence of three sets, the second being the closure of the weight vectors w with in_w(I) ≠ ⟨1⟩; this entry records the equality of the first and the third. The intersection cannot be restricted to a generating set of I in general, which is why tropical bases are needed for computation. The proof projects to the hypersurface case, Kapranov's theorem.
