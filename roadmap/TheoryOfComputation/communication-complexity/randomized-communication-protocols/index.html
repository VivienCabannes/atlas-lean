    Randomized communication protocols

    A public-coin protocol is a family of deterministic protocols indexed by a shared random string drawn uniformly from a finite set; its cost is the largest depth of a member and its error on an input pair is the fraction of random strings on which it answers wrongly. For equality of \(n\)-bit strings, hashing both inputs with a shared random function to \(k\) bits gives a protocol of cost \(k + 1\) that never errs on equal inputs and errs with probability exactly \(2^{-k}\) on distinct ones, whereas every deterministic protocol needs \(n + 1\) bits.

    More generally a randomized protocol is a finitely supported probability distribution on deterministic protocols, and \(R_\varepsilon(f)\) is the least cost of one whose error is at most \(\varepsilon\) on every input. Yao's minimax principle for communication identifies \(R_\varepsilon(f)\) with the largest, over input distributions \(\mu\), of the distributional complexity \(D^\mu_\varepsilon(f)\), the least depth of a deterministic protocol with error at most \(\varepsilon\) under \(\mu\); this is how lower bounds on randomized communication are proved. Newman's theorem, stated here as a wanted result, says that for \(0 < \varepsilon \le 1/2\) private coins cost at most \(\log_2(n/\varepsilon^2) + O(1)\) extra bits, at the price of doubling the error, which gives equality an \(O(\log n)\)-bit private-coin protocol. The presentation follows A. Rao and A. Yehudayoff, Communication Complexity (early draft), Chapter 3.

    MathlibExt.Computability.CommunicationComplexity.newman_private_coins

    There is a constant C₀ such that for every n ≥ 1 and 0 < ε ≤ 1 / 2: if g : {0,1}^n × {0,1}^n → {0,1} is computed by a public-coin protocol (with a nonempty finite set of random strings) of cost c and error at most ε on every input, then it is computed by a private-coin protocol, with nonempty finite sets of private random strings, of cost at most c + log₂(n / ε²) + C₀ and error at most 2ε on every input (A. Rao and A. Yehudayoff, Communication Complexity, early draft, Theorem 3.5, attributed there to Newman, 1991). The source leaves the range of ε implicit; its proof samples O(n / ε²) random strings, and some upper bound on ε is needed: for large ε a cost-0 protocol meets the hypothesis while log₂(n / ε²) is negative.
