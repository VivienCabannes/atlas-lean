    Cslib.Language.EncodedCFG.dfaSymFits

    Let \(D\) be an encoded DFA, \(R\) and \(S\) lists of natural numbers (the items found so far and the states where the rest of a right-hand side can start), \(X\) a grammar symbol and \(p \in \mathbb{N}\). The test is true exactly when either \(X\) is a terminal \(a\) and the step of \(D\) from \(p\) on \(a\) lies in \(S\), or \(X\) is a nonterminal \(B\) and some \(k\) in \(S\) has \(\langle B, \langle p, k \rangle \rangle\) in \(R\), where \(\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle\) is the natural-number pairing.

    def dfaSymFits (D : EncodedDFA T) (R S : List ℕ) (X : ℕ ⊕ T) (p : ℕ) : Bool :=
  match X with
  | .inr a => Decidability.memB S (D.step p a)
  | .inl B => S.any fun k => Decidability.memB R (Nat.pair B (Nat.pair p k))

    Cslib.Language.EncodedCFG.dfaStarts

    Let \(D\) be an encoded DFA, \(R\) a list of natural numbers (the items found so far) and \(q \in \mathbb{N}\). The start states of a right-hand side \(\beta\), a list of grammar symbols, for \(q\) given \(R\) are defined by recursion on \(\beta\): for the empty right-hand side they are the one-element list \([q]\), and for a symbol \(X\) followed by \(\beta'\) they are the states of \(D\), in order, at which \(X\) fits given \(R\) and the start states of \(\beta'\) for \(q\) given \(R\).

  rhs.foldr (fun X S => D.states.filter (dfaSymFits D R S X)) [q]

    For an encoded grammar \(g\), an encoded DFA \(D\) and a list \(R\) of natural numbers (the items found so far), one round produces the list of the codes \(\langle A, \langle p, q \rangle \rangle\), where \(\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle\) is the natural-number pairing, for every production \(A \to \beta\) of \(g\), every state \(q\) of \(D\) and every start state \(p\) of \(\beta\) for \(q\) given \(R\).
