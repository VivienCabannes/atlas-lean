    DefinitionPostorder encoding after a prefix#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Language.EncodedRegex.flattenAux

    For a regular expression \(r\) and a list \(l\) of nodes, the nodes of \(r\) are appended after \(l\) in postorder, by recursion on \(r\). For \(0\), \(1\) and a letter \(a\), append the single node \(0\), \(1\) or \(a\). For a sum \(s + t\) (respectively a concatenation \(st\)), append the nodes of \(s\) after \(l\), giving \(l_1\), then the nodes of \(t\) after \(l_1\), giving \(l_2\), then a sum (respectively concatenation) node with arguments \(|l_1| - 1\) and \(|l_2| - 1\). For a star \(s^*\), append the nodes of \(s\) after \(l\), giving \(l_1\), then a star node with argument \(|l_1| - 1\). The root of \(r\) is the last node.

    Cslib.Language.EncodedRegex.tm2Decidable_matches_eq

    Let \(\alpha\) be a finite alphabet with a primitive recursive encoding. Then equivalence of encoded regular expressions over \(\alpha\) is decidable by a multi-stack machine: some multi-stack machine decides, for a pair \((e_1, e_2)\) of encoded regular expressions over \(\alpha\), whether the regular expressions that \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) denote have the same language.

    This is the regular-expression analogue of Sipser, Theorem 4.5 (EQ_DFA). It is proved by bounded search: the membership decider is run on both expressions for every word shorter than a bound computed from them, and the answers are compared.
