    DefinitionHochman's aperiodic conjugacy question#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Dynamics.AMR43Problem1Wanted.conjecture

    Let \(X\) be the full 2-shift and \(Y\) the proper 3-colouring shift, write \(X \setminus \mathrm{Per}(X)\) and \(Y \setminus \mathrm{Per}(Y)\) for their aperiodic parts with the subspace topologies, and let \(\sigma\) be the left shift \((\sigma z)_i = z_{i+1}\) on each. The statement is that there is a homeomorphism \(e \colon X \setminus \mathrm{Per}(X) \to Y \setminus \mathrm{Per}(Y)\) with \(e(\sigma a) = \sigma(e(a))\) for every \(a \in X \setminus \mathrm{Per}(X)\), that is, the two aperiodic parts are topologically conjugate.

    def conjecture : Prop :=
  ∃ e : Homeomorph ↥AperiodicX ↥AperiodicY,
    ∀ a : ↥AperiodicX, e (shiftXRes a) = shiftYRes (e a)

    TheoremThe aperiodic parts of the full 2-shift and the 3-colouring shift are not conjugate#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Dynamics.AMR43Problem1Wanted.conjecture_refuted

    Let \(X\) be the full 2-shift, \(Y\) the proper 3-colouring shift, and \(\sigma\) the left shift on each. There is no homeomorphism \(e\) from the aperiodic part of \(X\) onto the aperiodic part of \(Y\), each with the subspace topology, such that \(e(\sigma a) = \sigma(e(a))\) for every \(a\) in the aperiodic part of \(X\); that is, Hochman's aperiodic conjugacy question has a negative answer.

    Salo proves that every conjugacy between infinite transitive shifts of finite type minus periodic points extends to a conjugacy of the shifts of finite type; since the full 2-shift has fixed points and the proper 3-colouring shift has none, the aperiodic parts are not conjugate.

    public theorem_wanted conjecture_refuted : ¬ conjecture
    def conjecture : Prop :=
  ∃ e : Homeomorph ↥AperiodicX ↥AperiodicY,
    ∀ a : ↥AperiodicX, e (shiftXRes a) = shiftYRes (e a)
