    Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy

    Sinai's entropy of a measure-preserving transformation measures the rate at which the dynamics produces information. A finite partition \(\xi\) turns every orbit into a stationary sequence of symbols; the entropy of \(T\) relative to \(\xi\) is the Shannon entropy per symbol of that sequence, and the Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy \(h_\mu(T)\) is its supremum over all finite partitions. It is an invariant of measure-theoretic isomorphism, it is computed by the generator theorem, and it gives \(-\sum_i p_i \log p_i\) for the Bernoulli shift with probability vector \(p\), which is how Kolmogorov and Sinai proved that the Bernoulli schemes \((\tfrac13, \tfrac13, \tfrac13)\) and \((\tfrac12, \tfrac12)\) are not isomorphic.

    DefinitionEntropy of a finite partition#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Dynamics.Ergodic.KolmogorovSinaiEntropy.partitionEntropy

    For a finite family \(\xi = (C_i)_{i \in I}\) of sets in a measure space \((X, \mu)\), its entropy is $\(H_\mu(\xi) = -\sum_{i \in I} \mu(C_i) \log \mu(C_i),\)$ with the convention \(0 \log 0 = 0\). For a partition of a probability space this is the Shannon entropy of the probability vector \((\mu(C_i))_i\), the average information gained by learning which atom contains a random point. The logarithm is natural, as in Sinai's article; Sarig uses \(\log_2\), which divides every entropy by \(\log 2\). The Lean definition evaluates measures through ENNReal.toReal, so it is meant for finite measures.

    noncomputable def partitionEntropy (μ : Measure α) {ι : Type*} [Fintype ι]
    (ξ : ι → Set α) : ℝ :=
  ∑ i, Real.negMulLog (μ (ξ i)).toReal

    DefinitionEntropy of a transformation relative to a partition#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Dynamics.Ergodic.KolmogorovSinaiEntropy.entropyRel

    For a measurable map \(T\) of a measure space \((X, \mu)\) and a finite partition \(\xi\), $\(h_\mu(T, \xi) = \limsup_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n}\, H_\mu\bigl(\xi \vee T^{-1}\xi \vee \dots \vee T^{-(n-1)}\xi\bigr),\)$ where the dynamical refinement has one atom for each word \((i_0, \dots, i_{n-1})\), the points whose first \(n\) symbols are \(i_0, \dots, i_{n-1}\), and \(H_\mu\) is the entropy of a finite partition. The value is taken in \([0, \infty]\).

    When \(\mu\) is a \(T\)-invariant probability measure, the sequence \(H_\mu(\xi \vee \dots \vee T^{-(n-1)}\xi)\) is subadditive, so the limit exists and the \(\limsup\) is that limit. Sinai writes the refinement with \(T^{-1}, \dots, T^{-n}\); by invariance this gives the same value.

    DefinitionKolmogorov–Sinai entropy#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Dynamics.Ergodic.KolmogorovSinaiEntropy.ksEntropy

    Let \(T\) be a measurable map of a measure space \((X, \mu)\). The Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy of \(T\) is $\(h_\mu(T) = \sup_\xi h_\mu(T, \xi),\)$ the supremum, over all finite measurable partitions \(\xi\) of \(X\), of the entropy of \(T\) relative to \(\xi\). It takes values in \([0, \infty]\).

    A partition \(\xi = \{C_1, \dots, C_r\}\) turns every point \(x\) into the sequence of symbols \(w_k(x) = j\) if \(T^k x \in C_j\), a stationary random process when \(\mu\) is an invariant probability; \(h_\mu(T, \xi)\) is the entropy per symbol of that process and \(h_\mu(T)\) the largest such rate. Kolmogorov introduced an entropy for a restricted class of systems; the definition over all finite partitions is Sinai's (1959). It is an invariant of isomorphism modulo null sets, it is computed by Sinai's generator theorem, and for the Bernoulli shift with probability vector \(p\) it equals \(-\sum_i p_i \log p_i\).

    In the Lean definition partitions are indexed by \(\{0, \dots, r - 1\}\) and are partitions modulo null sets (finite measurable partition). The planar statements of Pesin and Young in Entropy, dimension and Lyapunov exponents use an earlier real-valued variant whose supremum is replaced by \(0\) when it is infinite.

    DefinitionIsomorphism modulo null sets#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Dynamics.Ergodic.KolmogorovSinaiEntropy.IsIsomorphicMod0

    Two dynamical systems \((X, \mu, T)\) and \((Y, \nu, S)\) are isomorphic modulo null sets if there are measure-preserving maps \(\pi : X \to Y\) and \(\rho : Y \to X\) such that - \(\rho \circ \pi = \mathrm{id}\) \(\mu\)-almost everywhere and \(\pi \circ \rho = \mathrm{id}\) \(\nu\)-almost everywhere; - \(\pi \circ T = S \circ \pi\) \(\mu\)-almost everywhere and \(\rho \circ S = T \circ \rho\) \(\nu\)-almost everywhere.

    This is measure-theoretic isomorphism, written with maps defined almost everywhere. Sarig's definition asks for invariant sets of full measure \(X' \subset X\), \(Y' \subset Y\) and a measure-preserving bijection \(X' \to Y'\) with measurable inverse intertwining \(T\) and \(S\); on nonempty spaces such a bijection, extended by a constant off \(X'\) (and its inverse off \(Y'\)), gives maps \(\pi\) and \(\rho\) as above. Unlike an everywhere conjugacy, the notion ignores null sets such as the fixed points of a Bernoulli shift, which is why Kolmogorov's non-isomorphism and Ornstein's theorem are stated with it. Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy is invariant under it, and an everywhere conjugacy by a measure-preserving equivalence is a special case.

    def IsIsomorphicMod0 (μ : Measure α) (T : α → α) (ν : Measure β) (S : β → β) : Prop :=
