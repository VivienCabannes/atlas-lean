    Unipotent flows

    A one-parameter subgroup \(u_t\) of a Lie group \(G\) is unipotent when each \(u_t\) is a unipotent matrix, such as the horocycle subgroup \(u_t = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ t & 1 \end{pmatrix}\) of \(\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})\). For a lattice \(\Gamma\) in \(G\), Ratner's orbit closure theorem says that the closure of every orbit of the flow \(\Gamma x \mapsto \Gamma x u_t\) on \(\Gamma \backslash G\) is homogeneous: it is the image of \(xS\) for a closed connected subgroup \(S\) containing the \(u_t\), on which it has finite \(S\)-invariant volume. For \(\Gamma = \mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z})\) this leaves only closed and dense horocycle orbits. Margulis's proof of the Oppenheim conjecture, through the orbits of \(\mathrm{SO}(2, 1)\) on \(\mathrm{SL}(3, \mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathrm{SL}(3, \mathbb{R})\), is the archetypal application to number theory (D. W. Morris, Ratner's theorems on unipotent flows, arXiv:math/0310402, Sections 1.1 and 1.2).

    The horocycle one-parameter subgroup of \(\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})\) is \(u_t = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ t & 1 \end{pmatrix}\), \(t \in \mathbb{R}\); it satisfies \(u_{s+t} = u_s u_t\) and every \(u_t\) is unipotent. On \(\Gamma \backslash \mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})\) the flow \(\Gamma x \mapsto \Gamma x u_t\) is the horocycle flow.

    MathlibExt.Dynamics.UnipotentFlowsWanted.horocycle_orbit_isClosed_or_dense

    Let \(\Gamma = \mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z})\), viewed inside \(G = \mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})\), and let \(u_t = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ t & 1 \end{pmatrix}\). For every \(x \in G\), the orbit \(\{\Gamma x u_t : t \in \mathbb{R}\}\) of the horocycle flow on the space \(\Gamma \backslash G\) of right cosets, with the quotient topology, is either closed or dense.

    Morris deduces this from Ratner's orbit closure theorem: the closure of the orbit is the image of \(xS\) for a connected closed subgroup \(S \ni u_t\) such that \((x^{-1} \Gamma x) \cap S\) is a lattice in \(S\), and the only such subgroups are \(\{u_t\}\) and \(G\) (the lower-triangular group has no lattice). For cocompact \(\Gamma\) every horocycle orbit is dense (Hedlund, 1930s).

    theorem_wanted horocycle_orbit_isClosed_or_dense (x : SL(2, ℝ)) :
    IsClosed (Set.range fun t : ℝ =>
        (Quotient.mk (QuotientGroup.rightRel (SpecialLinearGroup.map (Int.castRingHom ℝ)).range)
          (x * horocycle t))) ∨
      Dense (Set.range fun t : ℝ =>
        (Quotient.mk (QuotientGroup.rightRel (SpecialLinearGroup.map (Int.castRingHom ℝ)).range)
          (x * horocycle t)))

    TheoremMargulis's theorem on values of quadratic forms (Oppenheim conjecture)#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Dynamics.UnipotentFlowsWanted.margulis_oppenheim

    Let \(Q\) be a real quadratic form in \(n \ge 3\) variables. Suppose that \(Q\) is indefinite (it takes a positive and a negative value), nondegenerate (no nonzero \(x\) satisfies \(Q(v + x) = Q(v - x)\) for all \(v\)) and not a scalar multiple of a form with integer coefficients, that is, not of the form \(x \mapsto c \sum_{i,j} a_{ij} x_i x_j\) with \(c \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(a_{ij} \in \mathbb{Z}\). Then \(Q(\mathbb{Z}^n)\) is dense in \(\mathbb{R}\).

    Each hypothesis is needed: a definite form has discrete values on \(\mathbb{Z}^n\), there are counterexamples in two variables, and a multiple of an integral form has values in a discrete set (Morris, Remark 1.2.4). The proof, for \(n = 3\), applies Ratner's orbit closure theorem (or Margulis's direct argument) to the orbit of the subgroup \(\mathrm{SO}(2, 1)^\circ\), which is generated by unipotent elements, on \(\mathrm{SL}(3, \mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathrm{SL}(3, \mathbb{R})\). The tests check that \(x_0^2 + x_1^2 - \sqrt2\, x_2^2\) satisfies the three hypotheses as written in Lean.
