    Gröbner bases

    A Gröbner basis of an ideal I in a polynomial ring, for a monomial order, is a subset of I (finite in practice) whose leading monomials generate the initial ideal of I. Division by a Gröbner basis has a unique remainder, which decides ideal membership, and Buchberger's criterion, that every S-polynomial reduces to zero, turns this into an algorithm (S-polynomials are Mathlib's MonomialOrder.sPolynomial). For the lexicographic order a Gröbner basis contains Gröbner bases of all elimination ideals, which is how Gröbner bases eliminate variables. Monomial orders and the division algorithm come from Mathlib (MonomialOrder.div_set).

    DefinitionGröbner basis#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.RingTheory.MvPolynomial.GroebnerBasis.IsGroebnerBasis

    For a monomial order m, a set G of polynomials is a Gröbner basis of the ideal I when G ⊆ I and the leading monomial of every nonzero f ∈ I is divisible by the leading monomial of some nonzero g ∈ G (m.degree g ≤ m.degree f componentwise). Equivalently, the leading terms of G generate the initial ideal in_m(I). Finite G is not required.

    def IsGroebnerBasis (G : Set (MvPolynomial σ K)) (I : Ideal (MvPolynomial σ K)) : Prop :=
  G ⊆ I ∧ ∀ f ∈ I, f ≠ 0 → ∃ g ∈ G, g ≠ 0 ∧ m.degree g ≤ m.degree f

    TheoremIdeal membership by remainder zero#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.RingTheory.MvPolynomial.GroebnerBasis.IsGroebnerBasis.mem_iff_remainder_eq_zero

    Let G be a Gröbner basis of an ideal I for a monomial order, and write f = ∑_{b ∈ G} q_b b + r where no monomial of r is divisible by the leading monomial of any b ∈ G, as the division algorithm produces. Then f ∈ I if and only if r = 0.

    If f ∈ I then r ∈ I; a nonzero r would have its leading monomial divisible by that of some element of G, which the shape of the remainder forbids. This is what makes Gröbner bases solve the ideal membership problem: one division, in any order, decides it.

    theorem IsGroebnerBasis.mem_iff_remainder_eq_zero {G : Set (MvPolynomial σ K)}

    (q : G →₀ MvPolynomial σ K) (r : MvPolynomial σ K)
    (hf : f = Finsupp.linearCombination _ (fun b : G ↦ (b : MvPolynomial σ K)) q + r)
    (hr : ∀ c ∈ r.support, ∀ b ∈ G, ¬ m.degree b ≤ c) :
    f ∈ I ↔ r = 0

    TheoremBuchberger's criterion#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.RingTheory.MvPolynomial.BuchbergerCriterion.isGroebnerBasis_of_sPolynomial

    Let m be a monomial order and G a set of nonzero polynomials over a field. If for all g, h ∈ G the S-polynomial S(g, h) can be written as ∑_{b ∈ G} q_b b with no summand of leading monomial above that of S(g, h), then G is a Gröbner basis of the ideal it generates.

    Such a representation is what a division of S(g, h) by G with zero remainder provides, so the criterion reduces the infinitely many checks in the definition of a Gröbner basis to finitely many divisions when G is finite. Buchberger's algorithm adds the nonzero remainders of S-polynomials to G until the criterion holds; it terminates by the ascending chain condition on monomial ideals. The converse, that every S-polynomial of a Gröbner basis has remainder zero, is S-polynomials of a Gröbner basis reduce to zero.

    As in Hassett's proof, the formal proof writes an element f = ∑_b q_b b of the ideal with the largest leading monomial δ of the summands as small as possible. If the x^δ terms do not cancel, δ is the leading monomial of f and is divisible by the leading monomial of some b. If they cancel, the identity c x^α g_i = (c lc(g_i)/lc(g_j)) x^{δ - deg g_j} g_j + (c/lc(g_j)) x^{δ - γ} S(g_i, g_j), with γ the least common multiple of the leading monomials, moves the top term of one summand onto another and puts the rest below δ; induction on δ and on the number of summands reaching it concludes. The S-polynomial is Mathlib's MonomialOrder.sPolynomial, lc(g) x^{γ - deg f} f - lc(f) x^{γ - deg g} g, which differs from the textbook one by the nonzero factor lc(f) lc(g).

    theorem isGroebnerBasis_of_sPolynomial (G : Set (_root_.MvPolynomial σ K))
    (hG0 : (0 : _root_.MvPolynomial σ K) ∉ G)

      m.sPolynomial g h =
          Finsupp.linearCombination _ (fun b : G ↦ (b : _root_.MvPolynomial σ K)) q ∧
        ∀ b : G, m.degree ((b : _root_.MvPolynomial σ K) * q b) ≼[m]
          m.degree (m.sPolynomial g h)) :
    IsGroebnerBasis m G (Ideal.span G)

    TheoremElimination Theorem#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.RingTheory.MvPolynomial.Elimination.elimination

    Let G be a Gröbner basis of an ideal I ⊆ k[x₀, x₁, …] for the lexicographic order x₀ > x₁ > ⋯, and let S be a final segment of the variables, such as {x_l, x_{l+1}, …}. Then the leading monomial of every nonzero polynomial of the elimination ideal I ∩ k[S] is divisible by the leading monomial of an element of G ∩ k[S]: the elements of G that involve only the variables in S form a Gröbner basis of I ∩ k[S], in the leading-monomial sense of the definition. The subring k[S] is Mathlib's MvPolynomial.supported K S.

    The proof rests on one property of the lexicographic order: a monomial that is lexicographically below a monomial in the variables of S is itself a monomial in the variables of S (Hassett: lexicographic order is an elimination order). So once the leading monomial of g ∈ G divides that of some f ∈ k[S], all of g lies in k[S].

    Computing one lexicographic Gröbner basis thus produces generators for all the elimination ideals at once, which is how Gröbner bases project varieties, implicitize parametrizations and bring polynomial systems to triangular form. The formal statement records the leading-monomial property; that these elements then generate I ∩ k[S] inside k[S], as Hassett states it, follows by the division argument of A Gröbner basis generates its ideal, carried out in k[S].

    theorem elimination {G : Set (_root_.MvPolynomial σ K)} {I : Ideal (_root_.MvPolynomial σ K)}
    (hG : IsGroebnerBasis lex G I) {S : Set σ} (hS : ∀ i ∉ S, ∀ j ∈ S, i < j) :
    ∀ f ∈ I, f ∈ supported K S → f ≠ 0 →
      ∃ g ∈ G, g ∈ supported K S ∧ g ≠ 0 ∧ lex.degree g ≤ lex.degree f
    def IsGroebnerBasis (G : Set (MvPolynomial σ K)) (I : Ideal (MvPolynomial σ K)) : Prop :=

    Related topics

    • Toric ideals and Markov bases: the reduced Gröbner basis of a toric ideal consists of binomials, and its moves form a Markov basis, which is how Diaconis and Sturmfels compute moves.
