    TheoremBudney–Gabai knotted three-spheres in \(S^1 \times S^3\)#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Topology.BudneyGabaiWanted.Topology.budney_gabai_knotted_three_spheres

    Let \(S^1 \subset \mathbb{R}^2\) and \(S^3 \subset \mathbb{R}^4\) be the unit spheres, and fix a point \(x_0 \in S^1\). There is a sequence \(e_0, e_1, e_2, \dots\) of smooth embeddings \(S^3 \to S^1 \times S^3\) such that the complement of the image of each \(e_n\) is connected, each \(e_n\) is homotopic to the standard cross-section \(p \mapsto (x_0, p)\), and for \(i \ne j\) the images of \(e_i\) and \(e_j\) are not smoothly isotopic: there is no smooth map \(H : [0, 1] \times S^3 \to S^1 \times S^3\) such that every slice \(H(t, \cdot)\) is a smooth embedding, \(H(0, \cdot)\) has the same image as \(e_i\), and \(H(1, \cdot)\) has the same image as \(e_j\).

    TheoremPardon's theorem: the p-adic integers cannot act faithfully and continuously on a connected 3-manifold#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Topology.HilbertSmith3DWanted.Topology.hilbert_smith_padic_dimension_three

    Pardon's theorem: the p-adic integers cannot act faithfully and continuously on a connected 3-manifold. Let \(p\) be a prime and \(M\) a connected, Hausdorff, second-countable topological space with an atlas of charts modelled on \(\mathbb{R}^3\), that is, a connected topological 3-manifold without boundary. Then there is no faithful action of the additive group \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) of \(p\)-adic integers on \(M\) that is jointly continuous as a map \(\mathbb{Z}_p \times M \to M\).

    This is the \(p\)-adic case of the Hilbert–Smith conjecture in dimension three; deducing that every locally compact group acting faithfully on a connected 3-manifold is a Lie group also needs the classical reduction to \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-actions, which is not part of this statement.
