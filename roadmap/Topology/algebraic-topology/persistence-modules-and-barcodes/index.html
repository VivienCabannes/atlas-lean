    Persistence modules and barcodes

    A persistence module is a functor from an ordered set, viewed as a category, to vector spaces: applying homology to a filtration of simplicial complexes gives one (G. Carlsson, Topology and data, Bull. AMS 46 (2009), Def. 2.9 and §2.3). The building blocks are the interval modules k_I, and the structure theorems say that, under finiteness hypotheses, every persistence module is a direct sum of interval modules in an essentially unique way; the multiset of intervals is the barcode (Carlsson, Prop. 2.12, for tame ℕ-modules via graded modules over k[t]; W. Crawley-Boevey, J. Algebra Appl. 14 (2015), Thm. 1.1, for pointwise finite-dimensional modules over ℝ). Distances make barcodes usable on noisy data: for q-tame modules (for instance pointwise finite-dimensional ones) the interleaving distance between modules and the bottleneck distance between their barcodes coincide (the isometry theorem, F. Chazal, V. de Silva, M. Glisse and S. Oudot, The structure and stability of persistence modules, arXiv:1207.3674, Thm. 4.11), so small perturbations of the data move the barcode by small amounts.

    The definitions, the symmetry of both notions and the triangle inequality for interleavings are proved, as is the stability of zero-dimensional persistence (single-linkage clusters) under perturbation of the dissimilarity; the decomposition, uniqueness and stability theorems are recorded as wanted statements.

    MathlibExt.Topology.TDA.intervalModule

    For an order-connected set I ⊆ T (an interval when nonempty), the interval module k_I : T ⥤ ModuleCat k has fibre k at every t ∈ I and 0 elsewhere, with identity structure maps between points of I and zero maps otherwise (Crawley-Boevey, J. Algebra Appl. 14 (2015), §1; Carlsson, Topology and data, Bull. AMS 2009, the modules U(m, n) before Prop. 2.12). Order-connectedness is what makes the structure maps compose.

    Structureδ-interleaving#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Topology.TDA.Interleaving

    For δ ≥ 0 and two functors U V : ℝ ⥤ C (persistence modules when C is a category of vector spaces), a δ-interleaving consists of maps φ t : U t ⟶ V (t + δ) and ψ t : V t ⟶ U (t + δ), natural in t, whose composites U t ⟶ V (t + δ) ⟶ U (t + 2δ) and V t ⟶ U (t + δ) ⟶ V (t + 2δ) are the structure maps (Chazal, de Silva, Glisse and Oudot, The structure and stability of persistence modules, arXiv:1207.3674, §3.2, diagrams (3.1); stated there for C the category of vector spaces). The interleaving distance is the infimum of the δ for which one exists.

    structure Interleaving (U V : ℝ ⥤ C) (δ : ℝ) where
  /-- The scale is nonnegative. -/
  nonneg : 0 ≤ δ
  /-- The shifted map from `U` to `V`. -/
  φ : ∀ t : ℝ, U.obj t ⟶ V.obj (t + δ)
  /-- The shifted map from `V` to `U`. -/
  ψ : ∀ t : ℝ, V.obj t ⟶ U.obj (t + δ)
  /-- `φ` is a homomorphism of degree `δ`. -/
  φ_natural : ∀ (s t : ℝ) (h : s ≤ t),
    U.map (homOfLE h) ≫ φ t = φ s ≫ V.map (homOfLE (by linarith : s + δ ≤ t + δ))
  /-- `ψ` is a homomorphism of degree `δ`. -/
  ψ_natural : ∀ (s t : ℝ) (h : s ≤ t),
    V.map (homOfLE h) ≫ ψ t = ψ s ≫ U.map (homOfLE (by linarith : s + δ ≤ t + δ))
  /-- `ψ ∘ φ` is the structure map of `U` over `2δ`. -/
  ψ_comp_φ : ∀ t : ℝ, φ t ≫ ψ (t + δ) = U.map (homOfLE (by linarith : t ≤ t + δ + δ))
  /-- `φ ∘ ψ` is the structure map of `V` over `2δ`. -/
  φ_comp_ψ : ∀ t : ℝ, ψ t ≫ φ (t + δ) = V.map (homOfLE (by linarith : t ≤ t + δ + δ))
      φ : ∀ t : ℝ, U.obj t ⟶ V.obj (t + δ)

    Definitionδ-matching of barcodes#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Topology.TDA.IsMatching

    Given two families of nonempty real intervals I : ι → Set ℝ and J : κ → Set ℝ (the bars of two interval-decomposable persistence modules, with multiplicity), a δ-matching is a partial bijection M between the indices such that matched bars have endpoints within δ (measured here by the Hausdorff distance of the intervals, which for intervals of ℝ is the ℓ^∞ distance between the endpoint pairs, with infinite endpoints compared as in the source) and every unmatched bar has length at most 2δ, i.e. lies within δ of the diagonal (Chazal, de Silva, Glisse and Oudot, arXiv:1207.3674, §4.2, the definition of a partial matching and of a δ-matching, for the undecorated diagrams).

    The scale is required to be nonnegative, as for interleavings (§3.2, "Let δ ≥ 0"): the bottleneck distance is an infimum over such δ, and without the sign condition the truncation ENNReal.ofReal δ = 0 would let a negative δ match identical bars. The identification of the Hausdorff distance with the endpoint distance is for nonempty order-connected sets; for closed bounded intervals it is the Hausdorff distance between closed intervals, and the Hausdorff distance only sees closures (Metric.hausdorffEDist_closure), so it ignores open or closed ends, as the undecorated diagram does.

    def IsMatching {ι κ : Type*} (I : ι → Set ℝ) (J : κ → Set ℝ) (δ : ℝ) : Prop :=
  0 ≤ δ ∧ ∃ M : ι → κ → Prop,
    (∀ i j j', M i j → M i j' → j = j') ∧ (∀ i i' j, M i j → M i' j → i = i') ∧
    (∀ i j, M i j → hausdorffEDist (I i) (J j) ≤ ENNReal.ofReal δ) ∧
    (∀ i, (∀ j, ¬ M i j) → ∃ a, I i ⊆ Set.Icc a (a + 2 * δ)) ∧
    (∀ j, (∀ i, ¬ M i j) → ∃ a, J j ⊆ Set.Icc a (a + 2 * δ))
