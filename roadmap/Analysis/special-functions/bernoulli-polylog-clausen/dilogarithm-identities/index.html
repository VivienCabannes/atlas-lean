    TheoremEntry 25 of Chapter 9 of Ramanujan's notebooks#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Ramanujan.Part1Ch9.Entry25Dixon.ramanujan_part1_ch9_entry25_dixon

    Let \(x, y \in [0, 1]\) and let \(\theta\) and \(\varphi\) be real numbers with \(x e^{i\theta} + y e^{i\varphi} = xy \, e^{i(\theta + \varphi)}\). Then \(1 - x \cos\theta \ne 0\) and \(1 - y \cos\varphi \ne 0\). Put \(A_x = 1 - 2x \cos\theta + x^2\), \(A_y = 1 - 2y \cos\varphi + y^2\), \(u_x = \arctan \frac{x \sin\theta}{1 - x \cos\theta}\) and \(u_y = \arctan \frac{y \sin\varphi}{1 - y \cos\varphi}\). Then \(A_x > 0\), \(A_y > 0\), the four series \(\sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{x^k \cos k\theta}{k^2}\), \(\sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{y^k \cos k\varphi}{k^2}\), \(\sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{x^k \sin k\theta}{k^2}\) and \(\sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{y^k \sin k\varphi}{k^2}\) converge absolutely, and

    \[ \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{x^k \cos k\theta}{k^2} + \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{y^k \cos k\varphi}{k^2} = \frac{\log A_x \log A_y}{8} - \frac{u_x u_y}{2}, \]
    \[ \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{x^k \sin k\theta}{k^2} + \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{y^k \sin k\varphi}{k^2} = -\frac{u_y \log A_x}{4} - \frac{u_x \log A_y}{4}. \]

    For real \(\rho\) and \(\alpha\) and \(k \ge 1\) let \(c_k(\rho, \alpha) = \rho^k \cos(k\alpha) / k^2\) and \(s_k(\rho, \alpha) = \rho^k \sin(k\alpha) / k^2\) be the terms of the dilogarithm series of Entry 24, and write \(C(\rho, \alpha) = \sum_{j \ge 0} c_{2j+1}(\rho, \alpha)\) and \(S(\rho, \alpha) = \sum_{j \ge 0} s_{2j+1}(\rho, \alpha)\).     TheoremRamanujan's Landen-type dilogarithm identity (Entry 26)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Ramanujan.Part1Ch9.Entry26Ramanujanpi.ramanujan_part1_ch9_entry26_ramanujanpi

    For real \(\rho\) and \(\alpha\) write \(C(\rho, \alpha) = \sum_{j \ge 0} \rho^{2j+1} \cos((2j+1)\alpha) / (2j+1)^2\) and \(S(\rho, \alpha) = \sum_{j \ge 0} \rho^{2j+1} \sin((2j+1)\alpha) / (2j+1)^2\).     TheoremBridgeman's dilogarithm series for a positive Pell solution#●ready to prove

    MetaMathlibExt.rogersDilog_pell_positive_series

    Write \(L(x) = \sum_{m \ge 1} x^m/m^2 + \frac{1}{2} \log x \log(1 - x)\) for the Rogers dilogarithm on \([0, 1]\) (with \(\log 0 = 0\)).     \[ L(1/u^2) = \sum_{k \ge 2} L(b^2/b_k^2), \]

    and if \(a\) and \(b\) are integers, then for every \(k \ge 0\) there is an integer \(m\) with \(b_k = bm\).
