    Nonlinear controllability and stabilisation

    For a nonlinear control system \(\dot x = f(x, u)\) with \(f(0, 0) = 0\), the first question is small-time local controllability at the equilibrium. If the linearised system \(\dot y = \partial_x f(0,0) \, y + \partial_u f(0,0) \, v\) is controllable, so is the nonlinear one (the linear test, Coron, Control and Nonlinearity, AMS 2007, Theorem 3.8). When the linear test fails, iterated Lie brackets of the vector fields take over. For a driftless control-affine system \(\dot x = \sum_i u_i f_i(x)\) the Lie algebra rank condition — the values at \(x\) of the fields and their iterated brackets span the state space — is sufficient for controllability: this is the Rashevskii–Chow theorem (Rashevskii 1938, Chow 1939; Coron, Theorems 3.18 and 3.19). For analytic systems it is also necessary for small-time local controllability (Hermann–Nagano, Coron Theorem 3.17).

    Controllability does not give stabilisability by continuous stationary feedback. Brockett's condition (Brockett 1983; Coron, Theorem 11.1) is necessary for it: the image under \(f\) of every neighbourhood of \((0, 0)\) must be a neighbourhood of \(0\). The nonholonomic integrator \(\dot x_1 = u_1\), \(\dot x_2 = u_2\), \(\dot x_3 = x_1 u_2 - x_2 u_1\) satisfies the Lie algebra rank condition, hence is small-time locally controllable, but violates Brockett's condition (Coron, Example 11.2). Stronger necessary conditions are the index condition of Krasnosel'skiĭ and Zabczyk (Coron, Theorem 11.4) and Coron's condition on stable homotopy groups (Coron 1990; Coron, Theorem 11.6). The obstruction disappears with time-varying feedback: every driftless system satisfying the Lie algebra rank condition on \(\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}\) is globally asymptotically stabilisable by smooth periodic time-varying feedback laws (Coron 1992, Coron Theorem 11.14).

    The return method. Coron introduced the return method in "Global asymptotic stabilization for controllable systems without drift" (Math. Control Signals Systems 5 (1992), 295–312) for that stabilisation problem, and first used it for the controllability of a partial differential equation in "Contrôlabilité exacte frontière de l'équation d'Euler des fluides parfaits incompressibles bidimensionnels" (C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris Sér. I 317 (1993), 271–276) and "On the controllability of 2-D incompressible perfect fluids" (J. Math. Pures Appl. 75 (1996), 155–188). The idea (Coron, Control and Nonlinearity, Chapter 6): to prove local controllability at an equilibrium \((0, 0)\) when the linearised system there is not controllable, look for a trajectory \((\bar x, \bar u)\) on \([0, T]\) that starts and ends at the equilibrium, \(\bar x(0) = \bar x(T) = 0\), with \(\bar u\) small, along which the linearised (time-varying) system is controllable. Local controllability along that trajectory (Coron, Theorem 3.6) then gives \(\eta > 0\) such that every state within \(\eta\) of \(0\) can be steered in time \(T\) to every other such state by a control close to \(\bar u\); since \(T\) and \(\lVert \bar u \rVert_\infty\) can be taken arbitrarily small, the system is small-time locally controllable. Iterated Lie brackets are of little use for many partial differential equations, while linear partial differential equations have powerful controllability methods (Chapter 2), and the return method reduces nonlinear controllability to those. It has given controllability results for the Euler and Navier–Stokes equations of incompressible fluids, the Burgers equation, the shallow water equations and a Schrödinger equation (Coron, Chapters 6 and 9), often combined with quasi-static deformations (Chapter 7) and power series expansions (Chapter 8).

    Here the definitions, the Lie algebra rank condition and the failure of Brockett's condition for the nonholonomic integrator are proved, and the Rashevskii–Chow theorem and Brockett's theorem are recorded as wanted. The linear test, the Hermann–Nagano theorem, time-varying feedback and the return method itself are not formalized.

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Control.iteratedLieBrackets

    For vector fields \(f_1, \dots, f_m\) on a real normed space \(E\), the set of iterated Lie brackets is the smallest set of vector fields containing every \(f_i\) and closed under the Lie bracket $\([X, Y](x) = DY(x) \, X(x) - DX(x) \, Y(x).\)$ This is the sign convention of Coron, Control and Nonlinearity (AMS 2007), Definition 3.10, and of Mathlib's VectorField.lieBracket. For smooth fields, the linear span of the iterated brackets is the Lie algebra \(\operatorname{Lie}(\{f_1, \dots, f_m\})\) generated by the fields (Coron, Definition 3.13), since by bilinearity every element of that Lie algebra is a linear combination of iterated brackets.

    inductive iteratedLieBrackets {m : ℕ} (f : Fin m → E → E) : Set (E → E)
  | base (i : Fin m) : iteratedLieBrackets f (f i)
  | bracket {X Y : E → E} :
      iteratedLieBrackets f X → iteratedLieBrackets f Y →
        iteratedLieBrackets f (VectorField.lieBracket ℝ X Y)

    DefinitionAccessibility space of a driftless system at a point#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Control.accessibilitySpace

    For the driftless control-affine system \(\dot x = \sum_{i=1}^m u_i f_i(x)\), the accessibility space at \(x\) is the span of the values at \(x\) of the fields \(f_i\) and of all their iterated Lie brackets, that is, \(\{ g(x) : g \in \operatorname{Lie}(\{f_1, \dots, f_m\}) \}\) (Coron, Control and Nonlinearity, AMS 2007, Definition 3.13 and the formula for \(A(x_e, u_e)\) before Definition 3.16, at \(u_e = 0\)). The Lie algebra rank condition at \(x\) (Coron, Definition 3.16) says that it is the whole state space. This driftless space does not see a drift: for constant input fields \(b_j\) it is just \(\operatorname{span}\{b_j\}\). Coron's \(A(x_e, u_e)\) for a control-affine system with drift \(f_0\) also contains the brackets with \(f_0\), and for a linear system \(\dot x = Ax + Bu\) it reduces to the Kalman subspace (Coron, (3.33)); that version is not formalized. A test checks that the constant coordinate fields on \(\mathbb{R}^n\) satisfy it.

  Submodule.span ℝ {v | ∃ X ∈ iteratedLieBrackets f, v = X x}

    TheoremRashevskii–Chow theorem#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Control.NonlinearControlWanted.rashevskii_chow

    Let \(\Omega\) be a connected nonempty open subset of a finite-dimensional real normed space \(E\) and \(f_1, \dots, f_m\) vector fields of class \(C^\infty\) on \(\Omega\) such that the Lie algebra rank condition holds at every point of \(\Omega\). Then for all \(x_0, x_1 \in \Omega\) and every \(T > 0\) there are a bounded measurable control \(u : [0, T] \to \mathbb{R}^m\) and a continuous trajectory \(x : [0, T] \to \Omega\) of the driftless system \(\dot x = \sum_i u_i f_i(x)\), in the Carathéodory sense \(x(t) = x_0 + \int_0^t \sum_i u_i(s) f_i(x(s)) \, ds\), with \(x(0) = x_0\) and \(x(T) = x_1\).

    This is Coron, Control and Nonlinearity (AMS 2007), Theorem 3.18, proved independently by Rashevskii (1938) and Chow (1939). Coron's controls are in \(L^\infty((0, T); \mathbb{R}^m)\); the Lean statement asks for a measurable control bounded on \([0, T]\), the same class. Since the fields are continuous on \(\Omega\) and the trajectory stays in \(\Omega\), the integrand is integrable and the integral equation is the usual notion of solution. Coron's proof also gives small-time local controllability at every point of \(\Omega\) (Theorem 3.19), which is not recorded here. The statement is recorded as wanted; a test checks that its hypotheses hold for the nonholonomic integrator on \(\Omega = \mathbb{R}^3\).

    DefinitionLocal asymptotic stability of an equilibrium of a continuous vector field#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Control.IsLocallyAsymptoticallyStable

    Let \(X : E \to E\) be a vector field on a real normed space. The origin is locally asymptotically stable for \(\dot x = X(x)\) if there is \(\delta > 0\) such that for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is \(M > 0\) with \(\lVert x(t) \rVert < \varepsilon\) for every solution \(x\) of \(\dot x = X(x)\) with \(\lVert x(0) \rVert < \delta\) and every time \(t > M\) in its interval of definition.

    This is Coron, Control and Nonlinearity (AMS 2007), Definition 10.6, at the equilibrium \(x_e = 0\). Because \(X\) is only continuous, the Cauchy problem can have many solutions, and Coron imposes the condition on every maximal solution. The Lean definition imposes it on every solution defined on an interval \([0, \tau)\), \(\tau \in \mathbb{R}\) (with the derivative taken within the interval, one-sided at \(0\)). Every maximal solution restricts to such solutions, and, for \(E\) finite-dimensional, every solution on \([0, \tau)\) extends to a maximal one (for continuous \(X\), by Peano's theorem and Zorn's lemma), so the two conditions agree. As in Coron's definition, the condition says nothing about a solution that ceases to exist before time \(M\); in finite dimension this is harmless, since for a smaller \(\delta\) the definition forces every solution to be global and attracted to \(0\). Coron's Theorem 10.8 shows the definition is equivalent to stability plus attractivity.

    def IsLocallyAsymptoticallyStable (X : E → E) : Prop :=
  ∃ δ > 0, ∀ ε > 0, ∃ M > 0, ∀ τ : ℝ, ∀ x : ℝ → E,
    (∀ t ∈ Ico 0 τ, HasDerivWithinAt x (X (x t)) (Ico 0 τ) t) → ‖x 0‖ < δ →
      ∀ t ∈ Ico 0 τ, M < t → ‖x t‖ < ε

    DefinitionStabilisability by continuous stationary feedback#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Control.IsLocallyAsymptoticallyStabilizable

    The control system \(\dot x = f(x, u)\), \(f : E \times U \to E\), is locally asymptotically stabilisable by continuous stationary feedback laws if there is a continuous \(u : E \to U\) with \(u(0) = 0\) such that \(0\) is locally asymptotically stable for the closed-loop system \(\dot x = f(x, u(x))\) (Coron, Control and Nonlinearity, AMS 2007, Definition 10.11). "Stationary" distinguishes these feedback laws from the time-varying feedback laws \(u(x, t)\) of Coron's Section 11.2.

  ∃ u : E → U, Continuous u ∧ u 0 = 0 ∧ IsLocallyAsymptoticallyStable fun x => f (x, u x)

    DefinitionBrockett's condition#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Control.BrockettCondition

    A map \(f : E \times U \to E\) satisfies Brockett's condition if the image under \(f\) of every neighbourhood of \((0, 0)\) is a neighbourhood of \(0\) in \(E\) (Coron, Control and Nonlinearity, AMS 2007, Theorem 11.1, after Brockett 1983). For \(f(0, 0) = 0\) this is necessary for local asymptotic stabilisation of \(\dot x = f(x, u)\) by continuous stationary feedback.

    def BrockettCondition (f : E × U → E) : Prop :=
  ∀ N ∈ 𝓝 ((0 : E), (0 : U)), f '' N ∈ 𝓝 (0 : E)

    TheoremBrockett's necessary condition for stabilisation#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Control.NonlinearControlWanted.brockettCondition_of_isLocallyAsymptoticallyStabilizable

    Let \(E\) and \(U\) be finite-dimensional real normed spaces and \(f : E \times U \to E\) of class \(C^\infty\) with \(f(0, 0) = 0\). If \(\dot x = f(x, u)\) is locally asymptotically stabilisable by continuous stationary feedback laws, then the image under \(f\) of every neighbourhood of \((0, 0)\) is a neighbourhood of \(0\).

    This is Brockett's theorem (Brockett 1983), as stated in Coron, Control and Nonlinearity (AMS 2007), Theorem 11.1. Coron allows \(f\) to be defined and smooth only on an open neighbourhood of \((0, 0)\); the Lean statement is the case of an everywhere defined smooth \(f\). Coron derives it from the stronger index condition (Theorem 11.4): there is a continuous feedback whose closed-loop vector field has index \((-1)^n\) at \(0\), and a vector field of nonzero degree on a small ball maps it onto a neighbourhood of \(0\). The statement is recorded as wanted. A test checks that the nonholonomic integrator satisfies the regularity hypotheses, so with this theorem the next result shows it cannot be stabilised by continuous stationary feedback.

    DefinitionThe nonholonomic integrator#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Control.nonholonomicIntegrator

    The nonholonomic integrator is the control system \(\dot x_1 = u_1\), \(\dot x_2 = u_2\), \(\dot x_3 = x_1 u_2 - x_2 u_1\) with state \(x \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and control \(u \in \mathbb{R}^2\) (Coron, Control and Nonlinearity, AMS 2007, (3.41) and (11.4); Brockett 1983). It is the driftless system \(u_1 f_1 + u_2 f_2\) with \(f_1(x) = (1, 0, -x_2)\) and \(f_2(x) = (0, 1, x_1)\), whose bracket \([f_1, f_2] = (0, 0, 2)\) completes \(f_1(x), f_2(x)\) to a basis; so it satisfies the Lie algebra rank condition everywhere and is small-time locally and globally controllable (Coron, Example 3.20). Coordinates are indexed from \(0\) in Lean.

    def nonholonomicIntegrator (p : (Fin 3 → ℝ) × (Fin 2 → ℝ)) : Fin 3 → ℝ :=
  ![p.2 0, p.2 1, p.1 0 * p.2 1 - p.1 1 * p.2 0]

    TheoremThe nonholonomic integrator violates Brockett's condition#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Control.not_brockettCondition_nonholonomicIntegrator

    No point \((0, 0, \eta)\) with \(\eta \ne 0\) is a value of the nonholonomic integrator \(f(x, u) = (u_1, u_2, x_1 u_2 - x_2 u_1)\): the first two coordinates force \(u = 0\), and then the third vanishes. So even the image of the whole space is not a neighbourhood of \(0\), and Brockett's condition fails (Coron, Control and Nonlinearity, AMS 2007, Example 11.2, after Brockett 1983). By Brockett's theorem the nonholonomic integrator, although controllable, cannot be locally asymptotically stabilised by continuous stationary feedback laws; it can be globally stabilised by smooth periodic time-varying feedback (Coron, Example 11.16, from Theorem 11.14).

    theorem not_brockettCondition_nonholonomicIntegrator :
    ¬ BrockettCondition nonholonomicIntegrator
