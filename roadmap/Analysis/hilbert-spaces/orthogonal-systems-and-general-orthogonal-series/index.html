    TheoremBanach's Problem 186: a complete orthonormal system with divergent Fourier series#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Analysis.FunctionalAnalysis.ScottishBook186Wanted.banach186_orthonormal_system_divergent_ae

    There is a sequence \((\varphi_i)_{i \ge 0}\) of square-integrable real functions on \([0, 1]\), with respect to Lebesgue measure, that is orthonormal, \(\int_0^1 \varphi_i \varphi_j \, dx = \delta_{ij}\) for all \(i, j\), and complete, in the sense that every square-integrable \(g\) on \([0, 1]\) with \(\int_0^1 g \varphi_i \, dx = 0\) for all \(i\) vanishes almost everywhere on \([0, 1]\), such that the following holds: for every continuous \(f \colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) that is not identically zero on \([0, 1]\), with Fourier coefficients \(c_i = \int_0^1 f \varphi_i \, dx\), the partial sums \(\sum_{i < N} c_i \varphi_i(t)\) are unbounded in \(N\) for almost every \(t \in [0, 1]\).

    The \(\varphi_i\) are not assumed continuous: the source imposes continuity only on \(f\), and continuity of the \(\varphi_i\) would make the claim false (taking \(f = \varphi_0\) would give Fourier coefficients \(\delta_{0i}\) and bounded partial sums).

    DefinitionCountability of orthogonal systems of sequences#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.FunctionalAnalysis.ScottishBook51Wanted.SequenceSystemsAreCountable

    This is the statement that every set \(S\) of real sequences \((f_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}\) with the following two properties is at most countable: no member of \(S\) is the zero sequence; and for any two distinct \(f, g \in S\) the series \(\sum_{n} f_n g_n\) converges absolutely and its sum is \(0\).

    The zero sequence is excluded because it is orthogonal to everything, so without this it would be a trivial member of every family.

    def SequenceSystemsAreCountable : Prop :=
  ∀ S : Set (ℕ → ℝ),
    (∀ f ∈ S, f ≠ 0) →
    (∀ f ∈ S, ∀ g ∈ S, f ≠ g →
      Summable (fun n => f n * g n) ∧ ∑' n, f n * g n = 0) →
    S.Countable

    DefinitionCountability of orthogonal systems of functions#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.FunctionalAnalysis.ScottishBook51Wanted.FunctionSystemsAreCountable

    Let \(\lambda\) be Lebesgue measure on \([0, 1]\). This is the statement that every set \(S\) of functions \([0, 1] \to \mathbb{R}\) with the following three properties is at most countable: no member of \(S\) is \(\lambda\)-almost everywhere equal to \(0\); any two members of \(S\) that are equal \(\lambda\)-almost everywhere are equal; and any two members \(f, g\) of \(S\) that are not equal \(\lambda\)-almost everywhere are measurable, have \(\lambda\)-integrable product \(fg\), and satisfy \(\int_{[0, 1]} f g \, d\lambda = 0\).

    No square-integrability is assumed. Without the second property, uncountably many almost-everywhere-equal copies of one function would satisfy the orthogonality condition vacuously. The first property excludes the indicator functions of single points, each of which is zero almost everywhere.

    def FunctionSystemsAreCountable : Prop :=
  ∀ S : Set (↥(Set.Icc (0 : ℝ) 1) → ℝ),
    (∀ f ∈ S, ¬ (f =ᵐ[MeasureTheory.volume] 0)) →
    (∀ f ∈ S, ∀ g ∈ S, f =ᵐ[MeasureTheory.volume] g → f = g) →
    (∀ f ∈ S, ∀ g ∈ S, ¬ (f =ᵐ[MeasureTheory.volume] g) →
      Measurable f ∧ Measurable g ∧
      MeasureTheory.Integrable (fun x : ↥(Set.Icc (0 : ℝ) 1) => f x * g x) ∧
      MeasureTheory.integral MeasureTheory.volume
        (fun x : ↥(Set.Icc (0 : ℝ) 1) => f x * g x) = 0) →
    S.Countable

    TheoremOrthogonal systems need not be countable#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Analysis.FunctionalAnalysis.ScottishBook51Wanted.orthogonal_systems_not_necessarily_countable

    Both of the following statements are false. (a) Every set \(S\) of real sequences \((f_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}\) in which no member is the zero sequence, and in which for any two distinct \(f, g \in S\) the series \(\sum_n f_n g_n\) converges absolutely with sum \(0\), is at most countable. (b) Let \(\lambda\) be Lebesgue measure on \([0, 1]\). Every set \(S\) of functions \([0, 1] \to \mathbb{R}\) in which no member is \(\lambda\)-almost everywhere equal to \(0\), no two distinct members are equal \(\lambda\)-almost everywhere, and any two members \(f, g\) that are not equal \(\lambda\)-almost everywhere are measurable with \(fg\) \(\lambda\)-integrable and \(\int_{[0, 1]} f g \, d\lambda = 0\), is at most countable.

    This answers both parts of Mazur's Problem 51 of the Scottish Book in the negative: Plichko and Razenkov construct a continuum-sized pairwise-orthogonal family of sequences, and from it an uncountable orthogonal system of functions on an interval. No square-integrability is assumed; in \(\ell^2\) or \(L^2\) an orthogonal family is at most countable, since those spaces are separable Hilbert spaces.

    MathlibExt.Analysis.FunctionalAnalysis.ScottishBook86Wanted.ScottishBookProblem86Claim

    The claim that for every real \(M > 0\) and every sequence \((\varphi_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}\) of functions \(\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) that are almost everywhere measurable on \([0, 1]\), orthonormal in \(L^2[0, 1]\) (that is, \(\int_0^1 \varphi_n \varphi_m \, dt\) is \(1\) if \(n = m\) and \(0\) otherwise) and satisfy \(|\varphi_n(t)| \le M\) for all \(n\) and all \(t \in [0, 1]\), there is a sequence \((\psi_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}\) of functions \(\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\), almost everywhere measurable on \([0, 1]\), orthonormal in the same sense and with \(|\psi_n(t)| \le M\) for all \(n\) and all \(t \in [0, 1]\), such that every \(\varphi_n\) equals some \(\psi_m\) and \((\psi_n)\) is complete: every \(g \in L^2[0, 1]\) with \(\int_0^1 \psi_n g \, dt = 0\) for all \(n\) vanishes almost everywhere on \([0, 1]\).

    The case of interest in the original problem is when infinitely many functions are necessary for the completion.
