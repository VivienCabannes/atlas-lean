    Sparse recovery under the restricted isometry property

    Compressed sensing recovers a sparse vector x ∈ ℝ^N from m ≪ N linear measurements y = Φ x by ℓ₁ minimization (basis pursuit), min ‖x̃‖₁ subject to Φ x̃ = y. The restricted isometry property asks that Φ act as a near-isometry on sparse vectors: the isometry constant δ_s is the least δ with (1 - δ) ‖x‖₂² ≤ ‖Φ x‖₂² ≤ (1 + δ) ‖x‖₂² for every vector with at most s nonzero entries. Following Candès (C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 2008), this topic proves the chain from the definition to exact recovery: δ_{2s} < 1 makes s-sparse solutions unique (no sparse vector is lost); disjointly supported sparse vectors have nearly orthogonal images (Lemma 2.1); for δ_{2s} < √2 - 1 every kernel vector satisfies the null space property ‖h_{T₀}‖₁ ≤ ρ ‖h_{T₀ᶜ}‖₁ with ρ = √2 δ_{2s} / (1 - δ_{2s}) < 1 (Lemma 2.2), hence the restricted nullspace property and exact recovery of every s-sparse vector by basis pursuit. The stable and noisy recovery bounds of Theorems 1.2 and 1.3, which control the error by the best s-term approximation error and the noise level, are recorded as wanted statements.

    Random matrices with m ≳ s log(N / s) rows satisfy the restricted isometry property with high probability; that probabilistic step is not formalized here.

    DefinitionThe restricted isometry property of order s with constant δ#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Approximation.SparseRestrictedIsometry.HasRIP

    Every s-sparse vector x, that is every x with at most s nonzero entries (hammingNorm x ≤ s), satisfies (1 - δ) ‖x‖₂² ≤ ‖Φ x‖₂² ≤ (1 + δ) ‖x‖₂².

    Candès's isometry constant δ_s is the least such δ. The set of admissible δ is an up-set (monotonicity), nonempty, closed (an intersection of closed half-lines) and, for s ≥ 1 and nonempty ι, bounded below by 0 (nonnegativity), so it has a least element and "δ_s < c" means HasRIP Φ s δ for some δ < c; the results of this topic are stated in that form. (The existence of the least element is a remark, not formalized here.) The norms are squared, unlike the restricted isometry property for low-rank matrices of Recht, Fazel and Parrilo, which bounds unsquared norms; the two constants are therefore not interchangeable.

    def HasRIP (Φ : Matrix (Fin m) ι ℝ) (s : ℕ) (δ : ℝ) : Prop :=
  ∀ x : ι → ℝ, hammingNorm x ≤ s →
    (1 - δ) * ∑ j, x j ^ 2 ≤ ∑ i, (Φ *ᵥ x) i ^ 2 ∧ ∑ i, (Φ *ᵥ x) i ^ 2 ≤ (1 + δ) * ∑ j, x j ^ 2

    Background
    Background
    • Restricted isometry implies the restricted nullspace property: the hypothesis δ_{2s} < √2 - 1 gives the restricted nullspace property over the support of x.
    • Exact recovery by basis pursuit: the restricted nullspace property is equivalent to exact recovery of every vector with that support.
    • Noiseless recovery, ℓ₁ error: the source's route, the stable bound (4) applied to an s-sparse x.

    Related topics

    • Exact recovery by basis pursuit: basis pursuit recovers every vector supported on S iff the restricted nullspace property holds over S; the restricted isometry property with δ_{2s} < √2 - 1 is a sufficient condition for it, uniform over all supports of size at most s.
    • Low-rank matrix recovery: the matrix analogue, with rank for sparsity and the nuclear norm for the ℓ₁ norm; its restricted isometry property bounds unsquared norms.
    • Sparse recovery by orthogonal matching pursuit: sparse recovery by a greedy algorithm under a coherence condition rather than by convex minimization.
    • Lasso error bounds under restricted eigenvalues: the statistical counterpart with noise, where a restricted eigenvalue condition on the cone plays the role of the restricted isometry property.
