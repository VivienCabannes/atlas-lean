    Low-rank matrix recovery

    Affine rank minimization asks for the matrix of least rank satisfying linear measurements \(\mathcal{A}(X) = b\); it is NP-hard in general. Recht, Fazel and Parrilo (2010) replace the rank by the nuclear norm \(\lVert X\rVert_* = \sum_i \sigma_i(X)\), its convex envelope on the unit ball of the operator norm, and prove that the two problems have the same solution when \(\mathcal{A}\) satisfies a restricted isometry property on low-rank matrices, the matrix analogue of compressed sensing. The uniqueness of the low-rank solution under \(\delta_{2r} < 1\) is proved; exact recovery by nuclear norm minimization under \(\delta_{5r} < 1/10\) is recorded as a wanted statement. Matrix completion, where the measurements are a uniformly random set of entries, is the case where no restricted isometry property can hold (a matrix with a single nonzero entry is invisible to most samples); Candès and Recht (2009) show that incoherence of the row and column spaces replaces it, and that nuclear norm minimization recovers a rank-\(r\) \(n_1 \times n_2\) matrix from \(m \gtrsim n^{5/4} r \log n\) random entries with high probability (\(n^{6/5}\) for ranks \(r \lesssim n^{1/5}\)), up to factors depending on the incoherence parameters. Their theorem is recorded as a wanted statement.

    DefinitionNuclear norm#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.LinearAlgebra.Matrix.LowRankRecovery.nuclearNorm

    The nuclear norm of a real \(m \times n\) matrix \(X\) is the sum of its singular values,

    \[ \lVert X\rVert_* = \sum_{i} \sigma_i(X), \]

    where \(X\) acts as a linear map between the Euclidean spaces \(\mathbb{R}^n\) and \(\mathbb{R}^m\). It is the dual norm of the operator norm (duality of the spectral and nuclear norms) and the convex envelope of the rank on the unit ball of the operator norm (Fazel; Theorem 2.2 of the source), which makes it the convex surrogate of the rank in rank minimization.

    noncomputable def nuclearNorm [DecidableEq n] (X : Matrix m n ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (Matrix.toEuclideanLin X).singularValues.sum fun _ s => s

    DefinitionRestricted isometry property#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.LinearAlgebra.Matrix.LowRankRecovery.IsRestrictedIsometry

    Let \(\mathcal{A}\) be a linear map from real \(m \times n\) matrices to \(\mathbb{R}^p\). It is a restricted isometry of order \(r\) with constant \(\delta\) if

    \[ (1 - \delta)\, \lVert X\rVert_F \le \lVert \mathcal{A}(X)\rVert \le (1 + \delta)\, \lVert X\rVert_F \]

    for every matrix \(X\) of rank at most \(r\), where \(\lVert X\rVert_F = (\sum_{i,j} X_{ij}^2)^{1/2}\) is the Frobenius norm. The \(r\)-restricted isometry constant \(\delta_r(\mathcal{A})\) is the least such \(\delta\); hence \(\delta_r(\mathcal{A}) < c\) exactly when \(\mathcal{A}\) is a restricted isometry of order \(r\) with some constant \(\delta < c\), which is how the conditions of this topic are stated. The norms are not squared, following the source; for diagonal matrices this is the restricted isometry property of Candès and Tao for sparse vectors.

    def IsRestrictedIsometry {p : ℕ} (𝒜 : Matrix m n ℝ →ₗ[ℝ] EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin p)) (r : ℕ)
    (δ : ℝ) : Prop :=
  ∀ X : Matrix m n ℝ, X.rank ≤ r →
    (1 - δ) * ‖X‖ ≤ ‖𝒜 X‖ ∧ ‖𝒜 X‖ ≤ (1 + δ) * ‖X‖

    TheoremSubadditivity of the rank#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.LinearAlgebra.Matrix.LowRankRecovery.rank_sub_le

    For real matrices \(A\) and \(B\) of the same size, \(\operatorname{rank}(A - B) \le \operatorname{rank} A + \operatorname{rank} B\), since the column space of \(A - B\) lies in the sum of the column spaces of \(A\) and \(B\).

    theorem rank_sub_le (A B : Matrix m n ℝ) : (A - B).rank ≤ A.rank + B.rank

    TheoremUniqueness of the low-rank solution under restricted isometry#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.LinearAlgebra.Matrix.LowRankRecovery.eq_of_isRestrictedIsometry_two_mul

    Let \(\mathcal{A}\) be a restricted isometry of order \(2r\) with constant \(\delta < 1\), that is, \(\delta_{2r}(\mathcal{A}) < 1\). If \(X\) and \(X_0\) have rank at most \(r\) and \(\mathcal{A}(X) = \mathcal{A}(X_0)\), then \(X = X_0\): \(X_0\) is the only matrix of rank at most \(r\) satisfying the measurements.

    Proof: \(Z = X_0 - X\) has rank at most \(2r\) by subadditivity of the rank and \(\mathcal{A}(Z) = 0\), so \(0 = \lVert\mathcal{A}(Z)\rVert \ge (1 - \delta)\lVert Z\rVert_F\) forces \(Z = 0\).

    This identifies the solution of rank minimization; that nuclear norm minimization finds it requires the stronger condition of exact recovery.

    Related topics

    • Sparse recovery under the restricted isometry property: sparse vectors are the diagonal case of low-rank matrices; on diagonal matrices the restricted isometry property of this topic becomes a vector restricted isometry property with unsquared norms, whereas Candès's isometry constants bound squared norms. The two are related but not equal: for δ ∈ [0, 1] a squared-norm constant δ is also an unsquared one, and an unsquared constant δ gives squared-norm bounds with constant 2δ + δ².

    • Exact recovery by basis pursuit: the exact vector analogue of nuclear norm recovery, \(\ell_1\) minimization recovering sparse vectors, characterized by the restricted nullspace property.

    TheoremExact recovery by nuclear norm minimization under restricted isometry#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.LinearAlgebra.NuclearNormRecoveryWanted.nuclearNorm_lt_of_isRestrictedIsometry_five_mul

    Let \(r \ge 1\) and let \(\mathcal{A}\) be a restricted isometry of order \(5r\) with a constant \(\delta < 1/10\), that is, \(\delta_{5r}(\mathcal{A}) < 1/10\). Let \(X_0\) have rank at most \(r\). Then \(X_0\) is the unique minimizer of the nuclear norm over the affine space \(\{X : \mathcal{A}(X) = \mathcal{A}(X_0)\}\): every other \(X\) with \(\mathcal{A}(X) = \mathcal{A}(X_0)\) has \(\lVert X_0\rVert_* < \lVert X\rVert_*\).

    The source states \(X_* = X_0\) for \(X_*\) the minimizer of the nuclear norm under the constraint (3.1) and \(X_0\) of rank \(r\). Under the weaker condition \(\delta_{2r} < 1\), \(X_0\) is already the only matrix of rank at most \(r\) satisfying the measurements; the theorem adds that nuclear norm minimization finds it. The statement for rank at most \(r\) follows, since \(\delta_{5r'} \le \delta_{5r}\) for \(r' \le r\), and for \(X_0 = 0\) it is immediate.
