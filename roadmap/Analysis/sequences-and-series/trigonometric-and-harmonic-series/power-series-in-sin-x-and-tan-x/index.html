    MathlibExt.Analysis.Ramanujan.Part1Ch9.Entry20Piseries1.ramanujan_part1_ch9_entry20_piseries1

    Let \(x\) be a real number with \(|x| \le \pi/2\), let \(M(x) = \frac{x^2}{2} \log|2\sin x|\) for \(x \ne 0\) and \(M(0) = 0\), and let \(\zeta(3) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k^{-3}\). Then \(|2\sin x| > 0\) if \(x \ne 0\), the series \(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k^{-3}\), \(A(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^{2k} (k!)^2 \sin^{2k+2} x}{(2k+1)! \, (2k+2)^2}\), \(B(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sin 2kx}{k^2}\) and \(E(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\cos 2kx}{k^3}\) are all summable, and \(A(x) = M(x) + \frac{x}{2} B(x) + \frac{1}{4} E(x) - \frac{\zeta(3)}{4}\).

    TheoremEntry 22 of Chapter 9 of Ramanujan's notebooks#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Ramanujan.Part1Ch9.Entry22Chudnovsky.ramanujan_part1_ch9_entry22_chudnovsky

    For real \(u\) and \(k \ge 0\) let \(T_k(u) = \frac{2^{2k} (k!)^2 \sin^{2k+2} u}{(2k+1)! \, (2k+2)^2}\). Let \(x\) be real with \(0 \le x \le \pi/4\). Then \(2 \cos x > 0\), the series \(\sum_{j \ge 0} \frac{1}{(2j+1)^3}\), \(\sum_{k \ge 0} \bigl(T_k(x) + T_k(\pi/2 - x)\bigr)\), \(\sum_{k \ge 0} \frac{\binom{2k}{k} \cos^{2k+1} x}{2^{2k} (2k+1)^2}\) and \(\sum_{k \ge 0} T_k(2x)\) converge absolutely, and

    \[ \sum_{k \ge 0} \bigl(T_k(x) + T_k(\pi/2 - x)\bigr) = -\frac{\pi^2}{8} \log(2 \cos x) + \frac{\pi}{2} \sum_{k \ge 0} \frac{\binom{2k}{k} \cos^{2k+1} x}{2^{2k} (2k+1)^2} + \frac{1}{4} \sum_{k \ge 0} T_k(2x) - \frac{1}{2} \sum_{j \ge 0} \frac{1}{(2j+1)^3}. \]

    Proof: Differentiate the factorial and central-binomial series termwise, reduce the logarithmic integral by Abel's limit and dominated convergence, and finish by the fundamental theorem of calculus. This follows Berndt's Entries 20–23 and Lehmer's arcsine series.
