    TheoremRamanujan's asymptotic expansion of power sums#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Ramanujan.Part1Ch7.Entry1Powersum.ramanujan_part1_ch7_entry1_powersum

    Let \(r \in \mathbb{C}\) with \(r \ne -1\), and let \(N\) be a positive integer with \(\mathrm{Re}\, r < 2N\). For natural \(n\) put \(A_N(n) = \zeta(-r) + \frac{n^{r+1}}{r+1} + \frac{n^r}{2} + \sum_{k=1}^{N} T_k(n)\), where \(T_k(n) = \frac{B_{2k}}{(2k)!}\, r(r-1)\cdots(r-2k+2)\, n^{r-2k+1}\) (a product of \(2k - 1\) factors), \(\zeta\) is the Riemann zeta function, \(B_{2k}\) are the Bernoulli numbers and complex powers are principal. Then \(\sum_{k=1}^{n} k^r - A_N(n) = O(n^{\mathrm{Re}\, r - 2N})\) as \(n \to \infty\).

    Proof: Euler--Maclaurin gives the expansion with an integrable periodic-kernel tail. The constant is identified with the Riemann zeta function first in its convergent half-plane, then everywhere needed by analytic continuation.

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Ramanujan.Part1Ch8.Entry19Gaussdigamma.ramanujan_part1_ch8_entry19_gaussdigamma

    For natural numbers \(m\) and \(x\) let \(S_m(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{x} (\log k)^m\), let \(c_m = (-1)^m \zeta^{(m)}(0)\), where \(\zeta^{(m)}(0)\) is the \(m\)-th derivative at \(0\) of the real function \(s \mapsto \operatorname{Re} \zeta(s)\), and let \(\psi_m(x) = S_m(x) - c_m\). Let \(n\) be a natural number. Then for every \(m \le n\),

    \[S_m(x) - \int_1^x (\log t)^m \, dt - \frac{(\log x)^m}{2} + (-1)^{m+1} m! \to c_m\]

    as \(x \to \infty\) through the natural numbers, and

    \[\sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^{n+k} \binom{n}{k} (\log x)^k \psi_{n-k}(x) = n! \, x - \frac{B_{n+1}}{(n+1) x^n} - \frac{n B_{n+2}}{2 (n+2) x^{n+1}} - \frac{n (n + 5/3) B_{n+3}}{8 (n+3) x^{n+2}} + O\left(\frac{1}{x^{n+3}}\right)\]

    as \(x \to \infty\) through the natural numbers, where \(B_j\) are the Bernoulli numbers with \(B_1 = -1/2\).

    Proof: Analytic continuation of ζ to re s > -1 by Euler–Maclaurin, Cauchy estimates for the remainder's derivatives, and the Euler–Maclaurin tail expansion of the log-power sums.

    Let \(r > -1\) be a real number. For a natural number \(n\) write \(\varphi_r(n) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} k^r \log k\) and \(S_r(n) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} k^r\), and for a natural number \(m\) let \(D_m(r) = \sum_{j=0}^{m} \prod_{0 \le q \le m,\ q \ne j} (r - q)\), the derivative of \(r(r-1)\cdots(r-m)\) with respect to \(r\). Let \(B_j\) be the Bernoulli numbers and \(\zeta\) the Riemann zeta function. Then there is a real constant \(C\) such that for every natural number \(N\), as \(n \to \infty\) through the natural numbers,

    \[ \varphi_r(n) - \log n \, \bigl(S_r(n) - \zeta(-r)\bigr) = C - \frac{n^{r+1}}{(r+1)^2} + \sum_{k=1}^{N} \frac{B_{2k}}{(2k)!} D_{2k-2}(r) \, n^{r-2k+1} + O\bigl(n^{r-2N-1}\bigr). \]

    Moreover, if \(r > 0\), then \(\Gamma(r+1) > 0\), the series \(\sum_{n \ge 1} \log n / n^{r+1}\) converges, and

    \[ C = \frac{2\Gamma(r+1)\zeta(r+1)}{(2\pi)^{r+1}} \Bigl(\sin\frac{\pi r}{2} \Bigl(\log(2\pi) - \frac{\Gamma'(r+1)}{\Gamma(r+1)}\Bigr) - \frac{\pi}{2} \cos\frac{\pi r}{2}\Bigr) + \frac{2\Gamma(r+1)\sin\frac{\pi r}{2}}{(2\pi)^{r+1}} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\log n}{n^{r+1}}. \]
