    Asymptotic expansion of the Catalan numbers

    TheoremBernoulli-polynomial expansion from a logarithmic difference equation#✓fully proved

    Real.tendsto_pow_mul_sub_bernoulli_sum_of_log_diff

    Let \(\alpha\), \(\beta\) and \(c\) be real numbers and let \((u_n)\) be a real sequence with \(u_n \to 0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\), \(u_{n+1} - u_n = \log(1 + \alpha/(n + c)) - \log(1 + \beta/(n + c)) + (\beta - \alpha) \log(1 + 1/(n + c))\). Let \(B_k(x)\) be the \(k\)-th Bernoulli polynomial and, for \(j \ge 0\), put \(a_j = (-1)^j \left(B_{j+2}(\alpha) - B_{j+2}(\beta)\right) / ((j + 1)(j + 2))\). Then for every natural number \(M\), \((n + c)^M \left(u_n - \sum_{j=0}^{M-1} a_j (n + c)^{-(j+1)}\right) \to 0\) as \(n \to \infty\).

    The normalization \(u_n \to 0\) fixes the otherwise undetermined additive constant.

    TheoremStirling-type asymptotic expansions of the Catalan numbers#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.catalan_stirling_asymptotic_expansions

    Let \(C_n\) be the \(n\)-th Catalan number and \(B_k\) the \(k\)-th Bernoulli number, and let \((E_k)\) be a sequence of integers with \(E_0 = 1\) and \(\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor} \binom{n}{2j} E_{n-2j} = 0\) for every \(n > 0\) (these are the Euler numbers \(1, 0, -1, 0, 5, \dots\)). For \(k \ge 1\) put \(c_k = (-1)^{k+1} \left((2^{-k} - 2) B_{k+1} - k - 1\right) / (k (k + 1))\) and \(d_k = 2^{-4k-2} (4 - E_{2k}) / k\). Then for every \(m \ge 0\), as \(n \to \infty\), \(\log\left(C_n \, n \sqrt{\pi n} / 4^n\right) = \sum_{k=1}^{m} c_k n^{-k} + o(n^{-m})\) and, writing \(x = n + 3/4\), \(\log\left(C_n \sqrt{\pi x^3} / 4^n\right) = \sum_{k=1}^{m} d_k x^{-2k} + o(x^{-2m})\).

    The first coefficient is \(c_1 = -9/8\), matching the classical expansion \(C_n = \frac{4^n}{\sqrt{\pi} \, n^{3/2}} \left(1 - \frac{9}{8n} + \dots\right)\).

    Proof: The Catalan recurrence gives a logarithmic first-difference equation. Telescoping its Bernoulli-polynomial expansion yields both all-orders formulas.

    TheoremUniqueness of the even-power shift in Catalan asymptotics#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.catalan_odd_terms_shift_unique

    Let \(C_n = \binom{2n}{n} / (n + 1)\) be the \(n\)-th Catalan number. For a real number \(\alpha\), there is a real sequence \((a_k)_{k \ge 0}\) with \(a_{2k+1} = 0\) for every \(k\) such that, for every \(N \ge 0\), the difference \(C_n \sqrt{\pi} \, (n + \alpha)^{3/2} / 4^n - \sum_{k=0}^{N} a_k (n + \alpha)^{-k}\) is \(o\left((n + \alpha)^{-N}\right)\) as \(n \to \infty\), if and only if \(\alpha = 3/4\).

    Elezović phrases this as the expansion "containing only odd terms"; the condition meant is that the odd-indexed coefficients vanish: his displayed series for \(\alpha = 3/4\) has only even powers, and his proof obtains \(\alpha = 3/4\) by setting the first odd coefficient to zero.

    Proof: The reverse implication exponentiates the all-orders logarithmic expansion of the Catalan numbers and uses the reflection symmetry of the Bernoulli polynomials to eliminate odd powers. The first logarithmic coefficient gives uniqueness, following Elezović.
