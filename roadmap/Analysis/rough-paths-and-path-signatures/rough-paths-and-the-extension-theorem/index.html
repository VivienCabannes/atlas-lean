    Rough paths and the extension theorem

    A rough path is a path together with enough of its iterated integrals to make its driven differential equations well posed. Lyons formalised this through multiplicative functionals: maps \((s, t) \mapsto X_{s,t}\) into the truncated tensor algebra \(T^{(n)}\) with \(X^0 = 1\) and \(X_{r,s} \otimes X_{s,t} = X_{r,t}\), the algebraic content of Chen's identity, controlled in \(p\)-variation by \(\|X^i_{s,t}\| \le \omega(s,t)^{i/p} / (\beta\,(i/p)!)\). The signature of a path of bounded variation is the basic example, with \(p = 1\). For a \(p\)-rough path, \(n = \lfloor p \rfloor\) levels are the right amount of data: the extension theorem says they determine all higher levels, in particular the signature, and the universal limit theorem (not formalised here) says that solutions of controlled differential equations depend continuously on these levels in \(p\)-variation, which gives a pathwise meaning to Stratonovich equations driven by Brownian motion lifted with its Lévy area, \(2 < p < 3\) (T. Lyons, Differential equations driven by rough signals, Rev. Mat. Iberoam. 14 (1998), §2.1–2.2).

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PathSignature.IsMultiplicativeFunctional

    A multiplicative functional of degree \(n\) on a set of times \(J\) is a map \((s, t) \mapsto X_{s,t}\), for \(s \le t\) in \(J\), into the truncated tensor algebra \(T^{(n)}\) (tensor series read through their coefficients on words of length at most \(n\)) such that \(X^0_{s,t} = 1\) and $\(X_{r,t} = X_{r,s} \otimes X_{s,t} \qquad (r \le s \le t),\)$ the product computed coefficientwise as in the product of tensor series. It abstracts Chen's identity: the signature of a path is one, in every degree.

    def IsMultiplicativeFunctional (n : ℕ) (X : ℝ → ℝ → List ι → ℝ) (J : Set ℝ) : Prop :=
  (∀ s ∈ J, ∀ t ∈ J, s ≤ t → X s t [] = 1) ∧
    ∀ r ∈ J, ∀ s ∈ J, ∀ t ∈ J, r ≤ s → s ≤ t → ∀ w : List ι, w.length ≤ n →
      X r t w = tensorMul (X r s) (X s t) w
    noncomputable def tensorMul (F G : List ι → ℝ) (w : List ι) : ℝ :=
  ∑ m ∈ Finset.range (w.length + 1), F (w.take m) * G (w.drop m)

    DefinitionControl function (Lyons)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PathSignature.IsControl

    A control on a set of times \(J\) is a function \(\omega(s, t)\) of \(s \le t\) in \(J\) that is continuous, nonnegative, zero on the diagonal and superadditive: $\(\omega(s,t) + \omega(t,u) \le \omega(s,u) \qquad (s \le t \le u).\)$ The \(p\)-th power of the \(p\)-variation of a path over \([s, t]\) is the basic example; the length \(\int_s^t |\dot X_u|\,du\) of a path of bounded variation is another. Lyons calls \(\omega\) regular when it is continuous near the diagonal and zero on it; the Lean definition asks for continuity on the whole simplex.

    def IsControl (ω : ℝ → ℝ → ℝ) (J : Set ℝ) : Prop :=
  ContinuousOn (fun q : ℝ × ℝ => ω q.1 q.2) {q | q.1 ∈ J ∧ q.2 ∈ J ∧ q.1 ≤ q.2} ∧
    (∀ t ∈ J, ω t t = 0) ∧ (∀ s ∈ J, ∀ t ∈ J, s ≤ t → 0 ≤ ω s t) ∧
    ∀ s ∈ J, ∀ t ∈ J, ∀ u ∈ J, s ≤ t → t ≤ u → ω s t + ω t u ≤ ω s u

    TheoremFactorial decay of the signature#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PathSignature.levelNorm_signature_le

    Let the velocity of a path \(X\) in \(\mathbb{R}^\iota\) (\(\iota\) finite) be continuous, and let \(s \le t\). Then for every \(i\), $\(\sum_{|w| = i} \big| S(X)^w_{s,t} \big| \le \frac{\omega(s,t)^i}{i!}, \qquad \omega(s,t) = \int_s^t \sum_j |v^j(u)|\, du ,\)$ where \(\omega(s,t)\) is the \(\ell^1\) length of the path between \(s\) and \(t\). So the levels of the signature decay factorially; this is the case \(p = 1\), \(\beta = 1\) of Lyons's control \(\omega^{i/p}/(\beta\,(i/p)!)\), and the reason the signature series of a linear controlled equation converges.

    theorem levelNorm_signature_le [Fintype ι] (hv : ∀ i, Continuous (v i)) {s t : ℝ} (hst : s ≤ t)
    (i : ℕ) :
    levelNorm (signature v s t) i ≤
      (∫ u in s..t, ∑ j, |v j u|) ^ i / i.factorial
    noncomputable def levelNorm [Fintype ι] (F : List ι → ℝ) (i : ℕ) : ℝ :=

    TheoremLyons's extension theorem: existence and uniqueness#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PathSignature.ExtensionTheoremWanted.exists_unique_extension

    Let \(p \ge 1\), \(n = \lfloor p \rfloor\), let \(J\) be an interval, and let \(X\) be a multiplicative functional of degree \(n\) on \(J\) with finite \(p\)-variation. Then there is a multiplicative functional \(Y\) of every degree, with \(Y^i_{s,t} = X^i_{s,t}\) for \(i \le n\) and \(s \le t\) in \(J\), of finite \(p\)-variation in every degree; and every multiplicative functional of some degree \(m\) with finite \(p\)-variation that agrees with \(X\) up to level \(n\) agrees with \(Y\) up to level \(m\).

    So a \(p\)-rough path, a multiplicative functional of degree \(\lfloor p \rfloor\), has unique higher iterated integrals, and hence a signature: for a path of bounded variation, \(p = 1\), the extension of its increments is its signature; for Brownian motion, \(2 < p < 3\), the two levels are the increments and the Lévy area. Fewer levels do not suffice: given a multiplicative functional of degree \(n < \lfloor p \rfloor\) and finite \(p\)-variation, an extension of finite \(p\)-variation, if one exists, is never unique (Lyons 1998, Remark 2.2.1(2); compare Chevyrev and Kormilitzin, §1.5.1, for paths of infinite \(p\)-variation for every \(p < 2\)).

    The Lean statement uses coordinates over words and the \(\ell^1\) norm of each tensor level, an admissible family of tensor norms in Lyons's sense.

    TheoremLyons's extension theorem: the factorial estimate#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PathSignature.ExtensionTheoremWanted.isControlledBy_extension

    Let \(p \ge 1\). There is \(\beta_0 > 0\) such that for every \(\beta \ge \beta_0\), every control \(\omega\) on an interval \(J\), and every multiplicative functional \(X\) of degree \(\lfloor p \rfloor\) whose levels satisfy $\(\|X^i_{s,t}\| \le \frac{\omega(s,t)^{i/p}}{\beta\, (i/p)!} \qquad (1 \le i \le \lfloor p \rfloor,\ s \le t \text{ in } J),\)$ the extension of \(X\) (multiplicative in every degree, of finite \(p\)-variation, agreeing with \(X\) up to level \(\lfloor p \rfloor\); unique by the existence and uniqueness part) satisfies the same inequality in every degree \(i\). Thus the extension is controlled by the same \(\omega\) with no loss of constant, and the factorial \((i/p)!\) makes the higher levels decay like the terms of an exponential series.

    The threshold \(\beta_0\) depends only on \(p\): in the Lean statement it is chosen before the alphabet. Lyons's Remark 2.2.1(1) gives an explicit sufficient value in terms of \(p\) and the Riemann zeta function, valid for any admissible family of tensor norms. Lyons states (2.20) and (2.21) with strict inequalities; at \(s = t\) both sides vanish, so the non-strict form is used here.
