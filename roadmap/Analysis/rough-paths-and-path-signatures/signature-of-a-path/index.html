    Signature of a path

    The signature of a path X : [s, t] → ℝ^d is the family of its iterated integrals S(X)^{i₁…i_k}_{s,t} = ∫_{s<t₁<⋯<t_k<t} dX^{i₁}_{t₁} ⋯ dX^{i_k}_{t_k}, one for every word i₁…i_k, read as the coefficients of a tensor series. It is the non-commutative exponential of the path: t ↦ S(X)_{s,t} solves the linear controlled equation dS = S ⊗ dX, S_s = 1, so the response of every linear controlled differential equation driven by X is a linear function of the signature (T. Lyons, Rough paths, signatures and the modelling of functions on streams, Proc. ICM 2014, Definition 4.5 and Lemma 4.6). Chen's identity makes the signature a homomorphism from concatenation of paths to the tensor product, which gives the signature of a piecewise-linear path as a product of exponentials of its increments; Ree's shuffle identity makes the coordinate iterated integrals an algebra of functions on paths, the starting point of the signature method in machine learning (I. Chevyrev and A. Kormilitzin, A primer on the signature method in machine learning, arXiv:1603.03788v2, §1.2–1.4).

    Here paths are absolutely continuous and described by their velocity; Chen's identity and the piecewise-linear formula need only a locally integrable velocity, the shuffle identity is proved for a continuous velocity.

    Let \(X\) be a path in \(\mathbb{R}^\iota\) described by its velocity \(v = (v^i)_{i \in \iota}\), so that \(dX^i_u = v^i(u)\,du\). For real \(s, t\) and a word \(w = i_1 \cdots i_k\) in the letters \(\iota\), the iterated integral of \(X\) along \(w\) over \([s, t]\) is $\(S(X)^{i_1 \cdots i_k}_{s,t} = \int_{s < t_1 < \cdots < t_k < t} dX^{i_1}_{t_1} \cdots dX^{i_k}_{t_k},\)$ defined recursively by \(S(X)^{\varnothing}_{s,t} = 1\) and $\(S(X)^{w i}_{s,t} = \int_s^t S(X)^{w}_{s,u}\, dX^i_u = \int_s^t S(X)^{w}_{s,u}\, v^i(u)\, du .\)$ The signature of \(X\) over \([s,t]\) is the whole family \(w \mapsto S(X)^w_{s,t}\), read as the coefficients of the tensor series \(S(X)_{s,t} = \sum_w S(X)^w_{s,t}\, e_w\); the integrals are oriented, so \(t < s\) is allowed.

    In Lean, signature v s t w is defined for any \(v\); the theorems about it assume that every \(v^i\) is interval integrable (an absolutely continuous path) or continuous (a \(C^1\) path).

    noncomputable def signature (v : ι → ℝ → ℝ) (s t : ℝ) (w : List ι) : ℝ :=
  go w.reverse t
where
  /- The iterated integrals indexed by reversed words: `go (i :: l) t` integrates the iterated
  integral of the reversed word `l` against `dXⁱ`. -/
  go : List ι → ℝ → ℝ
    | [], _ => 1
    | i :: l, t => ∫ u in s..t, go l u * v i u
