    Dyadic maximal function

    The dyadic intervals \(I_{k,m} = [m 2^{-k}, (m+1) 2^{-k})\) of the real line are nested: two of them are either disjoint or one contains the other. The dyadic maximal function \(M_d f(x)\) is the supremum of the averages of \(\|f\|\) over the dyadic intervals containing \(x\). Real-variable harmonic analysis in the tradition of C. Fefferman and E. M. Stein (Some maximal inequalities, Amer. J. Math. 93 (1971), 107–115; E. M. Stein, Harmonic Analysis, Princeton University Press, 1993) rests on one stopping-time idea, presented in T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, §§1, 3, 5 and 7, which these pages follow:

    1. Stopping time. For a height \(c\), the maximal dyadic intervals with average above \(c\) are disjoint (stopping level, finite-level estimate).
    2. Weak type (1,1). Summing over these disjoint intervals gives \(c\,|\{M_d f > c\}| \le \int_{\{M_d f > c\}} \|f\|\) (localized form, weak type (1,1)), proved here for every \(f\), with constant \(1\) and no covering lemma.
    3. Differentiation. For locally integrable \(f\) the averages of \(f\) over the dyadic intervals containing \(x\) converge to \(f(x)\) almost everywhere (dyadic Lebesgue differentiation); the form used below, for the averages of \(\|f\|\) with \(f\) integrable, is its norm version. Both are obtained from Mathlib's Vitali-family differentiation theorem; hence \(\|f\| \le M_d f\) a.e. (domination).
    4. Strong type. Integrating the localized bound gives the Doob-type inequality \(\int (M_d f)^p \le p' \int \|f\| (M_d f)^{p-1}\), and with Hölder's inequality the \(L^p\) bound \(\|M_d f\|_p \le p' \|f\|_p\), \(p' = p/(p-1)\), for \(1 < p < \infty\); the Marcinkiewicz splitting gives the cruder constant \(2^p p/(p-1)\) without any finiteness assumption, which the sharp proof needs.
    5. Calderón–Zygmund decomposition. For integrable \(f\) the stopping intervals at height \(c\) exist (existence), partition \(\{M_d f > c\}\), carry averages in \((c, 2c]\), and \(\|f\| \le c\) a.e. off their union (dyadic Calderón–Zygmund decomposition).

    The decomposition is the entry point for the weak type (1,1) of singular integrals, the John–Nirenberg inequality (see Related topics) and the Fefferman–Stein vector-valued inequality (recorded as a statement). Everything here is on \(\mathbb{R}\); the sources work on \(\mathbb{R}^d\) with dyadic cubes, where the constant \(2\) in step 5 becomes \(2^d\).

    DefinitionDyadic interval#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.HarmonicAnalysis.DyadicMaximal.dyadicInterval

    For integers \(k\) and \(m\), the dyadic interval of level \(k\) and index \(m\) is $\(I_{k,m} = [\,m 2^{-k},\ (m+1) 2^{-k}\,).\)$ For fixed \(k\) the intervals \(I_{k,m}\), \(m \in \mathbb{Z}\), partition \(\mathbb{R}\), they have length \(2^{-k}\), and every interval of level \(k\) is the disjoint union of two intervals of level \(k+1\). Larger \(k\) means finer intervals. This is the collection \(\mathcal{D}_k = \{2^{-k}([0,1)^d + m)\}\) of T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, §1.A, in dimension \(d = 1\).

    The Lean development identifies the level-\(k\) interval containing \(x\) through its index \(\lfloor 2^k x \rfloor\); the length of \(I_{k,m}\) is volume_dyadicInterval, and \(I_{k,m}\) agrees with the closed interval \([m 2^{-k}, (m+1) 2^{-k}]\) up to a null set (dyadicInterval_ae_eq_closedBall).

    def dyadicInterval (k m : ℤ) : Set ℝ := Ico ((m : ℝ) / 2 ^ k) (((m : ℝ) + 1) / 2 ^ k)

    DefinitionDyadic index#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.HarmonicAnalysis.DyadicMaximal.dyadicIndex

    The index of the level-\(k\) dyadic interval that contains \(x \in \mathbb{R}\) is \(\lfloor 2^k x \rfloor\): one has \(x \in I_{k,m}\) if and only if \(\lfloor 2^k x \rfloor = m\) (mem_dyadicInterval_iff). It is the integer \(m\) in the description \(2^{-k}([0,1) + m)\) of the level-\(k\) dyadic cubes of T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, §1.A (with \(d = 1\)). Through this index, every statement about which dyadic intervals contain a point becomes a statement about integer floors, and every set that is a union of level-\(k\) intervals is a preimage under \(x \mapsto \lfloor 2^k x \rfloor\), hence measurable.

    noncomputable def dyadicIndex (k : ℤ) (x : ℝ) : ℤ := ⌊x * 2 ^ k⌋

    TheoremNesting of dyadic intervals#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.HarmonicAnalysis.DyadicMaximal.dyadicIndex_of_le

    For levels \(j \le k\) and every \(x \in \mathbb{R}\), $\(\lfloor 2^j x \rfloor = \big\lfloor \lfloor 2^k x \rfloor / 2^{k-j} \big\rfloor,\)$ with integer division rounding down. Hence two points in the same level-\(k\) dyadic interval lie in the same level-\(j\) interval (dyadicIndex_eq_of_le): a finer dyadic interval is contained in exactly one interval of each coarser level. This is the nesting property of dyadic cubes (any two are nested or disjoint) used throughout T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, §1.A and Lemma 1.3, in dimension one.

    theorem dyadicIndex_of_le {j k : ℤ} (hjk : j ≤ k) (x : ℝ) :
    dyadicIndex j x = dyadicIndex k x / ((2 : ℕ) ^ (k - j).toNat : ℕ)
    noncomputable def dyadicIndex (k : ℤ) (x : ℝ) : ℤ := ⌊x * 2 ^ k⌋

    DefinitionDyadic average#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.HarmonicAnalysis.DyadicMaximal.dyadicAverage

    For \(f \colon \mathbb{R} \to E\), a level \(k \in \mathbb{Z}\) and \(x \in \mathbb{R}\), the dyadic average is $\(A_k f(x) = \frac{1}{|I_k(x)|} \int_{I_k(x)} \|f(y)\| \, dy \in [0, \infty],\)$ where \(I_k(x)\) is the level-\(k\) dyadic interval containing \(x\). It is Mathlib's set average ⨍⁻ of the extended norm ‖f‖ₑ for Lebesgue measure; it is defined for every \(f\) (a lower Lebesgue integral), with no measurability or integrability hypothesis. As a function of \(x\) it is constant on each level-\(k\) interval, and it is measurable (measurable_dyadicAverage).

    These are the averages \(\frac{1}{|Q|} \int_Q |f|\) over dyadic cubes in (1.4) of T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, with \(d = 1\).

  ⨍⁻ y in dyadicInterval k (dyadicIndex k x), ‖f y‖ₑ ∂volume

    DefinitionDyadic maximal function#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.HarmonicAnalysis.DyadicMaximal.dyadicMaximalFunction

    The dyadic maximal function of \(f \colon \mathbb{R} \to E\) is $\(M_d f(x) = \sup_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} A_k f(x) = \sup_{I \ni x \text{ dyadic}} \frac{1}{|I|} \int_I \|f\|,\)$ the supremum of the dyadic averages over all dyadic intervals containing \(x\); it takes values in \([0, \infty]\) and is measurable (measurable_dyadicMaximalFunction). This is definition (1.4) of T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014 in dimension \(d = 1\).

    Mathlib has no Hardy–Littlewood maximal function. The dyadic maximal function is the simplest maximal operator: dyadic intervals are nested, so the maximal intervals of any family of dyadic intervals with bounded lengths are disjoint, and no covering lemma is needed for its weak type (1,1) bound. On the line, every finite interval \(I\) lies in an interval \(J\) with \(3|I| < |J| \le 6|I|\) from the standard dyadic system or from one shifted copy of it (T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, Proposition 2.9), so the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function over intervals is at most \(6\) times the larger of the two dyadic maximal functions, and the dyadic bounds carry over.

    noncomputable def dyadicMaximalFunction (f : ℝ → E) (x : ℝ) : ℝ≥0∞ :=
  ⨆ k : ℤ, dyadicAverage k f x

    Background
    Background
    • Doob's maximal inequalities: on \([0,1)\) with Lebesgue measure, \(A_k f\) for \(k \ge 0\) is the conditional expectation of \(\|f\|\) given the \(\sigma\)-algebra generated by the level-\(k\) intervals, so \(\sup_{k \ge 0} A_k f\) is Doob's maximal function of a nonnegative martingale and the weak type (1,1) bound is Doob's maximal inequality.

    TheoremStopping-time estimate on finitely many levels#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.HarmonicAnalysis.DyadicMaximal.mul_volume_window_le

    For every \(f \colon \mathbb{R} \to E\), every \(c \in [0, \infty]\) and every pair of levels \(a \le b\), the set \(W = \{x : A_k f(x) > c \text{ for some } a \le k \le b\}\) satisfies $\(c\,|W| \le \int_W \|f\|.\)$

    The proof is the stopping-time argument. For \(a \le k \le b\) let \(S_k\) be the set of points whose first level in \([a, b]\) with average above \(c\) is \(k\). The sets \(S_k\) are disjoint with union \(W\). Membership in \(S_k\) depends only on the averages at levels \(\le k\), which by nesting depend only on the level-\(k\) interval of the point; so \(S_k\) is a union of level-\(k\) intervals, on each of which the average exceeds \(c\), that is \(c |I| \le \int_I \|f\|\). Summing over these disjoint intervals and over \(k\) gives the bound. These are the maximal dyadic intervals of the proof of T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, Proposition 1.5, restricted to the levels \(a, \dots, b\), where maximal intervals always exist.

    theorem mul_volume_window_le (f : ℝ → E) (c : ℝ≥0∞) (a b : ℤ) :
    c * volume {x | ∃ k ∈ Finset.Icc a b, c < dyadicAverage k f x} ≤
      ∫⁻ y in {x | ∃ k ∈ Finset.Icc a b, c < dyadicAverage k f x}, ‖f y‖ₑ

    TheoremWeak type (1,1) of the dyadic maximal function, localized form#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.HarmonicAnalysis.DyadicMaximal.mul_volume_lt_dyadicMaximalFunction_le

    For every function \(f \colon \mathbb{R} \to E\) and every \(c \in [0, \infty]\), $\(c \,\big|\{M_d f > c\}\big| \le \int_{\{M_d f > c\}} \|f(y)\| \, dy,\)$ where \(M_d\) is the dyadic maximal function.

    This is the sharper inequality obtained at the end of the proof of T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, Proposition 1.5 (case \(d = 1\)). The statement here is stronger than the source in one respect: it holds for every \(f\), not only for \(f \in L^1\). The source uses \(f \in L^1\) to guarantee that maximal dyadic intervals with average above \(c\) exist; the Lean proof instead restricts to the levels \(-N \le k \le N\), applies the finite-level estimate, and lets \(N \to \infty\) by continuity of measure along increasing unions. This is the localization in the proof of Proposition 1.9 of the same notes, which truncates to cubes of side length at most \(2^n\) for general measures, where the side-length argument of Proposition 1.5 is unavailable.

    theorem mul_volume_lt_dyadicMaximalFunction_le (f : ℝ → E) (c : ℝ≥0∞) :

      ∫⁻ y in {x | c < dyadicMaximalFunction f x}, ‖f y‖ₑ

    MathlibExt.Analysis.HarmonicAnalysis.DyadicMaximal.dyadicMaximalFunction_mul_volume_le_lintegral

    For every function \(f \colon \mathbb{R} \to E\) and every \(c \in [0, \infty]\), $\(c \,\big|\{M_d f > c\}\big| \le \int_{\mathbb{R}} \|f(y)\| \, dy,\)$ that is, \(|\{M_d f > c\}| \le \|f\|_1 / c\) for \(c > 0\): the dyadic maximal function maps \(L^1\) to weak \(L^1\) with constant \(1\).     TheoremDoob-type inequality for the dyadic maximal function#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.HarmonicAnalysis.DyadicMaximal.lintegral_rpow_le_lintegral_mul_dyadicMaximalFunction

    For \(1 < p\) and every \(f \colon \mathbb{R} \to E\) with \(\|f\|\) a.e. measurable, $\(\int_{\mathbb{R}} (M_d f)^p \le \frac{p}{p-1} \int_{\mathbb{R}} \|f\|\,(M_d f)^{p-1}.\)$ Proof: by the layer-cake formula \(\int (M_d f)^p = \int_0^\infty p t^{p-1} |\{M_d f > t\}| \, dt\); the localized weak type (1,1) bound gives \(t |\{M_d f > t\}| \le \int_{\{M_d f > t\}} \|f\|\); and Tonelli's theorem with \(\int_0^{M_d f(x)} p t^{p-2} dt = \frac{p}{p-1} M_d f(x)^{p-1}\) concludes. This is the argument behind Doob's \(L^p\) inequality, and the first step towards the \(L^p\) bound of T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, Theorem 3.8. The layer-cake formula used is a version for \([0, \infty]\)-valued functions (lintegral_rpow_eq_lintegral_volume_mul), proved from the identity \(a^q = \int_0^\infty \mathbf{1}_{t < a} \, q t^{q-1} \, dt\) for \(a \in [0, \infty]\), since Mathlib's layer-cake formula is stated for real-valued functions.

    TheoremL^p bound for the dyadic maximal function with constant \(2^p p/(p-1)\)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.HarmonicAnalysis.DyadicMaximal.ae_tendsto_dyadicAverage

    Let \(f \colon \mathbb{R} \to E\) with \(x \mapsto \|f(x)\|\) a.e. measurable and \(\int \|f\| < \infty\). Then for almost every \(x\), $\(\lim_{n \to \infty} A_n f(x) = \|f(x)\|,\)$ where \(A_n f(x)\) is the average of \(\|f\|\) over the level-\(n\) dyadic interval containing \(x\).     MathlibExt.Analysis.HarmonicAnalysis.DyadicMaximal.IsStoppingLevel

    A level \(k \in \mathbb{Z}\) is the stopping level of \(x\) at height \(c\) for \(f\) when $\(A_k f(x) > c \quad\text{and}\quad A_j f(x) \le c \text{ for all } j < k,\)$ so that the level-\(k\) dyadic interval \(Q\) containing \(x\) is a maximal dyadic interval whose average exceeds \(c\): these are the maximal cubes \(\mathcal{Q}_\lambda^*\) of the proof of T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, Proposition 1.5, in dimension one.

    Proved properties: the stopping level is unique (IsStoppingLevel.unique); every point of \(Q\) has the same stopping level (IsStoppingLevel.of_mem), so two stopping intervals are equal or disjoint (IsStoppingLevel.eq_or_disjoint, the dyadic case of T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, Lemma 1.3(2)); and \(A_k f(x) \le 2c\), since the parent interval has twice the length and average at most \(c\) (IsStoppingLevel.dyadicAverage_le_two_mul).

    MathlibExt.Analysis.HarmonicAnalysis.DyadicMaximal.calderon_zygmund

    Let \(f \colon \mathbb{R} \to E\) with \(\|f\|\) a.e. measurable and \(\int \|f\| < \infty\), let \(c \ne 0\), and let \(\Omega = \{M_d f > c\}\). Then:

    1. \(c\,|\Omega| \le \int \|f\|\);
    2. \(\|f(x)\| \le c\) for almost every \(x \notin \Omega\);
    3. every \(x \in \Omega\) has a stopping level \(k\), and the level-\(k\) dyadic interval \(Q \ni x\) satisfies $\(c < \frac{1}{|Q|} \int_Q \|f\| \le 2c,\)$ lies in \(\Omega\), and is the stopping interval of each of its points.

    By item 3 and IsStoppingLevel.eq_or_disjoint, the stopping intervals form a partition \(\Omega = \bigsqcup_j Q_j\) into maximal dyadic intervals, with \(\sum_j |Q_j| = |\Omega| \le \|f\|_1 / c\). This is the construction in the proof of T. Hytönen, Dyadic analysis and weights, lecture notes, University of Helsinki, version of 2 May 2014, Proposition 7.11, in dimension \(d = 1\), where \(2^d \lambda = 2\lambda\).

The Lean statement gives the cubes and the bounds that define \(g = f\,\mathbf{1}_{\Omega^c} + \sum_j \langle f \rangle_{Q_j} \mathbf{1}_{Q_j}\) and \(b = f - g\), but does not form \(g\) and \(b\); in that respect it is weaker than the source.
