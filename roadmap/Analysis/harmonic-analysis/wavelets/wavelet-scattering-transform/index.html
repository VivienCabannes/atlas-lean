    Wavelet scattering transform

    The scattering transform (S. Mallat, Group invariant scattering, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 65 (2012), 1331–1398, arXiv:1101.2286) builds translation-invariant representations of \(L^2(\mathbb{R}^d)\) that, unlike the Fourier modulus, are Lipschitz continuous to small deformations. One layer computes a low-pass average \(A_J f = f \star \varphi_{2^J}\) and the moduli of wavelet coefficients \(U[\lambda] f = |f \star \psi_\lambda|\); along a path \(p = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_m)\) the scattering propagator is the cascade \(U[p] f = |\,|f \star \psi_{\lambda_1}| \star \psi_{\lambda_2}| \cdots \star \psi_{\lambda_m}|\), and the windowed scattering is \(S_J[p] f = U[p] f \star \varphi_{2^J}\). The cascade is a convolutional network with fixed wavelet filters and modulus nonlinearities.

    Two properties of a single layer, nonexpansiveness and norm preservation (which the Littlewood–Paley condition and the contraction \(||a| - |b|| \le |a - b|\) give), already imply that the whole cascade is nonexpansive and satisfies the energy identity \(\|f\|^2 = \sum_{|p| < m} \|S_J[p] f\|^2 + \sum_{|p| = m} \|U[p] f\|^2\) at every depth; so the scattering preserves the norm exactly when the energy propagated to depth \(m\) vanishes as \(m \to \infty\). These structural results are proved here for an arbitrary layer. Mallat's analytic theorems are recorded in dimension one for real signals: for admissible wavelets the propagated energy does vanish, so the scattering preserves energy (Theorem 2.6), and it is Lipschitz continuous to \(C^2\) deformations (Theorem 2.12).

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Wavelet.ScatteringCascade.propagator

    For maps \(U[\lambda] : X \to X\) indexed by \(\lambda \in \Lambda\) and a path \(p = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_m)\), the scattering propagator is the path-ordered product $\(U[p] = U[\lambda_m] \cdots U[\lambda_2]\, U[\lambda_1], \qquad U[\emptyset] = \mathrm{Id}\)$ (S. Mallat, Group invariant scattering, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 65 (2012), 1331–1398 (arXiv:1101.2286), Definition 2.2, equation (15)). The first index of the path acts first, so \(U[p + p'] = U[p']\, U[p]\) for the concatenation \(p + p'\) (equation (17)). In Mallat's scattering \(U[\lambda] f = |f \star \psi_\lambda|\) and \(U[p] f = |\,|f \star \psi_{\lambda_1}| \star \psi_{\lambda_2}| \cdots \star \psi_{\lambda_m}|\) (equation (16)); here the maps are arbitrary, and paths of length \(m\) are functions \(\{0, \dots, m-1\} \to \Lambda\).

    def propagator (U : Λ → X → X) : {m : ℕ} → (Fin m → Λ) → X → X
  | 0, _ => id
  | _ + 1, p => propagator U (Fin.tail p) ∘ U (p 0)

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Wavelet.ScatteringCascade.cascade_le

    Let \(X\) be a set, \(a, c : X \to [0, \infty]\) and \(u_\lambda : X \to X\) for \(\lambda \in \Lambda\). If \(a(x) + \sum_\lambda c(u_\lambda x) \le c(x)\) for every \(x\), then for every depth \(m\), $\(\sum_{n < m} \sum_{|p| = n} a(u[p]\, x) + \sum_{|p| = m} c(u[p]\, x) \le c(x),\)$ by induction on \(m\), splitting each path along its first index. This is the bookkeeping of the proofs of Proposition 2.5 and Theorem 2.6 in S. Mallat, Group invariant scattering, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 65 (2012), 1331–1398 (arXiv:1101.2286), applied below to pairs of signals (nonexpansiveness) and to single signals (energy).

    theorem cascade_le (a c : X → ℝ≥0∞) (u : Λ → X → X)
    (h : ∀ x, a x + ∑' l, c (u l x) ≤ c x) (m : ℕ) (x : X) :
    ∑ n ∈ Finset.range m, ∑' p : Fin n → Λ, a (propagator u p x) +
      ∑' p : Fin m → Λ, c (propagator u p x) ≤ c x
    def propagator (U : Λ → X → X) : {m : ℕ} → (Fin m → Λ) → X → X
  | 0, _ => id

    Let \(E, F\) be (pseudo-e)metric spaces, \(A : E \to F\) and \(U[\lambda] : E \to E\) for \(\lambda \in \Lambda\), and suppose that one layer \(f \mapsto (A f, (U[\lambda] f)_\lambda)\) is nonexpansive: $\(\|A f - A h\|^2 + \sum_{\lambda} \|U[\lambda] f - U[\lambda] h\|^2 \le \|f - h\|^2 .\)$ Then for every depth \(m\), $\(\sum_{n < m} \sum_{|p| = n} \|A U[p] f - A U[p] h\|^2 + \sum_{|p| = m} \|U[p] f - U[p] h\|^2 \le \|f - h\|^2\)$ (the inequality chain in the proof of Proposition 2.5 of S.     MathlibExt.Analysis.Wavelet.ScatteringCascade.tsum_edist_sq_le

    If one layer \(f \mapsto (A f, (U[\lambda] f)_\lambda)\) is nonexpansive, then summing over all finite paths \(p\), $\(\sum_{p} \|A U[p] f - A U[p] h\|^2 \le \|f - h\|^2,\)$ that is \(\|S_J[P_J] f - S_J[P_J] h\| \le \|f - h\|\) for the windowed scattering \(S_J[p] = A_J U[p]\) (S. Mallat, Group invariant scattering, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 65 (2012), 1331–1398 (arXiv:1101.2286), Proposition 2.5, equations (27)–(28)). For Mallat's wavelet layer, nonexpansiveness follows from the unitarity of the wavelet transform \(W_J\) (Proposition 2.1) and \(||a| - |b|| \le |a - b|\). The statement holds for an arbitrary index set \(\Lambda\) and arbitrary, possibly nonlinear, maps.

    theorem tsum_edist_sq_le (A : E → F) (U : Λ → E → E)
    (hlayer : ∀ f h, edist (A f) (A h) ^ 2 + ∑' l, edist (U l f) (U l h) ^ 2 ≤ edist f h ^ 2)
    (f h : E) :
    ∑' p : (Σ n, Fin n → Λ), edist (A (propagator U p.2 f)) (A (propagator U p.2 h)) ^ 2 ≤
      edist f h ^ 2

    Let \(E, F\) be seminormed groups, \(A : E \to F\) and \(U[\lambda] : E \to E\), and suppose that one layer preserves the norm, \(\|A f\|^2 + \sum_\lambda \|U[\lambda] f\|^2 = \|f\|^2\).     MathlibExt.Analysis.Wavelet.Scattering1D.IsAdmissible

    A scattering wavelet \(\psi\) (with low-pass \(\varphi\)) is admissible (S. Mallat, Group invariant scattering, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 65 (2012), 1331–1398 (arXiv:1101.2286), Theorem 2.6, conditions (29)–(30), with \(d = 1\) and \(G = \{1, -1\}\)) if it satisfies the standing assumptions and there are \(\eta \in \mathbb{R}\) and an integrable \(\rho \ge 0\) with \(|\hat\rho(\omega)| \le |\hat\varphi(2\omega)|\) and \(\hat\rho(0) = 1\) such that $\(\alpha = \inf_{1 \le |\omega| \le 2} \sum_{j \in \mathbb{Z}} \sum_{r = \pm 1} \hat\Psi(2^{-j} r \omega)\, |\hat\psi(2^{-j} r \omega)|^2 > 0,\)$ where \(\hat\Psi\) is the admissibility function. The Lean definition adds two conditions that make the series meaningful: the series defining \(\hat\Psi\) converges at every \(\omega\) (its terms are nonnegative, since \(|\hat\rho| \le 1\) under the standing assumptions), and the lower bound \(\alpha\) is required of sums over \(\mathbb{Z}\) that converge unconditionally. Satisfiability (numerical, not formal). Mallat (Section 2.3, the remark after the proof of Theorem 2.6) reports that the analytic cubic-spline Battle–Lemarié wavelet is admissible in dimension one with \(\eta = 3\pi/2\), choosing for \(\rho\) a positive cubic box spline; a numerical evaluation of (30) gives \(\alpha = 0.2766 > 0\). Explicitly, with \(\psi_{BL}, \varphi_{BL}\) the cubic-spline Battle–Lemarié wavelet and scaling function: - \(\hat\psi = \sqrt2\, |\hat\psi_{BL}|\, \mathbf{1}_{\omega > 0}\) and \(\hat\varphi(\omega) = \hat\varphi_{BL}(\omega/2)\); both are real, and (9) with \(\beta = 1/2\) follows from the identities \(\sum_j |\hat\psi_{BL}(2^j\omega)|^2 = 1\) and \(|\hat\varphi_{BL}(\omega)|^2 + |\hat\psi_{BL}(\omega)|^2 = |\hat\varphi_{BL}(\omega/2)|^2\) of the Battle–Lemarié basis; - \(\rho\) the centred cubic B-spline, \(\hat\rho(\omega) = \operatorname{sinc}^4(\omega/2)\), so \(\hat\rho(0) = 1\) and \(|\hat\rho(\omega)| \le \hat\varphi_{BL}(\omega) = |\hat\varphi(2\omega)|\); - \(\eta = 3\pi/2\).

    For this choice the series defining \(\hat\Psi\) converges (\(\rho\) has finite variance), and the sums in (30) converge absolutely (\(|\hat\Psi|\) grows at most like \(\log^2|\omega|\), while \(|\hat\psi|^2\) decays like \(|\omega|^{-8}\) at infinity and vanishes to order \(8\) at \(0\)), so the two side conditions of the Lean definition hold. An independent numerical evaluation made during review also found \(\alpha > 0\). These are numerical checks: no instance of the definition is formalized.

    Let \(\psi\) be an admissible scattering wavelet and \(J \in \mathbb{Z}\). Mallat, Group invariant scattering, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 65 (2012), 1331–1398 (arXiv:1101.2286), Theorem 2.12, equations (49)–(50), in dimension \(d = 1\)). For a translation \(\tau = c\) the deformation terms vanish and \(K(\tau) = 2^{-J}|c|\). By contrast, the Fourier modulus, which is translation invariant, satisfies no such bound: a dilation by \(1 - s\) moves a frequency \(\xi\) by \(s\xi\), an error of order \(|s|\,|\xi|\,\|f\|\) (equation (3)).

    The bound is trivial when \(\tau\) or \(\tau''\) is unbounded, and the Lean statement assumes both bounded so that the suprema are finite. Mallat's constant does not depend on \(J\) (Lemmas 2.11 and 2.14 hold uniformly in \(J\)); the statement records the weaker form in which \(C\) may depend on \(J\).
