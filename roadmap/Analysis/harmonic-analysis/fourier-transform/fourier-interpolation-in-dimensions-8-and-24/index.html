    DefinitionFourier sample sequences#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.CKMRVInterpolationWanted.Analysis.CKMRVInterpolation.coeffs

    Let \(n \ge 1\) and \(k_0 \ge 0\) be integers, let \(f\) be a complex-valued Schwartz function on \(\mathbb{R}^n\) with Fourier transform \(\hat f(\xi) = \int f(x) e^{-2\pi i \langle x, \xi \rangle}\,dx\), let \(e_1\) be the first standard basis vector and let \(r_k = \sqrt{2k}\). The samples of \(f\) are the four complex sequences indexed by the integers \(k \ge k_0\) whose \(k\)-th terms are, in this order, \(f(r_k e_1)\), \(\hat f(r_k e_1)\), the derivative at \(r = r_k\) of \(r \mapsto f(r e_1)\), and the derivative at \(r = r_k\) of \(r \mapsto \hat f(r e_1)\).

    noncomputable def coeffs (n k₀ : ℕ) [NeZero n]
    (f : 𝓢(EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin n), ℂ)) :
    (Set.Ici k₀ × Fin 4) → ℂ :=
  Function.uncurry fun k ↦
    let r := Real.sqrt (2 * k)
    let fhat :=
      FourierTransform.fourierCLM ℂ (𝓢(EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin n), ℂ)) f
    ![f (realIncl r), fhat (realIncl r),
      deriv (f ∘ realIncl) r, deriv (fhat ∘ realIncl) r]

    DefinitionRadial Fourier interpolation property#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.CKMRVInterpolationWanted.Analysis.CKMRVInterpolation.CKMRV

    Let \(n \ge 1\) and \(k_0 \ge 0\) be integers. For a complex-valued Schwartz function \(f\) on \(\mathbb{R}^n\), let \(\hat f(\xi) = \int f(x) e^{-2\pi i \langle x, \xi \rangle}\,dx\) be its Fourier transform, let \(e_1\) be the first standard basis vector and let \(r_k = \sqrt{2k}\). The sampling map from index \(k_0\) sends \(f\) to the four complex sequences indexed by the integers \(k \ge k_0\) whose \(k\)-th terms are \(f(r_k e_1)\), \(\hat f(r_k e_1)\) and the derivatives at \(r = r_k\) of \(r \mapsto f(r e_1)\) and of \(r \mapsto \hat f(r e_1)\). Call \(f\) radial if \(f(x) = f(y)\) whenever \(|x| = |y|\), and call a sequence \((a_k)_{k \ge k_0}\) rapidly decreasing if \(k^m |a_k| \to 0\) as \(k \to \infty\) for every natural number \(m\). The property \(\mathrm{CKMRV}(n, k_0)\) holds when the sampling map from index \(k_0\) is a bijection from the radial Schwartz functions on \(\mathbb{R}^n\) onto the quadruples of rapidly decreasing sequences indexed by \(k \ge k_0\).

    The bijection is required to be the sampling map itself, so no interpolation basis is hidden in the definition.

    def CKMRV (n k₀ : ℕ) [NeZero n] : Prop :=
  ∃ e : RadialSchwartz n ≃ RapidSampleData k₀,
    ∀ f, (e f).1 = coeffs n k₀ f.1

    TheoremCohn–Kumar–Miller–Radchenko–Viazovska Fourier interpolation theorem#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Analysis.CKMRVInterpolationWanted.Analysis.CKMRVInterpolation.ckmrv_fourier_interpolation

    Let \((n, k_0)\) be \((8, 1)\) or \((24, 2)\). For a complex-valued Schwartz function \(f\) on \(\mathbb{R}^n\), let \(\hat f(\xi) = \int f(x) e^{-2\pi i \langle x, \xi \rangle}\,dx\) be its Fourier transform, let \(e_1\) be the first standard basis vector and let \(r_k = \sqrt{2k}\). The sampling map sends \(f\) to the four complex sequences indexed by the integers \(k \ge k_0\) whose \(k\)-th terms are \(f(r_k e_1)\), \(\hat f(r_k e_1)\) and the derivatives at \(r = r_k\) of \(r \mapsto f(r e_1)\) and of \(r \mapsto \hat f(r e_1)\). Call \(f\) radial if \(f(x) = f(y)\) whenever \(|x| = |y|\), and call a sequence \((a_k)_{k \ge k_0}\) rapidly decreasing if \(k^m |a_k| \to 0\) as \(k \to \infty\) for every natural number \(m\). Then the sampling map is a bijection from the radial Schwartz functions on \(\mathbb{R}^n\) onto the quadruples of rapidly decreasing sequences indexed by \(k \ge k_0\). So sampling starts at radius \(\sqrt 2\) in dimension \(8\) and at radius \(2\) in dimension \(24\).
