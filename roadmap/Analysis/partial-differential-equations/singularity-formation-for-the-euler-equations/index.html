    AbbreviationHölder continuity#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.FiniteTimeSingularityWanted.Holder

    Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be pseudo-extended metric spaces (distances take values in \([0, \infty]\)), let \(r \ge 0\) be a real number and \(f : X \to Y\). Then \(f\) is \(r\)-Hölder if there is a constant \(C \ge 0\) such that \(d_Y(f(x_1), f(x_2)) \le C \, d_X(x_1, x_2)^r\) for all \(x_1, x_2 \in X\).

    abbrev Holder (r : ℝ≥0) (f : X → Y) : Prop := ∃ C : ℝ≥0, HolderWith C r f

    The vorticity of a vector field \(u : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3\) is its curl \((\nabla \times u)(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{3} e_i \times Du(x)\,e_i\), where \(e_1, e_2, e_3\) is the standard basis of \(\mathbb{R}^3\), \(\times\) is the cross product and \(Du(x)\,e_i\) is the partial derivative of \(u\) at \(x\) in direction \(e_i\) (all taken to be \(0\) when \(u\) is not differentiable at \(x\)).

    StructureLocal solutions of the incompressible Euler equations#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.FiniteTimeSingularityWanted.FiniteTimeSingularity.IsLocalEulerEquationSolution

    Let \(T \in \mathbb{R}\), let \(u_0 : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3\), and let \(p : \mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) and \(u : \mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^3\). Write \(\partial_t u\) for the time derivative of \(u\), \((u \cdot \nabla_x) u\) for the spatial directional derivative of \(u\) in the direction of \(u\), and \(\nabla_x p\) for the spatial gradient of \(p\). Then \(u\) is a local solution of the incompressible Euler equations up to time \(T\), with pressure \(p\) and initial data \(u_0\), if \(u\) and \(p\) are continuously differentiable, as functions of \((x, t)\), on \(\mathbb{R}^3 \times [0, T)\), if for all \(x \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and \(t \in (0, T)\)

    \[\partial_t u(x, t) + (u \cdot \nabla_x) u(x, t) + \nabla_x p(x, t) = 0,\]

    if for every \(t \in (0, T)\) the divergence of \(u(\cdot, t)\) is \(0\) at every point of \(\mathbb{R}^3\), and if \(u(\cdot, 0) = u_0\).

    structure IsLocalEulerEquationSolution
    (p : ℝ³ → ℝ → ℝ) (u₀ : ℝ³ → ℝ³) (T : ℝ) (u : ℝ³ → ℝ → ℝ³) where
  solContDiff : ContDiffOn ℝ 1 u.uncurry (univ ×ˢ Ico 0 T)
  pressureContDiff : ContDiffOn ℝ 1 p.uncurry (univ ×ˢ Ico 0 T)
  eq : ∀ x, ∀ t ∈ Ioo 0 T, (∂ₜ u) x t + (u ·∇ₓ u) x t + (∇ₓ p) x t = 0
  div_free : ∀ t ∈ Ioo 0 T, div (u · t) = 0
  init : (u · 0) = u₀

    MathlibExt.Analysis.FiniteTimeSingularityWanted.FiniteTimeSingularity.IsOdd

    A vector field \(u : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3\) with components \(u_1, u_2, u_3\) is odd if, for each \(i \in \{1, 2, 3\}\), the component \(u_i\) is an odd function of the \(i\)-th coordinate and an even function of each of the other two coordinates, when the remaining coordinates are held fixed.

    def IsOdd (u : ℝ³ → ℝ³) : Prop :=
  (∀ x₁ x₂, (u !₂[·, x₁, x₂] 0).Odd ∧ (u !₂[·, x₁, x₂] 1).Even ∧ (u !₂[·, x₁, x₂] 2).Even) ∧

    TheoremElgindi's theorem: finite-time singularity of \(C^{1,\alpha}\) Euler flows#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Analysis.FiniteTimeSingularityWanted.FiniteTimeSingularity.theorem_1

    There exist a real number \(\alpha > 0\) and a vector field \(u_0 : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3\) that is odd, has divergence \(0\) at every point and lies in \(C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^3)\), together with a constant \(C > 0\) such that its vorticity satisfies \(|(\nabla \times u_0)(x)| \le C / (|x|^\alpha + 1)\) for every \(x \in \mathbb{R}^3\), and there exist functions \(p : \mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) and \(u : \mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^3\) such that \(u(\cdot, t)\) is odd for every \(t \in [0, 1)\), \(u\) is a local solution of the incompressible Euler equations up to time \(1\) with pressure \(p\) and initial data \(u_0\), \(u\) lies in \(C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^3 \times [0, T])\) as a function of \((x, t)\) for every \(T \in (0, 1)\), and

    \[\int_0^t \sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}^3} |(\nabla \times u(\cdot, s))(x)| \, ds \to +\infty\]

    as \(t \to 1^-\). Here \(|\cdot|\) is the Euclidean norm, differentiability is over \(\mathbb{R}\), and \(\mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R}\) carries the norm \(\max(|x|, |t|)\).

    Elgindi states the conclusion for the unique local odd solution in this class. Uniqueness of solutions up to any time \(T\) at which they exist was already known, so only existence is asserted here.
