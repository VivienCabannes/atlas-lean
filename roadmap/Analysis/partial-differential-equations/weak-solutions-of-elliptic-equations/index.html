    Weak solutions of elliptic equations

    The \(L^2\) theory of second-order elliptic equations in divergence form, \(Lu = -\sum_{i,j} (a^{ij} u_{x_i})_{x_j} + \sum_i b^i u_{x_i} + c u = f\) on a bounded open \(U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n\). A weak solution is a function \(u\) in the Sobolev space \(H^1(U)\) with \(B[u, v] = \int_U \big(\sum a^{ij} u_{x_i} v_{x_j} + \sum b^i u_{x_i} v + c u v\big) = \int_U f v\) for all test functions \(v\); the Dirichlet condition \(u = 0\) on \(\partial U\) is \(u \in H^1_0(U)\). Existence comes from the Lax–Milgram theorem: for \(-\Delta\), Poincaré's inequality makes \(B[u, v] = \int_U Du \cdot Dv\) coercive on \(H^1_0(U)\), so every \(f \in L^2(U)\) has a unique weak solution, which is also the minimizer of the Dirichlet energy \(\int_U \tfrac12 |Du|^2 - f u\). Regularity then upgrades weak solutions: with \(C^1\) principal coefficients, a weak solution lies in \(H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)\) with an interior estimate, and so solves \(Lu = f\) almost everywhere.

    Here the abstract Hilbert-space form of the existence theorem (a gradient-type operator with a Poincaré bound), the energy estimate and Dirichlet's principle are proved from Mathlib's Lax–Milgram theorem. Sobolev spaces on domains, weak derivatives and weak solutions are defined concretely, and existence in \(H^1_0(U)\) for Poisson's equation and interior \(H^2\) regularity are recorded as wanted.

    TheoremExistence and uniqueness of weak solutions of the abstract Poisson problem#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.WeakSolutions.existsUnique_weakSolution

    Let \(H\) be a real Hilbert space, \(G\) a real inner product space and \(D : H \to G\) a bounded linear map with \(\beta \|u\|^2 \le \|D u\|^2\) for some \(\beta > 0\). Then for every bounded linear functional \(L\) on \(H\) there is exactly one \(u \in H\) with

    \[\langle D u, D v \rangle = L(v) \quad \text{for all } v \in H.\]

    This is the abstract form of the application of the Lax–Milgram theorem to Poisson's equation: for \(H = H^1_0(U)\), \(D\) the gradient and \(L(v) = \int_U f v\), the solution is the weak solution of \(-\Delta u = f\), \(u = 0\) on \(\partial U\). The concrete statement on domains is recorded as wanted in this topic.

    ∃! u : H, ∀ v, ⟪D u, D v⟫ = L v

    DefinitionDirichlet energy of a bilinear form and a load#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.WeakSolutions.energy

    For a bounded bilinear form \(B\) and a bounded linear functional \(L\) on \(H\), the quadratic functional

    \[I[w] = \tfrac12 B[w, w] - L(w).\]

    For \(B[u, v] = \int_U Du \cdot Dv\) and \(L(v) = \int_U f v\) it is \(I[w] = \int_U \tfrac12 |Dw|^2 - f w\), whose Euler–Lagrange equation is Poisson's equation \(-\Delta u = f\).

    noncomputable def energy (B : H →L[ℝ] H →L[ℝ] ℝ) (L : H →L[ℝ] ℝ) (w : H) : ℝ :=
  B w w / 2 - L w

    TheoremDirichlet's principle#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.WeakSolutions.isMinOn_energy_iff

    Let \(B\) be a symmetric bounded bilinear form on \(H\) with \(B[v, v] \ge 0\) for all \(v\), and let \(L\) be a bounded linear functional. Then \(u\) minimizes \(I[w] = \tfrac12 B[w, w] - L(w)\) over \(H\) if and only if

    \[B[u, v] = L(v) \quad \text{for all } v \in H.\]

    Minimizers of the energy are exactly the weak solutions. Evans's notes prove the classical direction for \(C^2\) minimizers of \(\int_U L(x, u, \nabla u)\) (a minimizer solves the Euler–Lagrange equation); this abstract version is two-sided, because the energy is quadratic: \(I[u + h] = I[u] + (B[u, h] - L(h)) + \tfrac12 B[h, h]\). No coercivity is needed; with coercivity the minimizer exists and is unique.

    theorem isMinOn_energy_iff {B : H →L[ℝ] H →L[ℝ] ℝ} (hsymm : ∀ u v, B u v = B v u)
    (hpos : ∀ v, 0 ≤ B v v) (L : H →L[ℝ] ℝ) (u : H) :
    (∀ w, energy B L u ≤ energy B L w) ↔ ∀ v, B u v = L v
    noncomputable def energy (B : H →L[ℝ] H →L[ℝ] ℝ) (L : H →L[ℝ] ℝ) (w : H) : ℝ :=
  B w w / 2 - L w

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.EllipticRegularityWanted.IsTestFunctionOn

    For \(U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n\), a test function on \(U\) is a function \(\varphi : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}\) of class \(C^\infty\) with compact support contained in \(U\); for open \(U\) these are the functions of \(C^\infty_c(U)\) (written \(C^\infty_0(\Omega)\) by Schmidt).     DefinitionWeak partial derivative on an open set#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.EllipticRegularityWanted.IsWeakPartialDerivOn

    For \(i \in \{0, \dots, n - 1\}\), a function \(g\) is the weak partial derivative \(\partial_i u\) of \(u\) on \(U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n\) when

    \[\int_U u \, \partial_i \varphi \, dx = - \int_U g \, \varphi \, dx\]

    for every test function \(\varphi \in C^\infty_c(U)\). Hunter's Definition 3.1 also requires \(u, g \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)\); here that requirement is carried by the Sobolev notions built on this one, which ask for \(u, g \in L^2(U)\). The integrals are Lebesgue integrals, taken to be \(0\) when the integrand is not integrable; with \(u, g \in L^2(U)\) both integrands are integrable, because \(\varphi\) and \(\partial_i \varphi\) are bounded with compact support.

    def IsWeakPartialDerivOn {n : ℕ} (U : Set (E n)) (i : Fin n) (u g : E n → ℝ) : Prop :=
  ∀ φ : E n → ℝ, IsTestFunctionOn U φ →
    ∫ x in U, u x * partialDeriv i φ x = -∫ x in U, g x * φ x
    abbrev E (n : ℕ) := EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin n)

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.EllipticRegularityWanted.IsWeakSolutionOn

    Given coefficients \(a^{ij}, b^i, c\) and a right-hand side \(f\) on \(U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n\), a function \(u \in H^1(U)\) with weak gradient \(Du\) is a weak solution of

    \[Lu = -\sum_{i,j} (a^{ij} u_{x_i})_{x_j} + \sum_i b^i u_{x_i} + c u = f \quad \text{in } U\]

    \[\|u\|_{L^2(V)} + \sum_i \|\partial_i u\|_{L^2(V)} + \sum_{i,j} \|\partial_j \partial_i u\|_{L^2(V)} \le C \big( \|f\|_{L^2(U)} + \|u\|_{L^2(U)} \big).\]

The left side is a norm equivalent to the \(H^2(V)\) norm; since \(C\) is existential, the statement is equivalent to the source's estimate for \(\|u\|_{H^2(V)}\).
