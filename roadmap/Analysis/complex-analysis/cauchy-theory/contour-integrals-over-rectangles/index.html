    RectilinearRectangle

    A rectilinear rectangle is an axis-parallel rectangle in the complex plane, recorded by an ordered pair of complex numbers \(z\) and \(w\), its first and second corner, which are opposite corners of the rectangle.

    Degenerate rectangles, in which \(z\) and \(w\) have the same real part or the same imaginary part, are allowed. The corners and their order determine the orientation of the boundary integral over the rectangle, and the boundary integral over a degenerate rectangle is zero.

    DefinitionBoundary integral over a rectangle#✓fully proved

    RectilinearRectangle.boundaryIntegral

    Let \(E\) be a complex Banach space and let \(R\) be a rectilinear rectangle with first corner \(z\) and second corner \(w\). The boundary integral of a function \(f : \mathbb{C} \to E\) over \(R\) is

    \[\int_{\operatorname{Re} z}^{\operatorname{Re} w} f(x + i \operatorname{Im} z)\,dx - \int_{\operatorname{Re} z}^{\operatorname{Re} w} f(x + i \operatorname{Im} w)\,dx + i \int_{\operatorname{Im} z}^{\operatorname{Im} w} f(\operatorname{Re} w + iy)\,dy - i \int_{\operatorname{Im} z}^{\operatorname{Im} w} f(\operatorname{Re} z + iy)\,dy,\]

    where each integral is an oriented Bochner interval integral, so \(\int_a^b = -\int_b^a\).

    The corner order determines the sign; when \(\operatorname{Re} z \le \operatorname{Re} w\) and \(\operatorname{Im} z \le \operatorname{Im} w\) this is the integral of \(f\) over the positively oriented boundary of the rectangle.

    noncomputable def boundaryIntegral {E : Type*} [NormedAddCommGroup E]
    [NormedSpace ℂ E] [CompleteSpace E] (r : RectilinearRectangle) (f : ℂ → E) : E :=
  (∫ x : ℝ in r.first.re..r.second.re, f (x + r.first.im * Complex.I)) -
    (∫ x : ℝ in r.first.re..r.second.re, f (x + r.second.im * Complex.I)) +
      Complex.I • (∫ y : ℝ in r.first.im..r.second.im, f (r.second.re + y * Complex.I)) -
        Complex.I • (∫ y : ℝ in r.first.im..r.second.im, f (r.first.re + y * Complex.I))

    TheoremGluing rectangles along a horizontal side#✓fully proved

    RectilinearRectangle.boundaryIntegral_ofBounds_horizontal_concat

    Let \(E\) be a complex Banach space, let \(f : \mathbb{C} \to E\) and let \(x_0, x_1, y_0, y_1, y_2\) be real numbers such that the maps \(y \mapsto f(x_0 + iy)\) and \(y \mapsto f(x_1 + iy)\) are continuous on \(\mathbb{R}\). For real \(a, b, c, d\) write \(R(a, b, c, d)\) for the rectangle with corners \(a + ic\) and \(b + id\). Then the boundary integral of \(f\) over \(R(x_0, x_1, y_0, y_2)\) equals the sum of the boundary integrals of \(f\) over \(R(x_0, x_1, y_0, y_1)\) and over \(R(x_0, x_1, y_1, y_2)\).

    No ordering of \(y_0, y_1, y_2\) is assumed: the common horizontal side cancels and the vertical integrals concatenate as oriented integrals.

    theorem boundaryIntegral_ofBounds_horizontal_concat {E : Type*}
    [NormedAddCommGroup E] [NormedSpace ℂ E] [CompleteSpace E]
    (f : ℂ → E) (x₀ x₁ y₀ y₁ y₂ : ℝ)
    (h₀ : Continuous fun y : ℝ ↦ f ((x₀ : ℂ) + (y : ℂ) * Complex.I))
    (h₁ : Continuous fun y : ℝ ↦ f ((x₁ : ℂ) + (y : ℂ) * Complex.I)) :
    (ofBounds x₀ x₁ y₀ y₂).boundaryIntegral f =
      (ofBounds x₀ x₁ y₀ y₁).boundaryIntegral f +
        (ofBounds x₀ x₁ y₁ y₂).boundaryIntegral f

    RectilinearCycle.integral

    Let \(E\) be a complex Banach space and \(f : \mathbb{C} \to E\).
