    Mirror descent

    Mirror descent (Nemirovski and Yudin, 1983) is the subgradient method with the Euclidean geometry replaced by a proximal setup: a norm \(\|\cdot\|\) on the space, with dual norm \(\|\cdot\|_*\) for the subgradients, and a distance-generating function \(\omega\) that is strongly convex with respect to that norm. A step moves from \(x_t\) to the prox point \(x_{t+1} = \operatorname{argmin}_{u \in X}\{\gamma_t \langle g_t, u\rangle + V_{x_t}(u)\}\) of the Bregman divergence \(V\). One inequality drives everything: the one-step bound, obtained from the optimality condition of the prox step by the three-point identity, telescopes to a regret bound for arbitrary linear forms \(g_t\). With subgradients of a convex Lipschitz function it gives the rates for the best and the averaged iterate and, with a constant step, the efficiency estimate \(\sqrt{2\Omega}L/\sqrt{N}\) (constant-step rate), which, on \(\ell_1\) and \(\ell_2\) balls with the matching setups, no black-box first-order method can improve by more than a logarithmic factor while the number of steps stays below the dimension. With noisy subgradients it gives the pathwise bound of robust stochastic approximation (Nemirovski, Juditsky, Lan and Shapiro, 2009) and, in expectation, its efficiency estimate.

    The Euclidean setup \(\omega = \tfrac12\|\cdot\|_2^2\) recovers the projected subgradient method. On the simplex, the \(\ell_1\) norm with the entropy gives the Kullback–Leibler divergence, strong convexity by Pinsker's inequality, an \(\omega\)-diameter \(\log n\), and the prox step is the multiplicative-weights update, so that the entropic rate depends on the dimension only through \(\sqrt{\log n}\). Against the vertices of the simplex the telescoped bound gives a regret bound for prediction with expert advice, \(\log n/\gamma + \gamma N/2\) for losses in \([-1, 1]\).

    Results here assume strong convexity in the Bregman form \(V_x(u) \ge \tfrac{\alpha}{2}\|u - x\|^2\) and use each prox step only through its first-order optimality condition; existence of the prox point is not formalized.

    The proximal setup

    DefinitionBregman divergence (prox-function) of a distance-generating function#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Convex.MirrorDescent.bregman

    For \(\omega \colon E \to \mathbb{R}\) on a real normed space and a selection \(\omega' \colon E \to E^*\) of its derivative, the Bregman divergence (Nemirovski's prox-function) is

    \[ V_x(u) = \omega(u) - \omega(x) - \omega'(x)(u - x). \]

    It is the gap at \(u\) between \(\omega\) and its linearization at \(x\); for convex \(\omega\) it is nonnegative, and for \(\omega(x) = \tfrac12 \|x\|_2^2\) on a Euclidean space it is \(\tfrac12 \|u - x\|_2^2\). A proximal setup consists of a norm and a function \(\omega\) that is strongly convex with respect to that norm; mirror descent replaces the squared Euclidean distance of the projected subgradient method by \(V\), which adapts the method to the geometry of the feasible set.

    noncomputable def bregman (ω : E → ℝ) (ω' : E → E →L[ℝ] ℝ) (x u : E) : ℝ :=
  ω u - ω x - ω' x (u - x)

    TheoremThree-point identity for Bregman divergences#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Convex.MirrorDescent.bregman_three_point

    Throughout, \(E\) is a real normed space with norm \(\|\cdot\|\), linear forms \(\xi \in E^*\) (continuous linear maps \(E \to \mathbb{R}\)) carry the dual norm \(\|\xi\|_* = \sup_{\|x\| \le 1} \xi(x)\), \(\omega \colon E \to \mathbb{R}\) is a distance-generating function with a derivative selection \(\omega' \colon E \to E^*\), and \(V_x(u)\) is its Bregman divergence. For all \(x, y, u \in E\),

    \[ V_x(u) - V_y(u) - V_x(y) = \big(\omega'(y) - \omega'(x)\big)(u - y). \]

    Both sides expand to \(\omega'(y)(u - y) - \omega'(x)(u - y)\). This rearrangement turns the first-order optimality condition of a prox step into a telescoping inequality, and it is the only algebra behind the mirror descent and mirror-prox bounds.

    theorem bregman_three_point (ω : E → ℝ) (ω' : E → E →L[ℝ] ℝ) (x y u : E) :
    bregman ω ω' x u - bregman ω ω' y u - bregman ω ω' x y = (ω' y - ω' x) (u - y)
    noncomputable def bregman (ω : E → ℝ) (ω' : E → E →L[ℝ] ℝ) (x u : E) : ℝ :=
  ω u - ω x - ω' x (u - x)

    TheoremOne-step inequality of mirror descent#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Convex.MirrorDescent.bregman_prox_step_le

    Throughout, \(E\) is a real normed space with norm \(\|\cdot\|\), linear forms \(\xi \in E^*\) (continuous linear maps \(E \to \mathbb{R}\)) carry the dual norm \(\|\xi\|_* = \sup_{\|x\| \le 1} \xi(x)\), \(\omega \colon E \to \mathbb{R}\) is a distance-generating function with a derivative selection \(\omega' \colon E \to E^*\), and \(V_x(u)\) is its Bregman divergence. Let \(\alpha > 0\), \(\xi \in E^*\), and let \(x^+\) satisfy, at a point \(u\), the first-order optimality condition

    \[ \big(\xi - \omega'(x) + \omega'(x^+)\big)(u - x^+) \ge 0 \]

    of the prox step \(x^+ = \operatorname{argmin}_{v \in X} \{\xi(v) + V_x(v)\}\), together with \(V_x(x^+) \ge \tfrac{\alpha}{2} \|x^+ - x\|^2\). Then

    \[ V_{x^+}(u) \le V_x(u) + \xi(u - x) + \frac{\|\xi\|_*^2}{2\alpha}. \]

    The proof combines the optimality condition with the three-point identity and bounds \(\xi(x - x^+) - V_x(x^+) \le \|\xi\|_* s - \tfrac{\alpha}{2} s^2 \le \|\xi\|_*^2 / (2\alpha)\) with \(s = \|x^+ - x\|\). With \(\xi = \gamma f'(x)\) it is the step inequality from which every mirror descent rate follows.

    theorem bregman_prox_step_le (ω : E → ℝ) (ω' : E → E →L[ℝ] ℝ) {α : ℝ} (hα : 0 < α)
    {x xplus u : E} {ξ : E →L[ℝ] ℝ}
    (hopt : 0 ≤ (ξ - ω' x + ω' xplus) (u - xplus))
    (hsc : α / 2 * ‖xplus - x‖ ^ 2 ≤ bregman ω ω' x xplus) :
    bregman ω ω' xplus u ≤ bregman ω ω' x u + ξ (u - x) + ‖ξ‖ ^ 2 / (2 * α)
    noncomputable def bregman (ω : E → ℝ) (ω' : E → E →L[ℝ] ℝ) (x u : E) : ℝ :=
  ω u - ω x - ω' x (u - x)

    TheoremTelescoped mirror descent bound#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Convex.MirrorDescent.mirror_descent_sum_le

    Throughout, \(E\) is a real normed space with norm \(\|\cdot\|\), linear forms \(\xi \in E^*\) (continuous linear maps \(E \to \mathbb{R}\)) carry the dual norm \(\|\xi\|_* = \sup_{\|x\| \le 1} \xi(x)\), \(\omega \colon E \to \mathbb{R}\) is a distance-generating function with a derivative selection \(\omega' \colon E \to E^*\), and \(V_x(u)\) is its Bregman divergence. Let \(\alpha > 0\) and \(X^o \subseteq X \subseteq E\), and assume \(V_x(u) \ge \tfrac{\alpha}{2}\|u - x\|^2\) for \(x \in X^o\), \(u \in X\). Let \(x_0, x_1, \ldots \in X^o\), steps \(\gamma_t \in \mathbb{R}\) and arbitrary \(g_t \in E^*\) be such that each \(x_{t+1}\) satisfies the optimality condition of the prox step \(x_{t+1} = \operatorname{argmin}_{u \in X}\{\gamma_t g_t(u) + V_{x_t}(u)\}\):

    \[ \big(\gamma_t g_t - \omega'(x_t) + \omega'(x_{t+1})\big)(u - x_{t+1}) \ge 0 \qquad \text{for all } u \in X. \]

    Then for every \(u \in X\) and every \(N\),

    \[ \sum_{t < N} \gamma_t\, g_t(x_t - u) \le V_{x_0}(u) + \frac{1}{2\alpha} \sum_{t < N} \gamma_t^2 \|g_t\|_*^2 . \]

    The vectors \(g_t\) are arbitrary, so the bound is a regret bound for online linear optimization; with \(g_t\) subgradients of one convex function it yields the rates of mirror descent, and with noisy subgradients the bounds of stochastic mirror descent. The source states strong convexity of \(\omega\) in the monotone form \((\omega'(x) - \omega'(x'))(x - x') \ge \|x - x'\|^2\); its proof uses it in the Bregman form assumed here.

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Convex.MirrorDescent.mirror_descent_exists_le_sqrt

    In the setting of the best-iterate rate with \(L > 0\), let \(\Omega > 0\) bound \(V_{x_0}(x^\star)\) (for instance the \(\omega\)-diameter \(\max_{u \in X} V_{x_0}(u)\) when \(x_0\) minimizes \(\omega\) on \(X\)), and run \(N \ge 1\) steps with the constant step \(\gamma = \sqrt{2\alpha\Omega} / (L\sqrt{N})\). Then some iterate \(t < N\) satisfies

    \[ f(x_t) - f(x^\star) \le \frac{L\sqrt{2\Omega}}{\sqrt{\alpha N}} . \]

    With \(\alpha = 1\) this is the efficiency estimate \(\sqrt{2\Omega}\, L / \sqrt{N}\) of mirror descent. The dimension enters only through \(\Omega\) and the norm in which \(L\) is measured: the Euclidean setup gives the subgradient method's rate, while on the simplex the entropy setup has \(\Omega \le \log n\) and measures \(L\) in \(\|\cdot\|_\infty\). This rate is nearly optimal in the large-scale regime: for convex functions \(L\)-Lipschitz with respect to \(\|\cdot\|_p\), \(p \in \{1, 2\}\), on the radius-\(R\) ball of \(\|\cdot\|_p\) in \(\mathbb{R}^n\), every black-box first-order method needs at least of order \(\min[n, L^2R^2/\epsilon^2]\) steps to reach accuracy \(\epsilon\) on the whole class (Nemirovski and Yudin, 1983), while mirror descent with the Euclidean or the \(\ell_1\) setup needs \(O((\ln n)^{2/p - 1} L^2R^2/\epsilon^2)\).

Run

    \[ x_{t+1} = P\big(x_t, \gamma_t G(x_t, \xi_t)\big) \]

    from a deterministic \(x_0 \in X^o\) with steps \(\gamma_t > 0\). Then for every \(x^\star \in X\) with \(V_{x_0}(x^\star) \le D^2\) and every \(N \ge 1\), the weighted average \(\tilde x_N = \big(\sum_{t<N} \gamma_t\big)^{-1}\sum_{t<N} \gamma_t x_t\) satisfies

    TheoremThe prox step of the entropy is the multiplicative-weights update#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.Convex.MirrorDescentEntropy.entropy_prox_multiplicative

    Let \(x, x^+\) lie in the relative interior of the simplex with

    \[ x^+_i = \frac{x_i\, e^{-\gamma c_i}}{Z} \]

    for some \(Z > 0\) (necessarily \(Z = \sum_i x_i e^{-\gamma c_i}\)). Then \(x^+\) satisfies the optimality condition of the entropic prox step \(x^+ = \operatorname{argmin}_{u \in \Delta_n}\{\gamma \langle c, u\rangle + V_x(u)\}\) with equality:

    \[ \big(\gamma c - \omega'(x) + \omega'(x^+)\big)(u - x^+) = 0 \qquad \text{whenever } \textstyle\sum_i u_i = 1 . \]

    Indeed \(\log x^+_i = \log x_i - \gamma c_i - \log Z\), so the linear form is the constant \(-\log Z\) times \(\sum_i (u_i - x^+_i) = 0\). Mirror descent with the entropy on the simplex therefore runs the multiplicative-weights (Hedge, exponentiated gradient) update.

    \[ \sum_{t<N} \big(\langle c_t, x_t\rangle - c_{t,i}\big) \le \frac{\log n}{\gamma} + \frac{\gamma N}{2} , \]
