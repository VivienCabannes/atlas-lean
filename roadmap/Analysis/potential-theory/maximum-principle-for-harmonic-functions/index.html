    Maximum principle for harmonic functions

    The maximum principle is the first structural property of harmonic functions in the classical theory of Laplace's equation, and the reason the Dirichlet problem is well posed. On a bounded open set \(U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n\), a function \(u \in C^2(U) \cap C(\overline U)\) with \(\Delta u \ge 0\) attains its maximum over \(\overline U\) on \(\partial U\) (weak maximum principle); for harmonic \(u\) the same holds for the minimum, so two solutions of \(-\Delta u = f\) with the same boundary values coincide. On a connected \(U\), an interior maximum forces a harmonic function to be constant (strong maximum principle), a consequence of the mean-value property: a \(C^2\) function is harmonic exactly when its average over every ball \(\overline{B}(x, r) \subseteq U\) equals \(u(x)\).

    Here the weak maximum and minimum principles and uniqueness for the Dirichlet problem are proved, in any finite-dimensional real inner product space and for Mathlib's harmonic functions (InnerProductSpace.HarmonicOnNhd), by the perturbation argument: \(u + \varepsilon |x|^2\) has positive Laplacian, while the Laplacian of a \(C^2\) function is nonpositive at an interior maximum. In the plane the strong maximum principle is proved too, by writing a harmonic function locally as the real part of a holomorphic one and applying Mathlib's maximum modulus principle. The mean-value property in dimension \(n\), Harnack's inequality and the strong maximum principle in every dimension are recorded as wanted; Mathlib has the planar mean-value property through circle averages.

    TheoremSecond derivative along a line#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.LaplaceMaximumPrinciple.iteratedFDeriv_two_apply_eq_deriv_deriv

    Let \(E\) be a real inner product space and let \(f : E \to \mathbb{R}\) be \(C^2\) at \(z\). For every direction \(e \in E\), the second derivative of \(f\) at \(z\) evaluated at \((e, e)\) is the second derivative at \(0\) of the restriction of \(f\) to the line through \(z\) in the direction \(e\):

    \[D^2 f(z)(e, e) = \varphi''(0), \qquad \varphi(t) = f(z + t e).\]

    This is the chain-rule computation in the proof of the second-order necessary condition in Evans's optimization notes, stated for Mathlib's iterated derivative iteratedFDeriv.

    theorem iteratedFDeriv_two_apply_eq_deriv_deriv {f : E → ℝ} {z : E} (hf : ContDiffAt ℝ 2 f z)
    (e : E) :
    iteratedFDeriv ℝ 2 f z ![e, e] = deriv (deriv fun t : ℝ => f (z + t • e)) 0

    TheoremNonpositive second derivatives at a local maximum#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.LaplaceMaximumPrinciple.iteratedFDeriv_two_nonpos_of_isLocalMax

    Let \(E\) be a real inner product space and let \(f : E \to \mathbb{R}\) be \(C^2\) at a local maximum \(z\). Then \(D^2 f(z)(e, e) \le 0\) for every direction \(e\): the second derivative of \(f\) at \(z\) is negative semidefinite.

    Evans states the second-order necessary condition for minimizers, \(\nabla^2 f(x_0) \succeq 0\), and notes that it holds at local minima; this is the same statement for local maxima (apply it to \(-f\)). The proof restricts \(f\) to lines and uses the one-dimensional second-derivative test.

    theorem iteratedFDeriv_two_nonpos_of_isLocalMax {f : E → ℝ} {z : E} (hf : ContDiffAt ℝ 2 f z)
    (hmax : IsLocalMax f z) (e : E) : iteratedFDeriv ℝ 2 f z ![e, e] ≤ 0

    TheoremThe Laplacian is nonpositive at an interior maximum#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.LaplaceMaximumPrinciple.laplacian_nonpos_of_isLocalMax

    Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real inner product space and let \(f : E \to \mathbb{R}\) be \(C^2\) at a local maximum \(z\). Then \(\Delta f(z) \le 0\), where \(\Delta\) is Mathlib's Laplacian (InnerProductSpace.laplacian), the trace of the second derivative.

    This is the pointwise fact behind every classical maximum principle: at an interior maximum the second derivative is negative semidefinite, so its trace is nonpositive.

    theorem laplacian_nonpos_of_isLocalMax {f : E → ℝ} {z : E} (hf : ContDiffAt ℝ 2 f z)
    (hmax : IsLocalMax f z) : Δ f z ≤ 0

    TheoremLaplacian of the squared norm#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.LaplaceMaximumPrinciple.laplacian_norm_sq

    Let \(E\) be a real inner product space of finite dimension \(n\). Then \(\Delta \|x\|^2 = 2n\) at every point. In \(\mathbb{R}^n\) this is \(\Delta |x|^2 = \sum_i \partial_i^2 (x_1^2 + \dots + x_n^2) = 2n\).

    The function \(\varepsilon \|x\|^2\) is the perturbation that makes a subharmonic function strictly subharmonic in the proof of the weak maximum principle.

    theorem laplacian_norm_sq (z : E) :
    Δ (fun x : E => ‖x‖ ^ 2) z = 2 * Module.finrank ℝ E

    TheoremWeak maximum principle for subharmonic functions#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.LaplaceMaximumPrinciple.exists_mem_frontier_isMaxOn_of_laplacian_nonneg

    Let \(U\) be a nonempty bounded open subset of a nontrivial finite-dimensional real inner product space, for instance of \(\mathbb{R}^n\) with \(n \ge 1\). Let \(u\) be continuous on \(\overline U\) and \(C^2\) on \(U\) with \(\Delta u \ge 0\) on \(U\). Then \(u\) attains its maximum over \(\overline U\) at a point of \(\partial U\):

    \[\max_{\overline U} u = \max_{\partial U} u.\]

    Proof idea. For \(\varepsilon > 0\) the function \(v = u + \varepsilon \|x\|^2\) satisfies \(\Delta v = \Delta u + 2 n \varepsilon > 0\) on \(U\), so it has no maximum in \(U\); if \(u\) exceeded its boundary maximum somewhere, a small \(\varepsilon\) would produce one.

    This is Theorem 3.13 of Schmidt's notes for the operator \(L = \Delta\) (stated there for general elliptic \(L u \ge 0\) with coefficients continuous up to the boundary), and contains the weak maximum principle for harmonic functions. Nontriviality of the space excludes dimension \(0\), where the one-point space is a bounded open set with empty boundary.

    theorem exists_mem_frontier_isMaxOn_of_laplacian_nonneg [Nontrivial E] {U : Set E} {u : E → ℝ}
    (hU : IsOpen U) (hUb : Bornology.IsBounded U) (hne : U.Nonempty)
    (hc : ContinuousOn u (closure U)) (hd : ∀ x ∈ U, ContDiffAt ℝ 2 u x)
    (hΔ : ∀ x ∈ U, 0 ≤ Δ u x) :
    ∃ y ∈ frontier U, IsMaxOn u (closure U) y

    TheoremUniqueness for the Dirichlet problem for Poisson's equation#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.LaplaceMaximumPrinciple.eqOn_closure_of_neg_laplacian_eq

    Let \(U\) be a bounded open subset of a nontrivial finite-dimensional real inner product space and let \(f : U \to \mathbb{R}\) be any function. If \(u_1, u_2 \in C^2(U) \cap C(\overline U)\) both solve

    \[-\Delta u = f \text{ in } U, \qquad u = g \text{ on } \partial U\]

    for the same boundary values \(g\), then \(u_1 = u_2\) on \(\overline U\). No regularity of \(f\) or \(g\) is needed. In dimension \(0\) uniqueness fails, which is why the space is assumed nontrivial.

    theorem eqOn_closure_of_neg_laplacian_eq [Nontrivial E] {U : Set E} {u₁ u₂ f : E → ℝ}
    (hU : IsOpen U) (hUb : Bornology.IsBounded U)
    (hc₁ : ContinuousOn u₁ (closure U)) (hc₂ : ContinuousOn u₂ (closure U))
    (hd₁ : ∀ x ∈ U, ContDiffAt ℝ 2 u₁ x) (hd₂ : ∀ x ∈ U, ContDiffAt ℝ 2 u₂ x)
    (h₁ : ∀ x ∈ U, -Δ u₁ x = f x) (h₂ : ∀ x ∈ U, -Δ u₂ x = f x)
    (hb : EqOn u₁ u₂ (frontier U)) : EqOn u₁ u₂ (closure U)

    TheoremA planar harmonic function is constant near an interior maximum#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Analysis.PDE.PlanarHarmonicMaximumPrinciple.exists_ball_eqOn_of_isMaxOn_complex

    Let \(U \subseteq \mathbb{C}\) be open and let \(u : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{R}\) be harmonic on \(U\). If \(u\) attains its maximum over \(U\) at \(z \in U\), then \(u\) is constant on some disc around \(z\) contained in \(U\).

    Proof idea. On a disc \(B \subseteq U\) around \(z\), Mathlib writes \(u = \operatorname{Re} F\) with \(F\) holomorphic. Then \(|e^{F}| = e^{u}\) attains its maximum over \(B\) at the centre, so \(e^{F}\) is constant by the maximum modulus principle, and so is \(u = \log |e^F|\). Schmidt proves the same local step in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) from the mean-value property.

    theorem exists_ball_eqOn_of_isMaxOn_complex {U : Set ℂ} (hU : IsOpen U) {u : ℂ → ℝ}
    (hu : HarmonicOnNhd u U) {z : ℂ} (hz : z ∈ U) (hmax : IsMaxOn u U z) :
    ∃ r > 0, ball z r ⊆ U ∧ EqOn u (fun _ => u z) (ball z r)
