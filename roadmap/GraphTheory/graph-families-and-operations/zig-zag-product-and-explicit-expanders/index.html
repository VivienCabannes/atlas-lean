    Zig-zag product and explicit expanders

    Regular multigraphs given by rotation maps, and the graph operations that build constant-degree expanders from a single small one: squaring, which squares the spectral parameter and the degree, and the zig-zag product of Reingold, Vadhan and Wigderson, whose result inherits its size from a big graph, its degree from a small graph and its expansion from both.

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.RotationMap

    A rotation map on the vertex set V with edge labels L is an involution rot of V × L: rot (v, i) = (w, j) means that the edge with label i at v leads to w and carries label j at w. It describes an |L|-regular undirected multigraph (loops and parallel edges allowed) (O. Reingold, S. Vadhan and A. Wigderson, Ann. of Math. 155 (2002), Definition 2.1).

    DefinitionSquare of a rotation map#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.RotationMap.square

    The square G² has an edge labelled (i, j) from v to u for every two-step walk v → w → u that leaves v along label i and w along label j: Rot_{G²}(v, (i, j)) = (u, (j', i')) where (w, i') = Rot_G(v, i) and (u, j') = Rot_G(w, j) (O. Reingold, S. Vadhan and A. Wigderson, Ann. of Math. 155 (2002), Section 2.3, the t-th power with t = 2).

    def square (G : RotationMap V L) : RotationMap V (L × L) where
  rot p :=
    let q := G.rot (p.1, p.2.1)
    let r := G.rot (q.1, p.2.2)
    (r.1, (r.2, q.2))
  rot_rot p := by
    obtain ⟨v, i, j⟩ := p
    simp [G.rot_rot]

    DefinitionZig-zag product of rotation maps#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.RotationMap.zigzag

    For a rotation map G on V with labels K and a rotation map H on K with labels L, the zig-zag product G ⓩ H is the rotation map on V × K with labels L × L given by Rot((v, k), (i, j)) = ((w, l), (j', i')), where (k', i') = Rot_H(k, i), (w, l') = Rot_G(v, k') and (l, j') = Rot_H(l', j): a step in the small graph H, a step in the big graph G along the resulting label, and a second step in H (O. Reingold, S. Vadhan and A. Wigderson, Ann. of Math. 155 (2002), Definition 3.1). If G is |K|-regular on N vertices and H is |L|-regular on |K| vertices, G ⓩ H is |L|²-regular on N |K| vertices.

    def zigzag (G : RotationMap V K) (H : RotationMap K L) : RotationMap (V × K) (L × L) where
  rot p :=
    let a := H.rot (p.1.2, p.2.1)
    let b := G.rot (p.1.1, a.1)
    let c := H.rot (b.2, p.2.2)
    ((b.1, c.1), (c.2, a.2))
  rot_rot p := by
    obtain ⟨⟨v, k⟩, i, j⟩ := p
    simp [H.rot_rot, G.rot_rot]

    TheoremZig-zag theorem#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.RotationMap.IsNDLambdaGraph.zigzag

    If the rotation map G describes an (N₁, D₁, λ₁)-graph and the rotation map H on D₁ vertices describes a (D₁, D₂, λ₂)-graph, then their zig-zag product is an (N₁ D₁, D₂², λ₁ + λ₂ + λ₂²)-graph (O. Reingold, S. Vadhan and A. Wigderson, Ann. of Math. 155 (2002), Theorem 3.2, the bound f(λ₁, λ₂) ≤ λ₁ + λ₂ + λ₂², proved in their Section 4.1; see also Theorem 1.3).

    This is the bound of the paper's basic analysis (Section 4.1). The tighter bound of its Theorem 4.3 ("Thm. 3.2, improved", Section 4.2), which Reingold quotes as his Theorem 2.9, is not formalized.

    The proof follows Section 4.1 of the paper. The adjacency operator of the product factors as B̃ Ã B̃, where B̃ applies H inside every cloud {v} × [D₁] and Ã is the permutation of [N₁] × [D₁] given by the rotation map of G. A vector x orthogonal to the constants splits as x = x^∥ + x^⊥, with x^∥ constant on clouds and x^⊥ summing to zero on each cloud. Then B̃ fixes x^∥ and shrinks x^⊥ by λ₂, and ⟨Ã x^∥, x^∥⟩ is the quadratic form of G on the cloud averages, at most λ₁ ‖x^∥‖². Expanding ⟨B̃ Ã B̃ x, x⟩ = ⟨Ã B̃ x, B̃ x⟩ gives |⟨M x, x⟩| ≤ (λ₁ p² + 2 λ₂ p q + λ₂² q²) ≤ (λ₁ + λ₂ + λ₂²) ‖x‖² (normalised, with p = ‖x^∥‖, q = ‖x^⊥‖), and a polarization argument turns the quadratic-form bound into the norm bound of the (N, D, λ) definition.

    With G big and H small, the product keeps G's size up to the factor D₁, takes its degree D₂² from H alone, and has spectral parameter small when both factors have. Iterating G_{t+1} = G_t² ⓩ H from a single (D⁴, D, 1/5)-graph H gives an explicit family of (D^{4t}, D², 2/5)-graphs (Reingold, Vadhan and Wigderson, Theorem 1.4; proved here as the zig-zag expander family consists of expanders).

    theorem IsNDLambdaGraph.zigzag {G : RotationMap V K} {H : RotationMap K L} {lam₁ lam₂ : ℝ}
    (hG : G.IsNDLambdaGraph lam₁) (hH : H.IsNDLambdaGraph lam₂) :

    TheoremZig-zag theorem, spectral-gap form#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.RotationMap.IsNDLambdaGraph.zigzag_gap

    If the rotation map G describes an (N₁, D₁, λ₁)-graph and H a (D₁, D₂, λ₂)-graph with λ₂ ≤ 1, then the zig-zag product has spectral gap at least the product of the gap of G and the square of the gap of H: it is an (N₁ D₁, D₂², 1 - (1 - λ₁)(1 - λ₂)²)-graph; in particular λ ≤ λ₁ + 2 λ₂, and λ < 1 when λ₁, λ₂ < 1 (S. Vadhan, Pseudorandomness, Foundations and Trends in Theoretical Computer Science 7 (2012), Theorem 4.35, with γᵢ = 1 - λᵢ, where spectral gaps lie in [0, 1]; the source's restriction λ₁ ≤ 1 is not needed for the bound; compare O. Reingold, S. Vadhan and A. Wigderson, Ann. of Math. 155 (2002), Theorem 3.2, f(λ₁, λ₂) < 1 when λ₁, λ₂ < 1).

    The proof is Vadhan's matrix decomposition B̃ = (1 - λ₂) J̃ + λ₂ Ẽ applied to the vector: with the notation of the zig-zag theorem, B̃ x = (1 - λ₂) x^∥ + f where f = λ₂ x^∥ + B̃ x^⊥ has ‖f‖ ≤ λ₂ ‖x‖ because x^∥ ⊥ B̃ x^⊥; expanding ⟨Ã B̃ x, B̃ x⟩ bounds the quadratic form by (1 - λ₂)² λ₁ + 2 (1 - λ₂) λ₂ + λ₂² = 1 - (1 - λ₁)(1 - λ₂)².

    Like the zig-zag theorem, this is weaker than the improved bound of Reingold, Vadhan and Wigderson, Theorem 4.3, which is not formalized.

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.RotationMap.isNDLambdaGraph_zigzagFamily

    If H is a (|K|, |L|, 1/5)-graph, then every graph G_t of the zig-zag family is an (|K|^(t + 1), |L|², 2/5)-graph (O. Reingold, S. Vadhan and A. The induction: λ(G_t²) ≤ (2/5)² = 4/25, and the zig-zag theorem gives 4/25 + 1/5 + 1/25 = 2/5.

    No explicit seed H is formalized here: a (D⁴, D, 1/5)-graph needs a large degree D. The elementary lower bound λ ≥ √((N - D) / (D (N - 1))), about 1 / √D (proved here for simple graphs; the same test-vector argument works for multigraphs), forces D ≳ 25 for λ ≤ 1/5, and the Alon-Boppana bound 2 √(D - 1) / D is the asymptotic limit as N grows with D fixed; and the paper obtains one by exhaustive search or algebraic constructions (its Section 5). The theorem therefore reduces explicit constant-degree expanders of every size D^{4i} to a single constant-size expander.
