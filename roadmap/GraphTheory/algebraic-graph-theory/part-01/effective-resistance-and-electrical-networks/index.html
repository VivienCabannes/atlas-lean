    Effective resistance and electrical networks

    A weighted graph read as a resistor network with conductances w: potentials, the energy xᵀLx, effective conductance and effective resistance (D. A. Spielman, Spectral and Algebraic Graph Theory, Chapters 11 and 12). Effective resistances drive the sampling sparsifiers of Spielman and Srivastava, where an edge is kept with probability proportional to w_e R_eff(e).

    EndpointMapMultigraph.effectiveConductance

    For edge weights w, the infimum of the Laplacian energy xᵀ L x over all potentials x with x s = 0 and x t = 1, i.e. twice the minimum spring energy when s and t are held at distance one (D. A. Spielman, Spectral and Algebraic Graph Theory, draft of 2 April 2025, Section 12.2, where it is written C_eff(s, t)). It is 0 when s = t, where no potential is admissible.

    noncomputable def effectiveConductance (w : E → ℝ) (s t : V) : ℝ :=
  sInf {q | ∃ x : V → ℝ, x s = 0 ∧ x t = 1 ∧ q = x ⬝ᵥ (laplacian end₁ end₂ w *ᵥ x)}
    noncomputable def laplacian (w : E → ℝ) : Matrix V V ℝ :=
  orientedIncidence end₁ end₂ * diagonal w * (orientedIncidence end₁ end₂)ᵀ

    EndpointMapMultigraph.effectiveResistance

    The reciprocal of the effective conductance (D. A. Spielman, Spectral and Algebraic Graph Theory, draft of 2 April 2025, Section 12.2, R_eff(s, t) = 1 / C_eff(s, t)). With Lean's convention 0⁻¹ = 0 it is 0 when the effective conductance vanishes, for instance between different connected components.

    noncomputable def effectiveResistance (w : E → ℝ) (s t : V) : ℝ :=
  (effectiveConductance end₁ end₂ w s t)⁻¹

    EndpointMapMultigraph.effectiveConductance_mono

    If 0 ≤ w ≤ w' edgewise, then the effective conductance between any two vertices for the weights w is at most that for w': lowering conductances (raising resistances) cannot increase an effective conductance (D. A. Spielman, Spectral and Algebraic Graph Theory, draft of 2 April 2025, Theorem 12.3.1).

    If 0 ≤ w ≤ w' edgewise and the effective conductance between s and t is positive for w, then the effective resistance between them for w' is at most that for w: raising resistances cannot decrease an effective resistance (D.     TheoremEffective resistance is a potential difference#✓fully proved

    EndpointMapMultigraph.effectiveResistance_eq_sub_of_laplacian_mulVec

    For nonnegative weights and distinct vertices s and t, if the potentials v satisfy L v = δ_s - δ_t (one unit of current injected at s and extracted at t), then the effective resistance between s and t is v(s) - v(t), and this number is positive. Equivalently R_eff(s, t) = (δ_s - δ_t)ᵀ L⁺ (δ_s - δ_t), which is how D. A. Spielman, Spectral and Algebraic Graph Theory, draft of 2 April 2025, Section 12.1 defines it; the computation of Section 12.2 shows that its reciprocal is the minimum energy C_eff(s, t). No connectivity hypothesis is needed: if s and t lie in different components, no such v exists.

    theorem effectiveResistance_eq_sub_of_laplacian_mulVec {w : E → ℝ} (hw : 0 ≤ w) {s t : V}
    (hst : s ≠ t) {v : V → ℝ}
    (hv : laplacian end₁ end₂ w *ᵥ v = Pi.single s 1 - Pi.single t 1) :
    effectiveResistance end₁ end₂ w s t = v s - v t ∧ 0 < v s - v t

    EndpointMapMultigraph.sum_mul_effectiveResistance

    In a connected loopless multigraph with positive weights on n vertices, ∑_e w_e R_eff(e) = n - 1. The proof follows Spielman and Srivastava, Graph sparsification by effective resistances, SIAM J. Comput. 40 (2011), arXiv:0803.0929, Lemma 3(iii) and the identity ∑_e w_e R_e = Tr(Π) = n - 1 in the proof of Theorem 1: the numbers w_e R_eff(e) are the diagonal of an idempotent matrix (there W^{1/2} B L⁺ Bᵀ W^{1/2}, here Bᵀ P W for any P with L P = B) whose rank is that of the incidence matrix, n - 1.

    theorem sum_mul_effectiveResistance {w : E → ℝ} (hw : ∀ e, 0 < w e)
    (hloop : ∀ e, end₁ e ≠ end₂ e) (hconn : (underlyingGraph end₁ end₂).Connected) :
    ∑ e, w e * effectiveResistance end₁ end₂ w (end₁ e) (end₂ e) = (Fintype.card V : ℝ) - 1
    def underlyingGraph : SimpleGraph V :=
  SimpleGraph.fromRel fun u v => ∃ e, end₁ e = u ∧ end₂ e = v

    Related topics

    • Graph Laplacians and Cheeger's inequality: the Laplacian whose quadratic form is the network energy.
    • Spectral sparsification: Spielman and Srivastava sample edges in proportion to w_e R_eff(e).
    • Kirchhoff's matrix-tree theorem: for a spanning tree drawn with probability proportional to the product of its edge weights, w_e R_eff(e) is the probability that it contains e (Spielman, Spectral and Algebraic Graph Theory, Theorem 13.5.1).
    • Conjugate gradient method: electrical potentials solve Laplacian systems Lv = b, the systems that fast Laplacian solvers attack with preconditioned iterative methods.
