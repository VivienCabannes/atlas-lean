    Effective resistance and electrical networks

    A weighted graph read as a resistor network with conductances w: potentials, the energy xᵀLx, effective conductance and effective resistance (D. A. Spielman, Spectral and Algebraic Graph Theory, Chapters 11 and 12). Effective resistances drive the sampling sparsifiers of Spielman and Srivastava, where an edge is kept with probability proportional to w_e R_eff(e).

    In the probabilistic treatment of R. Lyons and Y. Peres, Probability on Trees and Networks, Cambridge University Press 2016, corrected online version of 19 August 2026, Chapter 2, the same quantities are organised around energy. Effective conductance is a minimum energy over potentials (Dirichlet's principle, Exercise 2.13), effective resistance is a minimum energy over unit flows (Thomson's principle, Section 2.4), and Rayleigh's monotonicity law follows from either; the Nash-Williams inequality (2.13) bounds effective resistance from below by disjoint cutsets. The network random walk enters through the commute-time identity (Corollary 2.21), and uniform spanning trees through Kirchhoff's effective resistance formula (Section 4.2). Here effective conductance is defined by the Dirichlet minimum; Lyons and Peres define it through escape probabilities, (2.4), and that agreement is not formalised.

    EndpointMapMultigraph.effectiveConductance

    For edge weights w, the infimum of the Laplacian energy xᵀ L x over all potentials x with x s = 0 and x t = 1, i.e. twice the minimum spring energy when s and t are held at distance one (D. A. Spielman, Spectral and Algebraic Graph Theory, draft of 2 April 2025, Section 12.2, where it is written C_eff(s, t)). It is 0 when s = t, where no potential is admissible.

    In R. Lyons and Y. Peres, Probability on Trees and Networks, Cambridge University Press 2016, corrected online version of 19 August 2026, effective conductance is defined probabilistically as C(a ↔ z) = π(a) P_a[τ_z < τ_a⁺], equation (2.4), and Exercise 2.13 (Dirichlet's principle) shows that it equals min { ∑_e c(e) (dF(e))² : F = 1 on A, F = 0 on Z }; with F = 1 - x this is the infimum taken here, which is attained (Dirichlet's principle). The probabilistic definition and its agreement with this one are not formalised.

    TheoremDirichlet's principle#✓fully proved

    EndpointMapMultigraph.isLeast_dirichletEnergy

    In a finite loopless multigraph with positive conductances, if s ≠ t lie in the same connected component then the effective conductance is attained: C_eff(s, t) = min { xᵀ L x : x(s) = 0, x(t) = 1 }, the minimum being attained by the normalised voltage x = (v(s) - v) / (v(s) - v(t)) for any v with L v = δ_s - δ_t (R. Lyons and Y. Peres, Probability on Trees and Networks, Cambridge University Press 2016, corrected online version of 19 August 2026, Exercise 2.13, second display, with F = 1 - x, A = {s}, Z = {t}).

    Effective conductance is defined here as an infimum; this page shows that it is a minimum, as in the source. On the path 0 - 1 - 2 with unit conductances, C_eff(0, 2) = 1 / 2, attained by x = (0, 1/2, 1) (see the test module). Together with Thomson's principle as a minimum, it gives the two dual variational formulas of Exercise 2.13.

    EndpointMapMultigraph.effectiveResistance_eq_sub_of_laplacian_mulVec

    For nonnegative weights and distinct vertices s and t, if the potentials v satisfy L v = δ_s - δ_t (one unit of current injected at s and extracted at t), then the effective resistance between s and t is v(s) - v(t), and this number is positive.     EndpointMapMultigraph.effectiveConductance_mono

    If 0 ≤ w ≤ w' edgewise, then the effective conductance between any two vertices for the weights w is at most that for w': lowering conductances (raising resistances) cannot increase an effective conductance (D. A. Spielman, Spectral and Algebraic Graph Theory, draft of 2 April 2025, Theorem 12.3.1).

    This is part (i) of Rayleigh's Monotonicity Principle in R. Lyons and Y. Peres, Probability on Trees and Networks, Cambridge University Press 2016, corrected online version of 19 August 2026, Section 2.4, for A = {s} and Z = {t}.
