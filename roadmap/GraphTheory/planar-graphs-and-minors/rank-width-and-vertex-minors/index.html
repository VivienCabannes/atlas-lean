    Rank-width and vertex-minors

    The cut-rank of a vertex set \(X\) of a graph is the rank, over the two-element field, of the adjacency matrix between \(X\) and its complement. Rank-width is the branch-width of the cut-rank function: the least width of a tree with all internal nodes of degree three whose leaves are the vertices, a tree edge having as width the cut-rank of the vertex set on one side. Local complementation at a vertex complements the graph induced on its neighbourhood. It preserves the cut-rank function (Oum), so rank-width is invariant under local equivalence and does not increase under vertex-minors, which play for rank-width the role that minors play for tree-width. Rank-width and clique-width are bounded on the same graph classes, since \(\mathrm{rw}(G) \le \mathrm{cw}(G) \le 2^{\mathrm{rw}(G)+1} - 1\) (Oum–Seymour), and rank-width is at most tree-width plus one, while complete graphs have rank-width at most one. Pages follow S. Oum, Rank-width and vertex-minors, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 95 (2005); S. Oum and P. Seymour, Approximating clique-width and branch-width, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 96 (2006); and S. Oum, Rank-width: algorithmic and structural results, Discrete Appl. Math. 231 (2017), arXiv:1601.03800v2 (author's preprints, as listed on each page).

    Rank-width and clique-width are recorded as predicates "at most \(k\)", with rank-width \(0\) for graphs with at most one vertex, as in the sources.

    DefinitionLocal complementation#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.localComplement

    Let \(G\) be a simple graph and \(v\) a vertex. The local complementation of \(G\) at \(v\) is the graph \(G * v\) on the same vertices in which two distinct vertices \(x, y\) are adjacent exactly when either \(xy\) is an edge of \(G\) or both \(x\) and \(y\) are neighbours of \(v\), but not both: $\(G * v = (V, E \,\triangle\, \{xy : xv, yv \in E,\ x \ne y\}).\)$ In words, the subgraph induced on the neighbourhood of \(v\) is replaced by its complement; the edges at \(v\) are unchanged.

    def localComplement (G : SimpleGraph V) (v : V) : SimpleGraph V where
  Adj x y := x ≠ y ∧ (G.Adj x y ↔ ¬(G.Adj v x ∧ G.Adj v y))
  symm := ⟨fun x y h => ⟨h.1.symm, by
    rw [G.adj_comm y x, and_comm (a := G.Adj v y)]; exact h.2⟩⟩
  loopless := ⟨fun _ h => h.1 rfl⟩

    DefinitionCut-rank function#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.cutRank

    Let \(G\) be a finite simple graph. For a vertex set \(X\), the cut-rank \(\rho_G(X)\) is the rank over the two-element field \(\mathrm{GF}(2)\) of the \(X \times (V \setminus X)\) matrix whose \((x, y)\) entry is \(1\) when \(x\) and \(y\) are adjacent and \(0\) otherwise. In particular \(\rho_G(\emptyset) = \rho_G(V) = 0\), and \(\rho_G(\{v\}) \le 1\).

    The cut-rank is symmetric and submodular, and rank-width is the branch-width of this function.

    noncomputable def cutRank (X : Set V) : ℕ :=
  ((G.adjMatrix (ZMod 2)).submatrix (fun x : X => (x : V)) (fun y : ↥Xᶜ => (y : V))).rank

    SimpleGraph.cutRank_inter_add_cutRank_union_le

    TheoremLocal complementation preserves the cut-rank function#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.cutRank_localComplement

    For every finite simple graph \(G\), every vertex \(v\) and every vertex set \(X\), $\(\rho_{G * v}(X) = \rho_G(X).\)$ The proof follows the source. By symmetry of cut-rank we may assume \(v \in X\). Then the cut matrix of \(G * v\) is obtained from that of \(G\) by adding the row of \(v\) to the rows of the neighbours of \(v\) in \(X\); this is left multiplication by an invertible matrix (it squares to the identity over \(\mathrm{GF}(2)\)), so the rank does not change. The source calls this observation fundamental: it is what makes vertex-minors the right containment relation for rank-width.

    theorem cutRank_localComplement (v : V) (X : Set V) :
    (G.localComplement v).cutRank X = G.cutRank X

    TheoremComplete graphs have cut-rank at most one#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.cutRank_top_le_one

    In a complete graph every cut matrix is an all-ones matrix, of rank at most one. Hence \(\rho_{K_V}(X) \le 1\) for every vertex set \(X\), and complete graphs have rank-width at most one, although \(K_n\) has tree-width \(n - 1\).

    theorem cutRank_top_le_one (X : Set V) : (⊤ : SimpleGraph V).cutRank X ≤ 1

    SimpleGraph.LocallyEquivalent

    Two simple graphs on the same vertex set are locally equivalent if one is obtained from the other by a finite sequence of local complementations. Since each local complementation is an involution, this is an equivalence relation.

    DefinitionVertex-minor#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.IsVertexMinor

    A graph \(H\) is a vertex-minor of a graph \(G\) if \(H\) is isomorphic to an induced subgraph of a graph locally equivalent to \(G\). Equivalently, \(H\) is obtained from \(G\) by a sequence of local complementations and vertex deletions; the source introduces the relation in this second form.

    Vertex-minors stand to rank-width as minors stand to tree-width; every pivot-minor is a vertex-minor.

    def IsVertexMinor {W : Type*} (H : SimpleGraph W) (G : SimpleGraph V) : Prop :=
  ∃ G' : SimpleGraph V, G.LocallyEquivalent G' ∧ ∃ f : W ↪ V, H = G'.comap f

    StructureRank-decomposition#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.RankDecomposition

    A rank-decomposition of a finite vertex set \(V\) is a pair \((T, L)\) of a finite tree \(T\) in which every node has degree one or three and a bijection \(L\) from \(V\) onto the leaves of \(T\). It does not depend on a graph; a graph enters only through the width of the decomposition.

    A rank-decomposition exists only when \(|V| \ge 2\); for graphs with at most one vertex the rank-width is defined to be \(0\).

    DefinitionWidth at most k#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.RankDecomposition.HasWidthAtMost

    A rank-decomposition \((T, L)\) has width at most \(k\) for a graph \(G\) if every edge \(ij\) of \(T\) has width at most \(k\), the width of \(ij\) being the cut-rank of its side. By symmetry of cut-rank either side gives the same width.

    def HasWidthAtMost [Fintype V] (G : SimpleGraph V) (k : ℕ) : Prop :=
  ∀ i j, D.T.Adj i j → G.cutRank (D.side i j) ≤ k

    InductiveGraphs generated by k-expressions#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.CWExpressible

    Fix \(k\). A \(k\)-expression builds a graph whose vertices carry labels in \(\{1, \dots, k\}\) (here Fin k) from

    • \(\cdot_i\): a single vertex labelled \(i\);
    • \(\oplus\): the disjoint union of two labelled graphs;
    • \(\eta_{i,j}\) with \(i \ne j\): add every edge between a vertex labelled \(i\) and a vertex labelled \(j\);
    • \(\rho_{i \to j}\): relabel every vertex labelled \(i\) as \(j\).

    A labelled graph is generated if, up to isomorphism, it is the value of a \(k\)-expression.

    SimpleGraph.CWExpressible · 17 lines 
    inductive CWExpressible (k : ℕ) : (V : Type u) → SimpleGraph V → (V → Fin k) → Prop
  /-- `·ᵢ`: a single vertex labelled `i`. -/
  | vertex (V : Type u) [Unique V] (i : Fin k) : CWExpressible k V ⊥ (fun _ => i)
  /-- `⊕`: disjoint union of two labelled graphs. -/
  | union {V W : Type u} {G : SimpleGraph V} {H : SimpleGraph W} {l : V → Fin k} {m : W → Fin k} :
      CWExpressible k V G l → CWExpressible k W H m →
      CWExpressible k (V ⊕ W) (G ⊕g H) (Sum.elim l m)
  /-- `η_{i,j}`, `i ≠ j`: join every vertex labelled `i` to every vertex labelled `j`. -/
  | join {V : Type u} {G : SimpleGraph V} {l : V → Fin k} (i j : Fin k) (hij : i ≠ j) :
      CWExpressible k V G l →
      CWExpressible k V (G ⊔ SimpleGraph.fromRel (fun x y => l x = i ∧ l y = j)) l
  /-- `ρ_{i→j}`: relabel every vertex labelled `i` as `j`. -/
  | relabel {V : Type u} {G : SimpleGraph V} {l : V → Fin k} (i j : Fin k) :
      CWExpressible k V G l → CWExpressible k V G (fun x => if l x = i then j else l x)
  /-- Isomorphic copies of generated labelled graphs are generated. -/
  | iso {V W : Type u} {G : SimpleGraph V} {l : V → Fin k} (e : V ≃ W) :
      CWExpressible k V G l → CWExpressible k W (G.map e.toEmbedding) (l ∘ e.symm)

    TheoremClique-width is bounded by an exponential in rank-width (Oum–Seymour)#●ready to prove

    SimpleGraph.HasRankWidthAtMost.hasCliqueWidthAtMost

    For every finite graph \(G\), \(\mathrm{cw}(G) \le 2^{\mathrm{rw}(G) + 1} - 1\): a graph of rank-width at most \(k\) has clique-width at most \(2^{k+1} - 1\). The source's proof builds a \((2^{k+1} - 1)\)-expression bottom-up along a rooted rank-decomposition, using that a matrix of rank at most \(k\) over \(\mathrm{GF}(2)\) has at most \(2^k - 1\) distinct nonzero rows.
