    Tree-width

    A tree-decomposition of a graph arranges its vertices in bags indexed by the nodes of a tree, so that the bags cover every vertex and every edge and the bags containing any fixed vertex form a subtree. Its width is the largest bag size minus one, and the tree-width of the graph is the least width of a tree-decomposition: trees with an edge have tree-width one and the complete graph \(K_n\) has tree-width \(n - 1\). Tree-width does not increase under taking minors, it is characterised exactly by brambles (Seymour–Thomas), large tree-width forces a large grid minor (Robertson–Seymour), and graphs of bounded tree-width are well-quasi-ordered by minors, the first step towards the graph minor theorem. Pages follow R. Diestel, Graph Theory, 6th edition, Sections 12.3, 12.4 and 12.6 (author's online preview of Chapter 12, PDF dated 28 June 2024).

    Tree-width is recorded as the predicate "tree-width at most \(k\)" for natural numbers \(k\), so the convention that the graph with no vertices has tree-width \(-1\) is never needed: that graph has tree-width at most every \(k\).

    StructureTree-decomposition#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.TreeDecomposition

    Let \(G\) be a graph. A tree-decomposition of \(G\) is a pair \((T, (V_t)_{t \in T})\) of a finite tree \(T\) and bags \(V_t \subseteq V(G)\), one for each node \(t\), such that

    • (T1) every vertex of \(G\) lies in some bag;
    • (T2) for every edge of \(G\) some bag contains both of its ends;
    • (T3) \(V_{t_1} \cap V_{t_3} \subseteq V_{t_2}\) whenever \(t_2\) lies on the path from \(t_1\) to \(t_3\) in \(T\).

    Condition (T3) is equivalent to asking that, for each vertex \(v\), the nodes whose bags contain \(v\) span a subtree of \(T\). The node type of the tree lives in the same universe as the vertex type of \(G\), and the tree is required to be finite, as for the finite graphs of the source.

    SimpleGraph.TreeDecomposition.HasWidthAtMost

    A tree-decomposition \((T, (V_t))\) has width at most \(k\) if every bag has at most \(k + 1\) vertices. The width of a tree-decomposition is \(\max\{|V_t| - 1 : t \in T\}\), so this says that the width is at most \(k\).

    def HasWidthAtMost (k : ℕ) : Prop :=
  ∀ t, (D.bag t).encard ≤ k + 1

    DefinitionTree-width at most k#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.HasTreewidthAtMost

    A graph \(G\) has tree-width at most \(k\), for a natural number \(k\), if it has a tree-decomposition all of whose bags have at most \(k + 1\) vertices. The tree-width \(\mathrm{tw}(G)\) is the least width of a tree-decomposition, so for a finite graph and \(k \ge 0\) this is \(\mathrm{tw}(G) \le k\). For a graph with infinitely many vertices the predicate is always false, because the decomposition tree is finite and so are the bags; it is meant for finite graphs only.

    The graph with no vertices has tree-width \(-1\) under the usual definition (a single empty bag has width \(-1\)); it satisfies this predicate for every natural number \(k\), which is consistent with that convention, and the value \(-1\) itself is not represented.

    def HasTreewidthAtMost (G : SimpleGraph V) (k : ℕ) : Prop :=
  ∃ D : G.TreeDecomposition, D.HasWidthAtMost k

    TheoremEvery clique lies in a bag#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.TreeDecomposition.exists_bag_superset

    Let \((T, (V_t))\) be a tree-decomposition of a graph \(G\) and let \(K\) be a finite set of pairwise adjacent vertices of \(G\). Then some bag \(V_t\) contains \(K\).

    The source derives this from its Lemma 12.3.4 (a vertex set contained in no bag contains two vertices separated by an adhesion set). The Lean proof is a Helly-type argument instead: adding the vertices of \(K\) one at a time, take the node of the subtree of bags containing the new vertex that is nearest, in \(T\), to a bag containing the vertices already treated; by (T3) its bag contains them all.

    theorem exists_bag_superset (K : Finset V) (hK : ∀ u ∈ K, ∀ w ∈ K, u ≠ w → G.Adj u w) :
    ∃ t, (K : Set V) ⊆ D.bag t

    TheoremTree-width of complete graphs#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.hasTreewidthAtMost_top_iff

    Let \(V\) be a finite set with \(n\) elements. The complete graph on \(V\) has tree-width at most \(k\) if and only if \(n \le k + 1\). Hence \(\mathrm{tw}(K_n) = n - 1\) for every \(n \ge 1\); for \(n = 0\) the statement says that the graph with no vertices has tree-width at most every \(k\).

    The upper bound puts all vertices in a single bag. The lower bound is the case \(K = V\) of the fact that every clique lies in a bag.

    theorem hasTreewidthAtMost_top_iff [Fintype V] {k : ℕ} :
    (⊤ : SimpleGraph V).HasTreewidthAtMost k ↔ Fintype.card V ≤ k + 1
    Background
    Background
    • Complete graphs have cut-rank at most one: in contrast, complete graphs have rank-width at most one, so tree-width is not bounded by any function of rank-width.

    TheoremTrees have tree-width at most one#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.IsTree.hasTreewidthAtMost_one

    Every finite tree has tree-width at most one. Root the tree \(G\) at a vertex \(r\); give each vertex \(v\) the bag consisting of \(v\) and its parent, the unique neighbour of \(v\) closer to \(r\) (the root has the bag \(\{r\}\)). With \(G\) itself as decomposition tree these bags satisfy (T1)–(T3) and have at most two vertices.

    A tree with at least one edge has tree-width exactly one, since a graph with an edge has tree-width at least one.

    theorem IsTree.hasTreewidthAtMost_one [Finite V] {G : SimpleGraph V} (hG : G.IsTree) :
    G.HasTreewidthAtMost 1

    SimpleGraph.Touch

    Two vertex sets \(X, Y\) of a graph \(G\) touch if they have a vertex in common or \(G\) has an edge joining a vertex of \(X\) to a vertex of \(Y\).

    def Touch (X Y : Set V) : Prop :=
  (X ∩ Y).Nonempty ∨ ∃ x ∈ X, ∃ y ∈ Y, G.Adj x y

    DefinitionBramble#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.IsBramble

    A bramble in a graph \(G\) is a set \(\mathcal{B}\) of vertex sets of \(G\) such that each member induces a connected subgraph (in particular it is nonempty) and any two members touch.

    A typical example is the set of \(k^2\) crosses (unions of a row and a column) of the \(k \times k\) grid, a bramble of order \(k\).

    def IsBramble (B : Set (Set V)) : Prop :=
  (∀ X ∈ B, (G.induce X).Connected) ∧ ∀ X ∈ B, ∀ Y ∈ B, G.Touch X Y

    DefinitionBramble of order greater than k#✓fully proved

    SimpleGraph.IsBramble.OrderGT

    A vertex set covers a bramble \(\mathcal{B}\) if it meets every member of \(\mathcal{B}\); the order of \(\mathcal{B}\) is the least size of a cover. The bramble has order greater than \(k\) when no set of at most \(k\) vertices covers it.

    def IsBramble.OrderGT {G : SimpleGraph V} {B : Set (Set V)} (_ : G.IsBramble B) (k : ℕ) : Prop :=
  ∀ S : Finset V, S.card ≤ k → ∃ X ∈ B, ∀ x ∈ X, x ∉ S

    MetaMathlibExt.hasTreewidthAtMost_iff_forall_bramble

    Let \(G\) be a finite graph and \(k\) a natural number. Then \(G\) has tree-width at most \(k\) if and only if \(G\) contains no bramble of order greater than \(k + 1\).

    The source states: for every integer \(k \ge 0\), a graph has tree-width \(< k\) if and only if it contains no bramble of order \(> k\). The statement here is the case \(k + 1\) of it, for \(k + 1 \ge 1\); the source's case \(k = 0\) concerns only the graph with no vertices and is not included. For example, the crosses of the \(k \times k\) grid form a bramble of order \(k\), so the grid has tree-width at least \(k - 1\).

    theorem_wanted hasTreewidthAtMost_iff_forall_bramble {V : Type*} [Finite V] (G : SimpleGraph V)
    (k : ℕ) :
    G.HasTreewidthAtMost k ↔ ∀ (B : Set (Set V)) (hB : G.IsBramble B), ¬hB.OrderGT (k + 1)

    TheoremGrid theorem (Robertson–Seymour)#●ready to prove

    MetaMathlibExt.exists_grid_minor_of_not_hasTreewidthAtMost

    For every natural number \(r\) there is a natural number \(k\) such that every finite graph whose tree-width is greater than \(k\) has the \(r \times r\) grid as a minor. The grid is the box product of two paths on \(r\) vertices: its vertices are the pairs \((i, j)\) with \(0 \le i, j < r\), and two pairs are adjacent when they differ by one in exactly one coordinate.

    The source states the theorem for graphs of tree-width at least \(k\); since \(k\) is existentially quantified, "greater than \(k\)" is the same statement.
