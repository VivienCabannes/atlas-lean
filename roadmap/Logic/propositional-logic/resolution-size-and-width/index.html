    Resolution size and width

    Resolution refutes an unsatisfiable CNF formula by deriving the empty clause. Proof complexity measures a refutation by its size, the number of lines, and by its width, the largest number of literals in a line. Following Ben-Sasson and Wigderson, derivations here allow the resolution rule (from \(E \vee x\) and \(F \vee \neg x\) derive \(E \vee F\)) and the weakening rule, and come in two shapes: tree-like derivations, where every derived line is used once, and general derivations given as a sequence of lines. Both are sound (tree-like, general); completeness is in the resolution topic.

    Ben-Sasson and Wigderson showed that short refutations are narrow: a tree-like refutation of size \(S\) yields one of width at most \(w(F) + \log_2 S\), and a general refutation of size \(S\) one of width \(w(F) + O(\sqrt{n \ln S})\) for formulas in \(n\) variables (statements only, proofs wanted). Read backwards, a linear lower bound on the refutation width of a formula of bounded width gives an exponential lower bound on its refutation size. This turned the classical size lower bounds for resolution (Haken's pigeonhole bound, Urquhart's bound for Tseitin formulas, the bound of Beame and others for random \(3\)-CNFs) into width arguments.

    InductiveTree-like resolution derivation#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Logic.PL.CNF.ResolutionTree

    A tree-like derivation of the clause C from the clauses of φ: a leaf is a clause of φ, an inner node applies the resolution rule (from E ∨ x and F ∨ ¬x derive E ∨ F) or the weakening rule (from E derive any clause containing all literals of E). Clauses of φ may be used at several leaves (Ben-Sasson and Wigderson, J. ACM 48 (2001), Section 2.1).

    The minimum, over tree-like refutations of \(F\), of the size is the tree-like refutation size \(S_T(F)\) of Ben-Sasson and Wigderson; general derivations are sequences of lines.

    inductive ResolutionTree (φ : CNF) : CNFClause → Type
  /-- A clause of the formula. -/
  | ax {C : CNFClause} : C ∈ φ → ResolutionTree φ C
  /-- Resolve a clause containing `(x, true)` with one containing `(x, false)`. -/
  | resolve {C D : CNFClause} (x : ℕ) : ResolutionTree φ C → ResolutionTree φ D →
      (x, true) ∈ C → (x, false) ∈ D → ResolutionTree φ (CNFClause.resolvent x C D)
  /-- Weaken a clause to one containing all of its literals. -/
  | weaken {C D : CNFClause} : ResolutionTree φ C → (∀ l ∈ C, l ∈ D) → ResolutionTree φ D
    abbrev CNF := List CNFClause

    DefinitionOne step of a resolution derivation#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Logic.PL.CNF.IsResolutionStep

    The clause \(C\) may follow the lines earlier in a resolution derivation from \(\varphi\) when it is a clause of \(\varphi\), the resolvent on some variable \(x\) of two earlier lines (one containing \(x\), the other \(\neg x\)), or a weakening of an earlier line (it contains all of that line's literals).

    def IsResolutionStep (φ : CNF) (earlier : List CNFClause) (C : CNFClause) : Prop :=
  C ∈ φ ∨
    (∃ x : ℕ, ∃ D ∈ earlier, ∃ E ∈ earlier,
      (x, true) ∈ D ∧ (x, false) ∈ E ∧ C = CNFClause.resolvent x D E) ∨
    (∃ D ∈ earlier, ∀ l ∈ D, l ∈ C)

    TheoremBen-Sasson–Wigderson for general resolution#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.TheoryOfComputation.BenSassonWigdersonWanted.refutation_narrow

    There is an absolute constant \(c\) such that if a CNF formula \(F\) in \(n\) variables has a resolution refutation with \(S\) lines, then it has a refutation of width at most \(w(F) + c\sqrt{n \ln S}\). Equivalently,

    \[S(F) = \exp\left(\Omega\left(\frac{(w(F \vdash 0) - w(F))^2}{n}\right)\right).\]

    The proof removes "fat" clauses, of width more than \(d = \lceil\sqrt{2n \ln S}\rceil\): by pigeonhole some literal lies in at least a \(d/2n\) fraction of them, and setting it to true removes that fraction, so after about \(\sqrt{2n \ln S}\) restrictions none are left; the restrictions are recombined as in the tree-like case. Since \(n\) appears in the bound, it gives strong size lower bounds only when the width lower bound is much larger than \(\sqrt{n}\), as for Tseitin formulas on expanders and random \(3\)-CNFs.

    public theorem_wanted refutation_narrow :
    ∃ c : ℝ, 0 < c ∧ ∀ (φ : CNF) (π : List CNFClause), IsResolutionRefutation φ π →
      ∃ π' : List CNFClause, IsResolutionRefutation φ π' ∧
        (derivationWidth π' : ℝ) ≤
          φ.maxClauseWidth + c * Real.sqrt (φ.vars.card * Real.log π.length)
    abbrev CNF := List CNFClause

    TheoremBen-Sasson–Wigderson for tree-like resolution#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.TheoryOfComputation.BenSassonWigdersonWanted.tree_refutation_narrow

    If a CNF formula \(F\) has a tree-like refutation with \(S\) lines, then it has a resolution refutation of width at most \(w(F) + \log_2 S\), where \(w(F)\) is the largest width of a clause of \(F\). It is stated as \(2^{\text{width}} \le 2^{w(F)} \cdot S\). Equivalently, the minimal tree-like refutation size satisfies

    \[S_T(F) \ge 2^{\,w(F \vdash 0) - w(F)},\]

    where \(w(F \vdash 0)\) is the minimal width of a refutation.

    The proof is by induction on \(\log_2 S\) and on the number of variables. The last step of the tree resolves the unit clauses \(x\) and \(\neg x\), and one of the two subtrees, say the one deriving \(x^{a}\) (where \(x^1 = x\) and \(x^0 = \neg x\)), has at most half the lines. Restricted by \(x = 1 - a\) it refutes \(F|_{x = 1-a}\), so by induction \(F|_{x = 1-a}\) has a refutation of width at most \(w(F) + \log_2 S - 1\), and Lemma 3.1 turns it into a derivation of \(x^{a}\) from \(F\) of width one more. Lemma 3.2 combines this derivation with a narrow refutation of \(F|_{x = a}\), obtained by induction on the number of variables.

    public theorem_wanted tree_refutation_narrow (φ : CNF) (t : ResolutionTree φ []) :
    ∃ π : List CNFClause, IsResolutionRefutation φ π ∧
      2 ^ derivationWidth π ≤ 2 ^ φ.maxClauseWidth * t.proofSize
    abbrev CNF := List CNFClause
