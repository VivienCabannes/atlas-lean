    Provability logic GL

    The provability logic GL (Gödel–Löb logic) is the normal modal logic obtained from K by adding Löb's axiom \(\Box(\Box p \to p) \to \Box p\). Reading \(\Box\) as "is provable in Peano arithmetic", GL is exactly the propositional modal logic of the provability predicate: every theorem of GL is arithmetically valid (soundness, which needs only the Hilbert–Bernays–Löb derivability conditions and the diagonal lemma), and conversely every modal formula all of whose arithmetical realizations are provable in PA is a theorem of GL (Solovay's arithmetical completeness theorem, 1976).

    The development follows the order in which the subject is usually taught.

    1. Axioms and first theorems. GL proves axiom 4, so it contains K4; it proves the modal form of Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, \(\neg\Box\bot \to \neg\Box\neg\Box\bot\); it is consistent, does not prove reflection \(\Box\bot \to \bot\), and strictly extends K4.
    2. Kripke semantics. Löb's axiom, hence every theorem of GL, is valid on every transitive, conversely well-founded frame, in particular on every finite strict partial order. Completeness for finite strict partial orders (Segerberg 1971) and the frame correspondence for Löb's axiom are recorded as wanted statements.
    3. Fixed points. Self-reference is eliminable: if \(p\) occurs in \(A(p)\) only under boxes, \(A\) has a fixed point \(H\) without \(p\), with \(\mathrm{GL} \vdash H \leftrightarrow A(H)\) (de Jongh and Sambin, 1975), and it is unique up to provable equivalence. Uniqueness and the case of a single boxed occurrence, \(A(p) = B(\Box C(p))\) with fixed point \(B(\Box C(B(\top)))\), are proved; the general existence theorem is a wanted statement. The fixed point of \(\neg\Box p\) is the consistency statement \(\neg\Box\bot\), the modal shadow of the Gödel sentence.
    4. Arithmetic. In an abstract theory with the derivability conditions D1–D3 and diagonal fixed points for the formulas \(\mathrm{Prov}(x) \to A\), the formalized Löb theorem, Löb's theorem and the arithmetical soundness of GL are proved by realizing K4 theorems.

    Solovay's arithmetical completeness theorem (R. M. Solovay, "Provability interpretations of modal logic", Israel Journal of Mathematics 25 (1976), 287–304) is not stated in Lean, because the library has no arithmetization of Peano arithmetic with a provability predicate on its sentences. In words: for every modal formula \(\varphi\), \(\mathrm{GL} \vdash \varphi\) if and only if \(\mathrm{PA} \vdash f(\varphi)\) for every realization \(f\), where \(f\) sends atoms to arithmetical sentences, commutes with the connectives and sends \(\Box\psi\) to \(\mathrm{Prov}_{\mathrm{PA}}(\ulcorner f(\psi) \urcorner)\). The direction from left to right is arithmetical soundness; the converse is proved by simulating a finite Kripke countermodel (from Segerberg's theorem) inside PA by a self-referential "Solovay function". It holds more generally for every \(\Sigma_1\)-sound theory extending \(\mathrm{I}\Delta_0 + \mathrm{EXP}\) with a natural provability predicate (de Jongh et al., 1991), as reported in R. Verbrugge, "Provability logic", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, current edition, §4.2 and §5.1. Solovay's paper also characterizes the sentences true in the standard model by the logic GLS; that part is not covered here.

    Cslib.Modal.Derivable.axiomFour_GL

    For every formula \(\varphi\), \(\mathrm{GL} \vdash \Box\varphi \to \Box\Box\varphi\).

    Proof: let \(\chi = \varphi \wedge \Box\varphi\). The formula $\(\bigl(\Box(\Box\chi \to \chi) \to \Box\chi\bigr) \to (\Box\varphi \to \Box\Box\varphi)\)$ is a theorem of K: from \(\Box\varphi\) one gets \(\Box(\Box\chi \to \chi)\), since at every successor \(\varphi\) holds and \(\Box\chi\) gives \(\Box\varphi\); and \(\Box\chi\) gives \(\Box\Box\varphi\). Its antecedent is an instance of Löb's axiom, so modus ponens gives the claim. The K theorem is obtained from the completeness of K; the single GL-specific step is the Löb instance for \(\chi\).

    Cslib.Modal.Derivable.fixedPoint_GL_of_box

    Let \(B\) and \(C\) be formulas and \(p\), \(r\) atoms, with \(p\) not occurring in \(B\), and let \(A = B[r := \Box C]\), so that \(A(p) = B(\Box C(p))\) and every occurrence of \(p\) in \(A\) is inside the boxed subformula \(\Box C\). Put $\(H = B\bigl[r := \Box\, C[p := B[r := \top]]\bigr], \qquad\text{i.e. } H = B(\Box C(B(\top))).\)$ Then \(\mathrm{GL} \vdash H \leftrightarrow A(H)\). Examples: for \(\neg\Box p\) (\(B = \neg r\), \(C = p\)) the fixed point is \(\neg\Box\neg\top\), equivalent to \(\neg\Box\bot\); for \(\Box(p \to q)\) (\(B = r\)) it is \(\Box(\top \to q)\), equivalent to \(\Box q\).

    Proof: let \(X = C(B(\top))\). On a transitive model, wherever \(\Box X\) holds it holds at all successors, so \(H\) and \(B(\top)\) agree on the generated cone and hence \(C(H)\) and \(X\) agree there; thus \(\Box X \to \Box C(H)\). Conversely, if \(\Box C(H)\) holds at a world where Löb's axiom for \(X\) holds, every successor satisfying \(\Box X\) satisfies \(X\), so Löb's axiom gives \(\Box X\). So \(\Box C(H) \leftrightarrow \Box X\) on the cone of any world where Löb's axiom for \(X\) and its necessitation hold, and there \(H \leftrightarrow A(H)\) by locality. This implication is a K4 theorem, and the Löb instance for \(X\) and its necessitation are GL theorems.

    StructureHilbert–Bernays–Löb provability structure#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Modal.HilbertBernaysLobStructure

    An abstract theory \(T\) over a set of sentences with an implication \(\to\) and a falsity \(\bot\) (with \(\neg A = A \to \bot\)): its theorems are closed under modus ponens and contain the axioms \(A \to (B \to A)\), \((A \to (B \to C)) \to ((A \to B) \to (A \to C))\) and \(\neg\neg A \to A\), and it has a provability operator \(\mathrm{Prov}\) on sentences satisfying the derivability conditions

    • D1: if \(T \vdash A\) then \(T \vdash \mathrm{Prov}(A)\);
    • D2: \(T \vdash \mathrm{Prov}(A \to B) \to (\mathrm{Prov}(A) \to \mathrm{Prov}(B))\);
    • D3: \(T \vdash \mathrm{Prov}(A) \to \mathrm{Prov}(\mathrm{Prov}(A))\).

    Peano arithmetic with its standard provability predicate is the intended instance; here the conditions are assumptions rather than consequences of an arithmetization. The hypotheses are jointly satisfiable together with the diagonal property used below, without \(\bot\) being a theorem: take true propositions as theorems and a provability operator that holds of every sentence, as in a theory proving its own inconsistency such as \(\mathrm{PA} + \neg\mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{PA})\) (see the test module).
