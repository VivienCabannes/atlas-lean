    Cslib.Bioinformatics.Phylogenetics.FelsensteinZone.paralinear

    The LogDet (paralinear) distance of a joint distribution matrix F (F i j = ℙ(X = i, Y = j)): -log |det F| + (log det Π_X + log det Π_Y) / 2, where the diagonal marginal matrices have determinants ∏ i, ∑ j, F i j and ∏ j, ∑ i, F i j. It is a distance only when det F ≠ 0 and the marginals are positive; otherwise the value is Lean's totalized logarithm (log 0 = 0).

    TheoremAdditivity of the LogDet distance at a separating vertex#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Bioinformatics.Phylogenetics.FelsensteinZone.paralinear_threePoint

    Additivity of the LogDet distance at a separating vertex (Steel 1994; Lockhart et al. 1994; Lake 1994). Let Y have distribution p (positive) and let X and Z be conditionally independent given Y, with transition matrices M (from Y to X) and N (from Y to Z) whose rows sum to 1 and whose determinants are nonzero. The joint distribution matrices are F_XY = Mᵀ Π_Y, F_YZ = Π_Y N and F_XZ = Mᵀ Π_Y N, and d(X, Z) = d(X, Y) + d(Y, Z).     TheoremAdditivity of the LogDet distance along a path#✓fully proved

    Cslib.Bioinformatics.Phylogenetics.FelsensteinZone.paralinear_pathProd

    Additivity of the LogDet distance along a path (Steel 1994; Lockhart et al. 1994): for a Markov chain v₀ → v₁ → ⋯ → v_n along a path, with positive initial distribution p and nonnegative nonsingular transition matrices M i with unit row sums, the LogDet distance between the ends is the sum of the distances of consecutive vertices.
