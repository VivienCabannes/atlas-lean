    TheoremFrisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem, uniqueness#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Biology.StatisticalGenetics.Eigenstrat.frischWaughLovell

    Frisch–Waugh–Lovell, uniqueness: in any least-squares decomposition y = c + β g + e with c ∈ V and e ⊥ V ⊔ span {g}, the coefficient β is the coefficient of the regression of the residualized y on the residualized g, and the residual e is the residual of that regression.

    theorem frischWaughLovell (V : Submodule ℝ E) (y g : E) (hg : adjusted V g ≠ 0) {c : E}
    (hc : c ∈ V) (β : ℝ) (he : y - c - β • g ∈ (V ⊔ Submodule.span ℝ {g})ᗮ) :
    β = ⟪adjusted V g, adjusted V y⟫_ℝ / ‖adjusted V g‖ ^ 2 ∧
      y - c - β • g = adjusted V y - β • adjusted V g
    noncomputable def adjusted (V : Submodule ℝ E) (y : E) : E :=
  y - V.starProjection y

    MathlibExt.Biology.StatisticalGenetics.Eigenstrat.chiSq

    The EIGENSTRAT association statistic (N - K - 1) ⟪r_g, r_y⟫² / (‖r_g‖² ‖r_y‖²) of the adjusted genotype r_g and adjusted phenotype r_y: (N - K - 1) times their squared cosine, which is their squared sample correlation when both are centered (Price et al. 2006). When either adjusted vector is zero the ratio is the junk value 0.
