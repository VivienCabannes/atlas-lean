    Wald covariance identities in an observational linear moment model

    StructureLinear moment model for instrumental variables#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Biology.StatisticalGenetics.MendelianRandomization.LinearMomentModel

    An observational linear moment model: variables Z, X, Y and U on a probability space, all square integrable, with the algebraic decomposition Y = α + β X + U. The residual U is an arbitrary observed-variable function. Consequently outcome_eq does not exclude direct effects of Z under interventions, and β is only the coefficient in this decomposition. Orthogonality (Cov(Z, U) = 0) and nonzero first stage (Cov(Z, X) ≠ 0) are hypotheses of the covariance identities that use them.

    TheoremWald ratio identity#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Biology.StatisticalGenetics.MendelianRandomization.LinearMomentModel.covariance_div_covariance

    The Wald covariance identity: if Cov(Z, U) = 0 and Cov(Z, X) ≠ 0, then Cov(Z, Y) / Cov(Z, X) = β. For a two-valued Z the ratio is the grouping estimator of Wald (1940), the difference of the group means of Y over that of X. This is a moment-condition identity, not a formal causal-identification result.

    theorem covariance_div_covariance (hZU : cov[M.instrument, M.residual; μ] = 0)
    (hZX : cov[M.instrument, M.exposure; μ] ≠ 0) :
    cov[M.instrument, M.outcome; μ] / cov[M.instrument, M.exposure; μ] =
      M.coefficient
