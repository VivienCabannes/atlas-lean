    The drift (admixture-graph) model

    StructureAdmixture-graph drift model#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Biology.PopulationGenetics.FStatistics.DriftModel

    The admixture-graph drift model, stated at the level of second moments.

    The allele frequency of population X is root + ∑ e, weight X e * incr e (see DriftModel.freq), where E is the (finite) set of edges of the graph.

    • root is the ancestral (root) allele frequency;
    • incr e is the drift increment (frequency change) along edge e;
    • len e is the drift length of edge e, i.e. the second moment 𝔼[incr e ^ 2];
    • weight X e is the probability that a lineage sampled in X traverses e: on an admixture graph it is the sum, over root-to-X paths through e, of the product of the admixture proportions on the path; on a tree it is the 0/1 indicator that e lies on the root-to-X lineage.

    f-statistics depend only on second moments of frequency differences, in which the root cancels, so only these are assumed: the increments are square integrable, pairwise uncorrelated (the mixed second moments 𝔼[incr e * incr f], e ≠ f, vanish, as they do for independent mean-zero drift on distinct edges), and 𝔼[incr e ^ 2] = len e.

    DefinitionBinary loadings of a drift model#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Biology.PopulationGenetics.FStatistics.DriftModel.HasBinaryLoadings

    The model has binary loadings if every lineage weight is 0 or 1.

    Every tree model has binary loadings: there weight X e is the indicator of e lying on the root-to-X path. The converse fails, since nothing forces the weight-1 edge sets to be compatible root-to-leaf paths: two edges of length 1 and four populations with weight vectors 00, 10, 01, 11 have binary loadings, but their f2 distances form a square, which violates the four-point condition of tree metrics. The results below use only the binary values, so they apply to every tree.

    def HasBinaryLoadings : Prop := ∀ X e, M.weight X e = 0 ∨ M.weight X e = 1
