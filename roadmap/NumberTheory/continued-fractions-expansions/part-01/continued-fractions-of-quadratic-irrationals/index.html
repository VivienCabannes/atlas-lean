    DefinitionConvergent denominators as continuants#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.convergentDen

    For a sequence of natural numbers \(a_0, a_1, a_2, \dots\), its convergent denominators \(q_0, q_1, q_2, \dots\) are the natural numbers defined by \(q_0 = 1\), \(q_1 = a_1\) and \(q_{i+2} = a_{i+2} q_{i+1} + q_i\) for \(i \ge 0\). They are the denominators of the convergents of \([a_0; a_1, a_2, \dots]\), and \(q_i\) is the continuant \(K(a_1, \dots, a_i)\); the term \(a_0\) is never used.

    The denominators \(r_i\) of the paper, those of the convergents of \([a_1; a_2, \dots, a_i]\), are the convergent denominators of the shifted sequence \((a_{n+1})_{n \ge 0}\) at index \(i - 1\), for \(i \ge 1\).

    def convergentDen (a : ℕ → ℕ) : ℕ → ℕ
  | 0 => 1
  | 1 => a 1
  | n + 2 => a (n + 2) * convergentDen a (n + 1) + convergentDen a n

    DefinitionContinued fractions with a prescribed period#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.HasPeriodicCF

    For a real number \(x\), natural numbers \(a_0\), \(l\), \(c\) and a sequence of natural numbers \(a_1, a_2, \dots\), we write \(x = [a_0; \overline{a_1, \dots, a_{l-1}, c}]\) when the regular continued fraction of \(x\) is the infinite expansion \([a_0; a_1, \dots, a_{l-1}, c, a_1, \dots, a_{l-1}, c, \dots]\): its integer part is \(a_0\), and for every \(k \ge 1\) its \(k\)-th partial quotient exists and equals \(c\) when \(l\) divides \(k\) and \(a_{k \bmod l}\) otherwise. The period \(l\) need not be minimal.

    def HasPeriodicCF (x : ℝ) (a₀ : ℕ) (a : ℕ → ℕ) (l c : ℕ) : Prop :=
  (GenContFract.of x).h = a₀ ∧ ∀ n : ℕ, (GenContFract.of x).partDens.get? n =
    some (if (n + 1) % l = 0 then (c : ℝ) else (a ((n + 1) % l) : ℝ))

    TheoremThe integer part of a periodic continued fraction is the floor#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.HasPeriodicCF.floor_eq

    Let \(x\) be a real number and let \(a_0\), \(l\), \(c\) and \(a_1, a_2, \dots\) be natural numbers. Suppose that the regular continued fraction of \(x\) has integer part \(a_0\) and that, for every \(k \ge 1\), its \(k\)-th partial quotient is \(c\) when \(l\) divides \(k\) and \(a_r\) otherwise, where \(r\) is the remainder of \(k\) modulo \(l\) (so \(r = k\) when \(l = 0\)). For \(l \ge 1\) this says that the continued fraction is \([a_0; a_1, \dots, a_{l-1}, c, a_1, \dots, a_{l-1}, c, \dots]\). Then \(\lfloor x \rfloor = a_0\).

    theorem HasPeriodicCF.floor_eq {x : ℝ} {a₀ : ℕ} {a : ℕ → ℕ} {l c : ℕ}
    (h : HasPeriodicCF x a₀ a l c) : ⌊x⌋ = a₀
