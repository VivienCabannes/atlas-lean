    DefinitionThe alternating polynomial \(X_1\)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.GeometricSumsTwoSquaresWanted.phi4pX1

    For a natural number \(p\), \(X_1\) is the polynomial with integer coefficients

    \[ X_1(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor (p+1)/2 \rfloor - 1} (-1)^i x^{p-1-2i}. \]

    For odd \(p \ge 5\) this is the even polynomial \(x^{p-1} - x^{p-3} + \dots - (-1)^{(p-1)/2} x^2 + (-1)^{(p-1)/2}\).

    noncomputable def phi4pX1 (p : ℕ) : ℤ[X] :=
  ∑ i ∈ Finset.range ((p + 1) / 2),
    (-1 : ℤ) ^ i • X ^ (p - 1 - 2 * i)

    TheoremEvery cyclotomic polynomial \(\Phi_{4q}\) is a sum of two squares#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.GeometricSumsTwoSquaresWanted.exists_sq_add_sq_eq_cyclotomic

    For every integer \(q \ge 1\) there are polynomials \(A, B \in \mathbb{Z}[x]\) such that the \(4q\)-th cyclotomic polynomial \(\Phi_{4q}\) over \(\mathbb{Z}\) satisfies \(\Phi_{4q} = A^2 + B^2\).

    The paper gives the two squares explicitly, from a product over the primitive \(4q\)-th roots of unity in the class \(1 \pmod 4\); only their existence is stated here.

    theorem_wanted exists_sq_add_sq_eq_cyclotomic (q : ℕ) (hq : 1 ≤ q) :
    ∃ A B : ℤ[X], Polynomial.cyclotomic (4 * q) ℤ = A ^ 2 + B ^ 2

    TheoremGeometric sums for primes \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and odd \(n\) are sums of two squares#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.GeometricSumsTwoSquaresWanted.geomSum_pythagorean

    Let \(p\) be a prime with \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), let \(n\) be a positive odd integer, and let \(S = \sum_{j=0}^{p-1} p^{nj} = 1 + p^n + p^{2n} + \cdots + p^{(p-1)n}\). Then every prime divisor of \(S\) is \(2\) or congruent to \(1\) modulo \(4\), and there are natural numbers \(a, b\) with \(S = a^2 + b^2\).

    theorem_wanted geomSum_pythagorean (p n : ℕ) (hp : p.Prime)
    (hp4 : p % 4 = 3) (hn : Odd n) (hn0 : 0 < n) :
    (∀ q : ℕ, q.Prime →
      q ∣ ∑ j ∈ Finset.range p, p ^ (n * j) → q = 2 ∨ q % 4 = 1) ∧
      ∃ a b : ℕ, ∑ j ∈ Finset.range p, p ^ (n * j) = a ^ 2 + b ^ 2

    TheoremAn alternating sum in coprime integers \(x, y\) is a sum of two coprime squares#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.GeometricSumsTwoSquaresWanted.altBigeom_pythagorean

    Let \(x\) and \(y\) be coprime integers, let \(n\) be a positive integer, and let \(S = \sum_{j=0}^{2n} (-1)^j x^{4n-2j} y^{2j}\), that is, \(S = x^{4n} - x^{4n-2} y^2 + \cdots - x^2 y^{4n-2} + y^{4n}\). Then every prime divisor of \(S\) is \(2\) or congruent to \(1\) modulo \(4\), and there are coprime natural numbers \(a, b\) with \(S = a^2 + b^2\).

    theorem_wanted altBigeom_pythagorean (x y : ℤ) (n : ℕ)
    (hxy : IsCoprime x y) (hn : 0 < n) :
    (∀ q : ℕ, q.Prime →
      (q : ℤ) ∣ ∑ j ∈ Finset.range (2 * n + 1),
        (-1 : ℤ) ^ j * x ^ (4 * n - 2 * j) * y ^ (2 * j) →
        q = 2 ∨ q % 4 = 1) ∧
      ∃ a b : ℕ,
        (∑ j ∈ Finset.range (2 * n + 1),
          (-1 : ℤ) ^ j * x ^ (4 * n - 2 * j) * y ^ (2 * j)) =
          (a : ℤ) ^ 2 + (b : ℤ) ^ 2 ∧ Nat.Coprime a b
