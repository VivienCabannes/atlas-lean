    DefinitionConsecutive primes#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.AreConsecutivePrimes

    Natural numbers \(p\) and \(q\) are consecutive primes when \(p\) and \(q\) are prime, \(p < q\), and every prime \(r\) satisfies \(r \le p\) or \(q \le r\); that is, no prime lies strictly between \(p\) and \(q\).

    This is the usual meaning of successive primes \(p_k, p_{k+1}\) used in the source papers.

    def AreConsecutivePrimes (p q : ℕ) : Prop :=
  Nat.Prime p ∧ Nat.Prime q ∧ p < q ∧ ∀ r, Nat.Prime r → r ≤ p ∨ q ≤ r

    MetaMathlibExt.IsRestrictedConsecutivePrimeBlock

    Let \(P\) be a predicate on natural numbers. A list \(l\) of natural numbers is a block of consecutive primes restricted by \(P\) when \(l\) is a block of consecutive primes and every entry of \(l\) satisfies \(P\).

    This covers, for example, the factorial- and primorial-bounded blocks in the source papers by choosing the corresponding predicate \(P\).

    DefinitionVardi's prime-gap asymptotic#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.ErdosProblem1138Wanted.vardiAsymptotic

    Let \(\pi\) be the prime-counting function and, for real \(x\), let \(d(x)\) be the largest prime gap \(p_{n+1} - p_n\) over the primes \(p_n < x\). The proposition states that for every real \(C > 1\),

    \[ \pi(\lfloor y + C\,d(x) \rfloor) - \pi(\lfloor y \rfloor) \sim \frac{C\,d(x)}{\log y} \]

    as \(x \to \infty\) over pairs \((x, y)\) of reals with \(x/2 < y < x\), where \(\sim\) means that the difference of the two sides is \(o\) of the right-hand side.

    This proposition is false (Erdős Problem 1138 was resolved in the negative).

    TheoremVardi's prime-gap asymptotic is false#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.ErdosProblem1138Wanted.vardiAsymptotic_refuted

    Let \(\pi\) be the prime-counting function and, for real \(x\), let \(d(x)\) be the largest prime gap \(p_{n+1} - p_n\) over the primes \(p_n < x\). It is not true that for every real \(C > 1\), \(\pi(\lfloor y + C\,d(x) \rfloor) - \pi(\lfloor y \rfloor)\) is asymptotically equivalent to \(C\,d(x) / \log y\) as \(x \to \infty\) over pairs \((x, y)\) with \(x/2 < y < x\).

    This resolves Erdős Problem 1138 in the negative (Sunder, Kumrawat and Cheri, with GPT 5.5; the disproof is Lean-verified).
