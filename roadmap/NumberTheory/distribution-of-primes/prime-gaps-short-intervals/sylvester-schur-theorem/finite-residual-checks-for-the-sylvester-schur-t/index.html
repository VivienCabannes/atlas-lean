    LemmaA numerical criterion for a prime divisor of \(\binom{N}{r}\) above \(r\) when \(N = 2r + i\)#✓fully proved

    Nat.SylvesterSchur.Internal.exists_prime_dvd_choose_of_central_offset_erdos_div_three_layers_bound

    Let \(r\), \(i\) and \(N\) be natural numbers with \(r \ge 4\), \(i \le r\), \(N = 2r + i\) and \(N \ge 6\). For a natural number \(n\) write \(n\#\) for the product of the primes \(p \le n\). Let \(L = \lfloor \log_2 N \rfloor\), let \(a_0 = \lfloor N/3 \rfloor\#\) and \(a_j = \lfloor N^{1/(j+1)} \rfloor\#\) for \(j \ge 1\), and put \(P = \prod_{j=0}^{L-1} a_j\). If \(r \cdot 2^i \cdot P < 3^i \cdot 4^r\), then some prime \(p\) with \(r < p\) divides \(\binom{N}{r}\).
