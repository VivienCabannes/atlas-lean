    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.ErdosSupersingularPrimesWanted.ErdosSupersingularPrimes.Solution

    For a natural number \(k\), a solution consists of a natural number \(l\) and integers \(n\), \(d\), \(y\) such that \(\gcd(n, d) = 1\) and

    \[\prod_{i=0}^{k-1} (n + i d) = y^l,\]

    that is, \(n (n + d) (n + 2d) \cdots (n + (k - 1)d) = y^l\).

    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.ErdosSupersingularPrimesWanted.ErdosSupersingularPrimes.theorem_2

    Let \(k_0\) be the threshold constant of this module. For every natural number \(k \ge k_0\), every natural number \(l\) that is prime, and all integers \(n\), \(d\), \(y\) with \(\gcd(n, d) = 1\) and

    \[\prod_{i=0}^{k-1} (n + i d) = y^l,\]

    we have \(y = 0\) or \(d = 0\) or \(l \le \exp(10^k)\).

    The source asserts that \(k_0\) is an effectively computable absolute constant; the formal statement leaves \(k_0\) to be supplied and does not enforce computability.
