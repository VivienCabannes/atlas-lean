    MetaMathlibExt.bisector_slope_equation

    Let \(a, b, c\) be real numbers with \(|a| \ne |b|\) and \(c \ne 0\), and suppose that the lines \(y = cx\) and \(y = -c^{-1} x\) both bisect the angles between the lines \(y = ax\) and \(y = bx\), that is,

    \[ \frac{|a - c|}{\sqrt{a^2 + 1}} = \frac{|b - c|}{\sqrt{b^2 + 1}} \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{|a + c^{-1}|}{\sqrt{a^2 + 1}} = \frac{|b + c^{-1}|}{\sqrt{b^2 + 1}}. \]

    Then \((a - c)^2 (b^2 + 1) = (b - c)^2 (a^2 + 1)\).

    A line \(y = sx\) bisects the angles between \(y = ax\) and \(y = bx\) exactly when the point \((1, s)\) is equidistant from both lines, that is, when \(|a - s| / \sqrt{a^2 + 1} = |b - s| / \sqrt{b^2 + 1}\).
