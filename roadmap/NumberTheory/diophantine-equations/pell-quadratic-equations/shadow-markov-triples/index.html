    DefinitionShadow Markoff mutation#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.shadowMarkoffMutate

    A state is a pair \((x, y)\) of a classical part \(x \in \mathbb{Q}^3\) and a shadow part \(y \in \mathbb{Q}^3\), with coordinates indexed by the positions \(0, 1, 2\). The shadow Markoff mutation at position \(i\) replaces \(x_i\) by \(x_i' = \bigl(\sum_{j \ne i} x_j^2\bigr) / x_i\) and \(y_i\) by \(\bigl(-x_i' y_i + 2 \sum_{j \ne i} x_j y_j\bigr) / x_i\), and leaves every other coordinate of \(x\) and \(y\) unchanged.

    Division by \(0\) gives \(0\): if \(x_i = 0\), the new \(x_i\) and the new \(y_i\) are both \(0\).

    def shadowMarkoffMutate (i : Fin 3) (s : (Fin 3 → ℚ) × (Fin 3 → ℚ)) :
    (Fin 3 → ℚ) × (Fin 3 → ℚ) :=
  let x' : ℚ := (∑ j ∈ Finset.univ.erase i, s.1 j ^ 2) / s.1 i
  (Function.update s.1 i x',
    Function.update s.2 i ((-x' * s.2 i + 2 * ∑ j ∈ Finset.univ.erase i, s.1 j * s.2 j) / s.1 i))

    InductiveLower nodes of a shadow Markoff tree#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.ShadowMarkoffTreeNode

    Fix \(v \in \mathbb{Z}^3\) and let \(r\) be the initial triple with shadow part \(v\), viewed as a state (a pair of a classical part and a shadow part, both in \(\mathbb{Q}^3\), with coordinates indexed by the positions \(0, 1, 2\)). The lower nodes of the shadow Markoff tree of \(v\) are pairs \((s, l)\) of a state \(s\) and a position \(l\), the position mutated last. They are defined inductively: the state obtained from \(r\) by the shadow Markoff mutation at position \(0\) and then at position \(1\), tagged with \(l = 1\), is a lower node; and if \((s, l)\) is a lower node and \(i \ne l\), then the mutation of \(s\) at position \(i\), tagged with \(i\), is a lower node. Nothing else is a lower node.

    The first lower node has classical part \((2, 5, 1)\), the paper's \((1, 2, 5)\); the lower nodes are the nodes of the tree below the paper's \((1, 1, 2)\).

    inductive ShadowMarkoffTreeNode (v : Fin 3 → ℤ) : (Fin 3 → ℚ) × (Fin 3 → ℚ) → Fin 3 → Prop
  | start :
      ShadowMarkoffTreeNode v (shadowMarkoffMutate 1 (shadowMarkoffMutate 0 (shadowMarkoffRoot v))) 1
  | mutate {s : (Fin 3 → ℚ) × (Fin 3 → ℚ)} {l : Fin 3} (i : Fin 3) :
      ShadowMarkoffTreeNode v s l → i ≠ l → ShadowMarkoffTreeNode v (shadowMarkoffMutate i s) i

    DefinitionShadow Markoff tree#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.shadowMarkoffTree

    For \(v \in \mathbb{Z}^3\), the shadow Markoff tree of \(v\) is the set of states \((x, y) \in \mathbb{Q}^3 \times \mathbb{Q}^3\) (classical part \(x\), shadow part \(y\)) consisting of the initial triple \(r\) with shadow part \(v\), the shadow Markoff mutation of \(r\) at position \(0\), and the state \(s\) of every lower node \((s, l)\) of the shadow Markoff tree of \(v\).

    def shadowMarkoffTree (v : Fin 3 → ℤ) : Set ((Fin 3 → ℚ) × (Fin 3 → ℚ)) :=
  {shadowMarkoffRoot v, shadowMarkoffMutate 0 (shadowMarkoffRoot v)} ∪
    {s | ∃ l, ShadowMarkoffTreeNode v s l}

    MetaMathlibExt.exists_polygon_shadowMarkoffTree_nonneg_iff

    There is a finite set \(V \subseteq \mathbb{Q}^3\) such that for every \(v = (\alpha, \beta, \gamma) \in \mathbb{Z}^3\) the following are equivalent: every state \((x, y)\) of the shadow Markoff tree of \(v\), the tree of the initial triple \((1 + \alpha\varepsilon, 1 + \beta\varepsilon, 1 + \gamma\varepsilon)\) with \(\varepsilon^2 = 0\), has all coordinates of its classical part \(x\) and of its shadow part \(y\) nonnegative; and \(v = tw\) for some rational \(t \ge 0\) and some point \(w\) of the convex hull of \(V\) in \(\mathbb{Q}^3\).

    In projective terms, the source describes the points \((\alpha : \beta : \gamma)\) with a nonnegative tree as a convex polygon in an affine chart of the rational projective plane; the statement above asserts only that some finite set \(V\) exists.

    MetaMathlibExt.shadowMarkoffTree_one_shadow_eq

    In the shadow Markoff tree of \(v = (1, 1, 1)\), the tree of the initial triple \((1 + \varepsilon, 1 + \varepsilon, 1 + \varepsilon)\) with \(\varepsilon^2 = 0\), every solution \((x, y)\) has shadow part \(y\) equal to its classical part \(x\).

    So the shadow parts of this tree reproduce the classical Markoff tree.
