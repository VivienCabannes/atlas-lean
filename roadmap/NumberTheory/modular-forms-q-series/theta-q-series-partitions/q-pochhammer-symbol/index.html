    DefinitionRaw infinite q-Pochhammer product#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.QPochhammer.qPochhammerInfRaw

    For complex numbers \(a\) and \(q\), the raw infinite q-Pochhammer product is the unconditional infinite product \(\prod_{n=0}^{\infty} (1 - a q^n)\), defined for every \(q\): when this product does not converge unconditionally (in particular for most \(q\) with \(|q| \ge 1\)), its value is \(1\) by convention.

    noncomputable def qPochhammerInfRaw (a q : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  tprod (fun n : ℕ => (1 - a * q ^ n))

    DefinitionInfinite q-Pochhammer symbol#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.QPochhammer.qPochhammerInf

    For complex numbers \(a\) and \(q\) with \(|q| < 1\), the infinite q-Pochhammer symbol is \((a;q)_\infty = \prod_{n=0}^{\infty} (1 - a q^n)\), the unconditional infinite product of the factors \(1 - a q^n\).

    The hypothesis \(|q| < 1\) is an argument of the definition, so the symbol cannot be applied to \(q\) outside the open unit disk.

    noncomputable def qPochhammerInf (a q : ℂ) (_hq : ‖q‖ < 1) : ℂ :=
  qPochhammerInfRaw a q
    noncomputable def qPochhammerInfRaw (a q : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  tprod (fun n : ℕ => (1 - a * q ^ n))

    TheoremConvergence of the infinite q-Pochhammer product#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.QPochhammer.hasProd_qPochhammerInf

    For all complex numbers \(a\) and \(q\) with \(|q| < 1\), the infinite product \(\prod_{n=0}^{\infty} (1 - a q^n)\) converges unconditionally to the infinite q-Pochhammer symbol \((a;q)_\infty\): the products of the factors \(1 - a q^n\) over finite sets of indices \(n\) tend to \((a;q)_\infty\).

    No nonvanishing hypothesis is imposed, so the cases where some factor is zero (and the product is \(0\)) are included.

    theorem hasProd_qPochhammerInf (a q : ℂ) (hq : ‖q‖ < 1) :
    HasProd (fun n : ℕ => (1 - a * q ^ n)) (qPochhammerInf a q hq)
    noncomputable def qPochhammerInf (a q : ℂ) (_hq : ‖q‖ < 1) : ℂ :=

    MetaMathlibExt.QPochhammer.qPochhammerInf_one_zero

    The infinite q-Pochhammer symbol satisfies \((1;0)_\infty = 0\), because its factor for \(n = 0\) is \(1 - 1 \cdot 0^0 = 0\) (with the convention \(0^0 = 1\)).

    TheoremThe raw q-Pochhammer product at a = 2 and q = 1 equals 1#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.QPochhammer.qPochhammerInfRaw_two_one

    The raw infinite q-Pochhammer product at \(a = 2\) and \(q = 1\) equals \(1\).

    Every factor \(1 - 2 \cdot 1^n\) equals \(-1\), so the finite partial products alternate between \(-1\) and \(1\) and the product does not converge; the value \(1\) is the default that the unconditional product takes in that case, not a limit of partial products. The q-Pochhammer symbol \((a;q)_\infty\), which requires \(|q| < 1\), excludes such arguments.

    theorem qPochhammerInfRaw_two_one : qPochhammerInfRaw 2 1 = 1
    noncomputable def qPochhammerInfRaw (a q : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  tprod (fun n : ℕ => (1 - a * q ^ n))

    DefinitionThe Euler products of partition theory#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.qEulerProduct

    For a natural number \(k\), the Euler product \(f_k = (q^k; q^k)_\infty = \prod_{m \ge 1} (1 - q^{km})\) is this infinite product in the ring of integer power series in \(q\), taken in the coefficientwise topology. It is used for \(k \ge 1\).

    noncomputable def qEulerProduct (k : ℕ) : ℤ⟦X⟧ :=
  ∏' m : ℕ, (1 - X ^ (k * (m + 1)))
