    TheoremMatveev's lower bound for linear forms in logarithms (real case)#●ready to prove

    MetaMathlibExt.matveev_lower_bound_linear_forms_logarithms

    Let \(L\) be a number field of degree \(D = [L : \mathbb{Q}]\) with a field embedding \(f : L \to \mathbb{R}\), let \(s \ge 1\) be a natural number, and let \(\eta_1, \dots, \eta_s \in L\) satisfy \(\eta_i \ne 1\) and \(f(\eta_i) > 0\) for every \(i\). Let \(d_1, \dots, d_s\) be nonzero integers and let \(B\) be a real number with \(B \ge 1\) and \(|d_i| \le B\) for every \(i\). Let \(A_1, \dots, A_s\) be real numbers with \(A_j \ge \max(D\,h(\eta_j), |\log f(\eta_j)|, 0.16)\) for every \(j\), where \(h\) is the absolute logarithmic Weil height. Put \(\Gamma = f(\eta_1)^{d_1} \cdots f(\eta_s)^{d_s} - 1\). If \(\Gamma \ne 0\), then

    \[\log|\Gamma| > -1.4 \cdot 30^{s+3} \, s^{4.5} \, D^2 (1 + \log D)(1 + \log B) \, A_1 \cdots A_s.\]

    No integrality is assumed on the \(\eta_i\), so the statement also covers non-integral algebraic numbers, such as the element in the source's application whose minimal polynomial has leading coefficient \(23\). The source states the bound with \(B = \max_i |d_i|\); since the right-hand side increases with \(B\), any real \(B \ge 1\) bounding every \(|d_i|\) may be used.

    TheoremSchmidt's subspace theorem#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.SchmidtSubspaceWanted.NumberTheory.SchmidtSubspace.schmidt_subspace

    Let \(\sigma\) be a finite set with at least two elements, and for \(i \in \sigma\) let \(L_i(x) = \sum_{j \in \sigma} a_{ij} x_j\) be linear forms whose coefficients \(a_{ij} \in \mathbb{C}\) are algebraic over \(\mathbb{Q}\) and whose coefficient vectors \((a_{ij})_{j \in \sigma}\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb{C}\). Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be real. Then there is a finite set \(S\) of nonzero vectors in \(\mathbb{Z}^\sigma\) such that every \(x \in \mathbb{Z}^\sigma\) with \(\left|\prod_{i \in \sigma} L_i(x)\right| < \|x\|^{-\varepsilon}\), where \(\|x\| = \max_{j \in \sigma} |x_j|\), satisfies \(\sum_{i \in \sigma} c_i x_i = 0\) for some \(c \in S\).

    Every proper subspace of \(\mathbb{Q}^\sigma\) lies in a hyperplane \(\sum_i c_i x_i = 0\) with a nonzero integer vector \(c\), so this is the usual form: these \(x\) lie in finitely many proper subspaces.
