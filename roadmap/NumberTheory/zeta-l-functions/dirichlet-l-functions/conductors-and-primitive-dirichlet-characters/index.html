    MonoidHom.IsDirichletCharacterModulus

    Let \(R\) be a monoid with zero, let \(\chi \colon \mathbb{Z} \to R\) be a multiplicative map with \(\chi(1) = 1\), and let \(q\) be a natural number. Then \(\chi\) is a Dirichlet character of modulus \(q\) if \(\chi(n + q) = \chi(n)\) for every integer \(n\), and, for every integer \(n\), \(\chi(n) \neq 0\) if and only if \(q\) and \(n\) are coprime.

    def IsDirichletCharacterModulus (χ : ℤ →* R) (q : ℕ) : Prop :=
  Function.Periodic χ (q : ℤ) ∧ ∀ n : ℤ, χ n ≠ 0 ↔ IsCoprime (q : ℤ) n

    TheoremModuli of a character with a given least period#✓fully proved

    MonoidHom.isDirichletCharacterModulus_iff

    Let \(R\) be a nontrivial monoid with zero in which nonzero elements can be cancelled on both sides, and let \(\chi \colon \mathbb{Z} \to R\) be a multiplicative map with \(\chi(1) = 1\). Suppose \(m \ge 1\) is the least positive period of \(\chi\): \(\chi(n + m) = \chi(n)\) for every integer \(n\), and every positive integer \(d\) with \(\chi(n + d) = \chi(n)\) for all \(n\) satisfies \(m \le d\). Then for every integer \(m' \ge 1\), \(\chi\) is a Dirichlet character of modulus \(m'\) if and only if \(m \mid m'\) and \(m' \mid m^k\) for some natural number \(k\).

    The hypothesis that \(R\) is nontrivial cannot be dropped: in the zero monoid every value of \(\chi\) is \(0\), so \(\chi\) is a Dirichlet character of no modulus.

    theorem isDirichletCharacterModulus_iff {χ : ℤ →* R} [Nontrivial R]
    [IsCancelMulZero R] {m m' : ℕ}
    (hm : 0 < m)
    (hper : Function.Periodic χ (m : ℤ))
    (hmin : ∀ d : ℕ, 0 < d → Function.Periodic χ (d : ℤ) → m ≤ d)
    (hm' : 0 < m') :
    χ.IsDirichletCharacterModulus m' ↔ m ∣ m' ∧ ∃ k : ℕ, m' ∣ m ^ k

    DirichletCharacter.PrimitiveInducer

    Let \(R\) be a commutative monoid with zero and \(m\) a natural number. A primitive inducer of modulus \(m\) with values in \(R\) consists of a natural number \(d\), its level, with \(d \mid m\), together with a Dirichlet character \(\psi\) of level \(d\) with values in \(R\) that is primitive, that is, whose conductor is \(d\). These are the elements of the set \(X(m)\) of Corollary 18.16; changing the level of \(\psi\) from \(d\) to \(m\) gives the induced Dirichlet character of level \(m\).

    DirichletCharacter.equivPrimitiveInducer

    Let \(R\) be a commutative monoid with zero and \(m \ge 1\) a natural number. This is the bijection from Dirichlet characters of level \(m\) with values in \(R\) to primitive inducers of modulus \(m\) (primitive Dirichlet characters with values in \(R\) whose level divides \(m\)) that sends \(\chi\) to the primitive character associated to \(\chi\), whose level is the conductor of \(\chi\). Its inverse sends a primitive character \(\psi\) of level \(d \mid m\) to the Dirichlet character of level \(m\) obtained from \(\psi\) by changing the level from \(d\) to \(m\).

    TheoremExistence and uniqueness of the primitive character inducing a Dirichlet character#✓fully proved

    DirichletCharacter.primitiveCharacter_isPrimitive_and_unique

    Let \(R\) be a commutative monoid with zero, let \(n \ge 1\), and let \(\chi\) be a Dirichlet character of level \(n\) with values in \(R\). The primitive character associated to \(\chi\), whose level is the conductor of \(\chi\), is primitive, and its change of level to \(n\) is \(\chi\). Moreover, if \(\chi'\) is a primitive Dirichlet character of some level \(d\) with \(d \mid n\) whose change of level to \(n\) is \(\chi\), then \(d\) is the conductor of \(\chi\) and \(\chi'\) is the primitive character associated to \(\chi\).

    public theorem primitiveCharacter_isPrimitive_and_unique (χ : DirichletCharacter R n) :
    χ.primitiveCharacter.IsPrimitive ∧
      changeLevel (conductor_dvd_level χ) χ.primitiveCharacter = χ ∧
      ∀ (d : ℕ) (χ' : DirichletCharacter R d), χ'.IsPrimitive →
        ∀ (hd : d ∣ n), changeLevel hd χ' = χ →
          ∃ (hlev : d = χ.conductor), hlev ▸ χ' = χ.primitiveCharacter
