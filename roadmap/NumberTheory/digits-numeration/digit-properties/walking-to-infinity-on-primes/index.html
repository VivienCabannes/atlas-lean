    Walking to infinity on primes

    DefinitionExtended Cunningham chain#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.extendedCunninghamChain

    For a natural number \(p\), the extended Cunningham chain generated by \(p\) is the sequence \(e_0, e_1, e_2, \ldots\) of natural numbers given by \(e_0 = p\) and \(e_k = 2 e_{k-1} + 1\) for \(k \ge 1\); in binary, \(e_k\) is obtained from \(p\) by appending \(k\) digits \(1\) on the right.

    The paper requires the generator \(p\) to be prime; here \(p\) may be any natural number, and results that need primality assume it explicitly.

    public def extendedCunninghamChain (p : ℕ) : ℕ → ℕ
  | 0 => p
  | k + 1 => 2 * extendedCunninghamChain p k + 1

    MetaMathlibExt.exists_prime_walk_appending_decimal_digits

    For every prime \(p_0\) there is a sequence of primes \(p_0, p_1, p_2, \ldots\) starting at \(p_0\) such that for every \(i \ge 1\) there are integers \(n_i \ge 1\) and \(k_i\) with \(0 < k_i < 10^{n_i}\) and \(p_i = 10^{n_i} p_{i-1} + k_i\); that is, each term is obtained from the previous one by appending \(n_i\) decimal digits on the right.

    TheoremA power of two minus a much smaller positive number is not a power of two#●ready to prove

    MetaMathlibExt.two_pow_sub_succ_ne_two_pow

    Let \(p\) and \(k\) be natural numbers with \(k \ge \lceil \log_2 (p + 1) \rceil + 2\). Then \(2^k - (p + 1)\) is not a power of \(2\): there is no natural number \(m\) with \(2^k - (p + 1) = 2^m\).

    Under this hypothesis \(2^k > p + 1\), so the subtraction is exact. The source assumes \(p\) is prime; the statement holds without that assumption.

    public theorem_wanted two_pow_sub_succ_ne_two_pow (p k : ℕ) (hk : Nat.clog 2 (p + 1) + 2 ≤ k) :
    ¬ ∃ m : ℕ, 2 ^ k - (p + 1) = 2 ^ m

    TheoremNo binary prime walk to infinity in steps of one or two digits#●ready to prove

    MetaMathlibExt.not_exists_binary_prime_walk_appending_at_most_two_digits

    There is no sequence of primes \(p_0, p_1, p_2, \ldots\) such that for every \(i \ge 0\) there are \(n \in \{1, 2\}\) and a natural number \(k < 2^n\) with \(p_{i+1} = 2^n p_i + k\). That is, starting from any prime, one cannot walk to infinity on primes by appending one or two binary digits at a time on the right.

    The appended blocks are the paper's \(0, 1, 00, 01, 10, 11\) (a block may begin with \(0\)).

    public theorem_wanted not_exists_binary_prime_walk_appending_at_most_two_digits :
    ¬ ∃ p : ℕ → ℕ, (∀ i, (p i).Prime) ∧
      ∀ i, ∃ n k : ℕ, 1 ≤ n ∧ n ≤ 2 ∧ k < 2 ^ n ∧ p (i + 1) = 2 ^ n * p i + k

    DefinitionCounting right-truncatable square-free numbers#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.rightTruncSquarefreeCount

    For natural numbers \(b\) and \(k\), \(L_{b,k}\) is the number of right-truncatable square-free numbers with exactly \(k\) digits in base \(b\): the number of natural numbers \(x\) with \(b^{k-1} \le x < b^k\) such that \(\lfloor x / b^{\ell} \rfloor\) is square-free for every \(\ell \in \{0, \dots, k-1\}\). When \(k = 0\) the range is empty, so \(L_{b,0} = 0\).

    public def rightTruncSquarefreeCount (b k : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  ((Finset.Ico (b ^ (k - 1)) (b ^ k)).filter
    (fun x => ∀ l ∈ Finset.range k, Squarefree (x / b ^ l))).card
