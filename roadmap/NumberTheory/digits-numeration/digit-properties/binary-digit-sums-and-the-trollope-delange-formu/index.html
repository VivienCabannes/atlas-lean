    Binary digit sums and the Trollope–Delange formula

    DefinitionThe \(q\)-weighted binary digit sum#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.qWeightedDigitSum

    Let \(q\) be an element of a commutative ring and let \(k\) be a natural number with binary expansion \(k = \sum_{i \ge 0} \omega_i 2^i\), where \(\omega_i \in \{0, 1\}\). The \(q\)-weighted binary digit sum of \(k\) is \(s_q(k) = \sum_{i \ge 0} \omega_i q^{i+1}\), a finite sum because only finitely many \(\omega_i\) are nonzero.

    def qWeightedDigitSum {R : Type*} [CommRing R] (q : R) (k : ℕ) : R :=
  ∑ i ∈ range (Nat.digits 2 k).length, ((Nat.digits 2 k).getD i 0 : R) * q ^ (i + 1)

    DefinitionThe Takagi–Landsberg function#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.takagiLandsberg

    For a real or complex parameter \(a\) and a real number \(x\), let \(\tau(y)\) denote the distance from a real number \(y\) to the nearest integer. The Takagi–Landsberg function is \(\mathcal{T}_a(x) = \sum_{n \ge 0} a^n \tau(2^n x)\), where the value is taken to be \(0\) when the series does not converge absolutely. The series converges for \(|a| < 1\), and at dyadic rationals \(x\) it is a finite sum.

    noncomputable def takagiLandsberg {𝕜 : Type*} [RCLike 𝕜] (a : 𝕜) (x : ℝ) : 𝕜 :=
  ∑' n : ℕ, a ^ n * ((|2 ^ n * x - round (2 ^ n * x)| : ℝ) : 𝕜)

    TheoremThe \(q\)-weighted Trollope–Delange formula#●ready to prove

    MetaMathlibExt.qWeighted_trollopeDelange

    Let \(q\) be a real number with \(|q| > 1/2\) and \(q \ne 1\), and put \(a = 1/(2q)\). For a positive integer \(n\), let \(k = \lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor\) and let \(u = \log_2 n - k\) be the fractional part of \(\log_2 n\). Write \(F(u) = 2^{1-u}\,\mathcal{T}_a(2^{u-1})\). Then

    \[\frac{S_q(n)}{n} = \frac{q}{2}\left(\frac{1 - q^{k+1}}{1 - q} - q^k F(u)\right),\]

    where \(S_q\) is the partial sum of the \(q\)-weighted binary digit sum and \(\mathcal{T}_a\) is the Takagi–Landsberg function.

      q / 2 * ((1 - q ^ (Nat.log 2 n + 1)) / (1 - q) -

    DefinitionCarry in adding a natural number to a dyadic integer#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.dyadicAddNatCarry

    Let \(\omega\) be a dyadic integer, written as its sequence \((\omega_0, \omega_1, \dots)\) of binary digits \(\omega_i \in \{0, 1\}\) (least significant digit first), and let \(j\) be a natural number with binary digits \(j_0, j_1, \dots\).     DefinitionOdometer partial sums#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.odometerDigitPartialSum

    Let \(q \in \mathbb{C}\), let \(\omega = (\omega_0, \omega_1, \dots)\) be a dyadic integer written as its binary digit sequence, let \(T\) be the dyadic odometer \(T\omega = \omega + 1\), and let \(s_q(\omega) = \sum_{i \ge 0} \omega_i q^{i+1}\), taken to be \(0\) when the series is not absolutely summable.     \[S_{q,\omega}(x) = \sum_{j < \lfloor x \rfloor_+} s_q(T^j \omega) + \{x\} \, s_q(T^{\lfloor x \rfloor_+} \omega),\]

    where \(\lfloor x \rfloor_+ = \max(\lfloor x \rfloor, 0)\) and \(\{x\} = x - \lfloor x \rfloor\).     DefinitionMaximal fluctuation of the odometer partial sums#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.odometerMaxFluctuation

    Let \(q\) be a complex number, let \(\omega = (\omega_0, \omega_1, \dots)\) be a dyadic integer (a sequence of binary digits), let \(l\) be a natural number, and let \(S_{q,\omega}\) denote the interpolated odometer partial sums. The maximal fluctuation is the supremum over \(t \in [0, 1]\) of \(|S_{q,\omega}(t l) - t\,S_{q,\omega}(l)|\).

    This is the scale of the canonical normalizing coefficient of the sources: "the normalizing coefficient \(R_n\) is canonically defined to be equal to the maximum in \(t \in [0, 1]\) of \(|\mathcal{S}(t \cdot l_n(x)) - t \cdot \mathcal{S}(l_n)|\)".

    noncomputable def odometerMaxFluctuation (q : ℂ) (ω : ℕ → Fin 2) (l : ℕ) : ℝ :=
  ⨆ t : Set.Icc (0 : ℝ) 1,

    MetaMathlibExt.IsStabilizingSeq

    Let \(q \in \mathbb{C}\), let \(\omega\) be a dyadic integer, let \(S_{q,\omega}\) be the interpolated odometer partial sum, and for a natural number \(l\) let \(M_{q,\omega}(l) = \sup_{t \in [0, 1]} |S_{q,\omega}(tl) - t S_{q,\omega}(l)|\) be the maximal fluctuation. A sequence \((l_n)\) of natural numbers is a stabilizing sequence for \((q, \omega)\) with normalizing sequence \((R_n)\) of complex numbers and limiting curve \(\varphi : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}\) when: every \(l_n\) is positive and \(l_n \to \infty\); every \(R_n\) is nonzero and \(|R_n| \to \infty\); there are real constants \(c > 0\) and \(C\) with \(c M_{q,\omega}(l_n) \le |R_n| \le C M_{q,\omega}(l_n)\) for all sufficiently large \(n\); \(\varphi\) is continuous on \([0, 1]\); and the functions \(t \mapsto (S_{q,\omega}(t l_n) - t S_{q,\omega}(l_n))/R_n\) converge uniformly on \([0, 1]\) to \(\varphi\).

    The normalizing sequence \(R_n\) is chosen of the order of \(M_{q,\omega}(l_n)\), not equal to it: with equality every normalized fluctuation would have supremum norm at most \(1\), so the limit in Theorem 2 of Article A61 could not be the negative Takagi–Landsberg function \(-\mathcal{T}_a\) (for example \(\mathcal{T}_{3/4}(1/3) = 4/3\)). This matches the sources' usage, where \(\varphi^{l_n,R_n}\) is parametrized by the chosen \(R_n\) (e.g. \(R_n = (2q)^{n-1}\) along \(l_n = 2^n\) in Article A61, Section 3); unbounded growth of \(R_n\) is the necessary condition for a limiting curve recorded in the odometer paper.
