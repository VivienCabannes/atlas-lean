    Pillai's gcd-sum function

    TheoremDirichlet series of the gcd-sum function#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.dirichlet_series_gcd_sum

    For \(n \ge 1\) let \(g(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \gcd(n, i)\). For every complex number \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re} s > 2\), the series \(\sum_{n \ge 1} g(n)/n^s\) sums to \(\zeta(s-1)^2/\zeta(s)\), where \(\zeta\) is the Riemann zeta function.

    The function \(g\) is the Dirichlet convolution of the identity function with Euler's totient function \(\varphi\), which gives the factorization \(\zeta(s-1) \cdot (\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s))\).

    theorem dirichlet_series_gcd_sum
    (s : ℂ) (hs : 2 < s.re) :
    (∑' n : ℕ, ((∑ i ∈ Finset.range (n + 1), (Nat.gcd (n + 1) (i + 1) : ℂ))
      / (((n + 1 : ℕ) : ℂ)) ^ s))
      = riemannZeta (s - 1) ^ 2 / riemannZeta s

    TheoremThe gcd-sum as a divisor sum of totients#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.gcd_sum_eq_totient_sum

    For every positive integer \(n\), \(\sum_{j=1}^{n} \gcd(j, n) = n \sum_{d \mid n} \varphi(d)/d\), where \(\varphi\) is Euler's totient function and \(d\) runs over the positive divisors of \(n\).

    Grouping the terms with \(\gcd(j, n) = e\), of which there are \(\varphi(n/e)\), shows that the sum equals \(\sum_{e \mid n} e \, \varphi(n/e)\).

    theorem gcd_sum_eq_totient_sum
    (n : ℕ) (hn : 0 < n) :
    (∑ j ∈ Finset.Icc 1 n, (Nat.gcd j n : ℚ)) =
      (n : ℚ) * ∑ d ∈ Nat.divisors n, (Nat.totient d : ℚ) / (d : ℚ)

    DefinitionThe mean gcd-sum function#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.gcdSumMean

    For a natural number \(n\), let \(P(n) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \gcd(j, n)\) be Pillai's gcd-sum function. The mean gcd-sum function is the real-valued function given by \(A(n) = P(n)/n\) for \(n \ge 1\) and \(A(0) = 0\).

    noncomputable def gcdSumMean (n : ℕ) : ℝ :=
  (∑ j ∈ Finset.Icc 1 n, (Nat.gcd j n : ℝ)) / n

    TheoremFactorization of the Dirichlet series of powers of the mean gcd-sum function#●ready to prove

    MetaMathlibExt.LSeries_gcdSumMean_pow_eq_riemannZeta_pow_mul

    Let \(k\) be a natural number and let \(A\) be the mean gcd-sum function. There are functions \(g, h \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{C}\) such that the Dirichlet series \(G_k(s) = \sum_{n \ge 1} g(n) n^{-s}\) converges absolutely for every complex \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re} s > 1/2\), the Dirichlet series \(\tilde G_{k,2}(s) = \sum_{n \ge 1} h(n) n^{-s}\) converges absolutely for every complex \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re} s > 1/3\), and for every complex \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re} s > 1\),

    \[\sum_{n \ge 1} A(n)^k n^{-s} = \zeta(s)^{2^k} G_k(s)\]

    and

    \[G_k(s) \, \zeta(2s)^{4^k - 3^k - \binom{2^k}{2}} = \tilde G_{k,2}(s).\]

    The exponent \(4^k - 3^k - \binom{2^k}{2}\) is a nonnegative integer, since \(3^k + \binom{2^k}{2} \le 4^k\). The paper also gives an explicit Euler product for \(\tilde G_{k,2}\), which is not stated here.

    public theorem_wanted LSeries_gcdSumMean_pow_eq_riemannZeta_pow_mul (k : ℕ) :
    ∃ g h : ℕ → ℂ, (∀ s : ℂ, 1 / 2 < s.re → LSeriesSummable g s) ∧
      (∀ s : ℂ, 1 / 3 < s.re → LSeriesSummable h s) ∧
      ∀ s : ℂ, 1 < s.re →
        LSeries (fun n => ((gcdSumMean n ^ k : ℝ) : ℂ)) s = riemannZeta s ^ (2 ^ k) * LSeries g s ∧
        LSeries g s * riemannZeta (2 * s) ^ (4 ^ k - 3 ^ k - (2 ^ k).choose 2) = LSeries h s
    noncomputable def gcdSumMean (n : ℕ) : ℝ :=
  (∑ j ∈ Finset.Icc 1 n, (Nat.gcd j n : ℝ)) / n

    TheoremAn alternating binomial sum vanishes#●ready to prove

    MetaMathlibExt.alternating_choose_two_pow_mul_pow_eq_zero

    Let \(k\) and \(m\) be natural numbers with \(k \ge 2\) and \(m \ge 2^k - 1\). Then, computed in the integers,

    \[\sum_{r=0}^{\min(2^k,\, m+1)} (-1)^r \binom{2^k}{r} (m + 1 - r)^k = 0.\]

    Since \(m + 1 \ge 2^k\), the upper limit of the sum is \(2^k\).

    This sum is the exponent \(\ell_m\) of the paper: the coefficient of \(p^{-ms}\) in \((1 - p^{-s})^{2^k} \sum_{a \ge 0} (a + 1)^k p^{-as}\), which is why it stops at \(r = m + 1\). The paper prints the sum up to \(r = 2^k\) for every \(m\); that sum vanishes for every \(m\), being a \(2^k\)-th difference of a polynomial of degree \(k\), which contradicts the paper's own \(\ell_2 = 3^k - 4^k + \binom{2^k}{2}\): for \(k = m = 2\) the truncated sum is \(-1\). So with the truncation the hypothesis \(m \ge 2^k - 1\) is needed. The paper's hypothesis \(k \ge 2\) is kept: for \(k = 0\) the term \(r = m + 1\) contributes \(0^0 = 1\) rather than the coefficient of a negative index, so the truncated sum is no longer the coefficient read off from the paper's Euler product.

    public theorem_wanted alternating_choose_two_pow_mul_pow_eq_zero (k m : ℕ) (hk : 2 ≤ k)
    (hm : 2 ^ k - 1 ≤ m) :
    ∑ r ∈ Finset.range (min (2 ^ k) (m + 1) + 1),
      (-1 : ℤ) ^ r * ((2 ^ k).choose r : ℤ) * ((m : ℤ) + 1 - r) ^ k = 0
