    TheoremNonzero integer polynomials have Mahler measure at least one#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.LehmerConstant.one_le_intMahlerMeasure

    If \(f \in \mathbb{Z}[X]\) is a nonzero polynomial, then its Mahler measure satisfies \(M(f) \ge 1\).

    Indeed, \(M(f) = |a| \prod_i \max(1, |\alpha_i|)\), where the leading coefficient \(a\) of \(f\) is a nonzero integer and the \(\alpha_i\) are the complex roots of \(f\) counted with multiplicity, so every factor is at least \(1\).

    theorem one_le_intMahlerMeasure {f : Polynomial ℤ} (hf : f ≠ 0) :
    1 ≤ intMahlerMeasure f

    DefinitionLehmer's question#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.LehmerConstant.LehmerQuestion

    The proposition that there are integer polynomials with Mahler measure arbitrarily close to \(1\) from above: for every real \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is a polynomial \(f \in \mathbb{Z}[X]\) whose Mahler measure \(M(f)\) satisfies \(1 < M(f) < 1 + \varepsilon\).

    Lehmer asked in 1933 whether this proposition holds.

    def LehmerQuestion : Prop :=
  ∀ ε : ℝ, 0 < ε → ∃ f : Polynomial ℤ,
    1 < intMahlerMeasure f ∧ intMahlerMeasure f < 1 + ε

    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.LehmerConstant.smythPolynomial

    Smyth's polynomial is \(X^3 - X - 1 \in \mathbb{Z}[X]\).

    DefinitionReciprocal polynomial#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.NumberTheory.LehmerConstant.IsReciprocal

    An integer polynomial \(f\) is reciprocal when reversing its coefficients gives \(f\) or \(-f\), that is, when \(X^d f(1/X) = f\) or \(X^d f(1/X) = -f\), where \(d\) is the degree of \(f\), taken to be \(0\) when \(f = 0\).
