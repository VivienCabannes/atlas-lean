    DefinitionComplementary pair of sequences#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.IsComplementaryPair

    Two sequences \(a, b : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\), indexed from \(0\), form a complementary pair when all their terms are positive, both are strictly increasing, no term of \(a\) equals a term of \(b\), and every positive integer is a term of \(a\) or a term of \(b\). In particular, the ranges of \(a\) and \(b\) partition the positive integers.

    public def IsComplementaryPair (a b : ℕ → ℕ) : Prop :=
  (∀ n, 0 < a n) ∧ (∀ n, 0 < b n) ∧
    StrictMono a ∧ StrictMono b ∧
    (∀ m n, a m ≠ b n) ∧
    ∀ n, 0 < n → (∃ m, a m = n) ∨ (∃ m, b m = n)

    DefinitionThe \(n\)-th non-value of a sequence#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.nthNonValue

    For \(f : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\) and \(n \ge 1\), the \(n\)-th non-value of \(f\) is the \(n\)-th smallest positive integer that is not of the form \(f(m)\) with \(m \ge 1\), or \(0\) if fewer than \(n\) such integers exist. At \(n = 0\) it takes the same value as at \(n = 1\).

    In the paper's notation, where \(f = f_{\neg P}\) lists the positive integers without a property \(P\), the \(n\)-th non-value of \(f\) is \(f_P(n)\), the \(n\)-th term of the complementary sequence.

    public noncomputable def nthNonValue (f : ℕ → ℕ) (n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  Nat.nth (fun x => 0 < x ∧ ∀ m, 1 ≤ m → f m ≠ x) (n - 1)
