    Narayana numbers as concatenations of repdigits

    DefinitionConcatenations of two repdigits in a general base#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.IsConcatTwoRepdigitsBase

    A natural number \(n\) is a concatenation of two repdigits in base \(b\) if there are digits \(a_1, a_2\) with \(1 \le a_1 \le b - 1\), \(0 \le a_2 \le b - 1\) and \(a_1 \ne a_2\), and block lengths \(l, m \ge 1\), such that \((b - 1) n = a_1 (b^l - 1) b^m + a_2 (b^m - 1)\). The digit conditions force \(b \ge 2\), and the equation says that the base-\(b\) expansion of \(n\) is \(l\) copies of \(a_1\) followed by \(m\) copies of \(a_2\).

    Because of the condition \(a_1 \ne a_2\), a repdigit such as \(4 = (11)_3\) is not a concatenation of two repdigits in base \(3\). For \(b = 10\) this is the notion of a concatenation of two decimal repdigits.

    public def IsConcatTwoRepdigitsBase (b n : ℕ) : Prop :=
  ∃ a₁ a₂ l m : ℕ, 1 ≤ a₁ ∧ a₁ < b ∧ a₂ < b ∧ 1 ≤ l ∧ 1 ≤ m ∧ a₁ ≠ a₂ ∧
    (b - 1) * n = a₁ * (b ^ l - 1) * b ^ m + a₂ * (b ^ m - 1)

    Let \((N_n)\) be Narayana's cows sequence and let \(\alpha\) be a real number with \(\alpha^3 = \alpha^2 + 1\) (so \(\alpha \approx 1.46557\) is the unique real root of \(x^3 - x^2 - 1\)).
