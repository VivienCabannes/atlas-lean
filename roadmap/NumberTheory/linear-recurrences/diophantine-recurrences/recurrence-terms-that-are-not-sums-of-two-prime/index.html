    Recurrence terms that are not sums of two prime powers

    DefinitionSums of two prime powers#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.IsSumOfTwoPrimePowers

    An integer \(n\) is a sum of two prime powers if \(n = p^a + q^b\) for some primes \(p\) and \(q\) and some natural numbers \(a\) and \(b\). Since the exponents may be \(0\), the number \(1 = p^0\) counts as a prime power here.

    public def IsSumOfTwoPrimePowers (n : ℤ) : Prop :=
  ∃ p q a b : ℕ, p.Prime ∧ q.Prime ∧ n = (p : ℤ) ^ a + (q : ℤ) ^ b

    MetaMathlibExt.infinite_thirdOrderRecurrence_with_infinite_not_isSum

    There are infinitely many sequences \((s_n)_{n \ge 0}\) of positive integers with all of the following properties: each value is taken by \(s_n\) for only finitely many \(n\); there are integers \(c_0, c_1, c_2\) with \(c_0 \ne 0\) and \(s_{n+3} = c_0 s_n + c_1 s_{n+1} + c_2 s_{n+2}\) for all \(n \ge 0\); there are no integers \(d_0, d_1\) with \(s_{n+2} = d_0 s_n + d_1 s_{n+1}\) for all \(n \ge 0\); and \(s_k\) is not a sum of two prime powers for infinitely many \(k\).

    The condition that each value is taken only finitely often excludes trivial examples such as periodic sequences taking the value \(1\) infinitely often: no integer below \(2\) is a sum of two prime powers.
