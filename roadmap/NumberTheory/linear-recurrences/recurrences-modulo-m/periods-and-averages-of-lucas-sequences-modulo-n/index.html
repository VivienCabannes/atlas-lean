    DefinitionPeriod of a Lucas sequence modulo \(m\)#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.lucasUPeriod

    For a Lucas sequence \(U = U(P, Q)\) and a natural number \(m\), the least period \(\pi_U(m)\) of \(U\) modulo \(m\) is the minimal period of the pair \((0, 1)\) under the map \((x, y) \mapsto (y, P y - Q x)\) on pairs of residues modulo \(m\), that is, the least \(r > 0\) with \(U_r \equiv 0 \pmod m\) and \(U_{r+1} \equiv 1 \pmod m\), and \(0\) if there is no such \(r\).

    For \(m > 0\), \(\pi_U(m) = 0\) exactly when \(U\) is not purely periodic modulo \(m\), that is, when \(\gcd(m, Q) > 1\). For \(m = 0\) the residues are integers and the congruences are equalities; this value has no meaning in the paper (for the Fibonacci sequence it is \(0\)).

    noncomputable def lucasUPeriod (params : LucasSequenceParams) (m : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  Function.minimalPeriod
    (fun x : ZMod m × ZMod m => (x.2, (params.p : ZMod m) * x.2 - (params.q : ZMod m) * x.1))
    (0, 1)

    MetaMathlibExt.lucasURestrictedPeriod

    For a Lucas sequence \(U = U(P, Q)\) and a natural number \(m\), the least restricted period \(\rho_U(m)\) of \(U\) modulo \(m\) is the least \(s > 0\) such that \(m \mid U_s\) and \(U_{s+1}\) is coprime to \(m\), and \(0\) if there is no such \(s\).

    In the paper's terms, \(U_{s+1} \equiv M \pmod m\) for a multiplier \(M\) coprime to \(m\); for \(s = \rho_U(m)\) this \(U_{s+1}\) is the multiplier \(M_U(m)\).

    DefinitionIntegral averages of all recurrences#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.lucasAllAveragesIntegral

    For integers \(P\) and \(Q \ne 0\) and a natural number \(n\), the property that \(n\) has integral averages means that every integer sequence \((w_k)_{k \ge 0}\) with \(w_{k+2} = P w_{k+1} - Q w_k\) for all \(k\) satisfies \(n \mid w_1 + \dots + w_n\); for \(n > 0\) this says that the average \(A_W(n) = (w_1 + \dots + w_n)/n\) is an integer.

    In the paper's notation \(a = P\) and \(b = -Q\), so the recurrence reads \(w_{k+2} = a w_{k+1} + b w_k\).

    (n : ℤ) ∣ ∑ i ∈ Finset.Icc 1 n, w i
