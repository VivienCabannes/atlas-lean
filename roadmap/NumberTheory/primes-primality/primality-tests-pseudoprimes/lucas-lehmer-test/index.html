    Lucas–Lehmer test

    TheoremLucas–Lehmer test with a general seed#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.acceptableSeed_of_jacobi

    Let \(n \ge 3\) be an odd natural number, let \(M_n = 2^n - 1\), and let \(s_1\) be an integer. Define \(s_{k+1} = s_k^2 - 2\) for \(k \ge 1\). If the Jacobi symbols satisfy \(\left(\frac{s_1 + 2}{M_n}\right) = -1\) and \(\left(\frac{s_1 - 2}{M_n}\right) = 1\), then \(M_n\) is prime if and only if \(M_n\) divides \(s_{n-1}\).

    In the terminology of Roettger and Williams, such a seed \(s_1\) is acceptable: the Lucas–Lehmer test started at \(s_1\) decides the primality of \(M_n\) (their Assertion A).

    Proof: Following Mathlib's Lucas-Lehmer order argument, sufficiency uses a quadratic extension and the least prime factor; necessity uses the Jacobi conditions and Frobenius conjugation as in Lehmer (1930) and Roettger-Williams (2025).

    public theorem acceptableSeed_of_jacobi
    (n : ℕ) (hn_odd : Odd n) (hn_ge : 3 ≤ n) (s₁ : ℤ) :
    jacobiSym (s₁ + 2) (mersenne n) = -1 ∧
      jacobiSym (s₁ - 2) (mersenne n) = 1 →
        (Nat.Prime (mersenne n) ↔
          (mersenne n : ℤ) ∣ Nat.iterate (fun x : ℤ => x ^ 2 - 2) (n - 2) s₁)

    Let \(n \ge 3\), let \(M_n = 2^n - 1\), and define \(s_1 = 6 \cdot 5^{-1}\) and \(s_{k+1} = s_k^2 - 2\) in \(\mathbb{Z}/M_n\mathbb{Z}\).     In both cases \(n\) is odd, so \(5\) does not divide \(M_n\) and \(5^{-1}\) is the inverse of \(5\) modulo \(M_n\).

    DefinitionLehmer's companion sequence#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.lehmerV

    For integers \(R\) and \(Q\), Lehmer's companion sequence \(\bar V_k(R, Q)\), \(k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots\), is the integer sequence with \(\bar V_0 = 2\), \(\bar V_1 = 1\), \(\bar V_2 = R - 2Q\), \(\bar V_3 = R - 3Q\) and \(\bar V_{k+4} = (R - 2Q)\,\bar V_{k+2} - Q^2\,\bar V_k\) for every \(k \ge 0\).

    With \(P = \sqrt R\) and the Lucas sequence \(V_k(P, Q)\) given by \(V_0 = 2\), \(V_1 = P\) and \(V_{k+2} = P V_{k+1} - Q V_k\), for \(R \ne 0\) the term \(\bar V_k(R, Q)\) equals \(V_k(\sqrt R, Q)\) for even \(k\) and \(V_k(\sqrt R, Q)/\sqrt R\) for odd \(k\).

    def lehmerV (R Q : ℤ) : ℕ → ℤ
  | 0 => 2
  | 1 => 1
  | 2 => R - 2 * Q
  | 3 => R - 3 * Q
  | k + 4 => (R - 2 * Q) * lehmerV R Q (k + 2) - Q ^ 2 * lehmerV R Q k

    DefinitionOdd squarefree part#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.IsOddSquarefreePart

    For an integer \(N\) and a natural number \(k\), \(k\) is an odd squarefree part of \(N\), written \(N^* = k\), when \(k\) is odd and squarefree and \(N = 2^a m^2 k\) for some natural number \(a\) and some integer \(m\).

    For positive \(N\) such a \(k\) is unique: it is the squarefree part of \(N\) when that is odd, and half of it otherwise.

    def IsOddSquarefreePart (N : ℤ) (k : ℕ) : Prop :=
  Odd k ∧ Squarefree k ∧ ∃ a : ℕ, ∃ m : ℤ, N = 2 ^ a * m ^ 2 * k

    DefinitionCondition (etar) on Jacobi symbols of \(M_n\)#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.MersenneEtaCondition

    Let \(s^-\), \(s^+\) and \(n\) be natural numbers and let \(M_n = 2^n - 1\). For a natural number \(s\) write \(\left(\frac{M_n}{s}\right)\) for the Jacobi symbol, the product of the Legendre symbols \(\left(\frac{M_n}{p}\right)\) over the prime factors \(p\) of \(s\) counted with multiplicity (so it is \(1\) for \(s = 0\) and \(s = 1\)). Condition (etar) holds for \(s^-\), \(s^+\) and \(n\) when \(s^- = 1\) or \(\left(\frac{M_n}{s^-}\right) = (-1)^{(s^- - 1)/2}\), and moreover \(\left(\frac{M_n}{s^+}\right) = (-1)^{(s^+ + 1)/2}\). Here the exponents are rounded down, and \(s^- - 1\) is read as \(0\) when \(s^- = 0\).

    In the source, \(s^-\) and \(s^+\) are the odd squarefree parts \(s_1^-\) and \(s_1^+\) of \(s_1 - 2\) and \(s_1 + 2\) for a seed \(s_1\). For squarefree \(s\) the Jacobi symbol \(\left(\frac{M_n}{s}\right)\) is the product of \(\eta_n(p) = \left(\frac{M_n}{p}\right)\) over the primes \(p\) dividing \(s\), which is how the source states the condition.

    def MersenneEtaCondition (sm sp n : ℕ) : Prop :=
  (sm = 1 ∨ jacobiSym (mersenne n) sm = (-1) ^ ((sm - 1) / 2)) ∧
    jacobiSym (mersenne n) sp = (-1) ^ ((sp + 1) / 2)
