    Definition\(D\)-Carmichael number#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.IsDCarmichael

    Let \(D\) be an integer. A natural number \(n\) is a \(D\)-Carmichael number when \(D \equiv 0\) or \(1 \pmod 4\), \(n > 1\) is odd and composite, \(D\) and \(n\) are coprime, and for all integers \(P\) and \(Q \ne 0\) with \(P\) and \(Q\) coprime, \(P^2 - 4Q = D\) and \(Q\) coprime to \(n\), the number \(n\) divides the term \(U_{n - \varepsilon_n}(P, Q)\) of the Lucas sequence \(U(P, Q)\), where \(\varepsilon_n = \left(\frac{D}{n}\right)\) is the Jacobi symbol.

    These are Williams' absolute Lucas \(D\)-pseudoprimes. The condition on \(D\) is the paper's standing assumption; for other \(D\) no Lucas sequence has discriminant \(D\), so the last clause would be vacuous.

    ∀ params : LucasSequenceParams, params.IsCoprime → params.p ^ 2 - 4 * params.q = D →
      IsCoprime params.q n → (n : ℤ) ∣ lucasU params (n - jacobiSym D n).toNat
