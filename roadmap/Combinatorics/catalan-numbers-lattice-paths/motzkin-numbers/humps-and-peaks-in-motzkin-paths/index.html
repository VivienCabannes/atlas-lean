    Humps and peaks in Motzkin paths

    TheoremHumps and peaks at a given height in Motzkin paths#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.motzkin_hump_peak_height_distribution

    Let \(n \ge 2\) and \(k \ge 1\). A Motzkin path of length \(n\) is a sequence of \(n\) steps, each up (\(+1\)), flat (\(0\)) or down (\(-1\)), whose partial sums are all nonnegative and whose total is \(0\). A hump at height \(k\) of such a path is a pair of positions \(i < j\) such that step \(i\) is up, step \(j\) is down, every step strictly between them is flat, and the sum of the steps up to and including step \(i\) equals \(k\); a peak at height \(k\) is such a hump with \(j = i + 1\). Let \(H_{n,k}\) be the number of pairs consisting of a Motzkin path of length \(n\) and a hump at height \(k\) of it, and let \(P_{n,k}\) be the same count for peaks. Then, as rational numbers,

    \[H_{n,k} = \sum_{j} \frac{4k}{n - j + 2k} \binom{n}{j} \binom{n - j - 1}{(n - j)/2 + k - 1}\]

    and

    \[P_{n,k} = \sum_{j} \frac{4k}{n - j + 2k} \binom{n - 1}{j} \binom{n - j - 1}{(n - j)/2 + k - 1},\]

    where both sums run over the integers \(0 \le j \le n - 2k\) with \(j \equiv n \pmod 2\).

    The three Motzkin steps are encoded as \(0 = D\) (down), \(1 = F\) (flat) and \(2 = U\) (up). The height change of the step encoded by \(s \in \{0, 1, 2\}\) is the integer \(s - 1\); thus \(D\) changes the height by \(-1\), \(F\) by \(0\) and \(U\) by \(+1\).
