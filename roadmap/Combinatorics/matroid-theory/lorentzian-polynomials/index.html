    DefinitionHessian of a quadratic form#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.LorentzianPolynomialsWanted.LorentzianPolynomials.H.hessian

    For a homogeneous polynomial \(f\) of degree \(2\) in \(\mathbb{R}[w_1, \dots, w_n]\), its Hessian is the real \(n \times n\) matrix \(\mathcal{H}_f\) of second partial derivatives of \(f\): the diagonal entry \((i, i)\) is twice the coefficient of \(w_i^2\) in \(f\), and for \(i \ne j\) the entry \((i, j)\) is the coefficient of \(w_i w_j\) in \(f\).

    noncomputable def H.hessian {n : ℕ} (f : H n 2) : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ :=
  fun i j ↦
    if i = j then
      2 * f.1.coeff (single i 2)
    else
      f.1.coeff (single i 1 + single j 1)
    def H (n d : ℕ) : Type := homogeneousSubmodule (Fin n) ℝ d

    DefinitionStrictly Lorentzian polynomials#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.LorentzianPolynomialsWanted.LorentzianPolynomials.Ŀ

    For natural numbers \(n\) and \(d\), the set \(\mathring{L}^d_n\) of strictly Lorentzian polynomials of degree \(d\) in \(\mathbb{R}[w_1, \dots, w_n]\) is defined by recursion on \(d\). Let \(P^d_n\) be the set of homogeneous polynomials of degree \(d\) in which every monomial of degree \(d\) has a strictly positive coefficient, and let \(\partial_i\) be the partial derivative with respect to \(w_i\). Then \(\mathring{L}^0_n = P^0_n\) and \(\mathring{L}^1_n = P^1_n\); \(\mathring{L}^2_n\) is the set of \(f \in P^2_n\) whose Hessian matrix \((\partial_i \partial_j f)_{i,j}\) is invertible and has exactly one positive eigenvalue, counted with multiplicity; and for \(d \ge 3\), \(\mathring{L}^d_n\) is the set of \(f \in P^d_n\) such that \(\partial_i f \in \mathring{L}^{d-1}_n\) for every \(i\).

    def Ŀ (n d : ℕ) : Set (H n d) := match d with
  | 0 => P n 0
  | 1 => P n 1
  | 2 => { f ∈ P n 2 | IsUnit f.hessian ∧ (f.hessian.charpoly.roots.filter (0 < ·)).card = 1 }
  | d + 3 => { f ∈ P n (d + 3) | ∀ i, ⟨f.1.pderiv i, f.2.pderiv⟩ ∈ Ŀ n (d + 2) }
    def H (n d : ℕ) : Type := homogeneousSubmodule (Fin n) ℝ d

    DefinitionThe set of Lorentzian polynomials#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.LorentzianPolynomialsWanted.LorentzianPolynomials.L

    For natural numbers \(n\) and \(d\), the set \(L^d_n\) of Lorentzian polynomials is a set of homogeneous polynomials of degree \(d\) in \(\mathbb{R}[w_1, \dots, w_n]\), defined by recursion on \(d\). For \(d \le 2\), \(L^d_n = S^d_n\), the set of homogeneous stable polynomials of degree \(d\) with nonnegative coefficients. For \(d \ge 3\), \(L^d_n\) is the set of \(f \in M^d_n\) (the homogeneous polynomials of degree \(d\) with nonnegative coefficients and M-convex support) such that \(\partial_i f \in L^{d-1}_n\) for every \(i \in \{1, \dots, n\}\), where \(\partial_i\) is the partial derivative with respect to \(w_i\).

    This is Definition 2.6 of Brändén and Huh. By their Theorem 2.25 it is the closure of the set of strictly Lorentzian polynomials, and in two variables it consists of the forms whose coefficients are nonnegative, ultra log-concave and without internal zeros (bivariate Lorentzian polynomials).

    TheoremLorentzian polynomials as the closure of strictly Lorentzian polynomials#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.LorentzianPolynomialsWanted.LorentzianPolynomials.theorem_2_25

    For all natural numbers \(n \ge 1\) and \(d\), the closure of the set \(\mathring{L}^d_n\) of strictly Lorentzian polynomials of degree \(d\) in \(n\) variables, taken in the space \(H^d_n\) of homogeneous polynomials of degree \(d\) in \(\mathbb{R}[w_1, \dots, w_n]\) with the Euclidean topology on coefficients, is the set \(L^d_n\) of Lorentzian polynomials of degree \(d\) in \(n\) variables.

    The formal statement assumes \(n \ge 1\), which excludes a degenerate case: for \(n = 0\) and \(d = 2\) the \(0 \times 0\) Hessian of a polynomial cannot have a positive eigenvalue, so \(\mathring{L}^2_0\) is empty, while \(L^2_0 = S^2_0\) contains the zero polynomial, which is homogeneous of degree \(2\).

    The theorem says that the two definitions of Lorentzian polynomials agree: limits of strictly Lorentzian polynomials (Definition 2.1) are exactly the polynomials of Definition 2.6, that is, as the paper notes after the theorem, the homogeneous polynomials with nonnegative coefficients and M-convex support whose quadratic derivatives \(\partial^\alpha f\) have at most one positive eigenvalue. The support condition cannot be dropped: for \(f = w_1^3 + w_2^3\) both partial derivatives are Lorentzian but \(f\) is not (Example 2.4). Brändén and Huh prove one inclusion with a Nuij-type homotopy (Theorem 2.13) and the other through \(c\)-Rayleigh polynomials (Theorem 2.23 and Proposition 2.19).

    DefinitionM-convex set#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Matroid.MConvex.IsMConvex

    A set \(J \subseteq \mathbb{N}^\iota\) is M-convex if for all \(\alpha, \beta \in J\) and every index \(i\) with \(\alpha_i > \beta_i\) there is an index \(j\) with \(\alpha_j < \beta_j\) and \(\alpha - e_i + e_j \in J\), where \(e_i\) is the \(i\)-th unit vector.

    This is the exchange axiom of Murota's discrete convex analysis. Brändén and Huh use it to describe the supports of Lorentzian polynomials: by their Theorem 2.25 the support of every Lorentzian polynomial is M-convex, and by Theorem 3.10 every nonempty M-convex set is the support of one. Every M-convex set lies in a single discrete simplex \(\{\alpha : \alpha_1 + \cdots + \alpha_n = d\}\), and the M-convex sets of zero-one vectors are exactly the sets of bases of matroids.

    The index type \(\iota\) is arbitrary here. The predicate M-convex sets used in the statement of Theorem 2.25 is this one for \(\iota = \{1, \dots, n\}\); its Lean body unfolds to this definition.

    def IsMConvex (J : Set (ι →₀ ℕ)) : Prop :=
  ∀ α ∈ J, ∀ β ∈ J, ∀ i, α i > β i → ∃ j, α j < β j ∧ α - single i 1 + single j 1 ∈ J

    TheoremBase vectors of a matroid are M-convex#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Matroid.MConvex.isMConvex_baseVectors

    For every matroid \(M\) on \(\iota\), the set of indicator vectors of its finite bases is M-convex.

    Write \(\alpha = e_{B_1}\) and \(\beta = e_{B_2}\). The condition \(\alpha_i > \beta_i\) says \(i \in B_1 \setminus B_2\), and the base exchange axiom gives \(j \in B_2 \setminus B_1\) with \((B_1 \setminus \{i\}) \cup \{j\}\) a base; then \(\alpha_j < \beta_j\) and \(\alpha - e_i + e_j\) is the indicator vector of that base.

    This is the implication (8) ⇒ (7) of Brändén and Huh's Theorem 3.10.

    TheoremBivariate Lorentzian polynomials#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.LorentzianToolboxWanted.bivariate_mem_L_iff

    Write a bivariate form of degree \(d\) as \(f = \sum_{k=0}^{d} a_k w_1^k w_2^{d-k}\). Then \(f\) is Lorentzian if and only if the sequence \(a_0, \dots, a_d\)

    • is nonnegative,
    • is ultra log-concave: for \(0 < k < d\), $\(\frac{a_k^2}{\binom{d}{k}^2} \ge \frac{a_{k-1}}{\binom{d}{k-1}} \cdot \frac{a_{k+1}}{\binom{d}{k+1}},\)$
    • and has no internal zeros: \(a_{k_1} a_{k_3} > 0\) implies \(a_{k_2} > 0\) whenever \(k_1 < k_2 < k_3 \le d\).

    This is Brändén and Huh's Example 2.26. It is the bridge from Lorentzian polynomials to inequalities between numbers: specialising a Lorentzian polynomial to two variables by a nonnegative substitution produces an ultra log-concave sequence. For a real-rooted polynomial with nonnegative coefficients the same conclusion is Newton's inequality, the stable case.

    In Lean, \(w_1\) is the variable 0 : Fin 2 and \(w_2\) the variable 1 : Fin 2.

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.LorentzianToolboxWanted.independenceGeneratingPolynomial_mem_L

    For a matroid \(M\) on \([n]\), the homogeneous polynomial of degree \(n\) in \(n + 1\) variables $\(f_M(w_0, w_1, \dots, w_n) = \sum_{A \text{ independent in } M} w^A \, w_0^{\,n - |A|}\)$ lies in \(L^n_{n+1}\).

    For all \(n\) and \(d\), every homogeneous stable polynomial of degree \(d\) in \(\mathbb{R}[w_1, \dots, w_n]\) with nonnegative coefficients lies in the set \(L^d_n\) of Lorentzian polynomials.
