    Hodge theory for matroids

    Adiprasito, Huh and Katz attach to every matroid \(M\) a graded ring \(A^*(M)_\mathbb{R}\), the Chow ring, which for a matroid realised by a hyperplane arrangement is the cohomology ring of the wonderful compactification of the arrangement complement. They prove that it satisfies Poincaré duality, the hard Lefschetz theorem and the Hodge–Riemann relations, although for a non-realizable matroid there is no variety behind it. The coefficients of the reduced characteristic polynomial are mixed degrees in this ring, and the Hodge–Riemann relations in degree at most one make them log-concave: this is the proof of the Heron–Rota–Welsh conjecture.

    The ring and its basic classes are defined and proved here; the degree map, the hard Lefschetz theorem, the Hodge–Riemann relations and the mixed-degree formula are wanted statements.

    AbbreviationNonempty proper flats of a matroid#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Matroid.Chow.ProperFlat

    For a matroid \(M\) on \(E\), the nonempty proper flats are the flats \(F\) of \(M\) (Mathlib's Matroid.IsFlat) with \(F \ne \emptyset\) and \(F \ne E\). They index the variables \(x_F\) of the Chow ring. For a loopless matroid \(\emptyset\) is a flat, and these are the flats strictly between the bottom and the top of the lattice of flats.

    abbrev ProperFlat : Type _ := {F : Set α // M.IsFlat F ∧ F.Nonempty ∧ F ≠ M.E}

    DefinitionThe Chow ideal \(I_M + J_M\) of a matroid#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Matroid.Chow.chowIdeal

    In the polynomial ring \(S_M = \mathbb{R}[x_F]\), with one variable for each nonempty proper flat \(F\) of \(M\), the ideal \(I_M\) is generated by the monomials \(x_{F_1} x_{F_2}\) for incomparable flats \(F_1, F_2\), and the ideal \(J_M\) by the linear forms $\(\sum_{F \ni i_1} x_F - \sum_{F \ni i_2} x_F\)$ for distinct elements \(i_1, i_2\) of the ground set. This is the ideal \(I_M + J_M\).

    The sums are finsums; for a finite matroid they are finite sums.

    noncomputable def chowIdeal : Ideal (MvPolynomial (ProperFlat M) ℝ) :=
  Ideal.span {p | ∃ F G : ProperFlat M, ¬ F.1 ⊆ G.1 ∧ ¬ G.1 ⊆ F.1 ∧ p = X F * X G} ⊔
    Ideal.span {p | ∃ i ∈ M.E, ∃ j ∈ M.E, i ≠ j ∧
      p = (∑ᶠ (F : ProperFlat M) (_ : i ∈ F.1), X F) - ∑ᶠ (F : ProperFlat M) (_ : j ∈ F.1), X F}
    abbrev ProperFlat : Type _ := {F : Set α // M.IsFlat F ∧ F.Nonempty ∧ F ≠ M.E}

    AbbreviationThe Chow ring \(A^*(M)_\mathbb{R}\) of a matroid#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Matroid.Chow.ChowRing

    The Chow ring of a matroid \(M\) is $\(A^*(M)_\mathbb{R} = \mathbb{R}[x_F]_{F} \,/\, (I_M + J_M),\)$ the polynomial ring with one variable for each nonempty proper flat, modulo the incomparability relations \(x_{F_1} x_{F_2} = 0\) and the linear relations \(\sum_{F \ni i_1} x_F = \sum_{F \ni i_2} x_F\).

    The presentation is due to Feichtner and Yuzvinsky. When \(M\) is realised by a hyperplane arrangement, \(A^*(M)_\mathbb{R}\) is the cohomology ring of the wonderful compactification of De Concini and Procesi; in general it is the Chow ring of the smooth, non-compact toric variety of the Bergman fan of \(M\).

    abbrev ChowRing : Type _ := MvPolynomial (ProperFlat M) ℝ ⧸ chowIdeal M
    abbrev ProperFlat : Type _ := {F : Set α // M.IsFlat F ∧ F.Nonempty ∧ F ≠ M.E}

    DefinitionGraded piece \(A^q(M)_\mathbb{R}\) of the Chow ring#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Matroid.Chow.gradedPiece

    \(A^q(M)_\mathbb{R}\) is the image in the Chow ring of the homogeneous polynomials of degree \(q\) in the variables \(x_F\). Since \(I_M + J_M\) is generated by homogeneous elements, these subspaces form a grading of \(A^*(M)_\mathbb{R}\). For a loopless matroid of rank \(r + 1\), \(A^r(M)_\mathbb{R} \cong \mathbb{R}\) through the degree map.

    noncomputable def gradedPiece (q : ℕ) : Submodule ℝ (ChowRing M) :=
  (homogeneousSubmodule (ProperFlat M) ℝ q).map
    (Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ ℝ (chowIdeal M)).toLinearMap
    abbrev ChowRing : Type _ := MvPolynomial (ProperFlat M) ℝ ⧸ chowIdeal M

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Matroid.Chow.x_mul_x_eq_zero_of_incomparable

    DefinitionThe class \(\alpha_M\)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Matroid.Chow.alphaClass

    For an element \(i\) of the ground set, \(\alpha_{M,i} = \sum_{F \ni i} x_F\), the sum over the nonempty proper flats containing \(i\). By the linear relations its class \(\alpha_M\) in \(A^1(M)_\mathbb{R}\) does not depend on \(i\). The class \(\alpha_M\) is nef (AHK Lemma 9.7), and for a loopless matroid of rank \(r + 1\) the class \(\alpha_M^r\) has degree \(1\) (AHK Proposition 5.10).

    noncomputable def alphaClass (i : α) : ChowRing M :=
  Ideal.Quotient.mk (chowIdeal M) (∑ᶠ (F : ProperFlat M) (_ : i ∈ F.1), X F)

    DefinitionStrictly submodular function#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.Matroid.Chow.IsStrictlySubmodular

    A real function \(c\) on the nonempty proper subsets of the ground set \(E\) is strictly submodular if $\(c(I_1) + c(I_2) > c(I_1 \cap I_2) + c(I_1 \cup I_2)\)$ for any two incomparable subsets \(I_1, I_2 \subseteq E\), where \(c(\emptyset)\) and \(c(E)\) are replaced by zero. In Lean \(c\) is a function on all sets with \(c(\emptyset) = c(E) = 0\); its values on sets not contained in \(E\) play no role.

    The rank function of a matroid, restricted to the nonempty proper subsets, is a limit of strictly submodular functions. A strictly submodular function gives a strictly convex piecewise linear function on the Bergman fan, that is, an ample class in the Chow ring.

    TheoremHodge–Riemann relations for matroids#●ready to prove

    MathlibExt.Combinatorics.MatroidHodgeTheoryWanted.hodgeRiemann

    Let \(M\) be a finite loopless matroid of rank \(r + 1\), \(\deg\) its degree map, and \(\ell = \ell(c)\) the class of a strictly submodular function \(c\). For every integer \(q\) with \(2q \le r\), the symmetric bilinear form $\(Q_\ell^q(a_1, a_2) = (-1)^q \deg(a_1 \, \ell^{r-2q} \, a_2)\)$ on \(A^q(M)_\mathbb{R}\) is positive definite on the kernel of multiplication by \(\ell^{r-2q+1}\): if \(a \in A^q(M)_\mathbb{R}\) is nonzero and \(\ell^{r-2q+1} a = 0\), then \((-1)^q \deg(a \, \ell^{r-2q} a) > 0\).

    This is part (2) of the main theorem of Adiprasito, Huh and Katz. Suppose \(r \ge 2\). The hard Lefschetz theorem gives the decomposition \(A^1(M)_\mathbb{R} = \langle \ell \rangle \oplus P^1_\ell\) with \(P^1_\ell = \ker(\ell^{r-1})\), orthogonal for the form \((a_1, a_2) \mapsto \deg(a_1 \ell^{r-2} a_2)\). The relation in degree \(0\) makes this form positive on \(\ell\) (\(\deg \ell^r > 0\)), and the relation in degree \(1\) makes it negative definite on \(P^1_\ell\). Together they say that the form has exactly one positive eigenvalue on \(A^1(M)_\mathbb{R}\) (this is the argument in the proof of AHK Lemma 9.6). A limiting argument for nef classes then gives the inequality \(\deg(\ell_1^2 \ell_2^{r-2}) \deg(\ell_2^r) \le \deg(\ell_1 \ell_2^{r-1})^2\) (AHK Lemma 9.6), the source of the log-concavity of characteristic polynomials. Huh's ICM survey explains this "one positive eigenvalue" consequence of the Hodge–Riemann relations as the common mechanism behind many log-concavity results.

    • Tropical varieties: the Chow ring of \(M\) is the Chow ring of the toric variety of its Bergman fan, the tropical linear space of \(M\).
    • Face numbers of convex polytopes: the face numbers of simplicial polytopes, where McMullen's hard Lefschetz theorem for simple polytopes, the model for the proof here, gives the necessity part of the g-theorem.
