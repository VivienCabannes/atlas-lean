    Keyword substitutions on binary words

    DefinitionApplicability of a keyword#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.KeywordApplicable

    Let \(a = a_0 a_1 \cdots a_m\) be a binary keyword of length \(m + 1\) and \(u = u_0 u_1 \cdots u_{n-1}\) a binary word of length \(n\). The keyword \(a\) is applicable to \(u\) at the (\(0\)-indexed) letter \(i\) when \(i + m < n\) and the subword \(u_i u_{i+1} \cdots u_{i+m}\) of \(u\) equals \(a\) or the letterwise negation of \(a\).

    def KeywordApplicable {m n : ℕ} (a : Fin (m + 1) → Bool) (i : ℕ) (u : Fin n → Bool) :
    Prop :=
  ∃ h : i + m < n,
    (∀ t : Fin (m + 1), u ⟨i + t, by have := t.isLt; omega⟩ = a t) ∨
      (∀ t : Fin (m + 1), u ⟨i + t, by have := t.isLt; omega⟩ = !a t)

    DefinitionSimple map#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.keywordSimpleMap

    Let \(a\) be a keyword, a binary word of length \(m + 1\), and let \(i \in \mathbb{N}\). The simple map \(\varphi_i\) sends a binary word \(u\) of length \(n\) to the word obtained by negating the letters of \(u\) at positions \(i, \dots, i + m\) (positions counted from \(0\)) if \(a\) is applicable to \(u\) at letter \(i\), that is, if \(i + m < n\) and the subword of \(u\) at positions \(i, \dots, i + m\) equals \(a\) or its letterwise negation \(\neg a\); otherwise \(\varphi_i(u) = u\).

    The paper counts letters from \(1\), so its simple map \(\varphi_i\) is \(\varphi_{i-1}\) in this numbering.

    noncomputable def keywordSimpleMap {m n : ℕ} (a : Fin (m + 1) → Bool) (i : ℕ)
    (u : Fin n → Bool) : Fin n → Bool :=
  if KeywordApplicable a i u then
    fun j => if i ≤ j.val ∧ j.val ≤ i + m then !u j else u j
  else u

    DefinitionSubstitution graph#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.keywordGraph

    Let \(a\) be a keyword, a binary word of length \(m + 1\), and let \(n \ge 0\). The substitution graph \(\mathcal{G}_n^{(a)}\) is the simple graph on the binary words of length \(n\) in which distinct words \(u\) and \(v\) are adjacent when \(\varphi_i(u) = v\) or \(\varphi_i(v) = u\) for some \(i \in \mathbb{N}\), where \(\varphi_i\) is the simple map of \(a\) at letter \(i\). Equivalently, \(u\) and \(v\) are adjacent when one is obtained from the other by replacing an occurrence of \(a\) by \(\neg a\) or an occurrence of \(\neg a\) by \(a\), where \(\neg a\) is the letterwise negation of \(a\).

    def keywordGraph {m : ℕ} (a : Fin (m + 1) → Bool) (n : ℕ) : SimpleGraph (Fin n → Bool) :=
  SimpleGraph.fromRel fun u v => ∃ i : ℕ, keywordSimpleMap a i u = v

    Let \(a\) be a binary keyword and let \(u\) and \(v\) be binary words of length \(n\). Then \(u\) and \(v\) are adjacent in the graph \(\mathcal{G}_n^{(a)}\) if and only if \(u \ne v\) and some simple map \(\varphi_i\) of \(a\) sends \(u\) to \(v\) or sends \(v\) to \(u\).

    DefinitionLists of simple maps acting completely#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.KeywordActsCompletely

    Let \(a\) be a binary keyword and \(u\) a binary word. A list \([j_1, \dots, j_r]\) of indices acts completely on \(u\) when applying the simple maps \(\varphi_{j_1}, \varphi_{j_2}, \dots, \varphi_{j_r}\) of \(a\) in this order changes the word at every step: with \(u_0 = u\) and \(u_q = \varphi_{j_q}(u_{q-1})\), we have \(u_q \ne u_{q-1}\) for \(1 \le q \le r\). In particular the empty list acts completely on every word.

    def KeywordActsCompletely {m n : ℕ} (a : Fin (m + 1) → Bool) :
    List ℕ → (Fin n → Bool) → Prop
  | [], _ => True
  | j :: l, u => keywordSimpleMap a j u ≠ u ∧ KeywordActsCompletely a l (keywordSimpleMap a j u)
