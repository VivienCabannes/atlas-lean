    DefinitionValid crossing sequences from a state of the bridge and torch problem#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.BridgeTorchGeneralizations.bridgeScheduleFrom

    Let \(c\) be a natural number and let \(P\) and \(L\) be finite sets of natural numbers, the people and the left bank. Whether a finite sequence \(A_1, \dots, A_k\) of finite sets of natural numbers (the crossings) is valid from the left bank \(L\) with the torch on a given side is defined by recursion on \(k\). The empty sequence is valid exactly when \(L = \emptyset\) and the torch is on the right. With the torch on the left, the sequence is valid when \(A_1\) is a nonempty subset of \(L\) with at most \(c\) elements and \(A_2, \dots, A_k\) is valid from the left bank \(L \setminus A_1\) with the torch on the right. With the torch on the right, it is valid when \(A_1\) is a nonempty subset of \(P \setminus L\) with at most \(c\) elements and \(A_2, \dots, A_k\) is valid from the left bank \(L \cup A_1\) with the torch on the left.

    def bridgeScheduleFrom (c : ℕ) (P : Finset ℕ) : Finset ℕ → Bool → List (Finset ℕ) → Bool
  | L, torchLeft, [] => decide (L = ∅) && !torchLeft
  | L, true, A :: σ =>
      decide (A.Nonempty ∧ A.card ≤ c ∧ A ⊆ L) && bridgeScheduleFrom c P (L \ A) false σ
  | L, false, A :: σ =>
      decide (A.Nonempty ∧ A.card ≤ c ∧ A ⊆ P \ L) && bridgeScheduleFrom c P (L ∪ A) true σ

    DefinitionBridge schedules#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.BridgeTorchGeneralizations.IsBridgeSchedule

    Let \(c\) and \(n\) be natural numbers. A finite sequence \(\sigma\) of finite sets of natural numbers (the crossings) is a schedule for the people \(1, \dots, n\) on a bridge of capacity \(c\) with one torch when the following holds. Start with all of \(1, \dots, n\) and the torch on the left bank. The crossings at even positions (counting from \(0\)) are forward moves: each is a nonempty set of at most \(c\) people then on the left bank, who cross with the torch to the right bank. The crossings at odd positions are returns: each is a nonempty set of at most \(c\) people then on the right bank, who cross with the torch back to the left bank. After the last crossing the left bank is empty and the torch is on the right bank.

    def IsBridgeSchedule (c n : ℕ) (σ : List (Finset ℕ)) : Prop :=
  bridgeScheduleFrom c (Finset.Icc 1 n) (Finset.Icc 1 n) true σ = true

    DefinitionTotal time of a schedule#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.BridgeTorchGeneralizations.scheduleCost

    For a finite sequence \(\sigma = (A_1, \dots, A_k)\) of finite sets of natural numbers (the crossings), the total time of \(\sigma\) is \(\sum_{i=1}^{k} \max A_i\), where \(\max \emptyset = 0\).     DefinitionOptimal crossing time of the bridge and torch problem#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.BridgeTorchGeneralizations.bridgeTime

    For a bridge of capacity \(c\) and \(n\) people \(1, \dots, n\), where person \(i\) has crossing time \(i\), the optimal crossing time is the least total time of a complete schedule with one torch; the total time of a schedule is the sum, over its crossings, of the largest crossing time among the people in that crossing. It is written \(T(n)\) for \(c = 2\) and \(T_3(n)\) for \(c = 3\). If no complete schedule exists (for example when \(n = 0\)), the value is \(0\).

    noncomputable def bridgeTime (c n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  sInf {t | ∃ σ, IsBridgeSchedule c n σ ∧ scheduleCost σ = t}

    DefinitionRight bank after a sequence of crossings#✓fully proved

    MetaMathlibExt.BridgeTorchGeneralizations.rightBank

    Let \(n\) be a natural number and let \(\tau = (A_1, \dots, A_k)\) be a finite sequence of finite sets of natural numbers. The right bank after \(\tau\) is the set of those \(p \in \{1, \dots, n\}\) for which the number of indices \(i\) with \(p \in A_i\) is odd.

    When \(\tau\) is a prefix of a schedule for the people \(1, \dots, n\), these are the people who have crossed an odd number of times, so they stand on the right bank.

    def rightBank (n : ℕ) (τ : List (Finset ℕ)) : Finset ℕ :=
  (Finset.Icc 1 n).filter fun p => Odd (τ.countP fun A => decide (p ∈ A))
