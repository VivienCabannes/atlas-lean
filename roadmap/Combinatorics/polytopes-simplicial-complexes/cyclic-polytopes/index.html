    Cyclic polytopes

    The cyclic polytope \(C_d(n)\) is the convex hull of \(n\) points on the moment curve \(\gamma(\tau) = (\tau, \tau^2, \dots, \tau^d)\) in \(\mathbb{R}^d\). The moment curve has order \(d\): no \(d + 1\) of its points lie on a hyperplane. A linear functional restricted to the curve is a polynomial of degree at most \(d\), which is how its facets (Gale's evenness criterion) and its low-dimensional faces (neighborliness) are found. Cyclic polytopes have the largest face numbers among polytopes with \(n\) vertices in dimension \(d\) (the upper bound theorem).

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Polytope.momentCurve

    For \(d \ge 0\) and \(t \in \mathbb{R}\), the moment curve in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) is $\(\gamma(t) = (t, t^2, \dots, t^d).\)$ A curve in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) has order (degree) \(d\) when no \(d + 1\) of its points lie on a common affine hyperplane; the moment curve is the standard example, and cyclic polytopes are convex hulls of finitely many of its points.

    TheoremThe moment curve has order \(d\)#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Polytope.affineIndependent_momentCurve

    Let \(m \le d + 1\) and let \(t_1, \dots, t_m\) be distinct real numbers. Then the points \(\gamma(t_1), \dots, \gamma(t_m)\) of the moment curve in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) are affinely independent. For \(m = d + 1\) this says that no \(d + 1\) points of the curve lie on a common affine hyperplane, so the moment curve has order \(d\) in the sense of the PCMI notes.

    Proof: an affine dependence \(\sum_i w_i \gamma(t_i) = 0\), \(\sum_i w_i = 0\) gives \(\sum_i w_i t_i^k = 0\) for \(0 \le k \le m - 1\), so \(w\) is in the left kernel of the Vandermonde matrix \((t_i^k)\), whose determinant \(\prod_{i<j}(t_j - t_i)\) is nonzero.

    theorem affineIndependent_momentCurve {d m : ℕ} (hm : m ≤ d + 1) {t : Fin m → ℝ}
    (ht : Function.Injective t) :
    AffineIndependent ℝ (fun i => momentCurve d (t i))

    DefinitionVertices of a cyclic polytope#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Polytope.cyclicVertices

    For parameters \(t_1, \dots, t_n \in \mathbb{R}\), the finite set \(\{\gamma(t_1), \dots, \gamma(t_n)\} \subset \mathbb{R}^d\) of points on the moment curve. When the parameters are distinct and \(d \ge 1\) these are \(n\) distinct points. For \(d \ge 2\) all of them are vertices of their convex hull, the cyclic polytope, because the parabola \((t, t^2)\) is strictly convex. For \(d = 1\) the points lie on a line and only the smallest and the largest are vertices.

    noncomputable def cyclicVertices (d : ℕ) {n : ℕ} (t : Fin n → ℝ) :
    Finset (EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin d)) :=
  Finset.univ.image fun i => momentCurve d (t i)

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Polytope.cyclicPolytope

    DefinitionGale's evenness condition#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Polytope.GaleEvenness

    A set \(S \subseteq \{0, 1, \dots, n - 1\}\) satisfies Gale's evenness condition if for any two indices \(j < k\) not in \(S\), the number of elements \(i \in S\) with \(j < i < k\) is even. For example, with \(n = 4\) the sets \(\{0, 1\}\), \(\{1, 2\}\), \(\{2, 3\}\) and \(\{0, 3\}\) satisfy it and \(\{0, 2\}\), \(\{1, 3\}\) do not: these are the edges and the diagonals of a convex quadrilateral.

    def GaleEvenness {n : ℕ} (S : Finset (Fin n)) : Prop :=
  ∀ j k : Fin n, j ∉ S → k ∉ S → j < k → Even (S.filter fun i => j < i ∧ i < k).card

    TheoremGale's evenness criterion#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Polytope.isExposed_cyclicPolytope_iff_galeEvenness

    Let \(t_1 < t_2 < \cdots < t_n\) be real numbers and let \(S\) be a set of \(d\) indices. Then the points \(\gamma(t_i)\), \(i \in S\), span a face of the cyclic polytope \(C_d(n) = \operatorname{conv}\{\gamma(t_1), \dots, \gamma(t_n)\} \subset \mathbb{R}^d\), that is, \(\operatorname{conv}\{\gamma(t_i) : i \in S\}\) is a face of \(C_d(n)\), if and only if \(S\) satisfies Gale's evenness condition. Since \(d\) points of the moment curve are affinely independent, for \(n > d\) such a face is a facet, so the criterion lists the facets of \(C_d(n)\); in particular the combinatorial type of \(C_d(n)\) does not depend on the parameters.

    Proof. A linear functional \(\ell\) on \(\mathbb{R}^d\) restricted to the moment curve is a polynomial \(\ell(\gamma(\tau))\) of degree at most \(d\) without constant term, and every such polynomial arises. If the face is cut out by \(\ell\) with maximum \(V\), the polynomial \(g(\tau) = V - \ell(\gamma(\tau))\) vanishes at the \(d\) points \(t_i\), \(i \in S\), and is positive at the other \(t_j\) (a point \(\gamma(t_j)\), \(j \notin S\), on the face would lie in the convex hull of the \(\gamma(t_i)\), which a degree-\(d\) polynomial vanishing exactly on \(S\) rules out). Hence \(g = c \prod_{i \in S} (\tau - t_i)\), whose sign at \(t_j\) is that of \(c\) times \((-1)^{|\{i \in S : i > j\}|}\); constant sign at all \(j \notin S\) is the evenness condition. Conversely, under the evenness condition \(\pm \prod_{i \in S} (\tau - t_i)\), with the sign chosen negative at the \(t_j\), \(j \notin S\), comes from a functional cutting out exactly this face. This is the argument sketched in the PCMI notes, where a hyperplane through \(d\) points of the curve meets it in no other point.

    Degenerate cases are covered: for \(n = d\) the only set is \(S = \{0, \dots, n-1\}\), the condition is vacuous and the face is \(C_d(n)\) itself; for \(d = 0\), \(S = \emptyset\) and the face is empty.

    theorem isExposed_cyclicPolytope_iff_galeEvenness {d n : ℕ} {t : Fin n → ℝ} (ht : StrictMono t)
    (S : Finset (Fin n)) (hS : S.card = d) :
    IsExposed ℝ (cyclicPolytope d t)
        (convexHull ℝ ((fun i => momentCurve d (t i)) '' (S : Set (Fin n)))) ↔
      GaleEvenness S

    TheoremCyclic polytopes are neighborly#✓fully proved

    MathlibExt.Geometry.Polytope.isExposed_cyclicPolytope_of_two_mul_card_le

    Let \(t_1, \dots, t_n\) be distinct real numbers and let \(T\) be a set of indices with \(2|T| \le d\). Then \(\operatorname{conv}\{\gamma(t_i) : i \in T\}\) is a face of the cyclic polytope \(C_d(n) \subset \mathbb{R}^d\).     MathlibExt.Geometry.Polytope.faceNumber_cyclicSimplex

    For \(d + 1\) distinct parameters, the simplex \(C_d(d+1)\) has $\(f_k = \binom{d+1}{k+1}\)$ faces of dimension \(k\), for every \(k \ge 0\) (in particular \(f_d = 1\) and \(f_k = 0\) for \(k > d\)).
